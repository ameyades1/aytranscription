## Opening and closing
**Opening (start):** The session picks up straight after the earlier telling of how Ashma Rishi taught Raja Janak [«जनक राजा को अश्मा ने»]. He asks for the Ashma Gita PPT to be opened quickly, saying "अब आपको सब समझ में आएगा", and begins with the Shibir's title slide.

**Closing (end):** The recording cuts off in the middle of a thought. He is explaining the Sharma ji / Verma ji story with the example of a donated bench on Marine Drive ("ये कुर्सी हमने दी है") and has not yet drawn the point from it [«शर्मा जी, वर्मा जी की कथा»].

## Teachings in order
1. **(start)** The Shibir's title was chosen with care to give clarity: "जब जीवन आपको तोड़े, अश्मा गीता जोड़े". The central question is how much of life is in the hands of प्रारब्ध and how much in the hands of पुरुषार्थ [«कितना part प्रारब्ध के हाथ»].
2. **(start)** The aim is to apply this to life today. "आज का युधिष्ठिर कौन है? वो मैं ही हूँ": I want to be king, I have to fight the war, and I must fight my own धर्मयुद्ध [«आज का युधिष्ठिर कौन है»].
3. **(start)** The book in their hands is Ashma Gita. He checks that the word अश्मा is now clear to them (it was explained earlier: recap).
4. **(early)** Ashma Gita is in the Shanti Parva. शांति means the war is over and all is quiet. The Ashvamedha and coronation come later, in a separate parva [«शांति मतलब युद्ध अभी»].
5. **(early)** Frame: Vaishampayan tells Janamejaya about the dialogue between Vyas and Yudhishthir. He does not go back over the background story because it was told before (recap).
6. **(early)** Each listener should see himself as Yudhishthir: धर्मराज, who fights धर्मयुद्ध and stays steady in his own life's war. To live happily as one's own master, one must become like a राजा. One must then go to a व्यास, the guru "जो टुकड़ा-टुकड़ा करके आपका problem solve करके दे सकता है". Ashma is the "rock solid" foundation [«मैं राजा युधिष्ठिर हूँ, ऐसा»].
7. **(early)** After winning, Yudhishthir refuses the throne. Guilt has come in: "मैं पापी हूँ". We do the same: "मेरी वजह से हुआ".
8. **(early)** Yudhishthir acted on the order of his guru and of Shri Krishna, and felt nothing while acting. His state is "more dangerous than अर्जुन". Arjun put down his bow *before* acting. Yudhishthir regrets *after* acting and winning [«it is more dangerous than अर्जुन»].
9. **(early)** General lesson: think before you act (business, a wrong deed, a sin), not after. Regret afterwards is the worse problem (see the love-marriage example).
10. **(early)** Yudhishthir had not even done wrong. He acted under the guru's guidance (नरो वा कुंजरो वा, Shikhandi). So why is he weeping now?
11. **(early–middle)** He goes through Yudhishthir's list of guilts with comments. Bhishma was killed through Shikhandi, by छल-कपट. Drona: "मैंने झूठ बोला", which betrayed Drona's complete trust in Dharmaraj, only because Krishna told him to. Karna was killed when his chariot wheel sank, felt to be by deceit. Abhimanyu was sent into the चक्रव्यूह on "our decision". Then the Kauravas, Duryodhan, Shakuni and the lakhs of soldiers [«मैंने झूठ बोल दिया, just because»].
12. **(middle)** "This Gita carries more meaning." The Bhagavad Gita has not become secondary, but when this problem comes into your life, "अश्मा गीता is more important than भगवद्गीता" [«This Gita carries more meaning»].
13. **(middle)** Yudhishthir decides to give up kingdom, food and life as प्रायश्चित and goes to the forest. "बहुत सारे लोग ऐसा करते हैं." He asks the audience whether this was a right decision or a foolish one.
14. **(middle)** This state is where the ब्रह्मविद्या of Ashma Gita begins.
15. **(middle)** Meaning of युधिष्ठिर: युद्ध + स्थिर (from the धातु स्था). It means one who stays calm in war, or fights with a calm mind, or handles a war-like situation properly [«युद्ध के समय शांत रहने वाला»].
16. **(middle)** Which war? धर्मयुद्ध, the one the guru tells you to fight. कुरुक्षेत्र is not only the place in the north. कुरु = कर्म, "जो मैं कर रहा हूँ". The field where my body acts should become a धर्मक्षेत्र. (Uncertain wording: "अगर धर्म उसमें डाल दिया तो problem होगा"; he may have meant "if dharma is *not* put in".) [«कुरु मतलब कर्म»]
17. **(middle)** The key dilemma: should I make a संधि with प्रारब्ध, or fight it with पुरुषार्थ? Accept, or change? "Many people's problem." He tells the two-patients story (see Stories).
18. **(middle)** Moral of the story: the medicine is right, but the patient applies it the wrong way round. "It is exactly same, ये प्रारब्ध का and पुरुषार्थ का खेल." There are two diseases [«It is exactly same, ये»].
19. **(middle)** **Eye disease:** failing to see God everywhere ("वासुदेवः सर्वम्"). Not seeing God in the person who quarrels with you or hurts you. "Lord in the form of XYZ person has come to teach me a lesson." The cure is पुरुषार्थ: it takes much effort before you can see God in the person who hurts you [«आँख की बीमारी क्या थी»].
20. **(middle)** **Stomach disease:** digesting the सुख-दुःख that प्रारब्ध has already brought, and earning money ("पापी पेट का सवाल है"). The cure is to accept प्रारब्ध. He corrects himself mid-sentence ("sorry") [«पेट की बीमारी है»].
21. **(middle–late)** Mapping onto the four पुरुषार्थ: अर्थ and काम are the stomach disease, cured by accepting प्रारब्ध. धर्म and मोक्ष are the eye disease, cured by doing पुरुषार्थ [«चार पुरुषार्थ बोलते हैं ना»].
22. **(late)** Which wife or husband you get (quarrelsome or good), what kind of boss, how much money: these are अर्थ/काम. Accept them as प्रारब्ध, "क्योंकि वही पत्नी या पति आने वाला था".
23. **(late)** But in that कुरुक्षेत्र, my धर्मक्षेत्र is also to be built: deciding what my dharma is with that person. That is पुरुषार्थ. Image: Shiva has come with his third eye open to burn me, so now find how to calm his anger, or whether to leave [«परमेश्वर शिवजी आ गए हैं»].
24. **(late)** "कहाँ क्या करना है, यही लोगों को समझ में नहीं आता." Only one who knows when to make a truce with प्रारब्ध and when to fight it can stay steady in war.
25. **(late)** That is why Yudhishthir is called धर्मराज: he understood real dharma. Dharma = कर्म, कर्तव्य कर्म. He could decide by the situation when to accept. "But big problem": he fought, and *then* the guilt came.
26. **(late)** Everyone has a war: money troubles, sour relationships, repeated failure. "महाभारत सबके जीवन में चल रहा है."
27. **(late–end)** At the root of these struggles are two forces: प्रारब्ध and ऋणानुबन्ध. "वही व्यक्ति आएगा, and उसके साथ अच्छा-बुरा होना". He refers back to the Sharma ji / Verma ji story (recap) and begins the bench example. Cut off.

## Main-text shlokas covered
None. Only the background slides (Vyas–Yudhishthir frame) are covered. No verse of Ashma Gita is read.

## Other scripture cited
- **"वासुदेवः सर>म्"** (middle): only this fragment is spoken. It probably comes from Bhagavad Gita 7.19 ("वासुदेवः सर्वमिति"), but he gives no reference. It supports the "eye disease" point: seeing God in everyone.
- **"नरो वा कुंजरो वा"** (early, and again middle): the well-known Mahabharata phrase about the Ashvatthama lie. It is a saying, not a shloka. It supports the point about Yudhishthir's guilt over Drona.
- Popular saying **"पापी पेट का सवाल है"** (middle): stomach = earning a living.

## Stories, examples and images
- **(start)** Title slides in Hindi, and in English "for modern लड़के who don't understand Hindi".
- **(early)** व्यास as the guru who solves your problem "टुकड़ा-टुकड़ा". Ashma as a "rock solid" foundation.
- **(early)** Arjun vs Yudhishthir: Arjun's विषाद came before acting, Yudhishthir's after winning, which is "more dangerous".
- **(early)** **Love-marriage example:** people make a love marriage, then say "मत करो" afterwards. "अरे! गलती हो गई." Think before, not after [«पहले love marriage कर दिया»].
- **(early–middle)** Mahabharata episodes as Yudhishthir's guilts: Shikhandi and Bhishma, the Drona lie and Drona's trust, Karna's sunk chariot, Abhimanyu in the चक्रव्यूह.
- **(middle)** **Two patients and one doctor:** both have eye and stomach trouble and get the same eye drops and चूर्ण. The first is cured in four days. The second comes back shouting and accusing the doctor ("तुम्हारे पिता से क्या problem था?"). It turns out he drank the drops and put the चूर्ण in his eyes. "दवा same, problem same". He laughs [«दो patients थे और एक doctor»]. He says he always tells this story.
- **(middle)** "Lord in the form of XYZ person has come to teach me a lesson" (said twice).
- **(late)** Quarrelsome wife/husband, boss, how much money: examples of प्रारब्ध to accept.
- **(late)** **Shiva with the third eye open**, come to burn me to ash: the image of a difficult person. Your पुरुषार्थ is how to calm the anger, or whether to leave.
- **(end)** Sharma ji / Verma ji story, referred to as already told (recap).
- **(end)** **Donated bench:** you never notice benches on Marine Drive, Mumbai. But if you donated ₹2000–5000 and your name plate is on one, you would detour every day: "ये कुर्सी हमने दी है". Cut off before the point is drawn [«लेकिन गलती से आपने कुछ»].

## Practices, instructions and Q&A
- **Instruction:** see yourself as Yudhishthir, the धर्मराज of your own life's war.
- **Rhetorical questions to the audience:** whether the title gives clarity, whether Yudhishthir's decision was right or foolish, whether the two-diseases idea is true, and repeated checks ("समझ में आया?").
- **Instruction:** listen carefully to the story ("ध्यान से सुनना").
- No meditation, no homework, and no real Q&A from participants.

## Key terms
- **अश्मा:** assumed already explained. Here linked to a "rock solid" foundation.
- **शांति पर्व:** the parva after the war, when everything has become quiet.
- **युधिष्ठिर:** युद्ध + स्थिर (धातु स्था). One steady and calm in war, who handles war-like situations well.
- **धर्मराज:** one who has understood real dharma and can decide by the situation when to accept and when to act.
- **धर्म:** = कर्म, कर्तव्य कर्म.
- **कुरुक्षेत्र / कुरु:** कुरु = कर्म, "what I do". The field of my bodily action should be a धर्मक्षेत्र.
- **व्यास:** the guru who solves your problem piece by piece.
- **प्रारब्ध:** results of past karma, to be accepted (the cure for अर्थ/काम, the "stomach disease").
- **पुरुषार्थ:** effort, the cure for धर्म/मोक्ष, the "eye disease". Also the four पुरुषार्थ: धर्म, अर्थ, काम, मोक्ष.
- **ऋणानुबन्ध:** bonds and resolves from past births. "वही व्यक्ति आएगा".
- **संधि (with प्रारब्ध):** truce or acceptance.
- **विषाद:** despair (Arjun's, before acting; Yudhishthir's after). Can be turned into strength.
- **प्रायश्चित, आत्मग्लानि, guilt:** Yudhishthir's state.
- **ब्रह्मविद्या:** what Ashma Gita teaches from this state onward.

## Sensitive or editorial items
- **(middle)** "अश्मा गीता is more important than भगवद्गीता" (with the qualifier that the Bhagavad Gita is not secondary). The comparison needs careful wording.
- **(early–middle)** Says Yudhishthir lied and Bhishma and Karna were killed by छल-कपट on Krishna's or the guru's instruction. This is his reading of the Mahabharata; check that it is framed as Yudhishthir's perception.
- **(middle)** The two patients and doctor story is a medical analogy, not a health claim. It is fine but should not read as medical advice.
- **(late)** Quarrelsome wife or husband to be accepted as प्रारब्ध: may need sensitive handling (for example where there is abuse).
- **(early)** The garbled "अगर धर्म उसमें डाल दिया तो problem होगा" needs checking against the recording.
- **(end)** The donation-bench example touches on giving and ego. It is unfinished, so its point is unknown.
