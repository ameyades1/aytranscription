# Digest: Day 1, Part 4

## Opening and closing
- **Opening:** The recording starts mid-sentence (`[«और तू निमित्त बनना था»]`). He is continuing an earlier discussion of the Kurukshetra war, Yudhishthira's guilt and the deceit of the Kauravas, and connects it to Gita 4.11.
- **Closing:** The session is cut off mid-thought. He is giving an example of what a guru might tell someone with a quarrelsome wife (`[«तीन महीने तक सहन कर»]`), and the recording ends at "ये बदलेगी" ("she will change").

## Teachings in order
1. (recap) (start) Someone (the "तू" is apparently Yudhishthira or Arjuna) was meant to be a निमित्त, and his time to become king had come. The Kauravas had used a great deal of छल. So, as the Gita says, "जिस रूप में भजोगे, उस रूप में भजूँगा" `[«और तू निमित्त बनना था»]`.
2. (start) Today's session is mostly भूमिका. The shlokas are short and will go quickly. "Main thing is भूमिका। भूमिका ही नहीं समझी तो कुछ नहीं समझेगा।"
3. (start) Gita 4.11 does not mean that God stoops to our level ("तू गुंडा तो मैं भी गुंडा"). God stays God. He comes to उद्धार us, and his unconditional love never goes. He teaches each person in the language that person understands (साम, दाम, दण्ड, भेद), always for that person's good `[«तो इसका मतलब, तू गुंडा»]`.
4. (start) Duryodhana did not understand, so God made him understand in the end, even by killing him. The Kauravas used छल-कपट, so they had to be killed through छल-कपट `[«दुर्योधन ने नहीं समझा»]`.
5. (start/early) Yudhishthira's guilt: at Karna's death, when Karna's chariot wheel was stuck, Krishna said to strike, Yudhishthira gave permission, and Arjuna killed him. Yudhishthira feels "I was the main person", that he had given permission for every lie, and that the whole war was based on lies. The transcript notes this passage was unclear in the raw text `[«उसके बाद वो बोलता है»]`.
6. (early) The theme of Ashma Gita: sometimes we have to take wrong-seeming steps. We may have to be harsh with a son or a wife, as a boss or employee, or as a judge who must give a harsh sentence. Afterwards we wonder how we could have done it, even when we did right or obeyed the guru. "This story revolves around such problems… This is the background" `[«तो वो अश्मा गीता से»]`.
7. (early) Who told Ashma Gita to whom: it "will have six characters." The first is Vaishampayana Rishi telling Janamejaya, which is the original frame of the Mahabharata `[«अश्मा गीता किसने किसको»]`.
8. (early) Vaishampayana was Vyasa's उत्तम शिष्य. Vyasa wrote the Mahabharata and Vaishampayana narrated it to Janamejaya, the प्रपौत्र of Arjuna and son of Parikshit, who wanted to hear his ancestors' history.
9. (early) The Mahabharata is told as a flashback, a story about Janamejaya's own family (Kauravas and Pandavas) `[«महाभारत पूरा ग्रन्थ है»]`.
10. (early) Meaning of जनमेजय: "who is born to be victorious", and "जन का मन जीतने के लिए जो आता है" `[«जनमेजय शब्द का मतलब»]`.
11. (early) Application: these characters should be inside us. First, be born to win others' hearts for their sake, not for show. Second, be born to be always victorious. That victory comes only when you rise above अपना-पराया and can give सही न्याय, doing for your own people what you do for others `[«तो आपको भी तो सीखना है»]`.
12. (early/middle) Second frame: Vyasa told Yudhishthira. Vyasa is the guru of gurus, celebrated on Vyas/Guru Purnima. "Vyasa" is not a name; his real name is कृष्ण द्वैपायन `[«उसके बाद किसने किसको»]`.
13. (middle) व्यास means "to divide". He divided the scriptures into small pieces and made the eighteen Puranas. Vyasa is a post, like PM. Krishna Dvaipayana is the 28th Vyasa, which means others before him had also tried to divide the scriptures to explain them `[«व्यास का मतलब है divide»]`.
14. (middle) A real guru breaks the scriptures into small pieces for you; he does not say "ये लो और पढ़ लो". He compares this with a mother, who would not do this much now, but a guru can `[«तो real गुरु वो होना चाहिए»]`.
15. (middle) The fruit analogy as a picture of Vyasa's work: from the ocean of अध्यात्म, the Vyasa finds the right scripture for the student's current problem, takes out each shlok, cleans it and explains it in simple प्राकृत language. Some people still "शास्त्र का वमन करके भाग जाते हैं". "That is called व्यास" (see Stories).
16. (middle) Yudhishthira is the main hero of this Gita. युधिष्ठिर means one who stays स्थिर in युद्ध, but only in धर्म युद्ध. War is always the last विकल्प, never the first `[«तो इस पूरी गीता का main hero»]`.
17. (middle) The order is साम, दाम, दण्ड, भेद, and दण्ड comes later. Within दण्ड, वाचिक दण्ड comes first (legal notice, threatening, shouting). Real war is the last option, and it is धर्मयुद्ध when fought to establish peace. That is why Yudhishthira is called धर्मराज: he fights for dharma, only when the guru says so `[«हमेशा साम, दाम, दण्ड, भेद»]`.
18. (middle) We are all fighting a युद्ध every day: grey hair, wrinkles, disease, court cases, neighbours, an ICU patient fighting to recover, WBCs fighting germs. "Every minute, there is a fight going on" `[«हाँ, अब युद्ध तो आप भी»]`.
19. (recap) (middle) He mentions "गुणवृत्ति विरोध", spoken of just before: we fight the गुणs too.
20. (middle) No one in the world is happy (Marathi saint line), and the world is दुःखालयम् (Gita 8.15). Everyone is fighting something: a loan (which he will repay by fair means or foul), or माया in the search for moksha `[«जगी ऐसा सुखी कोण आहे»]`.
21. (middle) Yudhishthira is a symbol of every person. "Every Gita is symbolic"; the outer characters are नाम मात्र and serve to explain "मैं कौन था". Here the figure is Yudhishthira, not Arjuna `[«तो युधिष्ठिर इसमें जो है»]`.
22. (middle) Where is dharma? धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे: dharma is in whatever work is ours, whether as father, son, boss, wife (पतिव्रता धर्म) or neighbour (पड़ोसी धर्म). It is कर्तव्य कर्म `[«जो-जो कार्य करना था»]`.
23. (middle/late) Transition to Gita 2.31. Our inner war is for survival instinct, for seeking सुख and fighting दुःख. If you accept grey hair and ageing with साक्षी भाव, there is no दुःख. Fighting comes from not accepting a natural process `[«अब आता है directly भगवद्गीता»]`.
24. (late) This does not mean do nothing. If what you do (for example, dyeing your hair) causes no trouble and gives you confidence, "stand firmly in that", as long as it creates no problem for others. The same principle applies all the way up to big court battles.
25. (late) Vyasa probably taught Yudhishthira the same thing Krishna taught Arjuna in 2.31, with small differences. The 2.31 slide is read: having regard to your own dharma, you should not fear `[«३१वाँ श्लोक, जो अर्जुन को»]`.
26. (late) Fear has no place, whatever you face: dyeing your hair, paying hospital bills, repaying a debt, a court case against your brother, a war, a divorce case `[«तो you don't have to worry»]`.
27. (late) Either ask the guru, or make your own decision confidently. Guilt is allowed neither before nor after (the Meerabai example is used here) `[«मीराबाई को क्या बोला»]`.
28. (late) The Gita is for everybody, not only Kshatriyas: "हम सब में एक क्षत्रिय बैठा हुआ है". We came to fight past दुःख toward moksha `[«अब बोलेगा, अच्छा हुआ»]`.
29. (late) A war becomes धर्मयुक्त when the guru, inner or outer, shows how to fight: whether to fight or drop the matter, fight or stay शांत, forgive or not. This is because the guru knows dharma and the शास्त्र `[«लेकिन धर्मयुक्त कब आएगा»]`.
30. (end) Example of a guru's guidance in daily domestic war: "endure this, you troubled her in a past life", or "this won't last, speak up now", or "endure three months, then speak, and she will change". The recording is cut off here `[«अब उसमें धर्मयुक्त»]`.

## Stories, examples and images
- (start/early) Karna's death: his chariot wheel is stuck, Krishna says to strike, Yudhishthira permits it, and Arjuna kills him. Yudhishthira feels he permitted every lie of the war.
- (start) Duryodhana is made to "understand" in the end, even by being killed.
- (early) Everyday parallels for harsh duty: being strict with a son or wife; a boss told to fire staff; a judge too soft-hearted to give a harsh sentence; obeying a guru and then feeling you did wrong.
- (early) The Mahabharata is a flashback told to Janamejaya about his own ancestors.
- (middle) Vyasa is a post like the PM: there have been many in succession, and Krishna Dvaipayana is the 28th.
- (middle) **The fruit analogy (about himself):** "मैं जंगल में जाकर आता हूँ". He finds good trees, climbs to pick the fruit, cleans and peels it, cuts it into pieces and puts it in your mouth, and some people still vomit it out. He then explains it as a symbol of the Vyasa's (and the guru's) work `[«तो मैं हमेशा आपको बोलता हूँ»]`.
- (middle) Students who say "क्या timepass है… कुछ आत्मा-आत्मा बोल रहे थे, कुछ समझ नहीं आया" are the ones who "vomit" the scripture.
- (middle) Everyday wars: black hair dye against grey hair, anti-wrinkle cream against old age, disease, court cases, neighbours, an ICU patient, WBCs against germs, loans, a seeker fighting माया.
- (late) **His father (personal):** he went bald gradually. His granddaughter (the speaker's sister's daughter) asked "आजोबा… ये तुम्हारे बाल किधर गए?", and he joked that a strong wind blew in one day through the open window and his hair flew away. He accepted ageing gracefully `[«तो I remember my father»]`.
- (late) Meerabai's guru told her to leave home, because she could not attain moksha there, and she left gracefully, without fear.
- (late) Self-deprecating responses: "I'm a businessman/Vaishya" or "I'm a Brahmin, so this verse isn't for me".
- (end) The quarrelsome wife: a rolling pin (बेलन), too much chilli in the food, the mother-in-law or one's own mother saying hurtful things. This is the daily war, followed by the guru's possible guidance (past-life karma, speak up now, endure three months).

## Key terms
- **भूमिका:** background or introduction; "the main thing" today.
- **निमित्त:** instrument (used of the Pandava, apparently Arjuna or Yudhishthira).
- **छल-कपट:** deceit (of the Kauravas).
- **उद्धार:** uplift or salvation, which is God's unchanging nature.
- **साम, दाम, दण्ड, भेद:** persuasion, inducement, punishment, division. These are the "languages" in which God or a person teaches. दण्ड comes late, and वाचिक दण्ड comes before real war.
- **जनमेजय:** जन्म + जय, "born to be victorious", and one who wins जन का मन.
- **व्यास:** "to divide". A post (not a name) for one who divides scripture into pieces. Krishna Dvaipayana is the 28th Vyasa.
- **कृष्ण द्वैपायन:** Vyasa's actual name.
- **युधिष्ठिर:** one who stays स्थिर in युद्ध, specifically धर्म युद्ध.
- **धर्मयुद्ध:** war as the last resort, to establish peace, and as guided by the guru.
- **धर्मराज:** Yudhishthira, who fights only for dharma.
- **वाचिक दण्ड:** verbal punishment (notice, threat, shouting).
- **धर्म / कर्तव्य कर्म:** duty in one's role; also पतिव्रता धर्म and पड़ोसी धर्म.
- **साक्षी भाव:** witnessing attitude, used for accepting ageing.
- **क्षत्रिय (inner):** the warrior in everyone, fighting दुःख toward moksha.
- **धर्मयुक्त:** made righteous by the inner or outer guru's guidance.
- **गुणवृत्ति विरोध:** conflict of the गुणs (recap, not explained here).
- **वमन:** vomiting out the scripture, i.e. rejecting teaching.
- **प्राकृत भाषा:** simple everyday language.
- **माया, मोक्ष:** used in passing.
