## Teachings in order
1. (start) (recap) "Accept the present tense" is the teaching for today, but this does not mean the past or history is of no use [«कल तक तो अच्छा था»].
2. (start) (recap) The causes of दुःख: we compare, we try to keep it multiplied [garbled]... and a fourth. A human being cannot cross दुःख completely; some of it will always come.
3. (start) Yoga Sutra 2.15 (slide): परिणाम-दुःख, ताप-दुःख, संस्कार-दुःख and the दुःख from गुणवृत्ति विरोध. To the विवेकी all कर्मविपाक is दुःख, and even the pleasures ordinary people call सुख are दुःख for him.
4. (start) An अवतार can pass beyond the first three kinds of दुःख by himself, but once he takes a human body he cannot escape the fourth [«लेकिन अवतार अगर होंगे»].
5. (early) गुणवृत्ति विरोध: the three गुण (सत्त्व, रज, तम) are in this world, and their workings oppose each other. Even God in साकार form must move within them: "श्रीराम अगर हैं तो रावण आएगा ही, श्रीकृष्ण हैं तो कंस आएगा ही".
6. (early) Whatever you do, some percentage of people will speak against it. If you start सत्संग, an opponent will come. That will be pain for you.
7. (early) If even an अवतार is not free of the four kinds of दुःख, nothing is guaranteed for me. All कर्मफल are दुःख रूपी. सुख shows at first, but "the more you will try to search सुख", the more दुःख you get.
8. (early) Gita 8.15: Krishna himself calls this world दुःखालयम्. Split the सन्धि one way and it reads शाश्वतम्; split it the other way and it reads अशाश्वतम्. The world is not शाश्वत ("सपने जैसा") but it is a दुःखालय.
9. (early) After this main भूमिका, "हम अपनी गीता में चले जाएँगे दो दिन" (the plan for the Shibir).
10. (early) Explaining 8.15: महात्माs in परम सिद्धि (the state of मोक्ष) do not return to rebirth. Krishna has defined birth itself as a house of दुःख. Patanjali spelled out the four kinds in more detail.
11. (early-middle) This is why all the saints said to go beyond सुख-दुःख. Asking God for दुःख indirectly brings सुख, and asking for सुख brings दुःख: "वो coin है, उल्टा". Going beyond both means seeking आनंद, सत्-चित्-आनंद परमेश्वर [«इसलिए सारे संतों ने क्या कहा»].
12. (middle) आनंद comes when you become a witness through साक्षी भाव, a witness of your own सुख-दुःख. "साक्षी होना ही आनंद स्थिति का लक्षण".
13. (middle) It is like watching a serial: you chose the channel yourself. Tears in your eyes mean "you become one with that serial", which is involvement, not साक्षी भाव. Either switch the channel off or make friends with the ghost and feel the मस्ती.
14. (middle) We are not the character. Part one is प्रकृति (शरीर, मन, बुद्धि, अहंकार) and part two is the आत्मा. They are superimposed on each other, and this is पुरुष and प्रकृति.
15. (middle) पुर means this town, the body. "पुरि शेते इति पुरुषः": the one who sleeps (or is awake) in this body is परमेश्वर. Jagannath ji is called पुरुषोत्तम. In me the पुरुष sleeps; in some ऋषि-मुनि it is awake.
16. (middle) पुरुष and प्रकृति have become one (तादात्म्य). It is the आत्मा that sees, not the eye (see the eye-donation example). Once this is understood, the तादात्म्य can break and I can become साक्षी.
17. (middle) Because I take myself to be the body and mind, I see only outward: other people, things and places. The इन्द्रियाँ are designed for बाह्य वृत्ति, so I see only that the other person is the problem or the अहंकारी. From this come सुख-दुःख and then राग-द्वेष.
18. (middle) Someone who acts as I wish gives me अनुकूल परिस्थिति and प्रेम: this is राग (likes). Someone who acts against me brings दुःख (द्वेष).
19. (middle) When the तादात्म्य breaks, I watch what this body does. First I see myself, not others: where I went wrong, or what right action brought this result. So the साक्षी neither jumps up and down at a good result nor sinks into शोक at a wrong one.
20. (middle) This process is not easy. Homework: try साक्षी भाव in tomorrow's home quarrel. साक्षी भाव comes in the अंतिम जन्म. सुख-दुःख come from our own कर्म, and the goal is to cross them.
21. (middle) Context of the Gita: he is not discussing that it was 5,200 years ago, but the situation at the time. A भीषण युद्ध was coming with one's own people facing each other, so दुःख and कष्ट came.
22. (middle) We feel दुःख only where something of mine, of the heart, is connected. News of accidents, cyclones or the war in Ukraine does not hurt, but the same thing next door or at home does at once.
23. (middle) Another cause of दुःख is attachment, आसक्ति ("मैं-मैं-मैं"), often mixed with अहंकार (see the trophy example). The major cause is not the good or bad event itself but my मैं attaching to it.
24. (middle-late) The Sharma-Varma story and the affair example both show that the event stays the same and only "मेरा" changes सुख into दुःख. "Root cause is अपना".
25. (late) Today many gurus point out that the gods hold weapons and say "एक हाथ में माला ... दूसरे हाथ में भाला" and "सनातन धर्म, जागो". The problem is not holding the भाला: Arjuna held every weapon and had won great wars. His problem was using them against his own people (अर्जुन विषाद योग).
26. (late) Most people are the same: they are fine with other children going astray, but not their own son.
27. (late) The doctor, the judge and Shivaji examples: rise above relations and you become legendary, महान.
28. (late) The more I am attached to or like something, the more it gives प्रेम, but it will surely give more दुःख. Since I am told to go beyond सुख-दुःख, "मुझे करना होगा सिर्फ कर्तव्य".
29. (end) Gita 1.1 is introduced as the first dialogue between Sanjaya and Dhritarashtra. He begins explaining मामकाः पाण्डवाश्च ("मेरे पुत्र और पाण्डु पुत्रों का...") and the recording cuts off.

## Key terms
- **परिणाम / ताप / संस्कार दुःख:** three of the four kinds in YS 2.15. Not explained individually in this session.
- **गुणवृत्ति विरोध:** the mutual opposition of the workings of सत्त्व, रज and तम; the दुःख even an अवतार cannot escape.
- **गुण:** सत्त्व, रज, तम.
- **कर्मविपाक / कर्मफल:** the fruits of action; to the विवेकी all of them are दुःख.
- **विवेकी:** the discerning person.
- **अवतार:** God taking a human body, and so subject to the गुणs.
- **दुःखालयम्, शाश्वतम् / अशाश्वतम्:** house of sorrow; the two सन्धि readings.
- **पुनर्जन्म:** defined by Krishna as a house of दुःख and क्षणभंगुर.
- **परम सिद्धि:** the state of मोक्ष.
- **आनंद, सत्-चित्-आनंद:** the state beyond सुख-दुःख.
- **साक्षी भाव:** being a witness of one's own सुख-दुःख, like a viewer and not a character.
- **प्रकृति:** शरीर, मन, बुद्धि, अहंकार.
- **पुरुष:** the आत्मा; "पुरि शेते इति पुरुषः".
- **पुर:** town, i.e. the body.
- **पुरुषोत्तम:** a name of Jagannath ji.
- **तादात्म्य:** the identification of पुरुष and प्रकृति ("they are so one").
- **बाह्य वृत्ति:** the outward orientation of the इन्द्रियाँ.
- **राग / द्वेष:** likes, and the dislike that comes from contrary behaviour.
- **अनुकूल परिस्थिति:** favourable circumstance.
- **आसक्ति:** attachment, "मैं-मैं-मैं".
- **अर्जुन विषाद योग:** Arjuna's inability to fight his own people.
- **धर्मयुद्ध:** the Mahabharata war.
- **कर्तव्य:** duty, the way beyond सुख-दुःख.
