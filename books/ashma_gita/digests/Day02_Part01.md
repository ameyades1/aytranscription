# Digest: Day 2, Part 1

## Opening and closing
- **Opening:** He greets the audience and goes back to Ashma Gita, saying the भूमिका (introduction) is now complete. He continues the previous day's theme of becoming युधिष्ठिर ("युद्ध में स्थिर") and recaps it at length before reading shlokas 1 to 7 [«तो वापस एक बार अश्मा गीता»].
- **Closing:** The session is cut off mid-thought. He is starting a story about an acquaintance who lived in Arab countries and Europe, earned well and saved a lot of money at home. The story stops there [«एक हमारे पहचान के थे»]. The slide giving the meaning of shlok 7 may also have continued.

## Teachings in order
1. (start) The introduction is complete. Become युधिष्ठिर, "युद्ध में स्थिर", steady in your own war. Everyone's life has a constant war of सुख-दुःख, and there is a war in सुख as well as in दुःख [«तो युधिष्ठिर बनने आए»].
2. (start, recap) Yesterday's shlok: knowledge, तत्त्वज्ञान and आत्मबोध all leave when extreme सुख or दुःख comes. तत्त्वज्ञान cannot be taught to someone अति सुखी, अति दुःखी, अति रोगी or अति ज्ञानी, and anyone who already has it will lose it [«ज्ञान, तत्त्वज्ञान, आत्मबोध»].
3. (start, recap) In दुःख a person says "सब में भगवान है, लेकिन इसमें नहीं है". In सुख a person says "मैं खुद भगवान हूँ". Both happen if we get stuck in सुख-दुःख.
4. (start, recap) The way beyond both is साक्षी भाव. The तादात्म्य (identification) of पुरुष and प्रकृति must break, and this cannot happen without the सान्निध्य (closeness) of a guru [«तो दोनों से परे जाने के लिए»].
5. (start, recap) The frame of the story: Vaishampayan tells the Mahabharata to Janamejaya. In the Shanti Parva, after the war, Vyasa tells Yudhishthira the story Ashma Rishi told King Janaka ("अश्मा उवाच").
6. (start, recap) Janaka had the "same problem" as Yudhishthira: दुःख. The main causes looked at were wealth that suddenly goes and one's own people who suddenly go. These are the major reasons दुःख comes [«जनक राजा को भी वही same problem»].
7. (early) News of strangers dying does not make us sad, so the cause of दुःख is आसक्ति (attachment): "Iness and Myness", अहंता and ममत्व. Without these there can be no सुख or दुःख. Someone else winning the lottery, or a stock rising in America, gives you no joy [«newspaper में कितनी तो भी news»].
8. (early) Belongingness creates सुख-दुःख: "my country won". When you become the game itself (आत्मतत्त्व, the "spirit of sportsmanship"), it does not matter who wins; "जीता क्या, हारा क्या, वो मेरा ही". This state should come slowly into our lives [«Country का होगा तो»].
9. (early) A ruler who comes from one state and looks after only that state cannot be the king. His "मैं" must grow to cover the whole country: not "बिहार में problem है" but "मेरे देश में problem है". "He has to become that full" [«अगर राजा है, तो वो किसी state से»].
10. (early) To become विश्वगुरु (world teacher), we should feel that anything happening anywhere, even in Israel, happens to the world and to me. Then no other religion can be an opponent, because it is your शिष्य (disciple), and one cannot think ill of a disciple. Until this feeling comes, सुख-दुःख and comparisons ("वो छोटा, वो ऊपर कैसे चला गया?") continue [«तो हमको विश्वगुरु बनना है»].
11. (early, recap) There are three kinds of fruit. The text names two, सुख and दुःख. सुख is an illusion and imaginary. दुःख is what is original and शाश्वत (permanent); he says Patanjali and Shri Krishna both teach this. The basundi/shrikhand with cyanide image is recalled. आनंद अवस्था is the state that enjoys both [«सुख-दुःख के तो तीन प्रकार»].
12. (early) The third kind is मिश्रित (or सम्मिश्रित) कर्मफल, where सुख and दुःख come together. Some कर्म bear the fruit that "I will not be able to enjoy this". All three kinds will be looked at [«तीसरी एक variety बन सकती है»].
13. (early) The reading plan: there is only one chapter, they had reached shlok 6 (shlokas 1 to 6 were apparently covered the day before), and he will read fast through six shlokas.
14. (early) Title slide on प्रारब्ध versus पुरुषार्थ. With a guru, पुरुषार्थ (one's own effort) becomes stronger and संकल्प (resolve) works. For the general public, प्रारब्ध (destiny from past actions) is always stronger [«तो प्रबल क्या है»].
15. (early, recap) The "eye disease" and the "stomach disease". The eye disease is the need to see God everywhere, or to see the reason behind things (ज्ञानचक्षु, the eye of knowledge). This takes great पुरुषार्थ, and only the guru can show it. The stomach disease ("पापी पेट का सवाल है") is about अर्थ and काम. Which spouse you get, angry or good, is प्रारब्ध. What you do with them, and whether you see शिव or the देवी in them, is पुरुषार्थ. A boss who tries to trap you is the same [«वो आँख की बीमारी, पेट की बीमारी»].
16. (middle) On shlok 4: someone will lose wealth, and someone will lose their people. Neither can be stopped, and fortunes rise and fall. The question is what a person who seeks कल्याण (true welfare) should decide. Someone who wants to stay with सुख-दुःख is a different case [«तो किसी का धन जाएगा ही»].
17. (middle) You must know your aim, which is to cross the river. Whatever happens on the way is secondary. If you want to dance in सुख-दुःख and enjoy excitement, that is रजोगुण: "do whatever you want". Chasing attractions on the way means you get stuck, and even after death you must come back here [«मुझे यहाँ से नदी के पार होना है»].
18. (middle) Know your destination. Know both your worldly goals and what of परमेश्वर you mean to gain through those goals; both must be clear [«you should know your destination»].
19. (middle) The question restated: सुख and दुःख keep coming, so "what should I do?" if I want मुक्ति (liberation).
20. (middle) Shlok 5: सुख and दुःख follow the body from birth, and Ashma states it as "this is a fact". Most people never accept this and expect only सुख, and that is what creates the problem [«तो "this is a fact" करके»].
21. (middle) The day you start an organization, someone will dislike it, and an existing business in the field not liking it "is a rule". Employees will make trouble or, by the three गुण, be lazy. Staff will not be like you; if they were, they would have their own company. Expecting otherwise leads to irritation, so accept it [«The day I will start an organization»].
22. (middle) At home and in the world, the दशा (condition) of husband, wife and others also keeps changing. Understand that सुख-दुःख will come into their lives too, and that you work within such combinations [«घर में, संसार में बोला था»].
23. (middle) Shlok 6: at any given point of time, one of the two is present. Knowledge, and आत्मज्ञान, leave as easily as wind blows away a cloud or a leaf, the moment one becomes अति सुखी or अति दुःखी [«तो बादल हो, पत्ता हो»].
24. (middle) The best example is wherever there is a lot of रजोगुण: reality-show contestants. Those with extreme emotions do not become legendary. The calm ones who kept सुख-दुःख balanced (स्थिर) later become great [«इसका best example कहाँ दिखेगा»].
25. (late) Shlok 7: when आत्मज्ञान goes in extreme सुख, three streams of अहंकार come: "मैं कुलीन हूँ, मैं सिद्ध हूँ, मैं कोई साधारण मनुष्य नहीं हूँ". They come in "knowingly, unknowingly" [«Automatic उसके मन में क्या आएगा»].
26. (late) कुलीन: even someone who stole, or whose family did wrong in a past life, will start thinking this way [«मतलब ये चोरी भी किया होगा»].
27. (late) When success follows success, this happens automatically. When a child comes first at school "सरस्वती की वजह से", the parents credit their parenting. "तुम जन्मदाता थे… भाग्यविधाता नहीं हो". The child brings their own प्रारब्ध, and the parents were only निमित्त (instruments) in it. Their getting such parents was itself the child's प्रारब्ध. The same applies to husband and wife [«जैसे ही unknowingly success»].
28. (end) The story of the acquaintance who lived abroad begins and is cut off.

## Stories, examples and images
- (early) **Newspaper deaths:** strangers' deaths cause no grief, which shows that attachment is the cause.
- (early) **Lottery and stock market:** someone else's lottery win, or stocks rising for someone in America, brings you no joy.
- (early) **Country winning a match:** "my country won" is belongingness; becoming the game itself is the spirit of sportsmanship. Cricket is mentioned.

- (early) **Birth then death:** a son is born, followed by a stillbirth, or a death after four or ten days, a "guest" who leaves. Mixed fruit, ending more in दुःख.
- (early) **Wedding and fall:** a son's wedding stuck for years (he is 35 or 36) finally happens, and the same day his father slips off the stage, breaks a leg and needs surgery. The source noted laughter here.
- (early, recap) **Eye disease and stomach disease:** the metaphors for ज्ञानचक्षु and for अर्थ-काम.

- (middle) **Crossing the river:** the aim is the far bank, and the journey is secondary.
- (middle) **Dolphin seen from the boat:** a trip memory of the group seeing a dolphin "today". If the boat chases the dolphin it goes round and round and never arrives. This is the attractions of संसार and माया.
- (middle) **Railway stations:** things come at every station; know where to get on and off, since the train goes on.
- (middle) **Starting an organization:** rivals will dislike it, and staff will not be like you or they would run a parallel company. The employee who does not keep "नौ से पाँच" hours, and the boss's "मैं कैसा करता हूँ?"

## Key terms
- **युधिष्ठिर:** "युद्ध में स्थिर", steady in your own war of सुख-दुःख.
- **तत्त्वज्ञान / आत्मबोध / आत्मज्ञान:** the knowledge that flies off in extremes.
- **साक्षी भाव:** the witness state; the way beyond सुख-दुःख.
- **तादात्म्य:** identification of पुरुष with प्रकृति; it must break, and only with the guru's सान्निध्य.
- **आसक्ति / अहंता / ममत्व:** attachment; "Iness and Myness"; the cause of सुख-दुःख.
- **आत्मतत्त्व:** becoming "that game itself", so every result is mine.
- **विश्वगुरु:** someone who feels for the whole world and sees other religions as disciples.
- **मिश्रित (सम्मिश्रित) कर्मफल:** fruit in which सुख and दुःख come together.
- **आनंद अवस्था:** the state that "enjoys" both सुख and दुःख.
- **प्रारब्ध / पुरुषार्थ:** destiny from past action versus effort. पुरुषार्थ is stronger with a guru; प्रारब्ध is stronger for most people.
- **ज्ञानचक्षु:** the eye that sees God, or the reason, everywhere.
- **कल्याण:** the true welfare the seeker aims at, the aim beyond सुख-दुःख; linked to मुक्ति.
- **रजोगुण:** the excitement of dancing in सुख-दुःख; also extreme emotions.
- **कुलीन:** from a good, respected family (ego: "हमारा घर ही बड़ा है").
- **सिद्ध:** in the ego sense, "I am accomplished".
- **निमित्त:** instrument; parents are only that in a child's प्रारब्ध.
- **जन्मदाता / भाग्यविधाता:** the one who gives birth, versus the one who decides fate.
- **माया:** the attractions on the way.
