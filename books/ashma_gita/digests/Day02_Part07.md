## Teachings in order
1. (start) Events beyond our control have to be faced, and we must "सज्जनता से सहना". Bearing them with grief, abuse or shouting ("समाज ऐसा ही है") is not real सहना [«उनका सामना करना। सज्जनता से»].
2. (start) Keeping आत्मिक संतुलन matters even more. आत्मिक ज्ञान should not slip away during such events.
3. (start) Yudhishthira followed dharma and still had to watch his guru, brothers and sons die. It was his प्रारब्ध: they were all to die by his hand. The same will come in my own life too [«देखनी ही पड़ी। देखना है»].
4. (early) तितिक्षा: cold and heat, gain and loss, pleasure and pain will come. Bearing them with समान भाव, निर्द्वंद्व, is योग, and योग means "unite with God" [«सर्दी-गर्मी, लाभ-हानि, सुख-दुःख, ये»].
5. (early) Applied to the day: the cold AC in the hall and the afternoon heat outside were also प्रारब्ध. Gain will not come every day, and neither will loss. सुख-दुःख is mental, so the teaching applies "at all levels".
6. (early, recap) Each shlok of Ashma Gita teaches something. Earlier came वैराग्य, and before that विवेकज्ञान. Now it is सहनशीलता. The more one learns, the more one goes beyond सुख-दुःख [«तो अश्मा गीता, सहनशीलता, एक-एक»].
7. (early) Sukh-dukh will still come. Even avatars could not escape it. Their joys were greater than ours, and so were their sorrows.
8. (early) It is wrong to ask God for "no suffering". Ask instead for the formula: how they bore their suffering and overcame it. No avatar, सद्गुरु or महापुरुष had fewer troubles [«किसको अभी तक चौदह साल»].
9. (early–middle) Gurus "and higher" suffer greatly, yet we ask them that no sorrow should come to us at all. That is not possible.
10. (middle) Speaking as God, he gives God's answer. Suffering came to me too. Ask me how I bore it, how I understood why it came, and how I faced it. That I can give you, and nothing else. I am not sitting here just to drink your हलाहल विष. Become that much of a भक्त, that much of a सखा, and then I will take your poison, free. "Otherwise nothing comes free in this world." [«वो बोलते हैं, मुझे भी»]
11. (middle) On sudden death or serious illness: we can weep but cannot prevent it, and this is where समझदारी begins. Ashma Gita says to learn it now. Then such a day is less likely to come physically, because "आप समाधि लगा के शरीर छोड़ोगे". Even if प्रारब्ध brings it, you will not fall into sorrow, will not become a पितर, and will go onto a path toward अमरत्व [«तो ये, इस स्थिति में»].
12. (middle) Sudden success is also नियति. Prestige, wealth or opportunity sometimes come without effort. Falling into अहंकार over it means forgetting it came from भाग्य/प्रारब्ध.
13. (middle) The lesson of the one-rupee story (see Stories). Good success can come suddenly from प्रारब्ध. "Many times, it may not be your पुरुषार्थ." Keep a cool head and do not let अहंकार grow [«तो what do we learn»].
14. (middle–late) More money means more responsibility and more expense. Examples: the Centre must grow and the work grows, and a bigger ashram has bigger costs, less personal time, and no family or personal life ("संसार को छोड़ के विश्व का"). Something is always given up in return.
15. (late) Success may be "for others", for someone else's enjoyment. Bear it quietly: "सफलता भी भोग लेना, दुःख भी भोग लेना". If you want something changed, come to the guru, who will say whether it can be changed, i.e. whether it is in your hands [«तो सफलता भी दिखती है»].
16. (late) A note on method: the shloks up to "thirty five या thirty eighth" (later "forty-eighth") depend on one story. Once the story is understood, those shloks will go quickly, in about 15 minutes.
17. (late) He introduces the story as a Bhagavata Mahapurana katha: "चित्रकेतु नाम का एक लड़का" who dies right after birth, bringing his parents joy and grief together [«ये कथा भागवत महापुराण की»].
18. (late, during the video) The king has every सुख, and dukh arrives at the same time.
19. (late, recap) "यही हमने पुत्र गीता में भी सीखा था." Many gurus and ऋषि-मुनि had no sons and still attained मोक्ष.
20. (late) The cause of the sorrow is अज्ञान: the belief that "इसी से मेरी मुक्ति होगी", a भ्रम of the चित्त. Co-relate the story with the shloks and the answers start to come.
21. (late) The blessing said this son would be the cause of both sukh and dukh.
22. (late) Of the other wives, one turned hostile and will spoil the others' minds as well. This shows how another cause of dukh grows. Each point of the next 15–16 shloks is in this story.
23. (end, recap) The very first shlok said to act भयरहित. "श्लोक खत्म हो गया, भूल गए सब." (cut off)

## Practices, instructions and Q&A
- Ask God and the guru for the "formula" of how to bear suffering, not for an end to suffering (early–middle).
- Learn equanimity now, before hard days come (middle).
- When success comes, keep a cool head and do not grow proud. Bear success and sorrow alike, quietly (late).
- For change, come to the guru, who will say whether it can be changed (late).

## Key terms
- **प्रारब्ध:** what is destined to happen and beyond our control, e.g. Yudhishthira's losses, the AC and heat, sudden success.
- **सहना (सज्जनता से):** bearing calmly and with grace, without abuse, complaint or shouting.
- **आत्मिक संतुलन:** inner balance. आत्मिक ज्ञान should not slip away.
- **तितिक्षा / सहनशीलता:** bearing opposites with समान भाव.
- **समान भाव / निर्द्वंद्व:** evenness, without being caught in the pairs of opposites.
- **योग:** "Unite with God", here meaning bearing opposites evenly.
- **वैराग्य, विवेकज्ञान:** named as earlier lessons (recap).
- **पुरुषार्थ:** one's own effort. Success "may not be your पुरुषार्थ".
- **नियति / भाग्य:** destiny. He uses these alongside प्रारब्ध.
- **हलाहल विष:** the poison of one's suffering, which God takes only from a भक्त or सखा.
- **पितर:** what one becomes if one dies in sorrow, which can be avoided.
- **समाधि लगा के शरीर छोड़ना:** leaving the body in samadhi.
- **अज्ञान / चित्त का भ्रम:** the cause of Chitraketu's sorrow.

## Sensitive or editorial items
- (middle) A claim that learning this means you will leave the body through samadhi, will not become a पितर, and will go onto a path of immortality. These are claims about death and the afterlife.
- (middle) The one-rupee story calls a wife and son burdens from past-life debts ("निकम्मा"). The wording may need care.
- (early) Comparisons with Jesus ("येशु ख्रिस्त"), Tukaram, Dnyaneshwar and Shivaji. The Dnyaneshwar sentence is incomplete.
- (late) Institutional: the Centre's growth, moving from a small ashram to a big one, and its expenses. This is an implied remark about Antar Yog's finances and sacrifices.
- (late) "Come to the guru… गुरु बोलेगा ये changeable है" is a claim of the guru's authority over प्रारब्ध.
- (late) The video is third-party material (Chinmaya Mission, Upanishad Ganga). He calls Chitraketu "एक लड़का", but in the Bhagavata Chitraketu is the king and it is his son who dies.
- (late) The shlok counts are inconsistent: 35/38, 48, and "15–16 more".
