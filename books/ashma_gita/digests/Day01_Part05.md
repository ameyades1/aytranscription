## Opening and closing
**Opening:** Begins mid-argument, continuing from the previous session on धर्मयुक्त लड़ाई (righteous fight) and when fighting a problem is right, with violence that establishes non-violence counted as अहिंसा (start) [«तो that धर्मयुक्त लड़ाई करनी»].
**Closing:** Cut off mid-sentence. He had just started a second point, that Yudhishthir was a king and so everyone is a king of something: "That's why युधिष्ठिर को ऐसा told, and वो..." (end).

## Teachings in order
1. A righteous fight is needed and some solution must calm the problem. Violence done to establish non-violence is not called हिंसा; it is called अहिंसा (start) [«हम बोलते हैं, वो हिंसा»]. (recap/continuation)
2. The Kauravas ruled wrongly. If they could strip Draupadi and take Indraprastha from princes, ordinary people would suffer far more under them. Killing the wrong-doer by a "wrong" route was strategy, and Krishna teaches Arjun not to feel guilt about it (start) [«तो ये जो युधिष्ठिर कर»].
3. Whether to fight or accept depends on the case and on the guru. If the guru says a married woman should stay with her husband, staying is right. If she fights that instead ("समाज के लिये संभाल के रहूँगी"), it becomes another, wrong war and she will incur more पाप. "Depends on case basis" (start) [«तो गुरु ने अगर आपको»].
4. Cosmetic surgery done for beautification can go wrong (swelling, darker skin). That was a wrong fight. If the guru had said accept gracefully, that would be the path. If the guru or नाड़ी ज्योतिष had said "yes, do the surgery", then it would be a धर्मयुक्त युद्ध (start) [«कभी-कभी surgery करने जाते»].
5. Gita 2.32: we never asked for our sufferings in this birth (a quarrelsome spouse, a difficult mother-in-law, an enemy). They came अनायास, on their own. There is a reason from past births, but he speaks only of this birth (early) [«अब आपके जो दुःख थे»].
6. What you call दुःख was really a संघर्ष, a युद्ध, in which you had to stay steady like Yudhishthir and fight a धर्मयुद्ध with the guru's understanding. That fight is an open door to heaven (स्वर्ग का द्वार) (early) [«पर वो आगे कुछ तो»].
7. The choices with a quarrelsome spouse are unconditional love, cancelling past-birth karma, or leaving if the guru says it is not right for you and you have other work (राष्ट्रकार्य). The question is whether you can carry it out: "How to fight that?" (early) [«झगड़ालू पत्नी के साथ मैं»].
8. First check whether you have क्षत्रिय भाव, the will to fight the problem. One who weeps or is afraid gets nothing. Rule 1 is to be fearless (भयमुक्त; recap). The second requirement is the capacity to fight (लड़ने की क्षमता) (early) [«तो पहले तुम्हारे अंदर वो»].
9. Gita 18.78 (partial): you need both a guru who has attained yoga (योगेश्वर) and an Arjun who holds the bow. Not an Arjun eating idli at the Udupi hotel saying "I gave offerings, you do it for me." "Unless you are willing to fight your problem..." (early-middle) [«तो "यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णः"»].
10. Krishna could have ended the war in a moment with his chakra, but it was not his problem. Draupadi and Indraprastha were the Pandavas', so the Pandavas had to fight. Krishna is only सारथी, guide and the one who shows the path: "Problem तेरा है" (middle) [«तो अर्जुन को कृष्ण बोलता»].
11. When the problem was Krishna's own (bringing Rukmini, killing Kamsa), he fought himself and took no help. So he is an inspiration, but your fight is yours (middle).
12. A real guide must have solved such a problem himself at some time. One who never suffered, or never saw such cases, is not a guru: "go to a correct गुरु" (middle) [«तो your guide should be»].
13. "भाग्यवान" means having the capacity to fight. The fearful cannot become Yudhishthir, will not find a fitting guru, and even if they find one will disrespect him, fail to fight and spoil their own भाग्य (middle) [«तो भाग्यवान क्षत्रिय लोगों को»].
14. Gita 2.33: not fighting slowly ruins you. Example: someone who wants black hair but neither dyes it nor accepts the white hair sinks into depression as confidence falls. A business owner who keeps tolerating a rival eventually has to close (middle) [«Suppose you did not fight»].
15. Hanuman (see Stories) shows that one fights for a duty given by the guru or master, whatever the cost (middle).
16. The suffering in this birth is what you came to fight or settle. People take the easy way, such as finding divorce themselves, though they were born to solve it with that person. Leave only if the guru says so. Otherwise do as Mirabai did: solve it, forgive, since your past-birth account was not in order (middle) [«If you don't... अरे, इस»].
17. Your real स्वधर्म is to settle what you came to settle: "अच्छा-अच्छा तो उपभोग था, जो बुरा था उसको ही solve करने आया था". Not doing it loses कीर्ति and brings पाप (middle).
18. Gita 2.34: people will speak of your disgrace (someone who only kept a court case going for thirty years, a ruined married life, "इनका बेटा ऐसा ही है", never disciplining the son). For the respected, disgrace is worse than death (middle) [«तो धीरे-धीरे, "अरे, इसने»].
19. Rather than "dying daily", either fight or drop the problem from the mind ("मेरा बेटा ही नहीं है"). "But check with the गुरु… अपनी मर्जी से नहीं" (middle) [«ऐसा जीने से तो मरना»].
20. Gita 2.35: daily quarrels and fearful avoidance erode the respect you once had. If you never file a court case against an oppressor, he will close your business, as he intended. The great warriors will think you fled out of fear (middle) [«पहले तुम्हारा सम्मान होता भी»].
21. A daughter-in-law who endures silently will at death regret not speaking once. At पितृ ऋण मुक्ति one realises "एक बार तो बोलना चाहिए था" (middle) [«कोई बहू बहुत सहन कर»].
22. Gita 2.36: enemies, from relatives to the public, will speak ill of you. "अन्याय करने वाले से अन्याय सहन करने वाला ज्यादा पापी है", which he says "ये अपने ही वेद बोलते हैं". So go to the guru instead of enduring (middle-late) [«फिर धीरे-धीरे रिश्तेदार से लेके»].
23. Gita 2.37: if you act as the guru says, either you are killed and reach heaven (or are corrected), or you win and rule. You get a fruit either way. If it fails, at least no पाप attaches. Hence युद्धाय कृतनिश्चयः (late) [«तो या तो तू युद्ध»].
24. This was the भूमिका (background) before Ashma Gita: are you ready to fight for your sufferings? Only then do they go. You must board the ज्ञान रूपी नौका, and that knowledge comes after much भक्ति and कर्म: "ये अश्मा गीता starts with this background" (late) [«तो ये अश्मा गीता शुरू»].
25. Preview of Ashma Gita: दुःख-सुख are the play of प्रारब्ध and काल. Each comes at its right time and is the fruit of your own karma (late) [«And उसमें वो बोलते हैं»].
26. "Destiny is pre-written, but also can be re-written", but few can do it. Like the ad where one germ survives out of 100, here it is reversed: only one टक्का (1%) change their destiny and the rest must undergo it (late) [«तो बताना क्या था? अश्मा»].
27. Those who come to the guru, hear the teaching, and fight a धर्मयुद्ध without fear or doubt, in शरणागति, as the Pandavas regained their kingdom through Krishna, can change. But how many can? (late).
28. Gita 7.3 (meaning only): among thousands one strives and among those one knows Me. In today's population it would be one in lakhs or crores. That one's destiny will surely change; the rest must accept प्रारब्ध (late) [«कोई हजारों में एक मेरी»].
29. This applies even to अवतार: they cannot intervene every time and also go through their time (even साढ़ेसाती). They stay in साक्षी भाव, which is why "they can change" (late) [«और ये अवतारों को भी»].
30. Ashma Gita says perhaps not even 1% achieve a 100% change of destiny, which is the "rare of the rare chance", including for an avatar. Exhausting karma by undergoing it is easier and quicker than changing it. Majority cases are solved by acceptance and 1% need a drastic change. This is काल की महिमा and प्रारब्ध (late) [«तो वो अश्मा गीता में»].
31. Acceptance applied to Yudhishthir: it is in your fate to be king, you cannot commit suicide, and you must accept that they (brothers, uncles) had to die (end) [«तो ये वो स्वीकार करने»].
32. Second point, cut off: Yudhishthir was a king, and so are you, each a king of something: society secretary, family head, the country, or at least king of yourself (end) [«दूसरा... युधिष्ठिर राजा था»].

- Instruction: don't endure injustice; go to the guru and ask what to do.

## Key terms
- **धर्मयुक्त लड़ाई / धर्मयुद्ध**: fighting one's problem in the right way, under the guru's guidance, case by case.
- **हिंसा / अहिंसा**: violence done to establish non-violence counts as अहिंसा.
- **अनायास / यदृच्छया**: suffering that comes on its own in this birth, unasked.
- **स्वर्ग का द्वार**: the struggle itself, rightly fought, is the open door to heaven.
- **क्षत्रिय भाव**: the inner will and capacity to fight one's problem.
- **भाग्यवान**: in his reading, one who has the capacity to fight and is not afraid.
- **भयमुक्त**: rule 1, being free of fear.
- **योगेश्वर**: a guru who has attained yoga and himself solved such problems.
- **स्वधर्म**: your real duty, to settle what you were born to settle (the bad, not the enjoyment).
- **कीर्ति / अपकीर्ति**: reputation and disgrace, worse than death for the respected.
- **प्रारब्ध**: destiny from your own past karma.
- **काल**: time; each joy or sorrow comes at its right time ("काल की महिमा").
- **ज्ञान रूपी नौका**: the boat of knowledge, reached after भक्ति and कर्म.
- **शरणागति**: surrendered listening to the guru, without doubt.
- **साक्षी भाव**: witness attitude; even avatars bear their time in it.
- **टक्का**: percent (one टक्का = 1%).
- **राष्ट्रकार्य**: work for the nation, as an alternative path the guru may assign.
- **पितृ ऋण मुक्ति**: the occasion when the regret of unspoken words surfaces.
- **नाड़ी ज्योतिष**: cited as a possible source of guidance on whether to act.
