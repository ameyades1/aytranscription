### Style: Contemporary Pravachan for a Gen Z-Friendly Audience

Use a **Contemporary Pravachan** style: preserve the depth, authority, spiritual vocabulary, and wisdom of a traditional discourse while making the reading experience contemporary, conversational, visually engaging, and accessible to younger readers.

The writing should feel like **a wise teacher personally speaking to the reader**, rather than a textbook or academic essay.

* Retain the direct, conversational, and intimate connection between the speaker and the audience.
* Keep the language natural, warm, reflective, and engaging.
* Use short, readable paragraphs, generally 2–4 sentences, with enough white space for comfortable reading on mobile devices.
* Avoid long, dense blocks of text.
* Use clear, descriptive headings that create curiosity and help the reader understand the progression of ideas.
* Where appropriate, frame sections around relatable human questions rather than only technical spiritual terminology. For example: “क्या मेरा जीवन केवल मेरे लिए है?” or “क्या करुणा के साथ मेरे पास विवेक भी है?”
* After explaining an important spiritual concept, where appropriate, invite the reader to pause and reflect on their own life through a short reflective question.
* Make the seven chakras and other sequential concepts easy to scan and understand. Where appropriate, briefly express the central life-orientation of each level in simple language before explaining it in depth.
* Use traditional Sanskrit spiritual terms such as **शरणागति, साधना, कुंडलिनी, शक्तिपात, चेतना** naturally and respectfully. Explain each important term in simple Hindi and, where useful, provide its English meaning in parentheses.
* Follow the principle: **traditional term → simple explanation → practical or relatable context**.
* Do not remove spiritual terminology merely to make the content modern.
* Do not make the writing artificially trendy, slang-heavy, overly casual, or dependent on temporary Gen Z expressions.
* Avoid phrases that sound like social-media marketing or forced youth language.
* The article should feel contemporary without becoming dated, so that it remains meaningful and readable years from now.
* Use English words only where they genuinely improve clarity or where they are naturally part of the speaker's original expression. Prefer clean, universally understood Hindi wherever a natural Hindi equivalent exists.
* Preserve important stories, examples, analogies, and teachings from the original discourse. Do not replace them with modern examples unless explicitly asked.
* Use **2–3 strong pull quotes** as visual pauses throughout the article. When presenting something as a quote from the speaker, keep it faithful to the original transcript and do not invent or substantially alter quotations.
* Create natural transitions so that the reader feels they are moving through a spiritual journey rather than reading disconnected sections.
* Aim for the overall feeling of: **Traditional Wisdom + Conversational Intimacy + Modern Readability + Visual Structure + Self-Reflection.**
* The final article should feel **2026-readable but 2036-relevant**, avoiding temporary slang or trends while remaining highly accessible to younger readers.
* Do not dilute, oversimplify, or sensationalize the spiritual teachings in the attempt to make them Gen Z-friendly.
* Preserve the dignity and authenticity of the speaker throughout.

### Tone & Intimacy (The Pravachan Style):

Retain the direct, conversational, and intimate connection between the speaker and the audience. Do NOT make it sound like a dry, rigid academic textbook.
Keep the language natural and accessible, mimicking how a refined saint or speaker's compiled lectures are published.

Vocabulary Transformation:
Elevate the language to clean, dignified Hindi (Shuddh Hindi) but avoid overly complex, obscure, or archaic Sanskritized (Tatsama) words that make reading difficult.
Convert informal mixed English words into their natural, universally understood Hindi counterparts (e.g., convert 'Noun/Verb' to 'नाम/क्रिया', 'Infinite' to 'अनंत', 'Computer' to 'संगणक या विज्ञान', 'Video' to 'चलचित्र').
Cleanly integrate explanation analogies without using the raw English filler words.

Structural Layout & Cleanup:
Strip away all spoken redundancies, false starts, vocal stutters, conversational filler loops (e.g., "तो जैसे कि", "मतलब", "you know", "right?"), and cross-talk.
Organize the monologue into cohesive, readable paragraphs with clear thematic transitions.
Use clean, structured Markdown headings and subheadings to divide the concepts logically.
