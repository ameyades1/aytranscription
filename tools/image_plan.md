# Blog image plan

Two images per blog: a 16:9 hero and one inline image placed after the named section.

| #      | Article                                                    | Image 1 — Hero                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Image 2 — Inline                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**  | **जहाँ जन्म, वहीं का अन्न**                                | **“The food of your land”** — A beautiful Indian regional thali on a rustic table, ingredients visibly local: grains, vegetables, spices, clay/wood textures. Background subtly suggests Indian farmland and soil. Warm natural morning light. **16:9.**                                                                      | **“Food from the soil”** — Close cinematic view of hands harvesting fresh vegetables/grains from Indian soil, with the harvested food in the foreground. Emphasize connection between *भूमि → अन्न → व्यक्ति*. Place after **“मिट्टी, पृथ्वी तत्व और प्रारब्ध”**. The article explicitly connects birthplace, soil, food and qualities. ([Ameya Desai][1]) |
| **2**  | **गणपति को लाल फूल और दूर्वा ही क्यों प्रिय हैं**          | **“Ganapati, red flowers & durva”** — Respectful devotional still life: traditional Ganapati murti, fresh red flowers and three-blade durva prominently arranged, soft temple light, red/orange/gold tones. No excessive ornamentation. **16:9.**                                                                             | **“The grass that bends”** — Macro image of green durva grass bending gently in wind, with a large tree softly blurred behind it. Visual metaphor for humility vs rigidity. Place after **“झुकना ही बचाता है”**. The article specifically uses bending durva as its metaphor. ([Ameya Desai][2])                                                           |
| **3**  | **खुश होने के लिए नहीं, खुश होकर कर्म करें**               | **“Work as joy”** — An Indian craftsperson/artist completely absorbed in making something by hand, smiling naturally but not posing; finished work nearby. The emphasis is on *joy in the action*, not reward. **16:9.**                                                                                                      | **“Work becomes play”** — A person working outdoors in a creative/professional setting with a light, energetic atmosphere; composition should visually communicate effortless engagement. Place after **“कर्मयोग का द्वार: काम खेल जैसा लगे”**. The article contrasts doing something *to become happy* with doing it *happily*. ([Ameya Desai][3])        |
| **4**  | **हर स्त्री के भीतर छिपी दिव्य शक्ति**                     | **“The feminine as Shakti”** — A dignified Indian woman standing confidently in natural light, surrounded subtly by symbolic visual elements inspired by strength, creation and nurturing. Avoid stereotypical “empowered woman” stock imagery. **16:9.**                                                                     | **“A mother’s formative power”** — Mother and young child, candid rather than posed, with the mother guiding/teaching the child. Warm, intimate light. Place after **“माँ की हज़ार गुना शक्ति”**. ([Ameya Desai][4])                                                                                                                                       |
| **5**  | **वेद पढ़ने की किताब नहीं, जानने की यात्रा है**            | **“Veda as a journey of knowing”** — Ancient Indian manuscript/palm-leaf manuscript aesthetic opening into a vast landscape/path toward sunrise. The visual should communicate *knowledge as a journey*, not “a book on a shelf.” **16:9.**                                                                                   | **“From knowledge to infinity”** — A person walking toward a vast horizon beneath an expansive sky, with subtle manuscript/knowledge textures in foreground. Place after **“वेद यानी जानना, पर क्या जानना?”**. ([Ameya Desai][5])                                                                                                                          |
| **6**  | **आपकी थाली में आपकी कमाई भी परोसी जाती है**               | **“What is behind the plate?”** — Beautiful Indian meal in foreground, with subtle visual connection behind it to honest work/earnings: hands preparing food, a working person's tools, natural ingredients. **16:9.**                                                                                                        | **“Two sources, one plate”** — Conceptual split composition: one side wholesome food and honest work; other side an elegant but morally ambiguous money/transaction scene. Keep it symbolic rather than portraying criminal activity. Place after **“धन और भोजन के अलग-अलग मेल”**. ([Ameya Desai][6])                                                      |
| **7**  | **भाग्य पहले से बह रहा है, बस रोड़ा हटाइए**                | **“The blocked canal”** — Indian agricultural field with a water canal; clear water flowing toward the field but blocked by one stone/debris. Cinematic aerial/low-angle hybrid feel. **16:9.**                                                                                                                               | **“Remove the obstruction”** — Close-up of a hand removing a stone from a small irrigation channel, with water immediately beginning to flow. Place after **“खेत, नहर और एक पत्थर”**. This is directly based on the article's central Patanjali analogy. ([Ameya Desai][7])                                                                                |
| **8**  | **मन को वश में करो, इससे पहले कि मन तुम्हें वश में कर ले** | **“Who holds the key to the mind?”** — Meditating person in a modern environment, with subtle visual distractions around them but the subject remaining centered and composed. Avoid cliché lotus-position imagery if possible. **16:9.**                                                                                     | **“Choosing what enters the mind”** — Person sitting before a screen/entertainment choices, consciously turning attention toward a calmer alternative; symbolic rather than literal. Place after **“सात्त्विक बुद्धि: पहले चुनाव, फिर एकाग्रता”**. ([Ameya Desai][8])                                                                                      |
| **9**  | **जन्मों की यात्रा एक साधना में**                          | **“The seven-chakra ascent”** — A human silhouette in meditation with a subtle vertical progression of seven luminous energy points, from base to crown. Elegant, restrained, not New Age/neon. **16:9.**                                                                                                                     | **“Seven levels of life”** — Symbolic ascending staircase/path with seven distinct stages, each subtly representing a different level of consciousness. Place after **“सात चक्र, सात तरह का जीवन”**. ([Ameya Desai][9])                                                                                                                                    |
| **10** | **आपकी संगति ही आपका भविष्य गढ़ती है**                     | **“The company you keep”** — A person walking with a small group of people along a path; the group naturally divides toward different directions/landscapes. The emphasis is on environment influencing direction. **16:9.**                                                                                                  | **“Changing your circle”** — Two contrasting social environments: one calm, constructive and purposeful; another chaotic/distracting. Keep people natural and non-caricatured. Place after **“It is the company you keep”**. ([Ameya Desai][10])                                                                                                           |
| **11** | **क्या मृत्यु के क्षण में मुक्ति संभव है?**                | **“The final breath / Om”** — Extremely calm spiritual image: meditating figure in stillness, soft dawn light, subtle circular/vibrational suggestion around the breath area. Avoid depicting death literally. **16:9.**                                                                                                      | **“Turning inward”** — Close, peaceful image of a meditating person with eyes closed, attention visually directed inward toward the heart/head; minimal environment. Place after **“दूसरा चरण: मन को हृदय में स्थिर करना”**. The article then moves through the Gita's stages of the final moment. ([Ameya Desai][11])                                     |
| **12** | **कर्म आपको जलाए उससे पहले आप कर्म को जला दो**             | **“Burning karma”** — Abstract but beautiful: old dried leaves/paper-like symbolic layers being consumed by a controlled sacred fire, with a calm background. Do **not** depict a person burning or suffering. **16:9.**                                                                                                      | **“Planets in relationships”** — Symbolic celestial bodies above a human social scene, suggesting that planetary themes are interpreted through relationships and conduct. Elegant, not astrology-horoscope cliché. Place after **“ग्रह आकाश में नहीं, आपके रिश्तों में हैं”**. ([Ameya Desai][12])                                                        |
| **13** | **धन, धर्म और मुक्ति का प्राचीन रहस्य**                    | **“Wealth, Dharma & liberation”** — Sophisticated Indian composition combining prosperity symbols, purposeful work and a spiritual path leading toward light. Avoid a literal pile of gold. **16:9.**                                                                                                                         | **“Beyond the job”** — Indian entrepreneur/creator working on something of their own in a workshop/studio/business setting, with a purposeful rather than corporate-stock aesthetic. Place after **“नौकरी से आगे का सपना”**. ([Ameya Desai][13])                                                                                                           |
| **14** | **महाशिवरात्रि पर जानिए शिवलिंग का असली रहस्य**            | **“The Shivalinga”** — Respectful close cinematic image of a Shiva lingam during abhishekam, water flowing over it, subtle temple light, stone texture, flowers kept restrained. **16:9.**                                                                                                                                    | **“Visible body, invisible presence”** — Artistic conceptual image of a human form fading from physical silhouette into a subtle luminous/abstract inner form. This should communicate *स्थूल → सूक्ष्म*, not death. Place after **“मृत्यु के बाद क्या बदल जाता है?”**. ([Ameya Desai][14])                                                                |
| **15** | **भीतर की तीन शक्तियों को जगाइए**                          | **“Kali, Lakshmi & Saraswati — three inner powers”** — Three symbolic feminine divine archetypes represented through **energy/color/objects**, rather than necessarily three literal deity portraits. Kali = transformative power, Lakshmi = abundance, Saraswati = knowledge. Elegant temple-inspired composition. **16:9.** | **“Symbols as a language”** — Carefully composed symbolic objects: Ganapati form, mouse motif, lotus/book/music element, etc., arranged as a visual language of spiritual symbolism. Place after **“देवताओं के रूप: प्रतीकों की भाषा”**. The article explicitly explains deity forms as symbolic representations. ([Ameya Desai][15])                      |
| **16** | **दैवी संपत्ति को जगाने की कला**                           | **“True Lakshmi / divine wealth”** — A serene person surrounded by subtle symbols of fearlessness, generosity, discipline, learning and inner clarity; prosperity shown as qualities rather than money. **16:9.**                                                                                                             | **“Fearlessness”** — Person standing calmly at the edge of a vast landscape at dawn, posture relaxed and confident, visual emphasis on absence of fear rather than physical bravery. Place after **“डर आता कहाँ से है?”**. ([Ameya Desai][16])                                                                                                             |
