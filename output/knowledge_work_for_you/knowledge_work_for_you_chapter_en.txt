# Chapter: Knowledge Is Not Enough, One Must Master One's Nature

## All Beings Are Ruled by Their Nature

> **sadṛśaṁ ceṣṭate svasyāḥ prakṛter jñānavān api**
> **prakṛtiṁ yānti bhūtāni nigrahaḥ kiṁ kariṣyati**
> "Even a man of knowledge acts according to his own nature. All beings follow their nature; what can restraint accomplish?"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.33)

What does this verse say? "All beings." What should we understand by a being (*prāṇī*)? We are not talking about dogs and cats here. Whoever has *prāṇa*, life-breath, within is a being. So we too are beings. Notice that it does not say "animals"; it says "beings."

All beings follow their *prakṛti*, that is, they act under the sway of their own *svabhāva*, their nature. "Sva" means I myself, and "bhāva" means your emotions. So this is about the mind. Every being acts under the control of its mind, under the sway of its own nature.

Everyone does things in their own way, don't they? We even say that the five fingers are not alike. People speak differently, behave differently, work with different qualities. Everyone acts under the sway of their own nature. There is nothing remarkable in this.

## Even the Learned Follow Their Nature

The verse goes on: even one who has acquired knowledge. This is not yet *tattvajñāna*, the knowledge of ultimate truth; that comes in the eighteenth chapter. This is ordinary knowledge: one has read many books, studied the scriptures, recited mantras, done all the spiritual practice there was to do, become learned, become a pandit. Even such a learned person acts according to their own nature. As their original destiny was, so is their nature. Then what can anyone's forcefulness achieve?

To put it in simple words:

> "Knowledge does not stop one from committing sin."

## The Gita Was First Told to Duryodhana

Do you know that Krishna first told the Gita to Duryodhana? He told it to Arjuna later. Krishna first tried to explain it to Duryodhana, to Dhritarashtra, but they did not understand.

The rule is that a bad person should be taught such scripture first, so that they never commit the wrong at all. If they never commit it, there will be no war. But a bad person is bad; they will not stop, and he did not stop. Therefore a good person must also learn to strike back when needed. Must I only endure? Endurance was spoken of in the second chapter. After that, the Lord does not say only "endure"; some counteraction is necessary.

So what did He say? Even the wise act according to their nature; then what can anyone's forcefulness achieve? This is exactly right.

## "Don't Give Me Knowledge"

When Krishna went to explain to Duryodhana or Dhritarashtra, all they said was: don't give me knowledge. Just as college boys say nowadays when you try to teach them something: "Don't give us knowledge, we have enough." Children in cities especially talk this way, and perhaps the same language has reached the villages too: "Don't give me knowledge, I know."

So did Duryodhana lack knowledge? He studied in the very same gurukul as Arjuna. Duryodhana said that this was not his problem. What is right and what is wrong, I understand too; my problem is something else. He said:

> "Dharma I know, but I have no wish to practise it; and adharma too I know, but I cannot give it up. If you have some remedy for this, tell me."

His problem is now clear. This is our problem too. Within us also hides a Duryodhana, a Dhritarashtra.

## Dhritarashtra Is the Mind, Gandhari the Intellect

Dhritarashtra is blind, isn't he? He is the symbol of our mind, the mind from which our nature arises. The mind is actually blind; that is, it gets caught in *māyā*. It is blind, not wise.

Whom was he married to? Gandhari. Did Gandhari have eyes or not? She did. She is the symbol of the intellect. The intellect is supposed to be higher than the mind; it should have shown the mind the way. Had she been a truly devoted wife to her blind husband, instead of binding her own eyes she should have guided him all her life. When you act wholly under the control of the mind, under the control of your nature, your intellect too deliberately becomes blind.

## A Hundred Kauravas: A Hundred Vices

And the result will be the Kauravas, that is, a hundred. You have ten senses: five organs of action and five organs of perception. Take ten vices for each, and ten times ten makes a hundred vices.

Duryodhana is the one who stays far from wealth. Wealth here does not mean money; it means **divine wealth**: compassion, forgiveness, love. These are called divine wealth. The person who stays far away from them is Duryodhana. Duhshasana is the one who cannot govern properly.

> "These are not persons outside; they are qualities within us."

Isn't this the problem of many people? This is why problems come into many people's lives: they have knowledge, but no wish to act on it. Even after going to the guru, they do not do what is right.

## The Solution: Do Not Fall Under Like and Dislike

See the solution further on in the Bhagavad Gita, right after that verse, in the thirty-fourth verse of the third chapter. What Duryodhana could not understand in his delusion, what Dhritarashtra could not understand, Arjuna understood:

> **indriyasyendriyasyārthe rāga-dveṣau vyavasthitau**
> **tayor na vaśam āgacchet tau hy asya paripanthinau**
> "Attachment and aversion reside in the objects of each sense. One should not come under their sway, for they are one's adversaries."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.34)

*Indriya* means your whole body, all your sense organs. In the objects of each sense, that is, in whatever we interact with in the world, whatever object, person or place we connect with, attachment and aversion lie hidden.

*Rāga* and *dveṣa* mean likes and dislikes. Everyone has them: I like this food, not that one. If one is to marry, I like this woman, not that one. So won't each person's likes and dislikes be different? They certainly will.

In the objects of each sense, attachment and aversion lie hidden. A person should not come under the sway of either. You are not to do something just because you like it, and not to avoid something just because you dislike it. For both are great enemies that obstruct one's path to wellbeing.

## Shreya and Preya

So what should one do then? The whole of the Bhagavad Gita that follows says this: do what the guru says is right and wrong. This is called **shreya** and **preya**. Shreya is the higher action, the action the guru has shown. Preya is what is pleasing to me.

One is not to sit doing things just because "I like it."

> "The scripture itself should become dear to me."
