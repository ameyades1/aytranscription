## 1. Summary

The talk takes on a problem almost every reader has felt: knowing what is right and still not doing it, and knowing what is wrong and still not giving it up. Acharya Upendra Ji answers it through Bhagavad Gita 3.33 (all beings, the learned included, follow their nature) and 3.34 (attraction and aversion sit in every sense object; do not fall under their sway). He reads the Mahabharata as an inner map (Dhritarashtra as the blind mind, Gandhari as the blindfolded intellect, the hundred Kauravas as a hundred distortions) and ends with the choice between shreyas and preyas. The main intent is emotional and informational ("why do I know but not do", "knowledge vs action") with a strong secondary verse intent (Gita 3.33 / 3.34 meaning). The opportunity is that the top results for this question are generic self-help articles about procrastination or the "knowing-doing gap"; a scripture-rooted answer with two cited verses, a clear diagnosis (svabhava, raga-dvesha) and a practical remedy (shreyas over preyas, guided by the Guru) can stand out in both languages. The source video (7:21) is short, so the blog is the deeper version.

## 6. FAQ

**Hindi**

1. **हम सही जानते हुए भी उसे क्यों नहीं कर पाते?** गीता ३.३३ के अनुसार सभी प्राणी, ज्ञानी भी, अपनी प्रकृति और स्वभाव के अनुसार कर्म करते हैं। केवल किताबी ज्ञान स्वभाव को नहीं बदलता, इसलिए ज्ञान होते हुए भी व्यवहार वैसा ही रहता है।
2. **गीता ३.३४ का अर्थ क्या है?** हर इंद्रिय के हर विषय में राग (पसंद) और द्वेष (नापसंद) छिपे हुए स्थित हैं। मनुष्य को इन दोनों के वश में नहीं होना चाहिए, क्योंकि ये दोनों ही कल्याण के मार्ग के महान शत्रु हैं।
3. **महाभारत में धृतराष्ट्र और गांधारी किसके प्रतीक हैं?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार अंधा धृतराष्ट्र हमारे मन का प्रतीक है, जो माया में अटक जाता है, और गांधारी बुद्धि का, जिसने आँखों पर पट्टी बाँध ली। सौ कौरव हमारे भीतर के सौ विकार हैं।
4. **श्रेय और प्रेय में क्या अंतर है?** श्रेय वह है जो श्रेष्ठ है, जो गुरु बताते हैं; प्रेय वह है जो हमें प्रिय लगता है। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि केवल "अच्छा लगता है" इसलिए कुछ करते नहीं रहना चाहिए, बल्कि शास्त्र को ही प्रिय बनाना चाहिए।

**English**

1. **Why can't we do what we know is right?** Bhagavad Gita 3.33 says all beings, even the learned, act according to their own nature and temperament. Book learning alone does not change that nature, so behaviour stays the same despite the knowledge.
2. **What does Bhagavad Gita 3.34 mean?** Attraction (raga) and aversion (dvesha) lie hidden in the objects of every sense. One should not come under their control, because both are enemies on the path to one's highest good.
3. **What do Dhritarashtra and Gandhari symbolise?** In Acharya Ji's reading, the blind Dhritarashtra stands for the mind, which gets trapped in maya, and Gandhari for the intellect, which has blindfolded itself. The hundred Kauravas are the hundred distortions within us.
4. **What is the difference between shreyas and preyas?** Shreyas is what is truly good, the path the Guru points out; preyas is what merely feels pleasant. "I enjoy it" is no reason to keep doing something; Acharya Ji says the scriptures themselves should become what we love.

The FAQ answers for 3.34 use the full Gita Press meaning (both halves of the verse) rather than the Hindi blog's partial meaning block (flag 3). The FAQ stays in this report; add FAQPage schema only if the editor puts the FAQ on the page.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Duryodhana's "I know dharma but cannot do it" (review issue 2, Major).** The statement paraphrases "जानामि धर्मं न च मे प्रवृत्तिः...", traditionally attributed to Duryodhana in the Pandava Gita, not found in his replies to Krishna in the Udyoga Parva; the "is there any cure" request is not in the verse. Recommendation: do not use this quote, or "Duryodhana asked Krishna for a cure", in titles, meta descriptions, FAQ answers or snippets. The SEO copy above avoids it. Do not target the "jaanami dharmam" query until the editor settles the attribution wording.
2. **Heading "The Gita Was First Offered to Duryodhana" (review issue 3, Major).** The Gita was spoken only to Arjuna; Krishna counselled Duryodhana during the peace mission. Recommendation: adopt the review's heading and never use "Gita was first told to Duryodhana" as a hook, however clickable.
3. **Hindi meaning of Gita 3.34 is incomplete (review issue 1, Major).** The Hindi block gives only the first half and is framed as paraphrase. The FAQ and direct answer use the full Gita Press meaning. Recommendation: fix the blog's meaning block as the review suggests; until then, featured-snippet copy should rely on the FAQ wording.
4. **Symbolic name readings (review issue 4).** "Duryodhana = far from wealth", "Dushasana = cannot govern himself" are Acharya Ji's teaching readings, not grammatical etymology, and "कौरव, यानी सौ" reads as if Kaurava means hundred. Recommendation: in any SEO copy, present these as "Acharya Ji reads..." rather than "Duryodhana means...".
5. **Daivi sampatti list (review issue 5).** "Love" is not in the Gita 16.1-3 list. Recommendation: do not write "the Gita's divine qualities: compassion, forgiveness and love" in meta or snippets; use "such as".
6. **"Moha (attachment)" gloss in English (review issue 6).** Moha is delusion; attachment is raga. The keyword "raga and dvesha meaning" depends on keeping these distinct. Recommendation: apply the review's gloss fix.
7. **"Strike back" teaching.** The line that a good person must learn to resist is the speaker's reading of the move from Gita chapter 2 to later chapters. Recommendation: do not promote it in titles or social snippets out of context (it could read as a call to retaliation); it is linked only to the talk on treating others on merit.
8. **Category.** `gallery_topics.txt` lists this talk under `success` (Success & Prosperity), and seo.json copies it as required. The content is closer to `mind` (Mind & Self) or `dharma` (Gita verses). Recommendation: the editor may want to move it to `mind` in `gallery_topics.txt`; no change made here.

Also noted (minor, from the review, no SEO conflict): English 3.34 meaning adds "response"; IAST ṁ vs ṃ; optional Katha Upanishad 1.2.1-2 reference for shreyas/preyas, which would strengthen E-E-A-T.

- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
