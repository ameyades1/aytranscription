# Why the Crow Eats the Pind and Why It Does Not

When Pitru Paksha arrives, Shraddha is performed in home after home, pinds are made, and people wait on rooftops for the crow. But have you ever wondered why?

Who are the Pitars? What is the true meaning of Shraddha and Tarpan? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji opens up these questions in very simple language. Let us understand the knowledge behind this tradition with him.

## What Is a Pind? This Body Is Also a Village

**Pind** means the body, mind and intellect of the very person performing the Shraddha. Every living being is called a pind.

In some languages like Punjabi, pind also means a village. Acharya Ji says that, in just the same way, this body too is a village.

In this village there are many **vikaras** (inner impurities), crooked and confused thoughts, and **vasanas** (desires). Setting all of these aside, this pind is to be offered to the Supreme.

*Which thoughts and desires live in this village within you today?*

## What Does the White Rice Pind Say?

The pind is made only of rice, so it is white. White is the symbol of **sattva** (purity, harmony).

It signifies that our mind, intellect and entire **chetana** (consciousness) should be sattvic. Our whole state should rise to that level. This is the first symbol of Pind Daan.

## Is the Crow Our Ancestor?

Many people believe that the crow that comes to eat the pind is some ancestor. Someone's uncle, someone's grandmother, someone's other relative.

But Acharya Ji raises a simple question: if the same crow goes to three or four homes and touches the pind, whose grandmother was it, whose aunt, whose father? He states plainly that the crow is no one's ancestor.

The crow, the dog or the cow have a special power. They can know whether the **Pitars** (departed ancestors) are nearby or not. They sense it; they experience it.

## Why Do the Ancestors Come?

In the Shraddha ritual, the ancestors' favourite food is deliberately placed so that they will come.

The second reason is their attachment to us. When we call them with love, the chance that they come quickly grows.

And if their mind is still caught somewhere, that is, if they are not yet liberated, they will surely come around us.

## The Crow: An Indicator

The crow understands that there is some negative presence nearby. It does not itself know whether this is called a Pitar or something else, but it does not come forward.

Acharya Ji suggests an experiment. On an ordinary day, feed a crow plain rice on your balcony, and it will eat at once. There is a little fear in it, but it understands that you will not harm it.

Now try it after specially **invoking** (avahan) the ancestors, with black sesame seeds placed on the rice. Then it will not come.

On the other hand, if someone's ancestors are already liberated and satisfied, there is no negative energy there. The crow feels no deception, and it eats immediately.

Acharya Ji explains that if the crow has touched the pind, it means those ancestors are liberated, and if it does not eat, it means they are not liberated.

That is why the tradition came to see the crow as an indicator.

*The next time you offer a pind, will you only wait for the crow, or will you also resolve to make the pind within you sattvic?*

## Key Takeaways

- Pind means the performer's own body, mind and intellect; this body too is a village.
- Setting aside impurities and desires and offering this pind to the Supreme is the spirit of Pind Daan.
- The white rice pind symbolises a sattvic mind, intellect and consciousness.
- The crow is no one's ancestor; the crow, dog and cow can sense the presence of ancestors.
- Favourite food, a loving call and a mind still caught somewhere draw the ancestors near.
- If the crow eats the pind, the ancestors are liberated; if it does not, they are still nearby.
