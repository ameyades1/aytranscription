# Chapter: Without a Guru, Scriptures Can Mislead

## The Illusion of a Shortcut Through Books

A boy thinks, "I will go to the market, get the scripture and read it myself." He goes to the market and brings the scripture home. He feels he has found a shortcut. Now he is free of the Guru, there is no need to learn anything, no need for any service, and the time of his life is saved as well.

Is your thinking not going the same way, that after all the Guru only taught from a book, and now that book is in my own hands?

The poor boy brings the book home. The dakshina (offering to the teacher) is saved too. He sits down in some hut or in his own house and begins to read the scripture.

## The Literal Meaning of Words

The scripture says, "Being broken is very good." From the very first sentence he learns that being broken is good, that God loves it greatly. Further it says that vomit is dear to God. And also that the cloth covering the dead is sacred.

Now this boy has "learned." When he wanders through the village, he thinks to himself, "I have become a scholar."

You remember the example taught earlier, "May I follow you" and the one about Queen Elizabeth? "BB Roy of Great Britain", meaning the wife of BB Roy, and go look for Great Britain in America! This is how blind belief begins. This boy too learns something exactly like that.

## Upside-Down Knowledge in the Village

He goes into the village and sees that in one family there are constant quarrels, everything looks broken. He says, "God likes this very much. All these relationships are broken, so this is very good."

He is firmly convinced that he has mastered the scripture. He goes to their house and says, "Get a divorce, quarrel." He leaves everyone fighting. "If you break apart, God will be pleased. I have read the scripture, I am the authority. My Guru also taught from a book, so I have also learned from a book." Before long he will have ruined the whole village.

Going a little further he sees a hospital, where many patients are vomiting. The ward staff, the doctors and nurses say, "Throw this away, clean it up." Hearing this he becomes very angry. "Oh, this is such a pure, sacred thing! Don't throw it away. You don't know its value, I know its value." He collects all of it and keeps it in a good place in large containers, to offer to God.

Then near the cremation ground he sees that people cremate the body and throw away the cloth and sheet that covered it. Again he becomes angry. "Oh, what are you doing? This has been called so sacred, keep this too."

Now he has become such a "wise man." This is the problem. The whole village starts turning upside down.

## The Arrival of Another Guru

Then suddenly another wise man, another Guru, comes to the village. (This boy had found his own Guru to be upside down, which is exactly why he did all this.)

The Guru asks, "What are you doing?"

He says, "I am a scholar. I have read it in the book, and I am acting according to it."

The Guru says, "Who said these are sacred things? All of these are impure, they should be thrown away. How can a scripture say such a thing? Do you have no Guru? Or is your Guru himself upside down?"

He replies, "I am my own Guru."

## Taking a Guru Is the First Duty

Then the Guru sits him down and explains, "How did it come about that you need no Guru to understand the scriptures? A Guru is absolutely necessary. The first duty is to take a Guru."

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Regard your mother as God, regard your father as God, regard your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

Your very first Guru should have been your mother. If she is not learned, then your father. In those times, men at least came back having learned, and through them the girls learned as well. Marriage happened in childhood, so the parents should have had knowledge and should have taught. If neither of them has the knowledge, the child should have been sent to an acharya (teacher).

## Scriptures Speak in Hints

Then the Guru explains the true meaning of the scripture.

"Inside a cow there is milk. When you draw it out, it separates from the cow, it breaks away, something has come out of it. The scripture was pointing to this: that it is for the abhishek (ritual bathing) of God, and God likes it very much. Something broken, like a fruit plucked from a tree. Something has separated, become independent. The scripture was hinting that such things are offered. Scriptures are written in hints like this."

"The honeybee takes the nectar of flowers and then brings it back up. That 'vomit' is honey, and you also perform God's abhishek with honey. God loves it. Do you understand now?"

"There are certain insects, such as silkworms. After they die, your cloth is made from them. So the cloth that came into being through the dead is what is to be offered. When you offer the heaviest silk sari, you say 'vastraṃ samarpayāmi' (I offer this cloth), don't you? That is how the scriptures are."

> "Your first duty is to go to a Guru and understand the scriptures, and to do your prescribed duties through that."

## The Tamasic Doer and the Sattvic Doer

Now do you understand what this boy's problem was? He also acted, but what was the mistake? He is a **tamasic doer**. You have to become a **sattvic doer**, one who acts according to the method of the scriptures.

And scripture is the Guru's own lived knowledge, which comes down into a book. That knowledge will never come directly from the book.

## Either Become Dattatreya, or Take a Living Guru

Either become as great as **Dattatreya**. Dattatreya Maharaj received knowledge directly from the world, from twenty-four Gurus. One must have that kind of capacity. If you do not, then a Guru in a physical body is absolutely necessary. Without practical knowledge, the scriptures cannot be understood like this.

> "Acting according to the scriptures means acting according to the Guru, acting according to a living Guru."

This is the first duty. Has the meaning of sattva guna now become properly clear?
