# Without a Guru Scriptures Can Mislead

## Can a Book Take the Place of a Guru?

A boy thinks, "I will get the scripture from the market and read it myself." He brings the scripture home and feels he has found a shortcut.

Now he is free of the Guru, no need to learn anything, no service. The dakshina (the offering given to the Guru) is saved, and so is the time of his life.

*Is your thinking not the same, that after all the Guru only taught from a book, and that book is now in my hands?*

## When Words Are Taken Literally

The boy sits at home and begins reading the scripture. The first sentence says: "Being broken is very good, it is dear to God." Then it says that vomit is dear to God. And that the cloth covering the dead is sacred.

That's it, he has "learned." Now as he wanders the village he thinks, "I have become a scholar."

Gurudev recalls that old example: "May I follow you", Queen Elizabeth, taking "BB Roy of Great Britain" to mean the wife of BB Roy and looking for Great Britain in America. **This is exactly how blind belief begins.**

## A "Wise Man" and a Village Turning Upside Down

In the village he sees a family where there are constant quarrels and everything is broken. He says, "The relationships are broken, this is very good, God likes it." And he advises them: get a divorce, quarrel.

His reasoning is, "I have read the scripture, I am the authority. My Guru also taught from a book, so I have also learned from a book."

In the hospital, when the doctors and nurses have the vomit cleaned up, he gets angry: "You are throwing away something so sacred? I know its value!" He fills large drums with it and keeps it to offer to God.

At the cremation ground the cloth covering the body is thrown away, and again the same anger: "Keep this too, it is sacred!"

Slowly the whole village starts turning upside down.

## "I Am My Own Guru"

Then another Guru comes to the village. (This boy had found his own Guru to be upside down, which is why he took this path.)

The Guru asks, "What are you doing? These are all impure things, they should be thrown away. Do you have no Guru?"

The boy says proudly, "I am my own Guru."

## Taking a Guru Is the First Duty

The Guru sits him down and explains that to understand the scriptures, a Guru is absolutely necessary. **The first duty is to take a Guru.**

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Regard your mother as God, regard your father as God, regard your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

The first Guru should have been the mother. If she is not learned, then the father. In those times men came back having learned, and through them the girls learned too. And if neither parent has the knowledge, the child should be sent to an acharya (teacher).

## Scriptures Speak in Hints

Now the Guru explains what the scripture really meant:

- **"Something broken"**: milk drawn from a cow, which has separated from the cow. A fruit plucked from a tree. This is what is meant for God's abhishek (ritual bathing) and for offering.
- **"Vomit"**: the honeybee takes the nectar of flowers and brings it back up, and that is honey. And God's abhishek is also done with honey.
- **"The cloth of the dead"**: silk, made after the silkworms die. When we offer a heavy silk sari, we say "vastraṃ samarpayāmi" (I offer this cloth).

That is how scriptures are. The letters say one thing, and the hint points to something deeper. That is why Acharya Ji says that our first duty is to go to a Guru and understand the scriptures, and to do our prescribed duties through that.

*Have you ever taken a spiritual teaching literally and drawn the opposite meaning from it?*

## The Tamasic Doer versus the Sattvic Doer

The boy also acted, but where did he go wrong? He was a **tamasic doer**, one who acts out of ignorance and inertia.

We have to become a **sattvic doer**, one who acts according to the method of the scriptures. And scripture is **the Guru's own lived knowledge**, which comes down into a book. That knowledge never comes directly from the book.

## Either Become Dattatreya, or Take a Living Guru

**Dattatreya** Maharaj received knowledge directly from the world, from twenty-four Gurus. If you have that kind of capacity, fine.

If not, a Guru in a physical body is absolutely necessary. Without practical knowledge, the scriptures cannot be understood.

Acharya Ji explains that acting according to the scriptures means acting according to the Guru, according to a living Guru. This is the right understanding of **sattva guna**.

## Key Takeaways

- Reading scripture from a book is not a shortcut around the Guru; scripture read literally can lead to blind belief and ruin.
- Scriptures speak in hints: "something broken" is milk and fruit, "vomit" is honey, "the cloth of the dead" is silk.
- Taking a Guru is the first duty: first the mother, then the father, then the acharya.
- Scripture is the Guru's lived knowledge; understand it from the Guru and only then do your prescribed duties.
- Be not a tamasic doer but a sattvic doer, one who acts by the method of the scriptures, that is, according to a living Guru.
- Either have the capacity of Dattatreya, or a living Guru is essential.
