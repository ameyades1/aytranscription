## 1. Summary

The talk is a parable: a boy who thinks he can skip the Guru buys the scriptures, reads three lines literally ("to be broken is dear to God", "vomit is dear to God", "the cloth of the dead is sacred") and turns his village upside down, until a visiting Guru explains the signs behind them (milk and fruit, honey, silk). The lesson: taking a Guru is the first duty (mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava), scripture is the Guru's lived knowledge, and a sattvic doer acts by a living Guru unless one has Dattatreya's readiness. The main intent is informational ("can I read scriptures without a Guru", "is a Guru necessary", "गुरु क्यों ज़रूरी है"), from self-taught readers of the Gita and Puranas and from people who meet literal, fear-based readings online. Competition is general "importance of Guru" content; the opportunity is the memorable parable and the three decoded "riddles", which no generic page has. It sits in the Guru cluster next to "what is a Guru" and close to "meaning of shastra".

## 6. FAQ

1. Can I understand scriptures by reading them on my own? / क्या मैं ख़ुद पढ़कर शास्त्र समझ सकता हूँ?
   Acharya Ji says no: scriptures speak in signs, and read literally they can lead to blind faith. Unless you have Dattatreya's readiness to learn from life itself, you need a living Guru.
2. What does "mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava" mean? / मातृदेवो भव का अर्थ क्या है?
   "Regard your mother, father and teacher as divine" (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2). Acharya Ji reads it as the order of Gurus: first the mother, then the father, then an acharya.
3. What did the "broken thing", "vomit" and "cloth of the dead" really mean? / "टूटी चीज़", "वमन" और "मृतक का वस्त्र" का असली अर्थ क्या था?
   Milk taken from the cow and fruit plucked from the tree; honey, which the bee brings up from nectar; and silk, made after the silkworms die. All are offered to God.
4. What is the difference between a tamasic and a sattvic doer? / तामसिक और सात्त्विक कर्ता में क्या अंतर है?
   In the talk, the tamasic doer acts out of ignorance and inertia; the sattvic doer acts by the scriptures as understood from a living Guru.
5. Who can learn without a human Guru? / किसे देहधारी गुरु की ज़रूरत नहीं?
   Dattatreya, who learned from twenty-four teachers found in the world. Without that readiness, a Guru in human form is essential.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. Sattvic doer definition (from the Sanskrit review, issue 1): the blog defines the सात्त्विक कर्ता as one who acts by शास्त्र-विधि. Gita 18.26 defines the sattvic doer differently (free from attachment and ego, steady, unmoved by success or failure); acting by शास्त्रविधि is Gita 16.23-24. Recommendation: do not use "Bhagavad Gita 18.26" in any title, meta or FAQ; the FAQ answer above says "in the talk". The editor may apply the review's wording that marks this as Acharya Ji's linking of the two teachings.
2. Partial mantra (review issue 2): Taittiriya 1.11.2 continues "अतिथिदेवो भव"; the blog quotes three of four phrases without marking an excerpt. The FAQ quotes only the three phrases with their meaning. Recommendation: add "(अंश)" / "(excerpt)" or the fourth phrase in the blog.
3. Double danda in the English IAST (review issue 3) and "vastraṁ" vs "vastraṃ" (review issue 4): minor notation; fix in the blog when edited. Not used in SEO fields.
4. "गुरुदेव याद दिलाते हैं" in the Hindi (review issue 5) may be read as one of the story's Gurus; the review suggests "आचार्य जी". Not used in SEO fields.
5. The parable's "scripture" lines are not traced to any text (review: could not verify). Titles, metas and FAQ call them riddles/signs from the talk and never attribute them to a named scripture. Keep it that way.
6. The talk's reading of the mantra as an order of Gurus (mother, father, acharya) is the speaker's teaching, not the literal translation; the FAQ gives the literal meaning first and then "Acharya Ji reads it as...". Keep this separation.

## 12. SEO checklist

### Keywords
- [x] Hindi and English each have a focus keyword
- [x] The focus keyword isn't used by another post (no cannibalization)
- [x] 2–4 secondary keywords per language
- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [ ] Within the first 100 words (preferred): neither focus keyword appears in the first 100 words (first use is in the "first duty" section)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best

### Category and tags
- [x] One category, from the approved 14
- [x] 3–6 tags per language
- [x] No tag repeats a category name
- [x] Existing tags reused; no near-duplicates
- [x] Each tag can be shared by at least 3 posts
- [x] No broad labels that only restate a category
- [x] Series tags named exactly as the series (no series; not applicable)
- [x] `festivals` only when the post is about the festival itself; Pitru topics in `pitru`

### Structured data (in this report, not on the page)
- [x] One JSON-LD block per language: BlogPosting, Person, Organization, VideoObject (if there is a video), BreadcrumbList
- [x] Hindi and English versions linked as translations

### Content trust
- [x] Titles, metas and snippets faithful to the talk
- [x] No unsupported scriptural claims
- [x] Health promises, fear-selling and attacks on other traditions flagged

### Not covered
- Live broken links (internal links only work once the linked post is published)
- Canonical and Open Graph tags (SureRank adds these itself)
