## 1. Summary

A two-minute Valentine's Day talk about love without discernment. Acharya Ji tells a story that Lakshmi must always end up with an asura (Sita taken by Ravana, Radha first married to Ayan), then sets the story aside: it is really about **hridaya tattva**, the nature of the heart. Rajoguna ends in suffering; whoever gives heart, money and life to anyone, even a drunkard, has to get hurt at least once, and only then turns to the Guru. "It is too bad to be too good": love led by the heart alone, without **viveka**, is **andhabhakti** (blind devotion). The main intent is emotional (people who feel they gave too much to the wrong person, "am I too nice", "why do good people get hurt"), with an informational side (the meaning of the phrase, love and viveka). The opportunity is a compassionate, tradition-grounded answer to a very common relationship question, where results are mostly pop-psychology listicles. The talk is short, so the post should target a narrow, emotional long-tail query rather than broad "true love" terms already covered by sibling Valentine's talks.

## 6. FAQ

For the page or WordPress FAQ block (no FAQPage schema; see section 8).

1. **"Too bad to be too good" का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "too bad to be too good" mean?**
   ज़रूरत से ज़्यादा भला होना भी अच्छा नहीं; बिना सोचे सब कुछ दे देने से चोट मिलती है। / Being kinder than the situation calls for does you no good; giving everything without thought leads to hurt.
2. **अच्छे लोगों को प्रेम में चोट क्यों लगती है? / Why do good-hearted people get hurt in love?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार जो केवल हृदय से चलता है और कहीं भी प्रेम करता है, उसे कम से कम एक बार ऐसे व्यक्ति से पाला पड़ता है जो उस प्रेम के योग्य नहीं। / According to Acharya Ji, whoever is led by the heart alone and loves anywhere will meet, at least once, someone unworthy of that love.
3. **रजोगुण और दुख का क्या संबंध है? / How is rajoguna linked to suffering?**
   रजोगुण आसक्ति और चंचलता का गुण है जो बिना सोचे खींच लेता है; आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि उसका अंत दुख है। / Rajoguna is the quality of passion and restlessness that pulls us without thought; Acharya Ji says it ends in suffering.
4. **अंधभक्ति क्या है? / What is blind devotion (andhabhakti)?**
   जब भावना तो हो पर विवेक न हो, वही अंधभक्ति है। / It is the state where feeling is present but discernment (viveka) is missing.
5. **चोट खाने के बाद क्या होता है? / What happens after one gets hurt?**
   आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि सीख मिलती है कि इतना नहीं देना है, और उसके बाद ही व्यक्ति गुरु की ओर आता है। / Acharya Ji says the lesson sinks in, do not give that much, and only then does one turn to the Guru.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Radha "married to an asura" (Sanskrit review issue 1, major).** No Gita Press Purana calls Radha's husband an asura (Brahmavaivarta Purana: Rayana, a gopa, married to Chhaya-Radha; Gaudiya: Ayan/Abhimanyu, a gopa). The Hindi says "आयन जैसे असुर", the English softens it. Recommendation: never use Radha, Ayan or "Radha's husband" in titles, meta, FAQ or snippets; the editor should apply the review's attributed wording. Avoid linking this post to `radha_krishna_divine_secret` until fixed.
2. **Lakshmi's "curse" stated as Puranic fact (review issue 2, major).** No scriptural source is known; Radha as Lakshmi's incarnation is also not the Vishnu Purana's view. Recommendation: do not target "लक्ष्मी का शाप" / "Lakshmi curse"; keep Lakshmi out of the title tag (it is only in the meta as "लक्ष्मी की कथा" / "the story of Lakshmi", which is accurate as a story). The H2 tweak in section 4 attributes it to Acharya Ji.
3. **"Puranas, the ancient Indian books of myth and legend" (review issue 3, minor).** Dismissive gloss in the English only. Recommendation: apply the review's correction; no SEO text repeats it.
4. **"आयन" vs "Ayan" (review issue 4, minor).** Spelling mismatch; usual form अयन. Editorial only.
5. **Latin script in the Hindi takeaway (review issue 5, minor).** "Too bad to be too good" in Latin script inside the Hindi list. Editorial only; the Hindi SEO fields use Devanagari.
6. **Valentine's Day framing.** The video is a Valentine's Day special but the blog text never mentions it. Using it as a tag and seasonal keyword is fair; it is kept out of titles and meta, and nothing claims the talk endorses or criticises the festival.
7. **Rebirth and "every lifetime" / the drunkard example.** "उसे हर जन्म में यही दोहराना पड़ता है" is the speaker's teaching, not a cited scripture; and the drunkard is one example of an unworthy recipient. FAQ and meta keep to "at least once you get hurt" and do not generalise into claims about addicts, partners or fate. Search intent here is emotional (people who were hurt); keep the framing compassionate, never "it was your fault".
