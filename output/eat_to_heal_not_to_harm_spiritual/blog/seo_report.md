## 1. Summary

A short talk giving the spiritual reason for vegetarianism. Acharya Ji starts from moksha: a seeker who experiences that everything is God, and that God is who they are, cannot kill or eat another being, just as no one would cut off their own limb. He then defines violence through examples: nails, hair and sweat grow back and cause little or no pain, so giving them up (as in temple hair offering) is not violence, while breaking a limb or killing is. Plant food takes only the part that grows back, feels little and keeps its prana long; he closes with the Devi's Shakambhari form (Durga Saptashati 11.49). The main intent is informational ("why be vegetarian", "शाकाहार क्यों"), with a strong values angle. The opportunity: most ranking pages argue vegetarianism through health, environment or scripture lists; this talk offers a non-dual, oneness-based reason and a clear, everyday definition of himsa that few pages give. The page is short, so it suits long-tail and "why" queries rather than broad head terms.

**English (58 words):** According to Acharya Upendra Ji, the spiritual reason for vegetarianism is the experience of oneness. A seeker of moksha sees God and their own self in every being, so they cannot kill or eat another. Giving up what grows back without pain is not violence, and plant food takes only the part of the plant that grows again.

## 6. FAQ

**Hindi**
1. **शाकाहार का आध्यात्मिक कारण क्या है?** आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि मोक्ष का साधक सबमें स्वयं को और ईश्वर को देखता है, इसलिए कोई अपने ही अंग को न मारना चाहेगा, न खाना।
2. **क्या बाल या नाखून काटना हिंसा है?** नहीं। ये लौट आते हैं और पीड़ा नहीं देते या नगण्य देते हैं। आचार्य जी के अनुसार किसी अंग को तोड़ना या किसी को मारना हिंसा है।
3. **पेड़-पौधों का आहार हिंसा क्यों नहीं माना गया?** आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि पौधे का केवल फल-फूल वाला भाग लिया जाता है, पूरा वृक्ष नहीं टूटता और वह भाग फिर उग आता है; उसमें संवेदना बहुत कम होती है।
4. **मंदिरों में बाल अर्पण का क्या अर्थ है?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार इसका वास्तविक अर्थ अपनी कामना और वासना भगवान को सौंप देना है।

**English**
1. **What is the spiritual reason for vegetarianism?** Acharya Ji says a seeker of moksha sees their own self and God in every being, so no one would want to kill or eat a part of their own self.
2. **Is cutting hair or nails violence?** No. They grow back and cause little or no pain. According to Acharya Ji, breaking off a limb or killing a being is violence.
3. **Why is eating plants not counted as violence?** Acharya Ji explains that only the part bearing fruit and flowers is taken; the tree stays whole, the part grows back, and plants feel very little.
4. **What does offering hair at a temple mean?** According to Acharya Ji, its true meaning is surrendering your desires and cravings to God.

(Every answer is attributed to Acharya Ji; no scriptural claim is added. The Shakambhari verse is left out of the FAQ until the review issues in section 10 are settled.)

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Shakambhari verse: the meaning covers more than the line quoted** (Sanskrit review, issue 1, Major). The quoted half-verse 11.49 says only "then I shall become renowned on earth as Shakambhari"; the nourishing-with-greens part is 11.48. Recommendation: apply one of the review's two options before publishing, and do not quote the current meaning in snippets, FAQ or social cards until then. The English half-verse also lacks a closing danda (review issue 5, minor, style only).
2. **The Shakambhari promise is presented as a dietary rule** (review issue 2, Major). In the Durga Saptashati the Devi feeds all beings with greens during a hundred-year drought, until the rains return; the text lays down no rule for human diet. Recommendation: attribute the link to vegetarianism to Acharya Ji, as the review suggests. For SEO, never write "the scriptures command vegetarianism" or "Durga Saptashati says humans must be vegetarian" in titles, meta or FAQ; "Shakambhari Devi" stays a secondary keyword only.
3. **Hair offering meaning given as historical fact** (review issue 3, Minor). The "kamana and vasana" reading is Acharya Ji's teaching; tradition usually speaks of giving up ego or fulfilling a vow. Recommendation: attribute it ("आचार्य जी बताते हैं..." / "Acharya Ji explains..."); the FAQ in section 6 already does.
4. **"वासना" rendered as "cravings"** (review issue 4, Minor). Vasana means latent tendencies, not only cravings. Recommendation: editor's choice; "desires (kamana) and latent tendencies (vasana)" is more precise.
5. **"Eat to Heal" is not a health promise.** The title comes from the video, but the talk makes no claim about curing illness; it is about non-violence. Recommendation: keep "heal" only in the H1 and title tag as the video's phrase; never write meta, FAQ or social copy implying vegetarian food heals disease. Keywords such as "vegetarian diet for healing" are not targeted.
6. **Unverified claims about plants.** "Plants feel very little", "prana stays in them a long time" and "eating them brings no curse" (शाप नहीं लगता) are Acharya Ji's teaching, not cited scripture or science (the review could not verify the "no curse" line). Recommendation: attribute them in any snippet, and do not present them as scientific findings.
7. **Respect for those who eat meat.** The talk argues from the seeker's own experience of oneness, not by condemning others. Recommendation: keep promotion in that tone; avoid framings such as "meat eaters are sinners" or fear-based "curse" hooks in titles and thumbnails.
