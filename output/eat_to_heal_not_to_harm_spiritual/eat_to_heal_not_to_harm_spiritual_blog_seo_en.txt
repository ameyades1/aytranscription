# Eat to Heal, Not to Harm

Food is more than a way to fill the stomach. What we eat is tied to how we see the world. In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji begins with a simple question: would anyone want to cut off a part of their own body?

According to Acharya Ji, the spiritual reason for vegetarianism is the experience of oneness. A seeker of moksha sees God and their own self in every being, so they cannot kill or eat another. Giving up what grows back without pain is not violence, and plant food takes only the part of the plant that grows again.

## Would Anyone Cut Off Their Own Limb?

If the goal of your life is **moksha** (liberation), killing another living being becomes impossible.

The reason is straightforward. Once you begin to experience that everything is God, and that God is who you are, you see the same Supreme Being within and around every single thing. After that, no one would want to eat or kill a part of their own self.

*Have you ever tried to see yourself in those you think of as "others"?*

## Nails and Hair: What Grows Back

Think of yourself as one complete whole. When a nail grows long, you trim it yourself. It doesn't hurt, and even if it does a little, you don't worry, because a new one will grow in its place.

But if someone told you to cut off a finger, how many people would do it?

India has great temples where people offer their hair, a practice called **baal arpan** (the offering of hair). Its true meaning was to surrender your **desires** and **cravings** to God. Hair arrives at these temples from all over the world, wigs are made from it, and money flows in.

People can give away so much hair because hair is **wealth that grows back**. However much you cut, more is bound to come.

## What If God Asked for a Finger?

Imagine an announcement from tomorrow that God will be pleased only if you offer a small piece of your finger. How many people would still go to the temple?

Probably no one. People know their hair will return within a month, but a finger never will.

## What Counts as Violence?

Suppose a part of you causes no pain when removed, or almost none, and it grows back. Is offering it an act of **violence**? Does anyone ever say, "Look how much hair he is cutting, what violence!"?

Hair is there for beauty, and perhaps for some purpose such as protecting you from the sun. Yet bald people live perfectly well. Losing your hair makes no real difference.

Take sweat as another example. If you collected your sweat and gave it to someone, or threw it away, would it hurt you? No, because more will come. Acharya Ji explains that none of this counts as violence. But if someone breaks off one of your limbs, or kills you, that is violence.

## Why Choose a Vegetarian Diet?

Here the secret of **vegetarianism** reveals itself. From trees and plants, you take only the part that bears fruit and flowers. The tree itself stays whole, and the part you picked grows again.

Plants feel very little. Their **prana** (life energy) stays alive in them for a long time, and eating them brings no curse upon you.

In the scriptures, the Goddess declares that she will come as the power called **Shakambhari** (She who nourishes the world with greens), and that human beings will live on plant food.

> **śākambharīti vikhyātiṃ tadā yāsyāmyahaṃ bhuvi**
> "Nourishing the world with the plants born of my own body, I shall then become renowned on earth as Shakambhari."
> (Shri Durga Saptashati 11.49)

In this way, the scripture makes plant food the clear choice for human beings.

*Did everything on your plate today reach you without destroying a life completely?*

## Key Takeaways

- Those who seek liberation see themselves in everyone, so they cannot kill or eat another being.
- Nails, hair and sweat grow back, so letting them go is not violence.
- Breaking off a limb or killing a being is violence.
- Offering hair at temples truly means offering up your desires and cravings.
- A vegetarian diet takes only the part of a plant that grows back, a part that feels little and holds its prana for a long time.
- The Goddess, as Shakambhari, shows human beings the way of plant food.
