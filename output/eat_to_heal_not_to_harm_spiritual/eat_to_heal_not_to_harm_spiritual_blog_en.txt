# Eat to Heal, Not to Harm

Food is not only a way to fill the stomach. What we eat is tied to how we see the world. In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji begins with a simple question: would anyone want to cut off a part of their own body?

## Would Anyone Cut Off Their Own Limb?

If the goal of life is **moksha** (liberation), killing another being becomes impossible.

The reason is simple. When one begins to experience that everything is God, that God is me, the same Supreme Lord is seen in and through everything. Then no one would want to eat or kill a part of their own self.

> "When I see that all is God, God is me, I will not want to eat a part of my own self, nor want to kill it."

*Have you ever tried to see yourself in those you consider "others"?*

## Nails and Hair: What Comes Back

Consider yourself one complete whole. When a nail grows long, we cut it ourselves. There is no pain, and even if there is, no worry, because a new one grows.

But if someone said, cut off your finger, how many people would do it?

India has great temples where people **offer their hair**. The real meaning of offering hair was handing over one's **desires** and **cravings** to God. Hair from all over the world reaches there, wigs are made from it, and money comes in.

People can give so much hair because hair is **capital that grows back**. Cut as much as you like, new hair is bound to come.

## What If a Finger Were Asked For?

Imagine it were announced that from tomorrow God will be pleased only by the offering of a small piece of your finger. How many people would go to the temple?

Probably no one. People know hair will be back in a month, but a finger will not.

## The True Meaning of Violence

Is offering a part that causes no pain, or negligible pain, and that grows back, **violence**? Does anyone say, "Oh, how much hair he is cutting, what violence"?

Hair is for beauty, and perhaps serves a purpose like protection from the sun. But bald people live too. Cutting hair makes no real difference.

Take the example of sweat. If you collected sweat and gave it away or threw it out, would you grieve? No, because it will come again.

> "Such a thing is not called violence. But if a limb of mine is broken off, or I myself am killed, that is violence."

## Why Vegetarian Food?

Here the secret of **vegetarian food** opens up. Only that part of a plant is plucked in which fruits and flowers come. The whole tree is not broken, and that part grows back again.

It has very little sensation. **Prana** (life energy) remains in it for a long time. And no curse attaches to it.

In the scriptures, the Goddess says she will come as the power of **Shakambhari**, and human food shall be vegetables.

> **śākambharīti vikhyātiṁ tadā yāsyāmy ahaṁ bhuvi |**
> "Then I shall become renowned on earth as Shakambhari."
> (Shri Durga Saptashati 11.49)

That is, the Goddess says that by nourishing the world with vegetables born of her own body, she will become renowned on earth as Shakambhari. In this way, a diet of plants became clear for human beings.

*Has what is on your plate today come to you without wholly destroying another?*

## Key Takeaways

- A seeker of liberation sees themselves in all, so they cannot kill or eat another.
- Nails, hair and sweat grow back, so letting them go is not violence.
- Breaking off a limb or killing someone is violence.
- The real meaning of offering hair at temples is offering one's desires and cravings.
- Vegetarian food takes only the part of a plant that grows back; it has little sensation and holds life energy for a long time.
- The Goddess's Shakambhari form shows humanity the way of a plant-based diet.
