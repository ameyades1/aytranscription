# Satyuga's Rules, Kaliyuga's Path: Bhairava Shakti and India as Vishwaguru

Are we still trying to win **Kaliyuga** (the present dark age) with the rules of **Satyuga** (the first age, the age of truth)? In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji raises a direct and uncomfortable question: will a path that succeeded in another age succeed just as well today?

In short, the spiritual path in Kaliyuga depends on the goal. Personal liberation is possible on any age's path, but to expand in the world in Kaliyuga, the Agamas and tantra work best: Satyuga rested on the Shruti, Treta on the Smriti, Dvapara on the Puranas and Kaliyuga on the Agamas. To choose among the four paths, ask the Guru.

His answer runs through the planet Venus, the Agamas and tantra, four paths of sadhana and the power of Bhairava. In the end it becomes a question for all of us: how will India become a **Vishwaguru** (teacher of the world) once again?

## Kalaya Tasmai Namah: Honouring Time

The scriptures say **"kālāya tasmai namaḥ"** (salutations to that Time). In other words, you have to live according to the age you are in.

Acharya Ji's example is simple. Go to the office today in the formal dress that was respected in the time of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. Or ride there on a horse, or in a chariot. Or send every message as a handwritten letter in a cloth pouch. Nobody will stop you, but could you truly succeed in today's world?

Those clothes were not wrong. Those means were not wrong. Their time has passed, that is all.

Kaliyuga has its own ways in exactly the same manner. In the time of King Parikshit, Kali, the spirit of this age, found his way in the moment his appointed hour arrived. In every age, Time is the one that holds the power.

*Do your sadhana and your way of working fit the times you live in?*

## Personal Liberation or Reaching the World?

There is a subtle distinction to understand here.

Your **personal liberation** (moksha) does not depend on any age. Whether you choose the path of Satyuga or of Treta, the second age, makes no difference to it.

If you want to **expand** in the world, though, and carry your message across the globe, the rule of Kali applies. Acharya Ji points out that even great souls such as Adi Shankaracharya said that in Kaliyuga, Kali's own instruments and methods are what will work.

## Four Ages, Four Sources of Knowledge

The tantric scriptures name a different foundation for each of the four ages:

> **kṛte śrutyukta ācāras tretāyāṁ smṛtisambhavaḥ**
> **dvāpare tu purāṇoktaḥ kalāv āgamasammataḥ**
> "In Satyuga, conduct followed the Shruti; in Treta, the Smriti; in Dvapara, the Puranas; and in Kaliyuga, the Agamas."
> (Kularnava Tantra)

- **Satyuga:** the **Shruti** (that which is heard), meaning the Vedas and Vedanta, knowledge received through the ears from the Guru.
- **Tretayuga:** the **Smriti** (that which is remembered), such as the Manusmriti, which lays down the rules of society, family and dharma (righteous duty).
- **Dvaparayuga:** the Puranas, where the flavour of devotion dominates.
- **Kaliyuga:** the **Agamas**, meaning tantra.

None of this makes worship wrong. Acharya Ji says it will serve you well for personal liberation. For large goals and quick results in this age, however, the knowledge of the Agamas becomes especially necessary.

You can travel from Kanyakumari to Delhi in a bullock cart, too. If you want to get there quickly, though, you have to pick the right vehicle.

## What Agama and Tantra Really Mean

In **Agama**, "ā" points to all-pervasiveness and "gama" to movement. It is the knowledge that brings **progress through speed**. Life is short in Kaliyuga, so speed matters.

**Tantra** is the knowledge that expands and protects every living being. At a deeper level, the syllable "tra" means **trana** (protection).

Seen this way, Acharya Ji calls even the internet a tantra: from where you sit, a message reaches the whole world in an instant. Those who learn to use it will flourish more in Kaliyuga.

## Artificial Intelligence: Servant or Teacher?

Artificial intelligence is also a **siddhi** (a special power). Knowledge, however, belongs to **sattva** (the quality of purity and clarity), so this tool should stay in sattvic hands.

Acharya Ji's own approach is worth noticing. Whenever he uses it, he asks: why did you write this? By what rule? What is your source? He learns from the answers and sharpens his own understanding.

The aim is to learn, not merely to take a shortcut.

*When you have AI write something for you, do you learn anything from it?*

## Venus: The Planet of Kaliyuga

The Shukra Stotra, the hymn to Venus, calls **Shukracharya** (the sage who rules the planet Venus) **"daityānāṁ paramaṁ gurum"** (the supreme Guru of the demons). Why, then, should anyone worship him?

Because in Kaliyuga, demons are in the majority.

All the qualities of the Agamas and tantra fall under Venus. Shukracharya held the **Mritasanjivani Vidya** (the knowledge that revives the dead), and he was "sarvaśāstrapravaktāram" (the master of every scripture). Today, the people who move ahead are those who understand data analysis, Shri Vidya and statecraft in the style of **Kautilya** (Chanakya, the ancient master of strategy).

## Two Gurus, One Question: Who Will Draw More Followers?

Acharya Ji offers a thought experiment.

One Guru teaches ideal celibacy and holds up Hanuman as the model. The teaching is sound and so are its benefits. The second teaches the science of the very same root centre, desire, and shows how even through it you can reach liberation by way of right effort.

One says, "All this is illusion and attachment. Let it go." The other says, "This is Shri Vidya. It will bring you wealth, and your mind will stay clear."

Whose followers will be more numerous in Kaliyuga?

Acharya Ji is clear that both paths are right. The only question is **which one will spread in this age**.

When the first ant reaches a lump of jaggery, lines of ants arrive from every direction within minutes. In the same way, once people realise that a path brings them benefit, crowds flock to it.

## The Gita's Instruction: The Wise Must Not Turn People Away from Action

> **na buddhibhedaṁ janayed ajñānāṁ karmasaṅginām**
> **joṣayet sarvakarmāṇi vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran**
> "The wise should not unsettle the minds of those attached to action by making them lose faith in it. Instead, performing all the actions the scriptures prescribe with full attention, they should inspire others to do the same."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.26)

Acharya Ji reads this as a direct instruction to the Guru. Marriage, business, earning money, worship: none of it is wrong. The Guru's task is to show how to do these things well.

Notice, though, that the verse speaks of actions **prescribed by the scriptures**. Teaching someone to run a liquor bar does not count.

If you teach suppression in Kaliyuga, problems will follow. Teaching control is one thing, but if you want to grow in this age, you have to teach people how to purify their desires.

## Turn the Other Cheek: The Irony of East and West

Acharya Ji draws attention to Swami Vivekananda's book *The East and the West*.

Jesus Christ taught the West that if someone strikes you on one cheek, you should offer the other, yet the West kept fighting wars to conquer the world. In India, every avatar and every deity held a weapon, and every Guru said, "Rise and fight," yet people here sat back in surrender.

## Chanakya's Lesson: Pull the Problem Out by the Roots

Once a thorn pierced Chanakya's foot. He found the plant it had come from and poured buttermilk all around it. When his disciples asked why, he explained that ants and insects would now come and eat the whole plant. With the plant gone, the thorn would be gone, and the problem would never return.

This is the skill Kaliyuga demands: **treating the root cause**.

## The Dakshina Path: Beautiful, but Is the Age That Simple?

The **Dakshina Marga** does not mean the southern direction. It means the right-hand path, the one the world considers normal and correct: "Human beings are born to love. Who has ever gained anything from hatred?"

This path is straightforward. But is the age straightforward?

In the fourteenth chapter of the Gita, Shri Krishna describes the three **gunas** (the qualities of nature: sattva, rajas and tamas). In the **Durga Saptashati** (seven hundred verses in praise of Maa Durga), demons such as Madhu and Kaitabha, Mahishasura, Shumbha and Nishumbha and Raktabija keep appearing. Even in Shri Rama's age, demons would not let the sages live in peace.

To carry out the work of non-violence, Acharya Ji says, you need people around you who are capable of striking back. Violence that establishes non-violence is not called violence. The army, the police and security guards all come under **Mahakali** (the fierce form of the Divine Mother). Even the white blood cells in our body exist to protect it.

## What Are the Four Paths: Dakshina, Vama, Siddhanta and Kaula?

Acharya Ji describes four levels of sadhana:

1. **Dakshina Marga:** the person who rings the temple bell and prays, trusts in destiny and is afraid to kill even a cockroach. There is nothing wrong with this. It is a path too.
2. **Vama Marga** (the left-hand path): the person who knows how to get things done, which official to meet and which form to file early. The scriptures say this path brings quick results.
3. **Siddhanta:** the elder brother of the Dakshina path, the Vedantin. Endowed with spiritual powers and stronger even than the Vama path, yet calm and set on the path of withdrawal from the world.
4. **Kaula:** the father even of the Vama path. Those who rise all the way to the top and then return to the lower chakras. This is called the state of an avatar, the path that Shri Krishna and Shri Rama could walk.

## A Slow Train, or a Fast One With No Brakes?

The Dakshina path is a train so slow that even after forty years you cannot be sure it has left the station. The Vama path is a train so fast that it does arrive, but with no brakes, crashing into the platform.

If the goal is to reach the destination, Acharya Ji says the Vama path is better, because at least it gets you there. Those who receive a jolt early also have an early chance to reform, the way Valya the robber became Maharshi Valmiki, the author of the Ramayana.

Is it safe, though? No. That is why two more paths lie beyond it.

*Which train are you on right now?*

## The Yoga of the Royal Sages

> **evaṁ paramparāprāptam imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ**
> **sa kāleneha mahatā yogo naṣṭaḥ parantapa**
> "Handed down in succession, this yoga was known to the royal sages. O scorcher of foes, over a long stretch of time it was lost to the world."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.2)

The Gita says this yoga was known to the **rajarshis** (royal sages). A king carries **rajas** (the quality of activity and drive), and he has to oversee defence, taxes, medicine, foreign policy and science. Rajas means action, action brings problems, and those problems have to be solved.

That is why Shri Krishna says again and again, "Arise and act." Yoga is not only sitting in meditation.

## Should We Really Reject Siddhis?

Many Gurus say that siddhis are like poison. In the Vibhuti Pada of the Yoga Sutras, Maharshi Patanjali also warns that they can lead to a fall.

With full respect, Acharya Ji asks: what, then, was Sant Dnyaneshwar making a wall move? Sai Baba lighting lamps with water? Hanuman's powers of **anima**, **laghima** and **garima** (becoming minute, weightless and immensely heavy)? Were all of these wrong? He points out that the highest, ninth form of the **Nava Durga** (the nine forms of Maa Durga) is called **Siddhidatri** (the giver of siddhis). How, then, can siddhis be wrong?

The real question is not the siddhi but **how you use it**. An ordinary person cannot handle it, so they are told to give it up. The Vama path holds fire and greater power. If you can handle it, that is your path. If not, choose the calm one.

## Bhairava: The Power of Fearlessness

**Bhairava** is the form of Lord Shiva whose radiance alone frightens an opponent before any fight begins. In Kaliyuga, those who carry Bhairava's power will be fearless, and others will trouble them less.

The Bhairava principle in your life means a friend, helper, disciple or well-wisher who stands by you and you alone.

- A Guru blessed by Bhairava gains devoted disciples.
- A king blessed by him gains soldiers ready to give their lives.
- Countless warriors gave their lives for Shivaji Maharaj and Maharana Pratap.
- Revolutionaries went to the gallows with a smile.

The Mughals used to say, "If you must have an enemy, let him be like Shivaji." They feared him, because Bhairava lived in him.

This power is not meant for fighting. It is meant to make a daughter-in-law so radiant that no one can mistreat her, to make sure no official can harass you, and to ensure no foreign power dares to cast its eye on the country.

## What Does Panchamakara Mean? The Five Means of Tantra

The Bhairava Tantra names five means of tantra, the **Panchamakara** (the five "M"s): **madya** (wine), **mamsa** (meat), **matsya** (fish), **mudra** (gesture) and **maithuna** (union).

On the surface they sound like pure Vama practice. Acharya Ji unpacks them on three levels.

**The tamasic level:** the direct, gross meaning, belonging to **tamas** (the quality of darkness and inertia). Alcohol shuts down the intellect and fixes whatever thought is already sitting in the mind. Acharya Ji mentions the Mumbai terror attacks, where the attackers first went to a hotel and drank. This is tamasic Bhairava, and it is dangerous.

**The rajasic level:** the same intoxication in a slightly altered form: fizzy drinks, burgers, pizza and mixed, heavy food.

**The sattvic level:** this is where the real secret lies.

- **Madya:** **somarasa** (the nectar of soma), meaning the nectar of devotion. Devotion to the nation and to the Guru so intoxicating that when the Guru says "Rise," you rise.
- **Mamsa:** the Manusmriti reads it as "māṁ sa bhakṣayitā" (he will eat me): the one I eat today will eat me tomorrow. Its sattvic meaning is restraint of speech, and offering yourself to the nation and the Guru, which is **atmanivedana** (complete self-offering).
- **Matsya:** the prana flowing through the **ida**, **pingala** and **sushumna** (the left, right and central energy channels), which lifts you above honour and insult, victory and defeat, gain and loss.
- **Maithuna:** the union of **Purusha** and **Prakriti** (consciousness and nature), the state of **Ardhanarishvara** (Lord Shiva as half male, half female). Wealth is Prakriti, and so is power. Live with them respectfully, without getting stuck in them.
- **Mudra:** actions that happen on their own, **akarma** (action without the sense of doing), divine play.

The day sattvic Bhairava power arises, Acharya Ji says, our becoming Vishwaguru will be assured.

*How much of devotion's "wine" flows in your life?*

## Sanatana Dharma: Do Not Frighten, Do Not Fear

The Ardhanarishvara state means being kshatriya enough to hold a gun to someone's head, and brahmin enough to let them go after a scare. If no blood needs to be shed, none should be.

A serpent need not bite, but it must hiss. In Acharya Ji's words, this is what **Sanatana Dharma** (the eternal way) teaches:

- Do not strike anyone, but do not take a beating from anyone either.
- Do not frighten anyone, but do not fear anyone either.
- We do not provoke anyone, but we do not let anyone who provokes us off lightly.

## Who Will Spread in Kaliyuga?

In Acharya Ji's analysis, other religions appear to be spreading today because they have the Vama path, even if it is tamasic or rajasic. Citing the Bhavishya Purana, he says they worship the **Kali tattva** (the principle of the goddess Kali).

And us? We kept fighting among ourselves. Worshipping wealth and Lakshmi alone will not do. In Kaliyuga, those who worship Kali and Bhairava are the ones who will grow great.

Imitating them is not the answer, though. If we also take on tamasic Bhairava, what difference would remain? The answer is **sattvic Bhairava**: fighting with scripture, not weapons, and showing results through the power of a fulfilled resolve.

## Kaula: What This Age Needs

The root of **Kaula** is **kula**: "ku" means the earth element, the root chakra, and "la" means dissolution. The point up to which the Kundalini rises and dissolves gives us the term "kula-kundalini."

The **kuladevata** (family deity) also comes from this. Acharya Ji explains that every lineage has its own goddess. Renuka Mata, for example, belongs to the heart chakra, and families devoted to her will produce more healers. **Varna** (one's social calling) is individual and can change, but kula is the blood that comes down from the ancestors.

The Siddhanta practitioner is a great Guru, but that kind of reach does not last long in Kaliyuga. Gautama Buddha taught renunciation, yet the generations that followed were not ready for it. The Vama path will reign for a long time, and it takes a Kaula to stop it: shrewd, strategic like Kautilya, and of the stature of an avatar.

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> **dharmasaṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge**
> "To protect the good, to destroy the wicked and to establish dharma, I take birth age after age."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

Establishing dharma also means cutting down wrongdoing. Among **sama, dana, danda** and **bheda** (persuasion, gifts, punishment and division, the four means of statecraft), punishment has its place too.

## Which Path Is Right for Me?

Do not decide this yourself. **Ask the Guru.**

- For those afraid to kill even a mosquito, who are unwell or simple at heart: the Dakshina path.
- For those who are somewhat practical and not fearful: the Guru may give the Vama path for a particular task.
- For those who are calm and fit to go to the Himalayas: Siddhanta.
- For those in whom Bhairava's power and that "intoxication" have already arrived: Kaula.

If you seek liberation and the Himalayas, go to a Siddhanta teacher, who will speak of dispassion and liberation. If you seek justice and wealth, go to a Kaula, who will offer both enjoyment and liberation.

## Women Must Rise First

Acharya Ji speaks to women in particular: they must learn **Bhairavi Shakti** (the feminine power of Bhairava). After two thousand years of subjugation, the freedom women have gained has mostly gone in rajasic and tamasic directions. Today's need, he says, is for women to rise first.

The coming age needs both kinds of women, like the Rani of Jhansi and like Mirabai, and both kinds of men, like Vivekananda and like Shivaji Maharaj.

## When the Guru Is Attacked, Do Not Break

Kaliyuga will bring a great deal of destruction. People will level only two charges against your Guru: about money, or about desire. Buddha, Vivekananda, Eknath, Tukaram: none of them escaped. Yet the disciples who carried Bhairava's power did not waver.

Acharya Ji asks: if someone speaks ill of your mother, do you first go and question her? Those who understand their mother understand **Guru Mauli** (the Guru as a loving mother), and they understand Bharat Mata, Mother India.

## When Will the Archer Rise?

> **yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanurdharaḥ**
> **tatra śrīr vijayo bhūtir dhruvā nītir matir mama**
> "Wherever there is Krishna, the Lord of Yoga, and wherever there is Arjuna, the archer, there will be prosperity, victory, abundance and steady righteousness. This is my conviction."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.78)

Krishna, the Lord of Yoga, is not enough by himself. You also need Arjuna, the archer ready to fight. The Guru comes to show the way, but the one who is suffering has to rise.

Superhuman power is not enough, Acharya Ji says. We need supreme divine power. And the whole Panchamakara lies within, in the inner world. That is why the name is **Antar Yog**, the yoga within.

His message is direct: **do it properly, or do not do it at all.** No half-hearted sadhana. Ask yourself where you went wrong, where your lineage and your country went wrong, and finally where the whole human family went wrong.

Acharya Ji believes that if we wake up at the right time, especially between 2029 and 2034, India will once again be Vishwaguru.

*When will the Arjuna within you awaken?*

## Key Takeaways

- **Kalaya tasmai namah:** every age has its own path. Personal liberation is possible on any path, but to expand in the world in Kaliyuga, the Agamas and tantra work best.
- Satyuga rests on the Shruti, Treta on the Smriti, Dvapara on the Puranas and Kaliyuga on the Agamas.
- Venus is the planet of Kaliyuga, and in this age it is not optional.
- Dakshina, Vama, Siddhanta and Kaula are the four paths. None is wrong, but in Kaliyuga only Kaula can stop Vama. Ask the Guru before choosing yours.
- Siddhis are not to be rejected; what matters is how you use them. The highest form of Nava Durga is Siddhidatri.
- Bhairava's power is the power of fearlessness and devoted allies. Without the Bhairava principle, discipleship loses its strength.
- The sattvic meaning of Panchamakara: the nectar of devotion, self-offering, balanced prana, the Ardhanarishvara state and effortless divine play.
- Sanatana Dharma: do not frighten anyone, but do not fear anyone either.
- Women must learn both Bhairavi Shakti and Vedanta.
- When the Guru is attacked, do not break. Do it properly, or do not do it at all.
