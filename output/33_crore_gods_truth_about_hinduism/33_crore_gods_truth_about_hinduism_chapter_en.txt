# Chapter: Thirty-Three Crore Gods and the One Supreme

## Only One God

In Hinduism too there is only one God. It is that one, same, single Lord. Then the question arises: what are these thirty-three crore gods? Who are all these different deities? People say Hindus have so many gods.

This is where the first problem begins. Someone says, why should we worship Ganesha? We are Shaivas, we will worship Shiva. Someone says, we are Shaktas, we will worship the Goddess. Someone says, we are Vaishnavas, we will worship Vishnu. Someone says, we belong to the solar dynasty, someone else to the lunar dynasty. Tell me, does this problem exist in India or not?

## The Question of Foreign Students

I teach outside the country too, so students from abroad also come. Sometimes they ask, "We have only one God. How do you have so many? And thirty-three crore at that!" Thirty-three crore is only the base; the more variations you add, the more it becomes, fifty crore and who knows how many.

So is there a problem in India because there are so many gods? No. The problem is that people do not understand the main God at all. And the foremost deity of them all is Ganesha himself; if you have not understood him, you have understood nothing. Is everyone now seeing where the problem lies?

> "Do you really, really know your God?"

## The Supreme Principle: One Infinite

I told those foreign students, "No, my friend, we too have only one God." I am telling you as well: in Hinduism too there is only one God. He is called the **Param Tattva**, the Supreme Principle; he is called the formless Omkar. We call him Omkar; they may call him Jesus Christ or Christ consciousness; someone calls him Buddha consciousness; someone calls him Allah.

When we say in Advaita Vedanta that there is no second here at all, it means exactly this: there is only one. Keep this firmly in mind first: **the Supreme is one.**

## The Deities: Departments of the One Supreme

Then what are these thirty-three crore? What are Ganapati, Parvati, all these? They are **departments**. The Supreme is one, and these are its different departments.

Understand it this way. You are a homemaker. Who are you in the kitchen? A cook; you know how to cook. But do you cook all day the way it is done in a hotel? You are also a mother, and the role of mother is also within you; it does not come from outside. You are someone's sister, you go and visit on Raksha Bandhan; you are someone's wife. Somewhere you will be someone's student, somewhere someone's guru. Does one person play a hundred roles or not?

Within the same person there can be different capacities. Sometimes we can play cricket, sometimes football, sometimes act or do mimicry. Are there not thousands of talents hidden in every human being? Sometimes I can speak in Hindi, sometimes Gujarati, Tamil, Telugu, Sindhi, Urdu, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish or Latin. If I learn, can I speak them or not? That is, the person is one, but his **expressions** can be many.

> "The person is one, but his expressions can be many."

## Lakshmi, Ganapati and Hanuman

In the same way, our God is one, called the Param Tattva. Listen carefully today. What people quarrel over has never been properly classified. These are the different powers of that One.

When a person earns money, that power of earning money has been given a name: **Lakshmi**. When the same person works properly and uses his intellect, that part is called **Ganapati**. Understand this basic point first. Complete devotion is expressed in the form of **Hanuman**. He is also the symbol of strength, which is why a gymnasium has a picture or an idol of Hanuman. Is this absolutely clear? Our God too is one.

## Rupa and Svarupa: Form and Essential Nature

Do you know the difference between **rupa** (outer form) and **svarupa** (essential nature)? Take the example of sugar. What is the form of sugar? Square or cube-like, small grains; if ordinary, white in colour. What is called khadi sakhar (rock sugar) is a little bigger in shape. If powdered, it is powder, but still white. To the touch you would say it is smooth; with a little moisture you would say it sticks to the hand. This outer identity is called **rupa**.

But what is its svarupa? Is it spicy? Suppose someone says, "Wow, today I ate sugar, it was so spicy, my eyes watered!" Would you believe it? You would tell him, "You did not eat sugar, you ate something else. You must have eaten a chilli." The svarupa must match the rupa. The svarupa of sugar must be sweet. Barfi's svarupa is also sweet, but its sweetness is different. The svarupa of each is sweet, yet there is a difference in the sweetness.

I hope this point is clear. **Sva-rupa** and mere **rupa**. Mere rupa means the outer shape, and the inner quality is called **svarupa**.

> "Mere rupa means the outer, and the quality within it is called svarupa."

## Many Shapes, One Omkar

In the same way, the body of every god has been given a different shape. One has a trunk, one has many arms, fifteen arms, twenty arms. No such human being exists in the world outside. So do not get stuck on the shape; know its svarupa. Omkar will be one. Take any god, ours or that of any other religion: it is that one same single Lord.

At the time of death we say, does it not, that he merged into the five elements. Can there be two infinites? The infinite can only be one.

## The Need for Form

Abroad, in other religions, they do not accept idol worship. But without a form, no one's work can be done. One who is himself finite cannot directly worship the infinite. Is this clear?
