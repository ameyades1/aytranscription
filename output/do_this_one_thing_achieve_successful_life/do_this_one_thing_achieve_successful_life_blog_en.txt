# Do Not Act to Become Happy, Act Happily

We are all busy doing something: a job, a business, marriage, running a home. But have you ever asked **why** you do all this?

Acharya Upendra Ji says one small thing that can change the direction of a whole life: there are two ways of doing work, and only one of them leads to true joy.

## Am I Working Only to Get Something in Return?

The first way is **acting in order to become happy**. I want a salary, I want a promotion, so I am working. I will get something in return, so I am doing it.

The same attitude enters relationships too. Marriage, because it will bring some joy, the house will run, a second salary will be added. Two doctors marry so that their hospital will run.

Here **expectation** has come in. And where there is expectation, there is no **unconditional love**. In the other person I saw my own benefit, that is, **selfishness**.

Acharya Ji says clearly: when work is done in order to become happy, the happiness does not last. After some time, only sorrow comes.

*Is the only reason for the work you do today what you get in return for it?*

## Acting Happily: When the Work Itself Is Joy

The second way is **acting happily**. You want to do business, but not to earn money. I know how to make something or offer some service, and that is my passion.

I enjoy it even without money. Now I am simply sharing it with others.

Acharya Ji gives the example of a painter. Painting is something they enjoy anyway. When they give those paintings to others, money comes too. But they are not painting to become happy; they are painting happily. Even without any reward, they already enjoyed it.

> "Act happily, not in order to become happy."

## The Door to Karma Yoga: Work Should Feel Like Play

Acharya Ji explains that the Bhagavad Gita points to the same thing: the one who does the work of their passion is the one who moves ahead. Do what you love, and love what you do.

Only then can you move towards **moksha** (liberation), only then do you qualify for **karma yoga**. Karma yoga means making action itself your spiritual practice.

The test is simple: work should feel not like work but like **play**. You should feel happiness in it.

*Is there any work in your day that feels like play to you?*

## Unconditional Love: For Others and for Yourself

This joy is not possible without unconditional love. Love for the one you are working for, and **for yourself as well**. While doing it, you yourself should feel good.

Acharya Ji gives the example of a woman who likes cooking. She will cook well in her in-laws' home, and here too. Because she is happy from the very beginning.

Her attitude is not "If I do this I will get this, if I do that I will get that." That work is her nature.

## Why Acharya Ji Himself Teaches

At the end, Acharya Ji gives his own example.

> "I love to teach, but I also love to learn."

So he keeps learning new things and keeps giving us what he has learned. This work is the most fitting for him, because it is done happily.

## Key Takeaways

- There are two ways to act: acting to become happy, and acting happily.
- Work done to get something in return brings expectation and selfishness, and in the end sorrow.
- Choose the work you would enjoy even without money or reward.
- Do what you love, and love what you do; this is what qualifies you for karma yoga.
- Work should feel like play.
- Unconditional love should be for others and for yourself too.
