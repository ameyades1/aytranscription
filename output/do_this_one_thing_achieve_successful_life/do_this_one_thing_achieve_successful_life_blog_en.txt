# Act from Happiness, Not in Search of It

All of us are busy doing something: a job, a business, a marriage, running a home. But have you ever stopped to ask **why** you do any of it?

Acharya Upendra Ji makes a small point that can change the direction of an entire life. There are two ways to act, and only one of them leads to true bliss.

## Am I Working Only to Get Something in Return?

The first way is **acting in order to become happy**. I work because I want a salary and a promotion. I do it because I will get something back.

The same attitude creeps into relationships. People marry for a little enjoyment, a well-run home, a second income. Two doctors marry so that their clinic will do well.

Here **expectation** has entered. Where there is expectation, there is no **unconditional love**. I looked at the other person and saw my own gain, which is **self-interest**.

Acharya Ji says it plainly: when you do something in order to become happy, the happiness does not last. Sooner or later, suffering follows.

*Is what you get in return the only reason you do the work you do today?*

## Acting from Happiness: When the Work Itself Is Joy

The second way is **acting from happiness**. You run a business, but not to make money. You know how to make something or offer a service, and that is your passion.

You would enjoy it even without being paid. Now you are just sharing it with others.

Acharya Ji gives the example of a painter. Painting already delights the painter. When those paintings go out to others, money comes too. Yet the painter is not painting to become happy, but painting out of happiness, and the joy was there long before any reward. We, too, must act from happiness, Acharya Ji says, not in order to become happy.

## The Gateway to Karma Yoga: Work That Feels Like Play

Acharya Ji points out that the Bhagavad Gita hints at the same thing: those who follow their passion are the ones who grow. Do what you love, and love what you do.

Only then can you move towards **moksha** (liberation), and only then do you become ready for **Karma Yoga** (the path of selfless action). Karma Yoga means turning your work itself into **sadhana** (spiritual practice).

The test is simple. Your work should not feel like work at all, but like **play**. It should leave you feeling glad.

*Is there anything in your day that feels like play?*

## Unconditional Love: For Others and for Yourself

This joy is impossible without unconditional love. You need love for the people you work for, and **for yourself as well**. You should enjoy the work while you are doing it.

Acharya Ji describes a woman who loves to cook. She will cook well in her in-laws' home, and she will cook well here, because she is happy from the very start.

She never thinks, "If I do this, I'll get that; if I do that, I'll get something else." Cooking is simply part of who she is.

## Why Acharya Ji Teaches

Finally, Acharya Ji offers his own example. He loves to teach, and he loves to learn just as much.

So he keeps learning new things and keeps passing on what he has learned. This is the work that suits him best, because he does it out of happiness.

## Key Takeaways

- There are two ways to act: in order to become happy, and from happiness.
- Work done for a return brings expectation and self-interest, and in the end suffering.
- Choose work you would enjoy even without money or reward.
- Do what you love and love what you do; this makes you ready for Karma Yoga.
- Your work should feel like play.
- Let your unconditional love include others and yourself.
