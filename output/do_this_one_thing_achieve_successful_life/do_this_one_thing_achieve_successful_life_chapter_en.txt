# Chapter: Do Not Act to Become Happy, Act Happily

## Two Ways of Doing Work

There are two ways to do any work. One is to do it in order to become happy, and the other is to do it happily. They sound alike, but there is a great difference between them.

## Acting to Become Happy: The Path of Expectation

What does it mean to act in order to become happy? I want a salary, I want a promotion, so I am doing this work. I will get this in return, so I am doing it.

Even marriage is done in this spirit: I will get some joy out of it. My household will run, and my spouse's salary will be added too. Two doctors marry each other so that their hospital will run.

Now expectation has come in. And what happens then? There is no unconditional love in it. What did I see in the other person? My own benefit. Selfishness has entered. When I do something in order to become happy, I cannot be happy in it. After a certain time, only sorrow will come.

## Acting Happily: The Path of Joy

What is the other way? To act happily. Suppose I want to do business; it is not for earning money. I know how to make something or how to offer some service, and that itself is my passion. I should enjoy it anyway, even without money. Now I am simply sharing it with others.

Will it be like a hobby? Yes. Suppose someone is a painter. I am good at painting anyway. So if I give my paintings to others as well, I will also get money for it. That is, I am not doing it to become happy; I am doing it already happy. I enjoyed it from before, without anyone giving a reward. So take up that very profession.

> "Act happily, not in order to become happy."

## Qualifying for Karma Yoga

The Bhagavad Gita also mentions that the one who does the work of their passion is the one who moves ahead. Do what you love, and love what you do. Then you can move towards liberation (moksha); then you qualify for karma yoga.

If you do not like that work, it does not come together. Work should feel like play. Work should feel not like work but like play. You should feel happiness in it; there should be joy in it.

## Unconditional Love: In Both Directions

This joy is not possible at all without unconditional love. This love should be for the one for whom you are doing the work, and for yourself as well. While you are doing that work, you yourself should feel good.

Take the example of a woman who likes cooking. She likes it anyway. So she will cook well in her in-laws' home and cook well here too. She should be happy from the very beginning. Act happily, not in order to become happy. Not in the spirit of "If I do this I will get this, if I do that I will get that." That work is simply her nature, so she does it.

## Acharya Ji's Own Example

I love to teach, but I equally love to learn. So I keep learning new things, and whatever I have learned I keep giving to you. That is why it is right that I do this work.

> "I love to teach, but I also love to learn."
