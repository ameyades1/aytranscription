## 1. Summary

A short discourse (the video is 4:17; about 650 words per language) in which Acharya Upendra Ji says that marriage is not a business or a deal for happiness: it is a relationship for giving, not taking. Asking for love is not our duty; each partner gives what they are able to give (cooking, earning, counsel, protection, shared travel), and these acts are kartavya karma done for chitta-shuddhi (purification of the mind), not for a return. What we receive comes from "thought alone". He adds that a woman lives at the level of the mind, so a husband needs enough understanding to treat her gently, and that a quarrelsome partner is prarabdha (the fruit of past actions) while how we treat them is our new karma. The main intent is informational with a strong emotional layer: married readers who feel unhappy or unloved, searching "शादी के बाद दुखी क्यों", "विवाह का उद्देश्य", "expectations in marriage", "how to deal with a difficult spouse". Generic "happy married life tips" results are crowded with listicles and relationship coaching; the realistic opportunity is the spiritual angle (marriage as karma yoga and self-purification, a difficult partner as prarabdha), which few pages answer from the tradition. The post belongs in the Love & Relationships cluster, next to the talks on expectation in love, pativrata and toxic relationships.

## 6. FAQ

For readers and the editor; not added to the page and not marked up as FAQPage.

1. **विवाह का उद्देश्य क्या है? / What is the purpose of marriage?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार विवाह लेने के लिए नहीं, देने के लिए है। वह व्यापार नहीं, जहाँ कुछ देकर बदले में कुछ लिया जाए। / According to Acharya Ji, marriage is for giving, not taking. It is not a business where we give something to collect something in return.
2. **क्या जीवनसाथी से प्रेम माँगना गलत है? / Is it wrong to expect love from your spouse?**
   आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि प्रेम माँगना हमारा कर्तव्य नहीं है; हमारा कर्तव्य है अपनी क्षमता और प्रतिभा से देना। / Acharya Ji says asking for love is not our duty; our duty is to give through our own abilities and talents.
3. **विवाह में देने का अर्थ क्या है? / What does giving mean in marriage?**
   कोई बड़ा त्याग नहीं: जो आपको आता है, वही दीजिए, जैसे भोजन बनाना, कमाना, सलाह देना, सुरक्षा देना या साथ घूमने ले जाना। ये कर्म अपने शुद्ध होने के लिए हैं, लाभ के लिए नहीं। / Not a great sacrifice: give what you know how to do, such as cooking, earning, counsel, protection or taking your partner along when you travel. These acts are for your own purification, not for gain.
4. **कठिन या झगड़ालू जीवनसाथी क्यों मिलता है? / Why do some people get a difficult spouse?**
   आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि यह पूर्व प्रारब्ध (पिछले कर्मों का फल) है और वह आपके कर्म काटने आया है। उसके साथ आप कैसा व्यवहार करते हैं, यह आपका नया कर्म है। / Acharya Ji says this is prarabdha (the fruit of past actions), and the partner has come to help you work off karma. How you treat them is your new action.
5. **क्या इसका अर्थ है कि हर हाल में सहते रहना चाहिए? / Does this mean one must endure everything?**
   ब्लॉग कहता है कि तलाक देना है या प्रेम से रहना है, यह आपका नया निर्णय है। दुर्व्यवहार सहने के विषय पर आचार्य जी की अलग वार्ता "सहना कोई गुण नहीं" देखें। / The blog says that whether you divorce or live together with love is your new decision. For enduring abuse, see Acharya Ji's separate talk "Putting Up with Abuse Is Not a Virtue".

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Chanakya attribution** (Sanskrit review, issue 1, Major). No Chanakya Niti verse speaks of twenty-seven births or advises "do not get into it". Recommendation: apply the review's wording ("a saying popularly attributed to Chanakya") before publishing. None of the titles, meta, snippets, FAQ or schema mention Chanakya, and no link anchor uses that passage.
2. **"Destiny" for prarabdha** (Sanskrit review, issue 2). Prarabdha is the portion of past karma bearing fruit now, not fatalistic destiny. The SEO texts use "prarabdha" and "the fruit of past actions", never "destiny". If the editor renames the English H2 "A Difficult Partner: Destiny and New Action" to "...Prarabdha and New Action" as the review suggests, update `also_read.after.en` in seo.json to match, or the Also read box will not be placed.
3. **"Tradition describes the many roles a wife plays"** (Sanskrit review, issue 3). This draws on the unattributed subhashita "कार्येषु दासी करणेषु मन्त्री..."; do not name a scripture for it in any SEO text or social snippet. None does.
4. **A difficult partner is not a reason to endure abuse.** The talk says a quarrelsome partner is prarabdha and leaves the choice (divorce or living with love) to the reader. A distressed reader could take "your partner has come to work off your karma" as advice to tolerate harm. The SEO texts do not say "endure", the FAQ states the choice as the blog does, and the in-text link on that choice points to stop_tolerating_toxic_love. Recommendation: keep it that way; do not target "abusive husband" queries with this post.
5. **Generalizations about women.** "A woman lives at the level of the mind ... she is delicate" is the speaker's teaching, addressed mostly to husbands. Titles, meta and snippets do not lead with it, to avoid framing the post as a statement about women in general; the FAQ does not repeat it. Leave the body as it is.
6. **"Thought alone" (विचार मात्र).** The talk says what we receive comes from thought alone. Do not reframe this as "law of attraction" or "manifest a happy marriage" for SEO; no keyword does.
7. **Focus keyword placement.** "विवाह का उद्देश्य" / "purpose of marriage" is not in the first 100 words of either text (section 4). Adding it is optional; the recommended title tag and meta carry the query in the meantime.
