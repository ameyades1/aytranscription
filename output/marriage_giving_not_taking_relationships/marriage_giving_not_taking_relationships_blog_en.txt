# Marriage Is About Giving, Not Taking

## Is Marriage a Deal for Happiness?

Many people quietly carry a mistaken belief: "I am married now, so it is my husband's duty to give me love." Acharya Ji puts it very plainly: asking for love is not our duty.

Marriage is not a **business**, where we hand over something and come to collect something in return.

A great many people think that you marry in order to become happy. Both partners feel, "Now that I am married, I am happy." And then those same two people spend their whole lives in misery.

Why? Because they forgot the essential point.

As Acharya Ji explains, this marriage was meant for giving, not for taking.

*In your relationships, do you think more about what you can give, or about what you can get?*

## Give What You Have

Giving does not mean some great sacrifice. It means offering what you know how to do, whatever your talent is.

If a wife knows how to cook well, let her give that. If she knows how to earn, let her give that too. Tradition describes the many roles a wife plays. As a friend and as a **mantri** (counsellor), she stands by her husband through conversation and planning. Like a mother, she cooks and feeds him. Tradition also gives her a role in the intimate joys of married life.

The same rule holds for the husband. If he knows how to earn, let him earn. If he loves to travel, let him take his wife along. If he can offer protection, let him protect her.

Keep one thing in mind, though: none of this is done for gain. You do it **to purify yourself**, purely for the sake of doing it.

## Dutiful Action Expects Nothing Back

Acharya Ji says that we are born to give, not to take.

Whatever we do now is our **kartavya karma** (duty-bound action). Its purpose is **chitta-shuddhi** (purification of the mind), not trade. The question "What will I get out of this?" has no place in it at all.

How, then, does anything come to us? Acharya Ji says that what we receive comes from somewhere else entirely: from **thought alone**.

Even the feeling "My wife is a goddess, I am making her happy, and she is happy now" is something you have to bring into your own thinking.

## A Woman's Mind: The Mystery of Twenty-Seven Lifetimes

You also have to learn what marriage asks of you. What is a woman like? What are her qualities?

Acharya Chanakya, the ancient sage of statecraft, says you might spend twenty-seven lifetimes trying to understand this and still not grasp it. When we cannot even understand our own minds, understanding a woman's mind is extremely hard. Chanakya's advice is: do not even get into it.

Still, understand this much: a woman lives **at the level of the mind**. She will be changeable, and her emotions will rise and fall at times.

A man who lives at the level of **body-awareness** or **intellect-awareness** can stay hard. But something that seems trivial to him becomes **a page of history** for her. She feels it deeply, weeps over it, and remembers every detail.

Acharya Ji says you should understand enough to know how to treat her, because she is delicate.

*Do you try to understand your life partner according to their own nature?*

## A Difficult Partner: Destiny and New Action

You also need to see whether your wife's nature is **rajasic** (active and full of desires), **tamasic** (marked by inertia and ignorance) or **sattvic** (calm and pure). What does she want? And am I giving her what she needs? This is the action that purifies me.

If you have a quarrelsome partner who nags constantly, that is your **prarabdha** (the destiny shaped by your past actions). Your partner has come to help you work off your karma.

What you do with them now is your new action. Whether you divorce or live together with love is your new decision, your new thought.

And in the end, everything comes back to **thought alone**.

## Key Takeaways

- Marriage is not a business; it is about giving, not taking.
- Asking for love is not our duty; giving through our abilities and talents is.
- Both husband and wife act to purify the mind, not to gain something in return.
- What we receive comes from thought alone.
- A woman lives at the level of the mind; treat her with an understanding of her gentle nature.
- A difficult partner is our destiny; how we treat them is our new action.
