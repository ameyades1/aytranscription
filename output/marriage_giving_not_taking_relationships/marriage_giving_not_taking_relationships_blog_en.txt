# Marriage Is a Relationship of Giving Not Taking

## Is Marriage a Deal for Happiness?

Many people carry this belief in their minds: "I am married now; it is my husband's duty to give me love." But Acharya Ji says something very simple: asking for love is not our duty.

Marriage is not a **business**, where we give something in order to take something back.

So many people think that marriage means going there in order to be happy. Both feel, "We are married, now I am happy." And then the same two people live unhappily all their lives.

Why? Because they forgot the essential point.

Acharya Ji explains that this marriage was not about taking; it was about giving.

*In your relationships, do you think more about giving, or more about receiving?*

## Give What You Have

Giving does not mean some great sacrifice. Whatever you know, whatever your talent is, that is what you give.

If the wife knows how to cook well, let her give that. If she knows how to earn, let her give that too. Tradition describes the many forms of a wife: as a friend, as a **minister** (an adviser), she supports through dialogue and planning. Like a mother, she cooks and feeds. A role in the intimate joy of married life is also described for her.

The same rule applies to the husband. If he knows how to earn, let him earn. If he enjoys travelling, let him take his wife travelling too. If he can offer protection, let him protect.

But keep one thing in mind: all these actions are to be done not for some gain, but **for your own purification**. Simply for the sake of doing them.

## Dutiful Action: Nothing in Return

Acharya Ji says that we are born to give, not to take.

The action we perform now is our **kartavya karma** (duty-bound action). Its purpose is **chitta-shuddhi** (purification of the mind), not trade. The question "what will I get out of this?" has no place in it.

Then how does one attain things? Acharya Ji says attaining things happens through something else: through **thought alone**.

"This wife is a goddess, I am making her happy, she has become happy": this feeling, too, is something you have to bring into your own thought.

## A Woman's Mind: A Mystery of Twenty-Seven Lifetimes

One must also gain knowledge of what is to be done in a marriage. What is a woman like? What are her qualities?

Acharya Chanakya says that trying to understand this, perhaps twenty-seven lifetimes will pass, and still it will not be understood. When we cannot even understand our own mind, understanding a woman's mind is extremely difficult. Chanakya's advice: do not even get into it.

But do understand this much: a woman lives **at the level of the mind**. She will be restless; at times there will be emotional ups and downs.

If a man lives by **awareness of the body** or **awareness of the intellect**, he can stay hard. But what is a small matter for him becomes **a page of history** for her. She feels it deeply, cries over it, and remembers everything.

Acharya Ji says that one should have enough knowledge of how to treat her, because she is delicate.

*Do you try to understand your life partner according to their nature?*

## A Difficult Partner: Prarabdha and New Karma

One must also see whether the wife's nature is **rajasic** (active, full of desires), **tamasic** (marked by inertia and ignorance) or **sattvic** (calm and pure). What is her desire? And am I giving her what she needs? That is my action for my own purification.

If you got a quarrelsome, nagging partner, that is your **past prarabdha** (the fruit of past actions). They came to cut away your karma.

What you now do with them is your new karma. Whether to divorce or to live with love is your new decision, your new thought.

And in the end, everything comes down to **thought alone**.

## Key Takeaways

- Marriage is not a business; it means giving, not taking.
- Asking for love is not our duty; giving from our abilities and talents is.
- The actions of both husband and wife are for purifying the mind, not for getting something in return.
- Things are attained through thought alone.
- A woman lives at the level of the mind; treat her with an understanding of her delicate nature.
- A difficult partner is past prarabdha; how we treat them is our new karma.
