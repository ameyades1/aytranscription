# Chapter: Tamasic Relationships and the Art of Saying "No" with Confidence

## Dhriti in Tamasic Relationships

Now let us look at tamasic relationships. In general, the *dhriti* (steadfastness) in a tamasic relationship means holding on to a relationship for a long time. The relationship may be between a girlfriend and boyfriend, between husband and wife, or anything else.

Such a relationship is dragged on for a long time, but in *tamoguna* it runs on ignorance. There is constant weeping and complaining, or it continues out of fear, and still the person will not leave. Out of fear of society, or worrying about someone else, they think:

> "What do I do? What will people say? It was my own decision. I married for love by my own choice. Now it has gone bad."

Oh, you can come out of it at any time. If a mistake was made once, will you carry it for the rest of your life?

## Enduring: A Virtue or Ignorance?

In a tamasic relationship, a person suffers emotional, verbal and even physical abuse. They are being tormented emotionally, shouted at every day, abused with insults every day, and in some cases even physically harmed. And they keep enduring it.

Now, where do enduring and tolerance, these virtues, belong? They belong to the *gunatita*, the one beyond the gunas. But these people endure what is wrong. This enduring is the result of low self-worth, or of the fear of being left alone: "What do I do, how do I manage?"

There is no knowledge here. And for knowledge, one has to go to a guru. But such a person does not understand this; they simply think, "It was my decision, I will bear it all."

Such people tolerate pain simply because they are used to it, and make no effort to create healthier boundaries.

## The Meaning of a Healthy Boundary

So what does a healthy boundary mean? Right after marriage, your own rules should be set: this I like, this I do not. "All right, this is your tradition, I have accepted it."

Even between a boss and an employee, terms and conditions are set from the start. In the same way it must be clear that **I will not tolerate abuse.** But such people never set this boundary; they simply do not have the courage to do it.

## Learn to Say "No" with Confidence

> "Learn to say no with confidence."

This never arises in them. The courage to say, with full self-confidence, "No, I will not do it" does not come to them.

And at times you must have the strength within you to say it, whether in society or anywhere else. Write this sentence down once: **learn to say no with confidence.**

## Where to Say "No"

But where should this "no" be said? Where there is abuse. Not in the wrong place. If the guru has given you the guru's work, will you confidently say, "No, Guruji, we won't do it"? No, that we will do! It is from you that we learned the guru's work.

Say "no" with confidence where someone is asking you to do something negative, is abusing you, is forcing you by saying, "No, you have to do this." There, learn to say firmly: "No, I will not do it."

You must know how to set that boundary with the full right to do so.
