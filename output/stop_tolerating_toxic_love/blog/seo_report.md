## 1. Summary

A short discourse (the video is 3:54; about 550 words per language) in which Acharya Upendra Ji describes the **tamasic relationship**: a bond held on to for a very long time out of ignorance and fear, even while it brings daily tears, shouting, insults and sometimes physical abuse. People stay because of "what will people say?", because "it was my own decision", out of low self-worth, fear of being alone, or simple habit. He says putting up with wrongdoing is not tolerance: true tolerance belongs to the gunatita. The way out begins with healthy boundaries set from the start ("I will not tolerate abuse") and learning to say a confident "no", though not to the work the Guru gives. The main intent is emotional / life problem: readers in a painful relationship searching "toxic relationship se kaise nikle", "why do people stay in toxic relationships", "is tolerating abuse a virtue". These results are crowded with psychology and coaching pages; the opportunity is the guna-based angle (tamas as the root of staying, tolerance as a quality of the gunatita), which almost no page answers from the tradition. The post belongs in the Love & Relationships cluster, with links into the Three Gunas and Guru clusters.

## 6. FAQ

Kept in the report only (not added to the page or to the schema).

1. **तामसिक रिश्ता क्या है? / What is a tamasic relationship?**
   A bond held on to for a very long time out of ignorance and fear, even though the tears and quarrels never stop. It can be between lovers, husband and wife, or any two people.
2. **क्या अत्याचार सहना सहिष्णुता है? / Is tolerating abuse a virtue?**
   No. Tolerance is a virtue, but it belongs to the gunatita, one who has risen above the three gunas. Putting up with wrongdoing comes from low self-worth or the fear of being alone, and is not tolerance.
3. **अगर रिश्ता मेरा अपना निर्णय था तो? / What if it was my own decision, like a love marriage?**
   Acharya Ji says you can step out of it at any time. A mistake made once is no reason to carry it for the rest of your life, and fear of "what will people say" should not be the chain that holds you.
4. **रिश्ते में स्वस्थ सीमा कैसी होती है? / What does a healthy boundary look like?**
   A few clear rules from the start, as an employer and employee settle their terms on day one. One of them must be: "I will not tolerate abuse."
5. **क्या हर जगह "नहीं" कहना चाहिए? / Should I say no to everything?**
   No. Say "no" when someone wants you to do something harmful, abuses you, or pressures you with "you have to do this". Do not say no to the work the Guru gives you.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Dhriti attached to the relationship** (Sanskrit review, issue 1, Minor). In the Gita (18.35, Gita Press) tamasic dhriti is a quality of the person, by which a dull-witted person does not let go of sleep, fear, grief, despondency and arrogance. Recommendation: apply the review's wording before publishing. None of the SEO texts, snippets or FAQ use the phrase "dhriti of a relationship"; they say the person clings out of ignorance and fear, which fits the verse. If the editor quotes 18.35 to strengthen E-E-A-T, cite it as Gita Press 18.35, but do not add it to titles or meta.
2. **"No, Guruji" missing in English** (Sanskrit review, issue 2, Minor). Apply the review's correction; no link anchor uses that sentence (the Guru link anchors on the sentence before it), so seo.json is unaffected.
3. **"Toxic" versus "tamasic".** The talk's own term is tamasic; "toxic" comes from the video title and is what readers search. The titles and meta use "toxic" only for the query and explain it as tamasic clinging; they do not present "toxic relationship" as a scriptural category. Keep it that way.
4. **Readers in danger.** The talk mentions physical abuse and says you can step out at any time, but the blog offers no safety guidance. Readers searching these queries may be in danger. Recommendation (editor's decision): consider a short, clearly separate note after the post pointing to local help (for example a women's helpline or emergency services). No SEO text promises that the post alone resolves abuse, and the snippets do not tell anyone to leave a particular marriage; they state the talk's own words.
5. **"Go to the Guru" and the Guru-karya caveat.** The talk says the missing knowledge is gained from the Guru, and that the confident "no" is not for the Guru's work. Do not shorten social snippets to "say no to everyone", and do not frame "go to the Guru" as advice to stay in an abusive situation until then. FAQ 5 keeps the caveat exactly as the blog gives it.
6. **Focus keyword placement.** "why do people stay in toxic relationships" is not in the first 100 words of the English text (section 4). Adding "toxic" to the opening question is optional; the title tag and meta carry the query in the meantime. The Hindi focus keyword is already in the first paragraph.
