# The Atharva Mind: Becoming the Ganadhipati of Your Own Life

## Why Does a Leader Need a Steady Mind?

Whenever a great task calls people onto the field, one leader steps forward and carries everyone along. The trouble is that this leader is often not **atharva** (calm and steady of mind).

This is not a call to avoid all conflict. You must stand against **adharma** (unrighteousness), and fight it when you have to. The real question is what kind of mind does the fighting.

In short, Acharya Ji teaches how to develop a steady mind: an atharva mind comes when you, the Atman, take charge of your inner gana of body, mind, intellect, chitta, breath and prana. Distance from overstimulation, pranayama and asanas bring breath and mind under your command, and the final step is not theory but surrender to the Guru.

**Tharva** (restless) describes a mind that wavers and shakes. Atharva is its opposite: a mind that stays cool and stable. What the world needs is a **Ganadhipati** (the leader of a group, or gana) with exactly that kind of steady mind, and **sadhana** (spiritual practice) is how you become one.

## "But I'm Never Going to Lead Anyone"

You might wonder what any of this has to do with you. Yet you may well be the leader of your household. And even setting the household aside, you are certainly meant to lead your own life. You should be able to steer it by your own will, instead of serving a thousand different masters.

## The Gana Within You: Who Rules Body, Mind and Breath?

You have a gana of your own: your body, mind, intellect, **chitta** (the storehouse of memory and impressions) and Self. You lead this entire group. And who exactly is that "you"? It is the **Atman (the Self)**.

When I enter the state of **Omkara** (absorption in Om, the primordial sound), my mind serves me the way a kingdom serves its king. My intellect stays in check, and my body listens instead of wandering off. My chitta, my breath and my **prana** (life force) come under my command as well.

## Does Your Breath Obey You?

When you rush about all day, steeped in **rajoguna** (the quality of restless activity), does your breath listen to you? It races. Your heart will not listen to you either.

It will listen, though, once you give it the right conditions: distance from whatever overstimulates you, along with **pranayama** (breath regulation) and asanas. The choice is yours. You can tell your body, "Settle down and do your pranayama," and tell your mind, "Be still." Then your prana, too, will answer to you.

Your lifespan rests on prana, on the breath. Whether you add years to your life or take them away is in your own hands.

Body, mind, intellect, chitta, ego, prana, the **jnanamaya kosha** (the sheath of knowledge) and the **anandamaya kosha** (the sheath of bliss): you yourself, the Atman, rule over this whole gana. The teaching does not apply only to some king in the outside world.

*In the gana within you, who is ruling today: you, or your restlessness?*

## Who Is the Real Atheist?

Once you have mastered your inner self, self-confidence follows. Swami Vivekananda used to say that the old religion called anyone who did not believe in God an atheist, while the true religion calls those who do not believe in themselves atheists.

You can perform a hundred thousand rituals and prayers, but if you cannot make a single decision for yourself, something essential is still missing.

## "That's Just How It Is"

One woman says, "What can I do? My husband is just like that." Another says, "That's how my mother-in-law is." They are not ghosts, after all! You should at least try speaking up once.

Nobody is talking about going to court or filing for divorce. There are gentler means, such as **sama** (persuasion) and **dana** (offering something in return). But if you never once voice what you think, your servitude becomes permanent. It means the voice of the Self within you has been silenced, your confidence has died, and you have accepted all this as a way of life. After that, no circumstance can make your mind happy.

*Is there something in your life that you have accepted with the words "That's just how it is"?*

## A Defeated Mind, a Defeated Nation

A servile mind sees only the glory of the other side: they won, and I lost. When mind after mind accepts defeat, the whole nation is defeated. When India was enslaved, the country as a whole took on the mindset of a slave.

If this lasts long enough, the defeated nation starts treating the **conqueror as its ideal**. That is why so many people still believe that anything English must be better. The conquerors were the British and the Mughals, and without realising it, the mind whispers, "At least they are powerful, so they must be right."

By nature, however, we are **jivanmukta** (free while still living). The Self declares "ahaṁ brahmāsmi" (I am Brahman); it is Brahman itself trying to recognise Brahman. Strength is life, and weakness is death.

Every great teacher has said this: Swami Vivekananda; Chanakya, the ancient strategist and statesman; and Shri Krishna, speaking to Arjuna.

## The Same Rule at Home

Apply to yourself what holds true for a nation. Suppose a woman keeps putting up with servitude in her own home, swallowing it day after day. Her mind has accepted defeat, and the people around her have become the conquerors. A voice inside her starts saying, "Just keep quiet and listen." Past a certain point, that mistreatment becomes her way of life.

It then passes to the next generation. Either the daughter is warned from the start, "Be careful, don't do this, don't go there," or she rebels and swings to the other extreme, speaking with great aggression. Both responses are wrong. The right way was to remain atharva and move forward calmly.

## Any One of You Could Become Ganapati

This is not just theory; it applies to your personal life as well. Who knows which of you will become a Ganadhipati, a **Ganapati** (Lord of the Ganas) or a **Vinayaka** (the supreme leader), both names of Lord Ganesha?

## Not Through Theory, but Through Surrender

Reading and listening alone will not get you there. The Katha Upanishad says:

> **nāyamātmā pravacanena labhyo**
> **na medhayā na bahunā śrutena**
> "This Self cannot be gained through discourses, nor through a sharp memory, nor through much listening."
> (Katha Upanishad 1.2.23)

How, then, is it gained? Through **sharanagati** (complete surrender), by going to the Guru and quietly listening to whatever he says.

After teaching the entire Gita, Shri Krishna arrives at the same point:

> **sarvadharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> **ahaṁ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Abandon all dharmas and take refuge in Me alone. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

That is the final answer.

## Karma-Rahasya: Who Knows the Secret?

This teaching is called **karma-rahasya** (the secret of action). A secret only means something if somebody knows it; otherwise the word is empty. The secret is hidden from us, not from the Guru. The principle of the Guru and the supreme principle are one and the same; Shri Krishna himself says that it is He who comes in the form of the living Guru.

Think of how a light switches on the moment you press a button. To someone from a remote tribe it would look like magic, while someone who has studied science would see nothing magical in it. Even so, most of us cannot explain how electrons actually move. No scientist has ever seen an atom or an electron either, yet they know what these do. In the same way, the Gita says that some see the Self, speak of it and hear of it "āścaryavat" (as a wonder) (Bhagavad Gita 2.29). Some things may seem like wonders even to the Guru, but the secret is known to him.

And what is that secret? **Silent obedience.** When told to rise, rise; when told to sit, sit. Whoever bows before one person will find the whole world bowing before them.

The one before whom the world bows is Ganapati, and that one is Vinayaka.

## Narada Bhakti Sutra: Rare, Hard to Walk, Unfailing

The **Narada Bhakti Sutra**, the classic text on devotion, teaches the same thing. You need a Guru who can connect you to God, and such company comes only by divine grace, and very rarely:

> **durlabho'gamyo'moghaś ca**
> "The company of great souls is rare, hard to reach, and unfailing."
> (Narada Bhakti Sutra 39)

- **Durlabha (rare):** Finding such a Guru is hard, and recognising one is harder still. He may turn up in a T-shirt, a kurta, a dhoti or a suit. Swami Vivekananda wore both a suit and saffron robes, and Shri Krishna came carrying a flute.
- **Agamya (hard to walk):** Even once you recognise him, his path is demanding. We are used to living as we please, so listening to the Guru feels like servitude. It is not servitude.
- **Amogha (unfailing):** Yet for anyone who actually walks it, the fruit is certain.

So make the effort to reach such a Guru, and turn the hard path into one you can walk. The unfailing result will then follow.

The sutras go on to pose a beautiful riddle: the Guru leads you to God, and God leads you to such a Guru. In the end they say the two are truly one, and whoever knows this is a true devotee:

> **tad eva sādhyatāṁ tad eva sādhyatām**
> "Strive for that alone, strive for that alone. Whoever does so attains liberation and gains all that they seek."
> (Narada Bhakti Sutra 42)

## Then What Should I Do? Prema Eva Karyam

Once I reach such a Guru and such a God, what is my task? Should I become a doctor, an engineer? The answer is "prema eva kāryam" (love alone is your work): your only task is to love.

This is not the love of the movies, where the hero chases the heroine around the trees. That is lust, or attachment. Love is not limited to a man and a woman. There is love for one's nation, for all living beings, for nature and for dharma, and there is the love between a mother and her son.

Love means **ekatva** (oneness). A mother feels her son is part of her own body, because he came into the world when the cord between them was cut. When that same oneness extends to your nation, the **vaishya bhava** (the trader's mindset of give and take) disappears. You no longer want anything in return.

*Does your love today run on give and take, or on oneness?*

## Love Needs a Steady Hand on the Wheel

Even a deeply negative person can become a suicide bomber. A kind of love has arisen in him as well, but it has strayed from its path, because he acts from a tharva, unstable mind. That is why love must go hand in hand with an atharva mind.

This pairing is absolutely essential if you want to build an atharva mind and become Ganapati.

## Key Takeaways

- **Atharva** means a cool, steady mind, the first requirement of true leadership.
- Within you lives a gana of body, mind, intellect, chitta, prana and the koshas, and you, the Atman, are its ruler.
- Pranayama, asanas and distance from overstimulation bring your prana and mind under your command.
- The real atheist is the one who does not believe in themselves.
- Accepting injustice as "just how it is" marks a defeated mind; neither silence nor aggression works, so speak up from an atharva mind.
- You gain the Self through surrender to the Guru, not through discourses, sharp memory or much listening.
- The company of great souls is rare, hard to reach and unfailing; the Guru and God are one.
- The seeker's work is love, meaning oneness, and an atharva mind gives that love its direction.
