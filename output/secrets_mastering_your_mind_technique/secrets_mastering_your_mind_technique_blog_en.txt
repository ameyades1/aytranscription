# The Atharva Mind: How to Become the Ganadhipati of Your Own Life

## Why Do We Need a Leader with a Steady Mind?

When one must step onto the field for some great work, one leader takes everyone along. But the problem is often that this leader is not **atharva**.

This is not about never opposing anything. One must stand against adharma, and fight if needed. But what kind of mind is fighting: that is the real question.

**Tharva** means restless, wavering (unsteady). **Atharva** means cool and stable (calm and stable). Such a steady-minded **Ganadhipati** (lord of the gana, the group) is what is needed, and for that there is **sadhana** (spiritual practice).

## "I'm Not Going to Be a Leader"

You may think, what does this have to do with me? But you can be the leader of your home. And leave the home aside, you must at least be the leader of your own life.

> "I should be able to lead my own life the way I want. One should not be the slave of a thousand people."

## The Gana Within You

You too have a gana: body, mind, intellect, chitta and Atma. You are the leader of this whole group. And who are "you"? The **Atma**.

When I go into that **state of Omkara**, only then is my mind under me, like a king. Only then does the intellect stay under control, the body listens to me and does not stray. Chitta, breath and prana also come under my control.

## Does Your Breath Listen to You?

When you run around all day, sunk in **rajoguna** (the quality of restless activity), does your breath listen to you? It runs fast. The heart doesn't listen to you either.

But it will listen when you give it a favourable environment. Distance from things that excite you, and **pranayama** and asanas. This is your choice. You can tell the body, "Do pranayama calmly," and the mind, "Be calm." Then prana too will be under you.

Our lifespan rests on prana, on the breath. Whether to add years or reduce them is in your hands.

Body, mind, intellect, chitta, ego, prana, jnanamaya kosha, anandamaya kosha: you yourself, the Atma, are the lord of this whole gana. This is not only about some outer king.

*Who rules the gana within you today: you, or your restlessness?*

## Who Is the Real Atheist?

The one whose Atma has come under their control, in them arises **atmavishvas**, self-confidence. Swami Vivekananda used to say:

> "The old religion said that he who does not believe in God is an atheist; the true religion says that he who does not believe in himself is an atheist."

Do a hundred thousand pujas, but if you cannot take a single decision for yourself, something is incomplete.

## "This Is Just How It Is"

Someone says, "What can I do, our husband is just like this." Someone says, "Our mother-in-law is like this." She is not a ghost! One should at least try speaking up once.

This is not about going to court or getting a divorce. There are ways: sama, dama. But if you never once put your point across, the slavery becomes confirmed. It means the voice of the Atma within has been suppressed, self-confidence is finished, and we have accepted this as a way of life. Then the mind is not happy in any situation.

*Is there something you have accepted by saying "this is just how it is"?*

## The Defeated Mind, the Defeated Nation

The attitude of a slave sees only the glorious form of the other: he won, I lost. When mind after mind is defeated, the whole nation is defeated. When India was enslaved, overall the whole country became enslaved.

When this lasts a long time, the defeated nation begins to take **the victorious nation as its ideal**. That is why even today many feel that whatever is British is good. The victors were the British and the Mughals, and unknowingly the mind within says, "At least they are powerful, so they must be right too."

Whereas by nature we are **jivanmukta** (liberated while living). The Atma is "Aham Brahmasmi"; Brahman itself is trying to recognise Brahman.

> "Strength is life, weakness is death."

Every guru has said this: Swami Vivekananda, Chanakya, and Shri Krishna to Arjuna.

## The Same Rule at Home

What applies to the nation, try applying it to yourself. If at home I keep bearing slavery, bearing it, then the mind is defeated, and the others have become the victors. Then a voice from within says, "Quietly listen." After a point, the same oppression becomes a way of life.

And it passes to the next generation. Either the daughter is taught from the start, "Be careful, don't do this, don't go there," or in rebellion, the other extreme: speaking very aggressively. Both methods are wrong. The way was to walk as **atharva**, calmly.

## One of You Could Become Ganapati

This is not only theory; it is for personal life too. Who knows which of you will become a **Ganadhipati**, a **Ganapati**, a **Vinayaka**.

## Not by Theory, but by Surrender

This does not happen just by reading and hearing. The Katha Upanishad says:

> **nāyam ātmā pravacanena labhyo na medhayā na bahunā śrutena**
> "This Atma is not attained by discourse, nor by intellect, nor by much hearing."
> (Katha Upanishad 1.2.23)

This Atma is not attained by discourse, nor by medha (a sharp memory), nor by much hearing. Then by what? By going to the Guru, through **sharanagati** (complete surrender), quietly listening to what he says.

After narrating the whole Gita, Shri Krishna too says this at the end:

> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> **ahaṁ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone; I shall free you from all sins, do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

The final answer is this.

## The Secret of Karma: Who Knows the Secret?

This is called **karma-rahasya**, the secret of karma. A secret by definition means someone knows it, otherwise it has no meaning. It is a secret for us, not for the Guru. The Guru-tattva and the Param-tattva are one; Shri Krishna himself says that it is I who come in the form of the living Guru.

Just as a light coming on at the press of a switch would seem like magic to a tribal person, but not to someone who has studied science. Yet we cannot explain how the electron moves. The scientist has not seen the atom or the electron either, yet knows their consequences. Likewise, the Gita says, someone sees the Atma "as a wonder", speaks of it, hears of it (Bhagavad Gita 2.29). Some things may seem a wonder even to the Guru, but the secret is known to him.

And what is that secret? **Quiet obedience.** If told to rise, rise; if told to sit, sit.

> "Bow down to one person, and the world will bow down to you."

The one to whom the world bows is Ganapati, is Vinayaka.

## Narada Bhakti Sutra: Rare, Inaccessible, Unfailing

The **Narada Bhakti Sutra** says the same. One needs a Guru who connects to the Supreme, and such company comes by divine grace, very rarely:

> **durlabho 'gamyo 'moghaś ca**
> "It is rare, inaccessible, and unfailing."
> (Narada Bhakti Sutra 39)

- **Durlabha (rare):** such a Guru is hard to find, and even if found, hard to recognise. He comes sometimes in a T-shirt, sometimes in a kurta, sometimes in a dhoti, sometimes in a suit. Swami Vivekananda wore a suit as well as saffron; Shri Krishna, just with his flute.
- **Agamya (hard to walk):** even if recognised, his path is hard. We are used to living freely, so listening to the Guru feels like slavery. But it is not slavery.
- **Amogha (unfailing):** yet the one who has done it, the result is certain.

So one must make the effort to reach such a Guru, and make the inaccessible path accessible. Then the unfailing result is assured.

Further on, the sutra poses a beautiful riddle: the Guru takes us to the Supreme, and the Supreme itself makes us meet such a Guru. At the end it says that in reality both are one, and the one who knows this is the true devotee:

> **tad eva sādhyatāṁ tad eva sādhyatām**
> "That alone should be attained, that alone should be attained."
> (Narada Bhakti Sutra 42)

That one alone will get moksha, that one alone will receive whatever is needed.

## Then What Should I Do? Prema Eva Karyam

Having gone to such a Guru and the Supreme, what should I do? Become a doctor, an engineer? The answer is: **prema eva karyam**, your only work is love.

This is not film love, going around between trees with the heroine. That is lust, or attachment. Love is not only between man and woman: there is love for the nation, for living beings, for nature, for dharma, and between mother and son.

Love means **oneness**. To a mother, her son feels like a part of her own body, because he came from her, with the cord cut. When such oneness comes with the nation, the **vaishya bhava** (the attitude of give and take) goes away. Then I want nothing in return.

*Does your love today run on give and take, or on oneness?*

## Let the Direction of Love Also Be Atharva

Even a negative person becomes a suicide bomber. A kind of love has come to him too, but his path is misguided, because he works with a tharva, unsteady mind. That is why love needs an atharva mind along with it.

To build an atharva mind and to become Ganapati, this is absolutely essential.

## Key Takeaways

- **Atharva** means a cool and steady mind; it is the first condition of true leadership.
- Within you is a gana of body, mind, intellect, chitta, prana and the koshas, whose lord is you, the Atma.
- Pranayama, asanas and distance from excitement bring prana and mind under your control.
- The one who does not believe in himself is the real atheist.
- Accepting injustice as "just how it is" is the sign of a defeated mind; neither silence nor extreme aggression is right, the way is to speak as atharva.
- The Atma is attained not by discourse, intellect or much hearing, but by surrender to the Guru.
- The company of great souls is rare, inaccessible and unfailing; the Guru and the Supreme are one.
- The seeker's work is love, that is, oneness, whose direction is set by an atharva mind.
