# Why the Same Action Can Be a Virtue or a Sin

Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, is called divine wealth. Maa Durga is called **trigunatmika** (made of the three gunas), which means she holds all three **gunas** (the fundamental qualities of nature). These three are **sattva** (purity, knowledge), **rajas** (movement, activity) and **tamas** (inertia, darkness).

Durga's form arises from the union of these three qualities. Hidden within them is the secret of sin and virtue.

## Whom Did Durga Slay?

The name Durga means the one who slew the demon Durgam. That demon is **maya** (the illusion that veils reality). Durgam also means "hard to cross", and what is hard to cross is this world itself.

To cross this world is to rise beyond the three gunas.

## Am I Really Meditating?

Knowingly or unknowingly, we keep leaping from one guna to another.

Someone may believe they have gone deep into meditation, into **yoga nidra** (a state of conscious, yogic sleep). In truth, they have often slipped into tamas and simply fallen asleep.

From the outside, the two are hard to tell apart. One person sits like a saint, free of every worldly worry, while another lies drunk. Neither of them is worried about anything, and telling them apart is very difficult.

*When you sit with your eyes closed, does your calm come from awareness or from sleep?*

## Alike on the Outside, Different Within

The scriptures say that **sattva and tamas look the same from the outside**.

Take charity. One person gives from a sincere heart, another gives to be seen, and a third gives to feed their ego. To an onlooker, all three acts look identical.

The act of giving belongs to **rajas**. The understanding and feeling behind it, however, can come from sattva or from tamas.

## A Simple Definition of Sin and Virtue

Lakshmi is activity; she is rajas. Rajas in itself is neutral. It becomes a problem only through what it joins with, where it acts, and the intention behind it.

Acharya Ji explains that when rajas draws its support from tamas, the scriptures call it sin. When the same rajas is backed by sattva, it becomes virtue.

Here is a simple example. You go to the gym and lift dumbbells. That is **raj shakti** (the power of rajas), and its benefit is a stronger body.

Now suppose someone uses that same body to become a thug, to beat people up or rob them. Rajas has joined with tamas, and that is sin. When the same strength joins with Saraswati, the goddess of knowledge, it becomes virtue.

*What intention are your energy and abilities serving today?*

## Let Go of the Guna, Keep the Shakti

The scriptures do not call Kali evil. They call her **tamas shakti** (the power of tamas), not the guna of tamas.

Every guna carries its own **shakti** (power):

- The guna of tamas, and tamas shakti
- The guna of rajas, and raj shakti
- The guna of sattva, and sattva shakti

Shakti means the Goddess. We are meant to use all three powers at all times, yet hold on to one guna alone: **the guna of sattva**. As Acharya Ji puts it, let every other guna fall away, but never give up its power.

## The Secret of Shivaji Maharaj

Whenever Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj rode into battle, he carried tamas shakti with him, the power of Kali. **Bhavani Mata** (the goddess Shivaji worshipped) is Kali herself.

He used that power to the full. And what was his guna? **Supreme sattva**.

## Key Takeaways

- Durga holds all three gunas; crossing the world means rising beyond sattva, rajas and tamas.
- Sattva and tamas look alike from the outside: meditation and sleep, sincere charity and show.
- The act behind every deed is rajas; the intention behind it decides what it becomes.
- Rajas joined with tamas is sin; rajas joined with sattva is virtue.
- Guna and shakti are different: use all three powers, but keep sattva as your only guna.
- Shivaji Maharaj took up the power of Kali, but his guna was supreme sattva.
