# When Will the Kalki Avatar Come?

All of us are waiting for someone. A great soul will come, the **Kalki** avatar (the tenth incarnation of Lord Vishnu, foretold to appear at the end of this dark age) will arrive, and everything will change. But is that really what the Bhagavad Gita says?

When will the Kalki avatar come? According to Acharya Ji, the Gita defines an avatar in verses 4.7 to 4.9, but nowhere does it tell us to wait for anyone. Whoever joins the work for the nation and the Guru's mission and recognises their Guru becomes Kalki too. Until the Kalki within us awakens, no Kalki will come from outside.

In this discourse, Acharya Ji draws on three verses from the fourth chapter of the Gita to explain what an **avatar** (a divine incarnation) truly is. Along the way, he raises a question that turns our whole way of thinking upside down.

## What Have We Gained from Reading About Great Souls?

Swami Vivekananda was great. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was great, and so was Eknath Maharaj. The **Nadi Granth** (ancient palm-leaf texts said to record individual destinies) described Shripad Shrivallabh as an avatar. Yet they have all gone. What good has it done you?

Acharya Ji's question is simple: can you become like even one of them? Their lives benefit you only when a fragment of what they were takes root in you.

We read about Shivaji Maharaj and Chandragupta, yet we are afraid to face our own brother in a property dispute. We cannot even manage two **malas** (rounds of chanting on prayer beads) of daily practice. What, then, is the point of reading these life stories? As Acharya Ji puts it, their existence means something to you only when a part of them comes alive within you.

*Which quality of the great souls you have read about actually shows up in your life today?*

## Should We Wait for Kalki?

Acharya Ji shares that, just as Shripad Shrivallabh's Nadi Granth spoke of his mission as an avatar, his own Nadi reading also says that he will do great work and transform the country.

The real problem lies elsewhere. Whenever we talk about **yuga parivartan** (a change of era), we start waiting for some Kalki to come. We will do nothing ourselves; someone will surely arrive, and then all will be well with us.

Acharya Ji is clear: the Bhagavad Gita never says any such thing.

## The First Verse: You Were Born in This Very Age

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānirbhavati bhārata**
> **abhyutthānamadharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmyaham**
> "O Bharata, whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, I manifest Myself."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

Acharya Ji asks: has **dharma** (righteousness, the right order of life) declined today? Has negativity grown? Is he the only one who sees it, or do you see it too? You see it as well.

You, too, were born in exactly such a time. You, too, have taken a form and appeared in the world. In other words, the **first condition** of an avatar applies to you as well.

When parents who had brought their children for **Saraswati diksha** (an initiation invoking the goddess of learning) asked him, "Are you an avatar?", he replied: why only me? The one asking is an avatar too. We are all avatars.

The only difference is that Acharya Ji has come to know who he is. Whoever recognises God and their own true self is an avatar. The same **Brahman** (the Absolute) dwells within you. All that remains is to recognise it.

## The Second Verse: Your Work Sets You Apart

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṃ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> **dharmasaṃsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge**
> "To protect the good, to destroy the wicked, and to establish dharma, I appear in age after age."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

Everyone is born. But you grow up to become a doctor, an engineer or an architect, while an avatar takes birth to serve the Guru's mission and the nation. This is what sets an avatar apart from an ordinary person.

An avatar stands ready to accomplish this through **sama, dama, danda** (persuasion, inducement, force), by every possible means, whether that calls for the discus or the bow. And **dharma sansthapana** (the re-establishment of dharma) is inevitably bound up with politics, because the whole system has to change. It calls for the work of both the Kshatriya, the warrior, and the Brahmin, the teacher.

So you passed the first condition. You fell short on the second because you are not doing this work.

*Does your life end at your family and career, or does it have room for a larger purpose?*

## The Third Verse: The One We Never Read

Does this mean you will never attain liberation? The Gita does not say that. Acharya Ji points out that we have never read the next verse, the ninth verse of the fourth chapter. Had we read it, we would not be sitting around waiting for some Kalki.

> **janma karma ca me divyamevaṃ yo vetti tattvataḥ**
> **tyaktvā dehaṃ punarjanma naiti māmeti so'rjuna**
> "O Arjuna, whoever truly knows the divine nature of My birth and My deeds does not take birth again after leaving the body, but comes to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.9)

The first message here is this: before you follow a Guru or a leader, check whether their birth and deeds are **divya** (divine). There are two tests of what is divine:

1. **Nirmal** (pure, free of impurity): a doctor who never removes a patient's organ without telling them, a politician who stays clear of corruption, a Guru who never does anything improper. Both mind and conduct are clean.
2. **Alaukik** (beyond the ordinary world): there must be something divine about them, some miraculous work, a voice and words to match.

Acharya Ji reminds us that superhuman power alone is not enough. We do not need a Superman; we need someone who carries the supreme divine power.

## Believing Is Not Knowing

The verse says "truly knows", not "believes".

To **believe** is to start worshipping someone because another person told you he is God. Tomorrow someone else will make the same claim, and you will worship that one too. To **know** is to be one hundred percent certain of what God is.

**Tattvatah** (knowing the essence) means knowing through both principle and experience. Acharya Ji offers a lovely example: write ten thousand pages about sugar, and you still will not know whether it is sweet or spicy. Taste a pinch, and you understand at once.

## The Four Steps to Liberation

You cannot gain this experience by sitting at home. Acharya Ji explains the four kinds of **mukti** (liberation) described in the scriptures:

1. **Salokya** (dwelling in the same realm): you live among spiritual people, come to the Guru and study a little. It is like being an admirer who quietly follows along.
2. **Samipya** (nearness): you draw close to the Guru, watch their life up close and spend time with them. Here you begin to know the essence.
3. **Sarupya** (sharing the same form): you take on their form, not of the body but of their nature. Your qualities, your speech and your actions begin to resemble theirs.
4. **Sayujya** (union): "yuj" means to join, to become one. You no longer become like them; you become them.

This is what it means to know the Guru in essence. Whoever reaches this point is certain to be liberated. If you follow an avatar, you too become worthy of being part of that avatar.

*Which step are you standing on today: just an admirer, or someone who is truly close?*

## An Avatar Never Fights Alone

Ravana carried away Sita Mata. You could call that Shri Rama's personal problem. Why, then, did Hanuman fight? Why did the whole army of vanaras, the forest dwellers who served Shri Rama, go to war?

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's goal was **swarajya** (self-rule), an India free of Mughal rule. Why, then, did the **Mavale** (the hill soldiers of the Maval region who formed Shivaji's army) fight? Why did Baji Prabhu lay down his life?

The answer is clear. An avatar may be born, but it never works alone. It works with a vast team. That team is made up of seekers who come close to it and gradually rise to the same state themselves.

## Hanuman's Answer: Three Levels, One Truth

As they set out for battle, Shri Rama asked Hanuman, "What is the relationship between you and me?" Hanuman, himself an incarnation of Lord Shiva, answered without a moment's thought, and his answer opens up three levels of philosophy.

**Dvaita** (duality): "If I look through the sense of the body, you and I are separate. You are the master and I am the servant." But this does not mean a lifetime of slavery. Hanuman was clear about who he was. Acharya Ji explains that this is the **Svadhishthana chakra** (the energy centre of one's own foundation): knowing your identity thoroughly.

**Vishishtadvaita** (qualified non-duality): "At the level of mind and intellect, you are the whole and I am a part of it. If you are the ocean, I am a drop. All the life of the sea lives within you, and ships sail upon you, while I hold nothing. Yet if the drops were taken away one by one, there would be no ocean left." In the same way, a king is a king because of his army, his people and his ministers.

Acharya Ji adds with a laugh that even a trade union leader understands this much. He tells the factory owner, "You are the master and I am the servant, but don't push it too far, or I'll call a strike." Hanuman, however, was no leader organising a strike of the vanara army.

**Advaita** (non-duality): "At the level of the **Atman** (the Self), I am two hundred percent certain that we are one and the same. You are the incarnation of Lord Vishnu, I am the incarnation of Lord Shiva. I have recognised you."

This is sayujya. This is the state those three verses describe.

## Kalki Will Awaken Within, Not Outside

Some person will have come to carry out the work of an avatar. Let them handle the politics, the re-establishment of dharma and everything else. Your task is to **recognise** them and join them.

Ask yourself: can I contribute to helping the good, defeating the wicked and establishing dharma? If I cannot do it myself, do I at least stand with someone who does?

When a person joins such **work for the nation and the Guru's mission** and recognises their Guru, in that very moment they become that same Kalki avatar. This, Acharya Ji says, is the true definition. A whole army accomplishes the work together, and you attain liberation.

An avatar comes only when people everywhere say, "We need a leader like this." In the same way, the Guru appears only when the disciple stands before him with a question. If we keep saying, "Let things go on as they are; let the corruption go on," no divine descent will happen. As Acharya Ji says, until the Kalki within us awakens, no Kalki is going to come from outside.

## Be a Hanuman, Be a Mavala

It is fine to be a servant like Hanuman, and fine to work like one of the vanaras. You can still reach liberation if you become one with that person, just as Hanuman recognised Shri Rama.

Shivaji Maharaj's Mavale never said, "We fought too and gave our lives, so we will sit on the throne as well." Their attitude was, "That is your work. Mine was to fight, and I have fought."

According to Acharya Ji, there are two paths. Either become a powerful Guru yourself, one who gives **shaktipat diksha** (initiation through the transmission of spiritual energy), or throw yourself into such work. Both lead to liberation.

*If such a Guru or such a mission came into your life today, would you recognise it?*

## Key Takeaways

- The Gita defines an avatar in verses 4.7 to 4.9, and nowhere does it tell you to wait for someone.
- You were born in a time when dharma has declined, so the first condition of an avatar applies to you too.
- What separates an avatar from an ordinary person is action: protecting the good, destroying the wicked and establishing dharma.
- Before you follow anyone, check whether their birth and deeds are pure and beyond the ordinary.
- Believing and knowing are different; knowing the essence means knowing through both principle and experience.
- Liberation has four steps: salokya, samipya, sarupya and sayujya.
- An avatar never fights alone; Shri Rama had Hanuman and the vanara army, and Shivaji Maharaj had the Mavale.
- Hanuman's answer reveals all three levels: duality, qualified non-duality and non-duality.
- When you join such work and recognise the Guru, in that moment you become Kalki too.
- No Kalki will come from outside until the Kalki within you awakens.
