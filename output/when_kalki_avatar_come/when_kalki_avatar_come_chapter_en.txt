# Chapter: When Will the Kalki Avatar Come?

## What Is the Benefit of Reading the Lives of Great Souls?

Today I will explain what an avatar means. In the Nadi Granth of their time it was said that they were an avatar. Who? Shripad Shrivallabh. Now the question arises: they are gone, so how will that help you? We pray to them, we do sadhana too. Swami Vivekananda was great; what is the benefit to you? Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was great; what is the benefit to you? Eknath Maharaj was great; what is the benefit to you?

My question is only this: can you become like one of them? Only if even a part of them has entered you is their existence of some benefit to you, is it not?

Yes, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was very great, Chandragupta was very great, but you are afraid to fight your own brother. You cannot even stand up in court in a property case. So what is the point of reading the life of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj? You cannot even do two malas of sadhana, so what is the point of reading the lives of great souls and saints? Should that change not come within you? And should its result not be seen now, at this very moment?

You say, "No, they are great, everything about them is great, I just do my puja and prayers." If I am a part of them, should I not get the result? This much I want: exactly the same, or a similar result.

## The Nadi Granth and the Wait for Kalki

So the question comes up that their Nadi Granth mentioned they would do some avatar's work. In exactly the same way, when my Nadi Shastra is read, it says exactly the same thing: you will do some great work, you will change the country. Some such description does come. I am telling you the real truth, just as I heard it.

You must have heard two verses of the Bhagavad Gita. When we speak of the turning of an era, of some great work, we wait for some Kalki to come. We will not do anything ourselves; some Kalki should come, someone should come, then things will be good for us. This was never written in the Bhagavad Gita. This is the famous verse:

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> **abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham**
> "O Bharata, whenever dharma declines and adharma rises, then I bring forth My form."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

You have heard this verse. So now I will explain who an avatar is.

## "You Too Are an Avatar"

Little children used to come to us for Saraswati Diksha, and their parents would ask me, "Guruji, are you an avatar?" They had heard it in the Nadi, you see. So I said, "Why only me? You too are an avatar."

> "We all are avatars."

First read its definition in the Gita, and then you will understand who an avatar is, what it is and why it comes. The only difference between you and me is that I have come to know who I am. The one who knows the Supreme, the one who knows the Self, is called an avatar. The same Brahman is within you too. It is only a matter of recognising it. You only have to recognise who you are. So read this definition right away.

## The First Condition: Born in a Time of Declining Dharma

O Bharata, whenever dharma declines... So has dharma declined out there today or not? Right now, at this very moment, has dharma declined or not? It has. And the rise of adharma, that is, has negativity grown or not? It has. Do only I see this, or do you see it too? You see it too.

Then, that is, only and only under such conditions, I create My form. So My form has been created; has your form not been created? You too were born, you too have a shape, do you not? If I was born in such a time, you too were born. Agreed?

So the first condition matches: you too can be an avatar. At least the first condition is fulfilled, that under such conditions we too were born, you too were born. That is, "I appear before people in a visible form." So have we come before the world or not? I have come before the world; have you come too or not? Tell me this much.

But you become a doctor, an engineer, an architect, and I become someone who does the Guru's work, the nation's work. That is what separates an avatar from an ordinary person.

## The Second Condition: The Avatar's Work

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> **dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge**
> "To protect the good, to destroy the wrongdoers, and to establish dharma, I appear age after age."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

For the protection of good people, for the destruction of those who do sinful deeds, whether the discus has to be used, or the arrow, or a bomb has to be dropped, the one who is ready to do this work by every means, sama, dama, danda; and to establish dharma well, I appear age after age.

You too have been born, but are you doing this work? First you have to understand that only when this work is done does one become an avatar. And establishing dharma means its connection with politics will certainly come. It cannot be done without a king. In an avatar's work, the work of both the Kshatriya and the Brahmin must come in, because the entire system has to be changed.

So are you doing this right now? You are not. So by the first condition you are worthy of being an avatar, but on the second condition, since you do not do this work, you have failed.

## The Third Verse: Divine Birth and Action

So will you then never attain moksha? The Gita does not say that. You too will attain moksha. Are you getting the point? We have never even looked at the verse that follows: chapter four, verse nine. Had we read it even by mistake, we would not be stuck waiting for some Kalki avatar. Look at the next verse, at what it gives:

> **janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ**
> **tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so 'rjuna**
> "O Arjuna, one who knows My divine birth and actions in their essence does not take birth again on leaving the body, but attains Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.9)

O Arjuna, first check whether the guru you are following, the leader you are following, how they were born and the actions they are doing, are divine. Divya means divine. We do not want a superman; we want something divine, something different. Superhuman power is not enough; first check whether they have supreme divine power.

O Arjuna, My birth and actions are divine. Divine means pure, without impurity. What does being without impurity mean? A doctor, but one who does not quietly remove a patient's organ. A politician, but one who does not take bribes. That is being pure. So check: a guru, yes, but are they doing something wrong? Both their mind and their conduct should be pure.

And alaukik. What does alaukik mean? That which is not laukik. Laukik means worldly. That which is beyond the worldly is called alaukik. There must be some such divine, miraculous work of theirs, such speech, such words.

## Believing and Knowing

"Thus the person who knows in essence." Here person means the sadhak. Who knows, not who believes. There is a difference between believing and knowing.

What does it mean when I say I believe in God? Tomorrow someone will say this too is God, and you will start worshipping it too. This is called believing: I have accepted it. Someone said Ganapati is God, so you accepted Ganapati as God. And the second is knowing. Knowing means I am one hundred percent certain what God is.

Knowing in essence means knowing through theory, and along with theory, knowing through experience. Suppose you have a cube of sugar. Even if I write ten thousand pages on sugar, you still will not understand whether it is sweet or spicy. But if you taste even a little of it, you will understand what sugar is. This is called knowing in essence. You know from theory and you know from experience. When this happens, it is called knowing in essence.

But what is to be known in essence here? First check whether the birth and actions of the guru you are following, the great soul, are divine. One, are they pure? Two, are they beyond the worldly? That is, miracles must certainly be seen. Divine and beyond the worldly: their work should be of this kind.

## The Four Steps of Liberation

I said that knowing in essence means knowing through experience. And when will experience come? Will it come just by sitting at home? The scriptures describe four kinds of liberation.

The first is **salokya**. Sitting here as you are now is what is called salokya. You are sitting among spiritual people; this is the first step. You come to the guru, you study a little at home; this is called salokya.

Going one step further is called **samipya**, going near the guru. You approach the guru, you study their life, you watch them. Whether it is a leader, or your favourite film star, or whoever it may be, whoever your ideal is, you will have to go near them. To watch their life closely, to stay with them. The first step is only being a fan; the second is staying with them, going near them. After samipya comes, will knowing the essence not begin?

After going near comes **sarupya**: my form like theirs. Not the form of the body, but my nature. My qualities, my speech start becoming like theirs. This is called sarupya.

And what will happen after sarupya? **Sayujya**. Yuj means to join, to become one. To become one with the Lord, or with the guru. Not like them; I have become the very same, the same nature.

So these are the four kinds of liberation that have been described. First salokya, above it samipya, above it sarupya and above it sayujya. The first is being in a spiritual community, or, for others, being a fan of some great soul, staying in the community and silently following. The second is going near them, staying with them for some time. Doing this, in their company, what will happen? Sarupya. Your form will gradually begin to become like theirs, your speech will begin to become like theirs, your actions will begin to become like theirs. And after that you will reach their very same state: sayujya.

This very state is called knowing that guru in essence. Once you have done this, "having left the body, they do not take birth again." Who? You yourself. That is, such a person too will attain moksha. And if by chance you are following an avatar, you too will become worthy of being part of that avatar itself. "But attains Me": they reach that very same state.

## An Avatar Does Not Work Alone

Let us understand this with a small example, because there is a great deal of theory to teach.

Everyone knows Shri Ram. Sita Mata was his wife, whom Ravana carried away. This could have been his own family problem, could it not? Someone could say that his wife was taken, so he should fight himself. Then why did Hanumanji fight, and why did the rest of the Vanar army fight with him? This question does arise, does it not? Shri Ram could have done it alone, yet he took such a large army with him.

In the same way there was Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. His goal was swarajya; the whole of India should be free of the Mughals. Then why did these Mavlas fight with him? Why did Baji Prabhu fight? Are you getting what I want to say? Even if an avatar is born, they still do not work alone. They work with a very vast team.

That vast team, you see, is the sadhaks. They are the ones who come near, into samipya. And can a person who has come into samipya know the guru in essence or not? Yes. So to know in essence, you have to go near that particular guru. When you do this, gradually you too reach that same avatar state.

## Hanumanji's Answer: Dvaita, Vishishtadvaita and Advaita

We spoke of Shri Ram and Hanuman, didn't we? They were going to fight Ravana, and on the way Shri Ram asked Hanumanji. Hanuman is an avatar of Shiva. Shri Ram asked Hanumanji only this one question, "What is the relation between you and me? According to you, what is our relation?"

Hanumanji answered at once, without a thought, "If I see through body-awareness, that is, I am one body and you are one body, which is called dvaita, dvaita meaning you and I are different, if I see through that awareness of duality, then you are my master and I am your servant."

This is clear, is it not? What was Shri Ram? A king. And Hanumanji says, I am your servant. But that does not mean I will be your slave all my life. He was completely clear about his identity. The Swadhisthana chakra means knowing your identity very well. And "aham brahmasmi": would Shivaji not know that he himself is Shiva? Shivaji certainly knows, so he immediately says:

"But I am not merely your slave. At the level of body-awareness I said you are the master and I am the servant. But if I speak at the level of the mind and intellect, which is called vishishtadvaita or dvaitadvaita, when I go into that state, I understand this much: you may be the whole." If you are the totality, everything, the infinite...

A king is a king because of his kingdom. The army, the people, the ministers, all must be there; only then does someone become a king. So I am a part of that. If the people leave one by one, the king does not remain either. A lone soldier can do nothing. You sit holding all of us together, but if we leave one by one, then you too are nothing.

So Hanumanji says, "When I think from the level of the intellect, from vishishtadvaita, this much is certain: if you are the whole, then I am definitely a part of it." If you are the ocean, then what am I? A drop, for sure. The drop says that within you lives all of marine life, plants live, fish live; a whole ship can sail on you, such is your power. None of this is within me; there is nothing in a single drop. But if each such drop leaves, you no longer remain the ocean. Agreed?

A trade union leader has this knowledge. He understands this vishishtadvaita state. He tells the owner, "You are the master, I am the servant, but don't overdo the drama. At the right time I can call a strike and take all my staff out." But Hanumanji was no union leader to have the Vanar army go on strike.

He says, "But when I think at a level higher than this, when I speak from the essence of the Self, which is the state of advaita, then I am two hundred percent certain, of one firm view, that the two of us are one. You are the same Lord, I am the very same Lord. If you are an avatar, then I too am an avatar. So I also know that you cannot mistreat me. I have recognised you very well. You are Shri Ram, that is, the avatar of Vishnu, and I too am the avatar of Shiva."

## The Kalki Within

This is exactly what those three verses say; this is sayujya. When you go into this state... there will be one person who has come to do an avatar's work. Their work will be great too; they will look after politics, the establishing of dharma, "I appear age after age"; let them do all of that. I only have to recognise. I have to go to such a person.

So what did I just say? Let such a person do work like Shri Ram's, but they cannot do it alone; this much you have understood. That is why recognition is necessary. What is to be seen? What am I doing at that time? Can I contribute something to helping the good people, defeating the bad people, establishing dharma? Can I do it myself? If I cannot do it myself, then there will be such a person at some point; do I at least join them?

And if I join their work, that is called the nation's work, the Guru's work. When a person joins in this, and recognises their guru, identifies the guru, in that very moment you too become that same Kalki avatar. This is the real definition. It is a whole army; it happens together. And the moksha is yours.

We keep waiting: when will they come, when will they come? Remember, an avatar descends only when all of creation, the people of all of nature, say that we need some such leader. Do you know when a leader or a guru arises? Only when a disciple stands up with a question does the guru come.

In the same way, when will change come? If you have not even thought of change, let things go on as they are, let corruption go on, then no avatar will descend. Understood? First the public must come forward; they too must want change.

> "Until the Kalki within you awakens, no Kalki from outside is going to come."

Is this much clearly understood or not?

## The Spirit of Service: Hanuman and the Mavlas

This is what that verse was saying: recognise whether there is such a person in your life. Go fully near them and follow their work. Being a servant like Hanumanji will do, working like a vanar will do, and still you can go as far as liberation, if you make yourself one with that person, the way Hanumanji recognised. Whether every vanar recognised, I do not know, but did Hanumanji recognise or not? Become like that.

Then do not say... those Mavlas of the Maratha empire never said, "Shivaji, we too fought that same battle of yours, we too gave our lives, so we too will sit on the throne." They did not say that. "That is your work; my work was to fight, and I have fought."

In this state, either become yourself such a powerful, great guru who gives Shaktipat Diksha, or throw yourself into such work. Moksha will come through both. Is this very clear?
