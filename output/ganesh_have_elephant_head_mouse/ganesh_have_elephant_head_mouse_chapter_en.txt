# Chapter: How Did Ganapati Become an Elephant? The Secret of Symbols

## A Question We Never Asked

How did Ganapati become an elephant? Do you know the meaning of the verses of the Ganesha Atharvashirsha? All of this has been given to us as symbols. Until we understand these symbols, our devotion will remain blind faith.

First tell me, how many of you have seen an elephant that walks like a human being? From here itself you will begin to understand whether this is a symbol or reality. This form was given for a reason, but we never understood that reason. And how many elephants sit on a mouse?

Suppose your son asks you, "Mother, Father, how did Ganapati become an elephant? And how does he sit on a mouse? Why doesn't the mouse get crushed?" It is surprising that this question has not come to your mind until now. All of this is taught in detail in the regular shivir.

## The Hands of the Deities and What They Hold

You have seen the Goddess with six or eight hands; the Goddess even has up to eighteen hands. Vishnu holds a conch in one hand, a discus in another, then a mace. Ganapati holds the noose and the goad. He is described like this:

> **ekadantaṁ caturhastaṁ pāśamaṅkuśadhāriṇam |**
> **radaṁ ca varadaṁ hastairbibhrāṇaṁ mūṣakadhvajam ||**
> "One-tusked, four-handed, bearing the noose and the goad, holding the tusk and the boon-giving gesture in his hands, with the mouse on his banner."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha)

In this way, every deity holds something in their hands. Saraswati holds the veena. Have you ever, even by mistake, asked what this could mean? Some people say that if the gods looked like us, people would not worship them. But it is not exactly like that. These symbols have been given to us, and until we understand them there will be a big problem; until then there will only be blind faith.

## The Idol Is a Medium

We take some stone, some picture, and believe that it will bring us something. Yet nothing comes from outside. The idol outside is a medium, to awaken the Supreme within you.

> "The idol outside is a medium, to awaken the Supreme within you."

How many of you have ever received an answer from an idol and had your work done? Now and then we hear that an idol is drinking milk. Some twenty or twenty-five years ago such a miracle did happen. But it is not a regular thing.

## Ganesha Is the Form of Omkara

Read the Upanishads sometime. Ganesha is also called the form of Omkara, do you know that? So what is this Om? Is it only a word? We close our eyes and chant Om. When it is said that Ganesha is Omkara, it is necessary to understand what this Om truly is.

## Where Is the Elephant in the Atharvashirsha?

Many people recite the Ganesha Atharvashirsha, and do sahasravartan (a thousand recitations) of it. But do they know its meaning? It says:

> **tvaṁ brahmā tvaṁ viṣṇustvaṁ rudrastvamindrastvamagnistvaṁ vāyustvaṁ sūryaḥ...**
> "You are Brahma, you are Vishnu, you are Rudra, Indra, Agni, Vayu and Surya."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha)

You are all of these. But "you are an elephant" is described nowhere. So does the elephant form have some connection with this or not? First reflect on this.

## The Connection Between the Form and the Formless

We teach the worship of the sakara form, the form in the idol, and also the worship of the nirakara, the formless. But both are connected, and this connection between form and formless has to be understood.

Tell me one thing: is your body limited right now or not? This body is limited right now. What you see, is it limited or infinite? As far as you can see, there is a limit. As much as you can hear, there is a limit too. So all of this is finite.

Now understand a simple example. Bring any finite ruler, any finite measuring tool. Can you ever measure the infinite with it? The infinite cannot be measured with any finite ruler or measuring tool.

Ultimately it is said that God, the Supreme Principle, is formless; it is called infinite. The infinite cannot be seen. The infinite cannot be touched by a finite thing. This has to be understood first.

> "The infinite cannot be seen. The infinite cannot be touched by a finite thing."

## Why the Rishis Gave Us a Form

The real worship is meant to be of that infinite, the Supreme Principle, but we are unable to do it. That is why the rishis and sages gave us a form. A form means a shape that is limited. The limited can see the limited; the form can see the form.

So I got a symbol. I could have kept a plain stone, or a round shape. But it was given hands, feet, an elephant-like form, which means it must have been given to describe that Supreme Principle. So each of its hands must have a meaning, and the objects held in its hands must have a meaning. Every deity has a vahana, a vehicle, so every vehicle must have a meaning too.

And none of this is outside. It is about certain qualities within you. Shiva is within you, Parvati is within you, Ganesha too is within you. What this truly is, is the first thing explained in the shivir.

## Why Obstacles Despite So Much Worship?

With as many modaks as are offered here, as much durva grass, as many red flowers, no obstacle should ever have come to India. Yet our country's happiness index is among the lowest in the world, and India is called the diabetes capital. So how does all this happen? If everything here is the most sacred, if everything is going well, then why is it so?
