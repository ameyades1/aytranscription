# Chapter: Why Satsang Does Not Change Your Life

## Who Is a Good Listener

A good listener is one who listens for their own spiritual progress, not to search for faults in others. Is that much understood? The first obstacle on the path of satsang is exactly this: we look not at ourselves but at the other person.

## The Ears Became Pure, but the Heart?

The satsang ended. Someone asked, "What was taught?" The reply came, "Oh, leave all that. The event was lovely, the food arrangements were excellent, this was there, that was there."

Is this a listener? No, this is not a listener. Did they take anything in? Ask, and they will say, "Oh, they were saying something about the soul, the soul. It was nice to hear."

The ears became pure. So when will the heart become pure? And the mind? Perhaps in the next birth! Such a listener is completely useless. They only went to purify their ears.

## A List of Other People's Faults

On top of that, the one who goes to satsang sees the other person's faults first. Observe yourself and see. The ego runs that deep.

The Guru's words come: "One must not get angry, otherwise this will happen." Immediately the mind says, "Right, that was said exactly for them. I will have to tell them later. Look, it came up, write it down, write it down, write it down, they did this."

"If the child is stubborn, this is what happens." "Much work from the previous birth is still pending, lust, and all this, so there will be trouble later. Write it down, write it down. The aunty next door, this sister-in-law, her child is like that too, write it down."

## "There Was Nothing for Me"

After doing all this, the discourse ends, and at the end they say:

> "Good, nothing was said for me at all. Whatever there was, it was all said for everyone else."

"This was said for sister-in-law: don't get angry. This was said for uncle: don't lie. This was said for aunt: don't shout. This was said for my wife: stay calm. And what was said for me? Go and teach. Because the Guru says, spread the message, bring people in. I am an anuchan, now I will go and set all of them right. My problem is solved, I am very good."

Oh, when in fact that entire Vedanta was for you. It was never for the neighbour at all.

## The Journey from Nar to Narayan

A good listener, free of ego, accepts the teaching upon themselves: "Where did I go wrong?" They take in just that much, for their own **journey from nar to Narayan** (from the human to the divine).

When I set myself right, seeing faults outside will stop on its own. If I fix my own, the rest will begin to be fixed too. And if others still do not change, only then can I become an anuchan for them, or send them to the Guru.

Is that much understood?

> "As long as I do not apply it in my own life, I am not qualified for it."
