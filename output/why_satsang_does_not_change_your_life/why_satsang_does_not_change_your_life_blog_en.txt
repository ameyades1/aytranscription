# Why Satsang Does Not Change Your Life

How many satsangs have you listened to? And how many of those teachings truly entered your life? In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji holds up a mirror in which almost every seeker will recognise their own face.

## Who Is a Good Listener?

Satsang means the company of truth. But one is in the company of truth only when the listener listens in the right spirit.

Acharya Ji says it plainly: a good listener listens for their own spiritual progress, not to see faults in others. And the **first obstacle** on the path of satsang is exactly this: we leave ourselves aside and start looking at the other person.

## "The Event Was Wonderful"

The satsang ended. Someone asked, "What was taught?" The reply came, "Oh, leave that. The event was wonderful, the food arrangements were great."

Did they take anything in, any **dharan** (holding the teaching within, internalising it)? Ask, and they will say, "They were saying something about the soul, the soul. It was nice to hear."

Acharya Ji asks: the ears have become pure, but when will the heart become pure?

Acharya Ji jokes that the mind will perhaps become pure in the next birth. Such a listener only went to purify their ears, and so they are completely useless.

*What did you bring back from your last satsang: an experience, or a transformation?*

## A Notebook of Other People's Faults

Now another kind of listener. They listen attentively, but for someone else.

The Guru says, "Don't get angry, otherwise this will happen." Immediately the mind says, "That was said exactly for them! Write it down, write it down, I'll have to tell them later." When the talk turns to a stubborn child, they remember their sister-in-law's child. When it turns to lust and karma from past births, they remember the aunty next door.

Acharya Ji says, observe yourself. The **ego** runs so deep that even the Guru's words stop being a mirror and become a tool for pointing fingers at others.

## "There Was Nothing for Me"

The discourse ends and this listener says with satisfaction that it is good, nothing was said for them at all, and whatever there was, it was all said for everyone else.

For sister-in-law: don't get angry. For uncle: don't lie. For aunt: don't shout. For my wife: stay calm. And for me? "Go and teach, spread the message." I am an **anuchan**, now I will go and set everyone right.

But the truth is the reverse. That entire **Vedanta** (the self-knowledge of the Upanishads) was for you. It was never for the neighbour at all.

*The teaching that felt like it was "for someone else" today: was it really not for you?*

## The Journey from Nar to Narayan

A good listener, free of ego, applies every teaching to themselves: "Where did I go wrong?" They take in just that much, for their own journey from **nar to Narayan** (from the human to the divine).

When there is correction within, seeing faults outside stops on its own. If I fix my own, the rest will begin to be fixed too. And if others still do not change, only then can I become a guide for them or bring them to the Guru.

Acharya Ji says that as long as I do not apply it myself, I am not qualified for it.

## Key Takeaways

- A good listener listens for their own spiritual progress, not to find faults in others.
- The first obstacle of satsang is looking at others instead of yourself.
- Merely listening purifies the ears; internalising purifies the heart.
- Every word of the Guru is first for you, not for your neighbour.
- When you correct yourself, seeing faults outside stops.
- Until you live the knowledge yourself, you are not qualified to teach it to others.
