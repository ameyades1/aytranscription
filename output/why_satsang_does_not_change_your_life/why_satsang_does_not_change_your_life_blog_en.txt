# Why Satsang Does Not Change Your Life

How many satsangs have you listened to? And how much of what you heard has truly entered your life? In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji holds up a mirror in which almost every seeker will recognise their own face.

## Who Is a Good Listener?

**Satsang** (the company of truth) means being in the presence of truth. Yet that presence becomes real only when you listen with the right attitude.

Acharya Ji puts it plainly: a good listener listens for their own spiritual growth, not to find fault with others. The **first obstacle** on the path of satsang is exactly this habit of looking away from ourselves and watching someone else instead.

## "The Event Was Wonderful"

The satsang is over, and someone asks, "What did you learn?" The reply comes back: "Oh, never mind that. The event was wonderful, and the food was excellent."

Did this person take anything to heart through **dharana** (holding a teaching within and letting it settle inside you)? Ask them, and they will say, "He was talking about the Atman or something. It sounded nice."

Acharya Ji asks: your ears have been purified, but when will your heart be?

With a smile, he adds that the intellect may perhaps get purified in the next life. A listener like this went only to purify their ears, and so the whole exercise is wasted.

*What did you bring home from your last satsang: an experience, or a change?*

## A Notebook of Other People's Faults

Now consider another kind of listener. This one listens carefully, but on behalf of someone else.

The Guru says, "Do not give in to anger, or this is what will happen." At once the mind jumps in: "That was meant exactly for them! Write it down, write it down, I'll have to tell them later." When the talk turns to a child's stubbornness, they think of their sister-in-law's child. When it turns to lust and the karma of past lives, the auntie next door comes to mind.

Acharya Ji says: try turning your attention to yourself. **Ego** runs so deep that even the Guru's words stop being a mirror and become a finger to point at others.

## "There Was Nothing in It for Me"

The discourse ends, and this listener remarks with satisfaction that, thankfully, nothing was said for them. Everything was meant for everyone else.

For the sister-in-law: do not get angry. For the uncle: do not lie. For the aunt: stop shouting. For the wife: stay calm. And for me? "Go and teach. Spread the message." After all, I am an **anuchana** (a learned scholar of the Vedas), so now I will go and set everyone right.

The truth is the exact opposite. All of that **Vedanta** (the knowledge of the Self taught in the Upanishads) was meant for you. It was never meant for your neighbour.

*Was the point that seemed meant for "someone else" today really not meant for you?*

## The Journey from Human to Divine

A good listener sets ego aside and applies every word to themselves: "Where did I go wrong?" They take in what they need for their own journey of **nar se Narayan** (from the human to the Divine).

Once you begin to change within, you naturally stop hunting for faults outside. When I set myself right, everything around me begins to fall into place too. And if others still do not change, only then can I guide them, or bring them to the Guru.

Acharya Ji says that until I live a teaching myself, I have not earned the right to pass it on.

## Key Takeaways

- A good listener listens for their own spiritual growth, not to find fault with others.
- The first obstacle in satsang is watching others instead of yourself.
- Listening alone purifies the ears; holding the teaching within purifies the heart.
- Every word of the Guru is meant first for you, not for your neighbour.
- When you correct yourself, you stop seeing faults outside.
- Until you bring knowledge into your own life, you have not earned the right to teach others.
