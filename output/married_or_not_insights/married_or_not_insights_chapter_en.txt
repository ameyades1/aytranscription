# Chapter: The Radiance, Voice and Discernment of a Pativrata

## Who Is a Pativrata?

Who is called a *pativrata*, a devoted wife? She has *tej*, a radiance, within her. She has been called a form of the Goddess. It is even said that before her, a demon too would become Govinda. That is, she certainly has the power to transform someone into something like the Divine.

But if she herself is living suppressed, where is the Goddess's power in her? **Strength is life, weakness is death.** So she must also know how to speak with her own voice.

## The Power to Transform and the Form of Mahishasuramardini

There is the story of Saint Eknath Maharaj's daughter. She too had been married to the wrong man. Eknath Maharaj told her to teach her husband one verse of the Gita every day. Doing this, little by little, the man reformed. There are many such stories.

One can try to transform everything. But where one has to become divine, one must then also become Mahishasuramardini, the slayer of the demon Mahisha. You must have the capacity to bring down the other person's ego.

## We Are Not What We Think We Are

If that is not so, then Swami Vivekananda says that you are not at all what you think you are. In truth, the thought will be in your own mind that you should leave this, but you cannot leave, because of what four people will say. Then the fault is yours, not his.

There is not even the courage to leave: "What will society say? I have stayed only for Chunnu and Munnu, otherwise I would have left long ago." There is no joy in this. This is not the life of a pativrata. There is no energy in it at all.

## The Answer to a Question

Someone had asked this question. Whether they are with us today, I do not know, but many people used to ask me this. The question comes in private messages too, it comes during sadhana too. So I had said that I would answer it today.

I hope that all of you, especially the sisters, have received the answer. May God never let such a situation come into any woman's life, but this is nature; it does come into someone's life.

So tell me, is the answer right? I am asking the sisters especially: was the answer right or wrong? I know it is right; I just want to hear it from your own mouth. Does the woman within you accept it? That is what is needed.

## She Has the Power to Speak

The Indian woman has had to live suppressed for a thousand years. So she may feel, "Should I say yes or not? If I say yes, there will be talk at home." But first tell me whether this is true or not.

She has that power. She can speak. And that is her occasion to speak: "Give this up, then this will work." If she does not do this, then in Vivekananda's words, you are not at all what you say you are. Then she is not called a pativrata.

## Learn to Say "No" with Confidence

Then one has to learn a little. You will say that one has to listen to the mother-in-law a little, has to do this, has to do that; there are many such reasons. But at some point you too must learn **to say "no" with confidence**.

In the first two years of marriage an image is formed. At that time we show a false image, that we do this much and that much. Then that same image carries on for the rest of life. In the third year, if you do not come somewhere or do not do something, people say, "Now what has happened to her?"

So in the first two years itself, when something does not feel right, say "no". Keep your real image in front: "I do not like this, I like this", at least say this much. But do not say it in a harsh tone. **The voice is important.**

## Not Living by Killing One's Own Wishes

Even in marriage, a woman must never live by killing her own wishes. The Vishnu Purana gives a complete account of the householder's dharma. I am telling you a few things from it.

It says there that a woman should express herself. If she wants to do a management course, part-time study, or something else, wants to go to a class of her interest, and the mother-in-law says, "There is so much work at home; who does not understand the value of this study?", then she should state her case in a sweet voice.

Because this too is a duty. If you do not fulfill your interest, your passion, there is rebirth for that too. And if you do not fulfill your household duty, there is rebirth for that too.

So she should say, "Mother-in-law, I will do all of this. I will certainly do it after I return from class." In this way the promise "I will do it" is kept, and her interest is fulfilled too. Or the reverse: "After finishing this work, I am going there for an hour or two." She should have that much courage.

## Talking to the Husband, and Distance for a While

If she does not have this much courage, she should know how to talk to her husband. The scriptures speak of a separate family, that is, living apart for a while. Not by breaking the relationship, but she should also know how to say that she will go away for only that much time.

This is not "I will go away to my parents' home", but "I will take my husband along with me." Because here it is a case of the mother-in-law. Her heart is caught up in both: shouting at the daughter-in-law is her habit, and keeping her son under her control is there too. When both go together, this short distance works. This is the role that has been described.

I hope that whoever asked this question on behalf of women has received the answer. From the angle of karma yoga, this much is the answer given. But for this, she must have radiance, she must have sadhana.

## The Last Option: Liberation Above All

A last option has also been given, as Mirabai was told by her guru. **The path of liberation is the final aim; the husband is not the final aim.** The dharma of a pativrata is one part of her life. She has done much good, one part is complete, but liberation is not in it. Your karma should lead you towards liberation. Only if you want liberation can you be a karma yogi.

Mirabai was told that whoever comes as an obstacle on your path of liberation, whether your relatives or anyone at all, regard them as equal to crores of enemies.

> **jāke priya na rāma baidehī,**
> **tajie tāhi koṭi bairī sama, jadyapi parama sanehī.**
> "One to whom Rama and Vaidehi (Sita) are not dear should be given up as if crores of enemies, even if supremely loving."
> (Goswami Tulsidas, Vinay Patrika, Pada 174)

That is, one to whom Sita and Rama are not dear, however dear that person may be, should be given up, regarding them as equal to crores of enemies.

Whoever comes in the way of liberation, even if one gets a husband who says only this much, "You are not going to satsang tomorrow", he is equal to crores of enemies. So the last step, the last option, has been given as this: your liberation is the first priority. Then you also need to be prepared to leave this. In any case, there is no meaning in living such a life.

But keep in mind, this is the **last option**; it cannot be the first option.

## Never Tolerate Atrocity

Meanwhile, if there are atrocities, as still happen in villages today and may happen in cities too at times, such as burning the daughter-in-law with something hot, troubling her, torturing her, then it has been said that **injustice is not to be tolerated.**

If the matter is within limits, she should answer it herself immediately. If it is beyond limits, she should call her parents. After telling them, some action, strict action, should be taken.

## The Mother's Invisible Bond

The second thing, which I always say: most of the time, the person responsible for trouble in that woman's home is her own mother.

Because the bond between mother and daughter is very deep, and with the son too it is very deep. The thought that comes in the mother's mind automatically and immediately affects the daughter, the son. And the more worry and tension you think with, the faster it works.

This invisible bond of the mother is called the **silver cord**. Even when the physical cord is cut, this bond continues. That is why if the mother offers even a small prayer, it works faster for her own child, and affects the daughter very immediately. So mothers too should be responsible.

## Not Complaining to Mother About Everything

The daughter too should not complain to her mother about everything. What used to happen earlier? A letter would go. Now messages go every minute.

Whatever happens in the in-laws' home, she will tell her mother, and the mother will take her side. She will tell today's event, but she will never tell the whole history of what happened in the fifteen days before today. "I wanted to bring a red curtain, they did not let me, they told me to bring a black one. They seem to be tamasic people; I am rajasic." Anything could happen.

Keeping today's main point in mind, or perhaps having heard all the earlier things too, "this is not the only issue", the mother can form the image that things are going very badly for her daughter there. Then she will advise, "You too say this, you too do this." This is an instant reading, and from there she can damage the entire relationship.

## The Home After Kanyadaan

So there is no need to tell everything again and again either. The true pativrata says, "Now this is my home. From there I have been given away; *kanyadaan*, the gift of the daughter, has been done. I do not need to go there again and again."

Earlier, daughters did not go to their parents' home again and again, and the problems here were not told there either. The in-laws' home was called her home. Her surname and middle name have changed too. Now this has been called her real home. So she need not keep saying there what is bad here, what is bad.

## The Age of Phones and Messages

Nowadays this goes on continuously. Phone, messages, photos, videos, video calls; now problems are bound to come. According to the scriptures, calling every minute is not a good sign of a pativrata. The question arises: does she live in this home or in that home? This too is a sign that needs attention.

If you call your parents' home too much, there will be problems in your household; this is certain. And your mother will be responsible for it, because she has a good heart. It is her excessive goodness that will cause this problem. She will not be able to take a fair side, and from half-knowledge, because of attachment, wrong guidance will go.

## When Should One Make Contact?

What did I say? When there is a real problem, then you must contact them. When it goes beyond your control, then it has to be done.

Otherwise it is fine: there is some general function, come; there is some occasion, come. Ideally that much contact should be there. Or if there is some good news to share, share it. But three calls every day is not described as the sign of a pativrata. It seems she has no goal at all in that home; everything is going on here only, just because of science and technology.

## Telling the Truth Is My Duty

I am telling the truth. The sisters may not find it appropriate, but telling the scriptures is my job. **Too much contact with your parents' home leads to problems in your married life.**

Four calls a day! You went to America; who sent you? The neighbour? The government? You went yourself, didn't you; the marriage is there. Now if you feel lonely, make friends with the people there. And then a call here every day; according to the scriptures this is not a good sign. This applies even when living locally; and if she has gone to America, all the more, let her live her own life.

## The Same Holds for Sons

This also happens: the son went to America, and the mother calls him again and again, "Did you eat? What is this food?" Now I am talking not only about the daughter, but about the son too. One day he will get fed up and say, "No, Mother, I will look after myself."

This is too much. You have sent him there, so let him live independently for some time. Otherwise you should have kept him here, at home, next to you. So mothers too have to take care. **We all have to be very careful.**
