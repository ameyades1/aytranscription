# What Love Is, and What It Is Not

Whether it is Valentine's season or any other day, we all say the word "love" with great ease. But do we really know what love is?

This discourse makes a plain and challenging point: what we have called love all our lives is often not love at all.

What is the true meaning of love? Acharya Upendra Ji teaches that love is God, and its signs are compassion, fearlessness and forgiveness. What we usually call love is often attachment, control, insecurity or lust. True love flows both ways, like the heart (hridaya) itself: taking, giving, and honouring the bond for a lifetime.

## Why Did Even the Incarnations of Love Part?

It begins with a question. Those who were themselves incarnations of love still had to live apart. Why? And how did they remain whole within, even while separated?

Why is their bond never called adultery? Because, across many lifetimes, they are Lakshmi and Vishnu themselves, who came to play out their **leela** (divine play). This is not something you grasp in a day, or in a moment.

## Love Is God

The principle is simple: **love is God**, and love is the **Lakshmi tattva** (the Lakshmi principle).

Look at the marks of the **daivi sampatti** (divine qualities) spoken of in the worship of Mahalakshmi:

- **Nirhetuk daya**: compassion that needs no reason
- **Abhaya** (fearlessness): the feeling that this whole world is my own
- **Kshama** (forgiveness): holding no anger even towards someone who has done wrong

Every one of these is a sign of love. And these are the very qualities that lead us to the **heart chakra**.

## Does Love Live Only Between Husband and Wife?

We assume that love runs deepest between husband and wife. Yet the Devi Purana says that in **Kali Yuga** (the present dark age) it will be weakest there. It will be stronger outside the home, because we spend more time in contact with people outside. Acharya Ji makes clear that these are not his words; they come from Swami Vivekananda and the Purana.

Picture the scene. A quarrel has just broken out at home, and a guest arrives. Instantly the voice changes and turns sweet. Why is that sweetness missing where love ought to be?

*Do you save your gentlest voice for your own family, or for outsiders?*

Here Acharya Ji clarifies that he is not encouraging adultery. He is only showing why it happens. When two people are of opposite sexes, **kama** (sexual desire) enters as well. That is why you first need to understand love and desire clearly, each on its own.

## Marriage Does Not Bring You to God

If love is God, did you find God the moment you entered **grihastha ashrama** (the householder stage of life)? That cannot be.

If it were so, there would be no need for **vanaprastha** (the stage of withdrawal from worldly life), **sannyasa** (renunciation), **jivanmukti** (liberation while still alive), **samadhi** (complete absorption in the Divine) or **kaivalya** (absolute freedom). God is found after all of these, and only then does true love make sense.

> **bahūnāṃ janmanām ante jñānavān māṃ prapadyate**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "At the end of many births, the wise one takes refuge in Me, knowing that Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

If God, which is to say love, is found only in the final birth, what was it that we called love before then?

## What Do We Really Mean When We Say "Love"?

Acharya Ji's list is honest, and it stings a little. What we call love may be:

- **Attachment**
- A desire to control, a sense of ownership over someone (possessiveness)
- **Insecurity**
- Keeping someone tied down, forcing them, making them dependent on you, **vashikaran** (binding someone to your will)
- Or simply **lust**

Acharya Ji says that all the other relationships we call love are not love at all.

The very nature of God is love, and the Gita says:

> **īśvaraḥ sarvabhūtānāṃ hṛddeśe'rjuna tiṣṭhati**
> "O Arjuna, God dwells in the heart of every being."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.61)

When that nature awakens within you, like a mother's pure feeling for her son, even for a moment, you can call it love.

*How much attachment, fear or control is hidden in what you call love today?*

## Is One-Sided Love Really Love?

Remember the familiar film scene. The hero plucks the petals of a flower one by one: she loves me, she loves me not, she loves me... On the last petal he is so overjoyed that he jumps off the roof.

Acharya Ji says that one-sided love does not count as love at all. In his words, it is a waste of time. Love exists when the person you love returns it. It cannot belong to Shri Krishna and not to Radha. It must be shared by both, like **Sita and Shri Rama**.

The example of a country says the same. Suppose someone declares, "I love my nation," yet the nation sees him as a traitor and puts him in jail because he stole. Where is the love in that? It must flow equally from both sides.

## Hridaya: Taking, Giving and Staying the Course

The three syllables of **hridaya** (the heart) hold the whole definition of love:

1. **Hri (harati, to take)**: I run about giving to people, and people give to me as well.
2. **Da (dadati, to give; dana)**: I give something of mine to the other, and they give me something special of theirs.
3. **Ya (yam, to sustain)**: I keep honouring this bond until my last breath, even if I pause now and then along the way.

## A Mother's Love, or a Mother's Attachment?

A mother goes on loving her son no matter what. He has left home, and still she says, "The property is yours; I will give it to you alone." She will not offer it to the Guru, because "blood is thicker than water."

Acharya Ji says this has never been called love, because nothing comes back in return. It is a **mother's attachment**.

## The First Step of Shri Vidya

Until you understand the **Lakshmi tattva**, the subject of love, advanced **Shri Vidya** (a profound tradition of spiritual practice) cannot even begin. Without it, a seeker cannot tell where to love and where not to, or whether what they feel is really attachment.

## When the Nation Gives Back

When a sportsman serves his country and the country honours him with an award like the **Bharat Ratna** (India's highest civilian honour), as happened with the "God of Cricket," that is right; the love flowed both ways. A warrior who wins the **Param Vir Chakra** (India's highest award for gallantry in war) brings victory, and the nation honours him with pride. This is love.

## The True Meaning of a Devoted Wife

If a wife follows her husband everywhere while he looks elsewhere, that is not love. Nor is it **pativrata dharma** (a wife's sacred duty of devotion to her husband).

Acharya Ji explains that if a wife's love can free her husband from drinking, she is truly devoted.

If he asks for alcohol and she keeps pouring it for him, that is not devotion. His drinking may be his **prarabdha** (destiny shaped by past actions), but what she does about it is where love shows itself.

*Does your love help the other person become better, or does it simply go along with everything they want?*

## Key Takeaways

- Love is God, and love is the Lakshmi tattva; compassion, fearlessness and forgiveness are its signs.
- Entering married life does not bring you God or true love; you understand love only at the end of many lifetimes of **sadhana** (spiritual practice).
- What we call love is often attachment, control, insecurity or lust.
- One-sided love is not love; love must flow equally from both sides.
- Hridaya means taking, giving and staying the course for a lifetime.
- Blind clinging where nothing returns, like a mother's for her son, is attachment.
- A truly devoted wife's love reforms her husband instead of feeding his faults.
