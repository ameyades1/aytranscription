## 1. Summary

A Valentine's Day talk that asks what love really is, and answers that most of what we call love is not love. Acharya Ji's thread: **love is God** and love is the **Lakshmi tattva**, marked by daivi sampatti (causeless compassion, fearlessness, forgiveness); entering married life does not bring God or true love, which is understood only at the end of many births (Gita 7.19); what passes for love is usually attachment, possessiveness, insecurity, control or lust; one-sided love is not love, because love must flow both ways (illustrated by the nation, the Bharat Ratna and the Param Vir Chakra); **hridaya** is read as taking, giving and sustaining; a mother's one-way clinging is **moh**; and a true pativrata's love reforms her husband instead of feeding his drinking. The main intent is **informational / emotional**: "सच्चा प्रेम क्या है", "true meaning of love", "love vs attachment". These queries are crowded with quote sites and relationship-coach listicles; a definition rooted in the Gita and the Lakshmi tattva, with a clear love-versus-moh test, is a strong angle that only this talk offers. Strong seasonal value: refresh 4–6 weeks before 14 February together with the other Valentine's talks.

## 6. FAQ

For the page or WordPress FAQ block (no FAQPage schema; see section 8).

1. **प्रेम और मोह में क्या अंतर है? / What is the difference between love and attachment?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार प्रेम दोनों ओर से बहता है और सामने वाले को बेहतर बनाता है; जहाँ बदले में कुछ लौटता नहीं और केवल अंधा लगाव रहता है, वह मोह है। / In Acharya Ji's teaching, love flows both ways and makes the other person better; blind clinging where nothing comes back is attachment (moh).
2. **क्या एकतरफ़ा प्रेम, प्रेम है? / Is one-sided love real love?**
   नहीं। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि प्रेम तब है जब जिससे प्रेम करते हैं, उसका भी लौटकर आए, जैसे सीता और राम दोनों का। / No. Acharya Ji says love exists when the person you love returns it, as with Sita and Rama.
3. **क्या विवाह से सच्चा प्रेम मिल जाता है? / Does marriage bring true love?**
   नहीं। यदि प्रेम ही ईश्वर है, तो गृहस्थ आश्रम में प्रवेश से ही ईश्वर नहीं मिलता; गीता (७.१९) के अनुसार बहुत जन्मों के अंत में ज्ञानी "सब कुछ वासुदेव है" जानकर शरण आता है। / No. If love is God, entering married life does not bring God by itself; the Gita (7.19) says it is at the end of many births that the wise one takes refuge, knowing that Vasudeva is everything.
4. **हृदय शब्द प्रेम के बारे में क्या बताता है? / What does the word hridaya say about love?**
   आचार्य जी इसके तीन अक्षरों को लेना, देना और जीवन भर निभाना पढ़ते हैं। / Acharya Ji reads its three syllables as taking, giving and sustaining the bond for life.
5. **सच्ची पतिव्रता कौन है? / Who is a truly devoted wife?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार जिसका प्रेम पति की शराब जैसी बुराई छुड़ा सके, न कि उसे बढ़ावा दे। / In Acharya Ji's words, one whose love can free her husband from a fault such as drinking, rather than feed it.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Gita 18.61 quoted as a half verse (from the Sanskrit review, marked Major).** Only the first half is quoted, and the reference does not say so; the Hindi meaning also drops "हे अर्जुन". Recommendation: the editor marks the reference "(श्रीमद्भगवद्गीता १८.६१, पूर्वार्ध)" / "(Bhagavad Gita 18.61, first half)". No SEO text here cites 18.61.
2. **हृदय syllables (review, minor).** The first syllable is "हृ", not "ह"; and the take/give/sustain reading is the speaker's own nirukti, not the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad's (5.3.1). Recommendation: apply the review's correction; FAQ 4 and the snippet present it as "Acharya Ji reads", and "hridaya meaning" stays Low priority so the post is not sold as the standard etymology.
3. **Abhaya gloss (review, minor).** Hindi gives only the speaker's explanation; add "(निर्भयता)". The snippet uses "fearlessness / अभय", which is the literal meaning. Daivi sampatti can be referenced to Gita 16.1–3 if the editor wishes.
4. **Unnamed divine pair and "adultery" (review, minor).** The opening section never names the pair, and "कई जन्मों से / across many lifetimes" sits poorly with avatars. Recommendation: the editor names the pair the speaker meant after checking the talk. Do not use this section in titles, meta or social snippets; none of the SEO text here does.
5. **Devi Purana and Swami Vivekananda claim (review, unverified).** No passage was found saying love will be least between husband and wife in Kali Yuga. Recommendation: soften as the review suggests, or trace the source. Keep it out of the title, meta, snippet and FAQ (it is not used there).
6. **Gita 7.19 Hindi meaning (review, minor).** Should read "मेरी शरण आता है" (takes refuge in Me). FAQ 3 follows the corrected sense.
7. **गुरुजी / आचार्य जी (review, minor).** Use "आचार्य जी" throughout the Hindi; all SEO text here already does.
8. **"Shri" used for Krishna and Rama but not Radha and Sita (review, minor).** Make it consistent in the body; FAQ 2 uses "Sita and Rama" without honorifics in both languages.
9. **Mother's love called moh.** The talk calls a mother's one-way clinging "माँ का मोह", which may hurt readers searching for a mother's love. Recommendation: keep the talk's framing (it is about clinging where nothing returns, not about mothers in general) and do not turn it into a headline claim such as "a mother's love is not love". `when_love_turns_into_control` presents the mother's love without accounting as the highest state; the in-text link to it gives the balancing view.
10. **One-sided love and the pativrata example.** "One-sided love is a waste of time" and "a pativrata's love can free her husband from drinking" can be read by people in pain (unrequited love, a partner's addiction) as blame or advice. Recommendation: present them as Acharya Ji's teaching on what love is, not as counselling; do not promise that love will cure addiction, and do not imply a wife is responsible for her husband's drinking. The meta and FAQ keep to the talk's own wording; "एकतरफ़ा प्रेम" is a Medium-priority keyword for this reason.
11. **Valentine's Day framing.** The talk is a Valentine's Day special and the blog mentions the season only in its first line. Using it as a tag and seasonal keyword is fair; do not put it in the title or claim the talk endorses or criticises the festival.
