# What Love Is and What It Is Not

Whether it is Valentine's season or any other day, we all say the word "love" so easily. But do we truly know what love is?

This discourse says something very direct and challenging: what we have called love all our lives is often not love at all.

## Why Did Even the Incarnations of Love Part?

It begins with a question. Why were those who were themselves the incarnations of love separated? And how did they remain whole within despite parting?

Why is their relationship not called adultery? Because for many births they have been Lakshmi and Vishnu themselves, who came to play out a divine **leela**. This is not understood in a day, or in a moment.

## Love Is God

The principle is simple: **love is God**, and love is the **Lakshmi principle**.

Look at the signs of the **divine wealth** (divine qualities) spoken of in the worship of Mahalakshmi:

- **Causeless compassion** (nirhetuk daya): kindness without any reason
- **Fearlessness** (abhaya): the feeling that this whole world is my own
- Not becoming angry even at one who errs, **forgiveness**

These are all signs of love. And they lead us to the **heart chakra** (heart centre).

## Is Love Only Between Husband and Wife?

We think love is greatest between husband and wife. But the Devi Purana says that in Kaliyuga it will be least there. It will be more outside, because there is more time of contact outside. Guruji says clearly that this is not his word but that of Swami Vivekananda and the Purana.

Picture a scene. There has just been a quarrel at home, and a guest arrives. At once the voice changes, becomes all sweet. And where love should be, why not there?

*Is your gentlest voice for your own people, or for outsiders?*

Here Guruji makes clear that he is not promoting adultery. He is only showing why it happens. If the two are of opposite sexes, **desire** comes in too. So **love** and **desire** must first be properly understood as separate.

## Marriage Does Not Bring God

If love is God, did one find God on entering household life? That simply cannot be.

If it were so, there would be no need for **Vanaprastha**, **Sannyasa**, **liberation while living**, **samadhi** and **kaivalya**. God is found after all of these, and only then is real love understood.

> **bahūnāṃ janmanām ante jñānavān māṃ prapadyate**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "At the end of many births, the wise one, knowing 'all is Vasudeva', surrenders to Me; such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

At the end of many births the wise one, knowing that "all is Vasudeva", takes refuge in God. Such a great soul is extremely rare.

So if God, that is love, is found in the final birth, what was it that we called love before that?

## What Is It That We Call Love?

Guruji's list is honest and a little stinging. It may be:

- **Attachment** (moha)
- The wish to control, possessiveness
- **Insecurity**
- Holding someone stuck, force, making them subordinate, **casting a spell** (vashikaran)
- Or else **lust** (kama-vasana)

> "All the other relationships that you call love are not love."

The nature of God is love, and the Gita says:

> **īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṃ hṛd-deśe 'rjuna tiṣṭhati**
> "O Arjuna, the Lord dwells in the heart of all beings."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.61)

God dwells in the heart of all beings. When that nature awakens within, like a mother's pure feeling toward her son even for a moment, then it can be called love.

*How much attachment, fear or control is there in what you call love today?*

## One-Sided Love Is Not Love

Remember that film scene. The hero plucks the petals of a flower: she loves me, she loves me not, she loves me... and on the last petal he jumps from the terrace in joy.

> "One-sided love is not called love at all."

In Guruji's words, it is a waste of time. It is love when the love of the one you love also comes back. Krishna's without Radha's does not work. **Sita and Rama**, both are needed.

The example of the nation says the same. Someone says "I love the nation", but the nation puts him in jail as a traitor because he stole: where is the love in that? It must be equal from both sides.

## Hridaya: Taking, Giving and Sustaining

The three syllables of the word **hridaya** (heart) are the whole definition of love:

1. **Hri (harati)**: I run to give to people, and people give to me too.
2. **Da (dadati, giving)**: I give something of my own to the other, and they give something special of their own to me.
3. **Ya (yama)**: till my last breath, even taking small breaks, I keep sustaining this.

## A Mother's Love or a Mother's Attachment?

A mother keeps on loving her son. The son left home, yet she says, "The property is yours, I will give it only to you." No gift to the guru; "blood is thicker than water."

Guruji says this is not called love, because nothing comes back in return. It is **a mother's attachment**.

## The First Step of Shri Vidya

Until the **Lakshmi principle**, the subject of love, is understood, advanced **Shri Vidya** (a profound tradition of spiritual practice) does not even begin. The seeker cannot tell where to love and where not to, and whether this is attachment.

## When the Nation Gives Back

When a player serves the country and the country gives him an honour like the Bharat Ratna, as happened with the "God of Cricket", that was right; the love was from both sides. The soldier who wins the Param Vir Chakra brings victory, and the nation honours him. This is love.

## The True Meaning of Pativrata

The wife walks behind her husband and the husband looks elsewhere: this is not love, nor is it **pativrata dharma** (a wife's devotion).

> "If her love can make him give up liquor, then she is a pativrata."

If the husband asks for liquor and the wife keeps serving it, that is not being a pativrata. His drinking may be his **prarabdha** (destiny), but what the wife does with him, that is love.

*Does your love make the other person better, or does it simply go along with everything they do?*

## Key Takeaways

- Love is God, love is the Lakshmi principle; compassion, fearlessness and forgiveness are its signs.
- Entering household life does not bring God or true love; it is understood at the end of many births of practice.
- What we call love is often attachment, control, insecurity or lust.
- One-sided love is not love; love must be equal from both sides.
- The meaning of hridaya: taking, giving and sustaining for a lifetime.
- Blind attachment where nothing comes back in return, like a mother's toward her son, is moha.
- True pativrata love reforms the other, it does not feed their faults.
