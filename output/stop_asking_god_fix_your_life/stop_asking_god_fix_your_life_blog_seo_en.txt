# Don't Ask God to Remove Your Problems; Ask for the Strength to Solve Them

## Is a Life Without Problems Really Possible?

Look at the lives of the greatest achievers. Look at the lives of the **avatars** (divine incarnations) and the great souls. Every one of them faced crises.

That is why the prayer "Let no problem ever come my way" is itself the problem.

There is nothing wrong with asking the Lord for something. But ask how to solve the problem in front of you, and how to find your way back from **nar** (the human) to **Narayan** (the Divine).

So here is what to ask God in prayer: not a life without problems, but devotion, strength, detachment, good health, and the intelligence, strength and talent to solve the problems you face. The effort is yours, and that is what makes the achievement yours.

## What Should You Ask for in Prayer?

"O Lord, grant me devotion, grant me strength, grant me **vairagya** (detachment), grant me good health." You are free to ask for all of this.

Ask for the intelligence, strength and talent the work demands. Then say, "I will do the rest myself. Learning to do the work is my responsibility."

That is why Acharya Ji says: ask God for the ability, and we will do the rest ourselves.

## The Effort Is Yours, and So Is the Joy

Someone prays, "O Lord, let me marry that girl." But you are the one who has to speak to her kindly. You are the one who has to do everything.

You will be the one to win her heart and you will be the one to enjoy the happiness, yet you expect God to deliver it?

Should God sit the exam while you walk off with the first-rank certificate? The studying is yours to do. Ask God for the capacity to study, the power to remember, the grace of Saraswati (the goddess of learning), the skill to solve the questions and the ability to write well. On top of that, you put in the hard work, and you pass.

Otherwise, what have you really achieved?

*Are you asking God for the result today, or for the ability to reach it?*

## Three Kinds of People: Which One Are You?

Earlier we recited "oṃ namaste gaṇapataye" (salutations to Lord Ganapati). We looked at examples of great leadership, such as that of Ganapati.

Read history to learn what these great souls did and what crises they have already lived through. What does reading and hearing history give you? It lets you learn from what others have already suffered through their mistakes.

Acharya Ji explains that the best of people learn from the mistakes of others, the average learn from their own, and the lowest never improve, whatever mistakes they make.

The lowest kind never learns at all. This is **tamas** (inertia, the darkness of ignorance). That is why the finest disciple learns from the experience of others.

## Astrology: Superstition or Collected Experience?

And what is astrology, after all? It is a collection of the experiences of countless people.

It is a **kaal-ganana** (a reckoning of time): a record of what happens when the Sun, the Moon or Mars reaches a certain position. Whether or not it proves true in your case, millions of people have lived through these results. So why should I take the risk? The same could happen to me. Isn't it better to be prepared?

In a sense, astrology is statistical data. Many people have gone through a certain experience during a particular **dasha** (a planetary period in Vedic astrology), so it can give me a signal.

That does not mean I accept it and sit back with folded hands. If I want to change the outcome, I can make a new plan. But hear it only from a qualified Guru. Used this way, astrology too can become a form of **Vighnaharta** (the remover of obstacles, a name of Lord Ganesha), one more way of clearing the path.

## The One Test: Inner Strength

Acharya Ji says: do whatever increases your **atmabal** (inner strength and self-confidence).

If listening to astrology weakens your heart and mind, stop listening to it. Study the scriptures with the Guru, decide in their light whether to go ahead or not, and then take the risk with courage. After that, leave the horoscope alone.

If hearing astrology strengthens someone's resolve, they should certainly use it. If someone draws strength from doing **puja** (ritual worship) before setting out, let them do it. The one condition is that it must not become blind faith. The purpose is to give you confidence in your own plan.

## Do Rings and Remedies Work? The Law of the Catalyst

Someone says their work goes well when they wear yellow. Acharya Ji says he has no objection to this. He does not see it as blind faith; it is a **catalyst**.

Someone else says a ring brings them success. That is not forbidden either, provided your plan is ready, you have full confidence in it, and you have consulted the Guru. Once that is in place, anything extra that boosts your confidence is welcome, whether or not people call it superstition. Even the greatest achievers do this.

Of course, once you become **gunatit** (beyond the three gunas: sattva, rajas and tamas), you rise above all of it.

But it is a mistake to think "If I wear this shirt, the work will get done", "I put on the ring and it worked", or "I paid the Guru, so he will perform a remedy for me." Life does not work that way.

Acharya Ji explains that all of these are catalysts, and a catalyst never does the main work.

Think of chemistry. The main reaction is something else entirely; the catalyst only helps it move faster.

*Is any remedy in your life taking the place of your effort, or only speeding it up?*

## The Higher You Climb, the More Obstacles You Meet

**Adhi** means mental affliction and **vyadhi** means physical disease. To understand the root causes of illness and of psychosomatic disorders, it would be worth holding a **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching) on something like the science of healing.

The higher you rise, the more obstacles you will meet, because you are meant to become a **Ganadhipati** (a leader of the people, a title of Lord Ganesha). You are no longer small. Your responsibilities have grown and your vision has widened, so the evil eye will find you too.

That is why the worship of Maa Durga always begins with a recitation to **Mahakali** (the fierce, protective form of the Divine Mother). Look at Shri Vidya as well. When power, wealth and knowledge arrive, what comes first? **Protection.**

## Protection Is Part of Spiritual Practice

You will need to secure your copyrights, your patents and the like. That is protection on the physical level. Install CCTV cameras too. But a camera captures a picture; it does not catch the person.

Acharya Ji says that to carry out the work of non-violence, you need people who can use force.

That means police, an army, guards. Put all of this in place, and won't the danger gradually shrink? The chance of trouble falls before it ever arrives.

The same holds for the body. If you have taken the right medicine, built up your immunity, kept your white blood cells healthy and had regular blood tests, your army is ready. Then the risk of a crisis is low.

## Obstacles Never Arrive Suddenly

One day your blood sugar has suddenly shot up, and all at once every problem comes into view. The truth is that nothing happens suddenly. Everything builds up gradually; we just never went for regular check-ups.

One day a car's brakes suddenly fail. But they did not wear out in a day. They grew looser over time because no one maintained them.

Accidents work the same way. The rule is plain: leave early, drive slowly, arrive safely. Yet we leave late, rush and jump signals. With that habit, an accident is bound to happen one day, and it does not come about overnight.

When the traffic constable fails to catch you, you feel the thrill of having got away with it. But you haven't. The **Guru-tattva** (the Guru principle, the divine presence that guides and witnesses) has recorded everything you do. One day the consequence will suddenly surface.

The belly grows little by little, and cholesterol and fat build up, but you never notice. Then one day heart trouble strikes, and it seems as though the obstacle came out of nowhere.

Acharya Ji says that obstacles never come suddenly; they build up step by step.

You did not use your intelligence, and you did not learn from the crises of others.

*What "slowly loosening brake" in your life are you failing to notice right now?*

## Young at Heart, but Prepared

Every day someone is carried to the cremation ground. Someone from your own family has probably gone as well. Death will not come suddenly either. We are moving steadily toward old age, yet we stay unaware of it.

Even a ninety-nine-year-old feels young, and that is the problem. Acharya Ji's advice is clear: stay young at heart, but be prepared.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask God not to remove your problems, but for the intelligence, strength and talent to solve them.
- The effort is yours, and only then is the achievement yours.
- The best learn from others' mistakes, the average from their own, and the lowest never learn.
- Astrology is collected experience; hear it from a qualified Guru and plan with it as a signal.
- Do what builds your atmabal; drop what weakens your mind.
- Remedies such as rings, colours and puja are catalysts, not the main work.
- Obstacles grow as you rise, so put protection in place first.
- Obstacles never arrive suddenly; regular check-ups and maintenance are your defence.
- Stay young at heart, but be prepared.
