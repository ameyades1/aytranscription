# Loving a Picture, or Meeting God?

Have you ever asked yourself whether you truly know the God you worship? Every day we light lamps, perform aarti and bow before the murti. But if someone asked you, "Who is He? What is His real nature?", how would you answer?

In this discourse, Acharya Ji makes a point that is plain yet profound: worship performed without understanding its essence is empty. To show why, he offers two everyday examples, one about marriage and one about a map.

Here is how to know God: loving His form is not enough, because without understanding its essence that love is only infatuation. The murti and the scriptures are the map, and a living Guru is the driver who knows today's road. Take diksha from such a Guru and travel with open eyes, map in hand, to reach the original.

## Could You Marry a Photograph?

Imagine you are about to marry. Marriage means **oneness**, two people becoming one. That is why husband and wife are each called the other's **ardhanga** (half of one shared body), much like **Ardhanarishvara** (Lord Shiva in his form that is half male, half female).

Now suppose someone hands you a photo of a stranger, pulled off the internet, or perhaps a statue, and says, "This is your life partner. Live with them." You know nothing else: not their name, not where they live, not even what they really look like.

Could you ever become one with that person? Could you love them, let alone marry them? And if someone fell in love with the photograph itself, wouldn't we call it madness?

## The Journey Goes Beyond the Picture

The natural course is that, once you have seen the photo, you ask to be introduced. Who is she, what does she do, where does she live, who is in her family? Then you go to meet her. The wedding follows, a shared life begins, and from that union the next generation is born.

The photograph is only where it starts. Anyone who stops at the picture will never reach the union.

## Have We Also Stopped at the Picture?

Here Acharya Ji holds up a mirror to us. We say, "I love Lord Ganesha, I love Goddess Lakshmi, I love Hanuman." Yet we have never looked into who they are or what their true nature is. Acharya Ji calls this our foolishness: whether or not we accept it, we ought to know our own God.

Before we ask anything of Him, we should first understand who He is. Gazing at a stranger's picture produces, at most, **infatuation**. It is not love, and it is certainly not union.

*You bow before your chosen deity every day, but do you know what that form means, or only how it looks?*

## Why Did the Saints Worship Murtis?

None of this means that worshipping **sakar** (God with form) is wrong. Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped Maa Kali. The saints Tukaram and Namdev called out "Panduranga, Panduranga!" to **Panduranga** (Lord Vitthal, the form of Shri Krishna worshipped at Pandharpur).

The difference is that they understood its **marma** (inner essence). For them the murti was a symbol, the first step of the ladder. Once a seeker reaches that higher state, worshipping the murti is no longer needed at all.

But who will reveal this secret to you? Those saints have long since passed into **samadhi** (final absorption in the Divine). Only a **living Guru** can tell you.

## Why Do You Need a Living Guru? Only Someone Who Has Seen Can Describe

When someone brings you a marriage proposal, they tell you, "The young man is six feet tall, he has a good job, and I have met him myself." Only a person who has actually seen him can describe him.

In the same way, only someone who has seen the ultimate nature of God can describe it. That is why you must first go to a saint or a Guru, learn this knowledge, and receive proper **diksha** (initiation). The saints do not reject the murti. They simply tell you to look at the picture and then go on to the original.

## What Do the Scriptures Say: Who Is God's Form For?

The scriptures said this long ago:

> **cinmayasyādvitīyasya niṣkalasyāśarīriṇaḥ |**
> **sādhakānāṁ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā ||**
> "Brahman is pure consciousness, one without a second, without parts and without a body. A form for it is imagined for the benefit of seekers."
> (Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7, as commonly cited)

A **sadhaka** (spiritual seeker) is someone still refining life, still purifying themselves. A **sadhu** (a realised holy person) is someone who has mastered life, who is wholesome in every respect, and whom nothing can stain. A seeker must receive **sadhana** (spiritual practice) from a Guru who has already reached the destination.

## A Finger on the Map Will Not Take You to Kashi

A seeker cannot get there directly, so they are handed a map. Suppose you trace your finger on a map from Mumbai to **Kashi** (Varanasi, the holy city on the Ganga) and announce, "Look, I've arrived!" Is that an achievement, or madness?

Even so, the map has its use. It shows you where to turn and where not to. As Acharya Ji puts it, a map alone gets you nowhere, yet without one the road is hard.

Without a map you can wander off in any direction. Picture yourself blindfolded like **Gandhari** (the queen in the Mahabharata who bound her own eyes for life), spinning round and round, setting out to find the station. Once in a billion tries you might stumble onto it by chance, but there are endless other places to end up. Lakhs of lifetimes could slip by.

That is why blind faith will not work. You have to walk with your eyes open and the map in hand.

## The Murti Is the Map, the Guru Is the Driver

The murti is your map. But a map cannot show you where the potholes lie. If a bridge collapsed somewhere today, you might have to take a new route.

That is why you need a **living Guru**, a driver for today, who knows which signals are working now and which are not. Every journey can bring fresh obstacles, so until you become a skilled driver yourself, you will need both the driver and the map.

Your map is the **scriptures**: the Puranas, the sacred texts, the Upanishads. Your driver is the **Guru**. Together they bring you to the destination once. After that you can make the journey as often as you like, enter samadhi, remain there and worship there.

*You have the map, but do you have a driver who knows today's road?*

## Don't Bow to Miracles

Once you learn to bow in the right way, everything else follows on its own. Acharya Ji turns a popular saying on its head: do not bow to miracles; bow properly, and miracles will happen by themselves.

## The Form Is Imagined, the Meaning Is Real

The saints, Gurus and avatars who reached that state saw that we cannot see it, just as you cannot see Kashi or London from where you stand. So they drew us a map. This is what the verse calls **rupakalpana** (an imagined form).

This particular form of Lord Ganesha is not the real one, but what Lord Ganesha stands for is real. He is **nirakar** (formless) and pervades everything, and that is the truth. The real Kashi exists, and so does the Kashi drawn on the map. But the map is never the final destination.

## Key Takeaways

- Worship without understanding its essence is empty; loving only the form is infatuation, not union.
- The murti and worship of God with form are the first step; the saints worshipped knowing the essence.
- Only someone who has seen God's nature can describe it, so diksha from a living Guru is essential.
- The scriptures are the map and the Guru is the driver; a map alone is useless, but the road is hard without one.
- Leave blind faith behind and walk with your eyes open.
- Don't bow to miracles; bow properly, and miracles will follow on their own.
- The form is imagined and its meaning is real; with the map and the Guru, you reach the original.
