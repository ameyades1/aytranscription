# Loving a Photograph or Meeting God

Have you ever wondered whether you actually know the God you worship? Every day we light lamps, perform aarti, bow before the idol. But if someone asked, "Who is He, what is His real form?", what would we answer?

In this discourse Gurudev says something simple yet deep: worship done without knowing its essence is meaningless. To explain it, he gives two simple examples: one of marriage, and one of a map.

## Can You Marry a Photograph?

Suppose you are to be married. Marriage means **oneness**, two becoming one. That is why husband and wife are called each other's **ardhanga** (other half), like the form of **Ardhanarishvara**.

Now someone hands you a photograph of a stranger picked from the internet, or an idol, and says, "This is your life partner, live with this." No name, no address, not even what the person looks like.

Can you become one with it? Is love or marriage possible with it? And if someone falls in love with the photograph itself, is that not madness?

## There Is a Journey Beyond the Photograph

The natural path is that after seeing the photograph you ask for the details. Who she is, what she does, where she lives, who is in her family. Then you go to meet them. Then the marriage happens, life together begins, and from that union comes the next generation.

The photograph is only the beginning. One who stays stuck at the photograph will never have the union.

## Have We Too Stopped at the Photograph?

Here Gurudev holds up a mirror. We say, "I love Ganapati, Lakshmi ji, Hanuman ji." But who they are, what their real form is, we have neither seen nor known.

> "That is your foolishness. Believe it or not, you should know your God."

What we are asking from, that much should be understood first. What happens on seeing a stranger's photograph is at most **attraction** (infatuation). Not love, and certainly not union.

*The one you bow to every day: do you know their meaning too, or only their form?*

## Why Did the Saints Worship Idols?

This does not mean that worship of **sakara** (God with form) is wrong. Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped Kali. Tukaram and Namdev called out "Pandurang, Pandurang."

The difference was that they did it knowing its **essence**. For them the idol was a symbol, the first step. After reaching that state, idol worship is no longer needed at all.

But who will tell you this secret? Those saints have gone into samadhi. Only a **living Guru** can tell you.

## Only One Who Has Seen Can Tell

When someone brings you a marriage proposal, they tell you, "The boy is six feet tall, he has a job, I have seen him." Only one who has seen can describe.

In the same way, only one who has seen the final form of God can describe it. So you must first go to a saint or Guru, understand that knowledge, and take proper **diksha** (initiation). The saints do not reject the idol; they say, after the photograph, go to the original as well.

## A Pointer From Scripture: The Form Is for Seekers

Scripture has already said this:

> **cinmayasyādvitīyasya niṣkalasyāśarīriṇaḥ |**
> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā ||**
> "For the One who is pure consciousness, without a second, without parts and without a body, a form of Brahman is imagined for the good of the seekers."
> (Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7, popular reading)

The form of Brahman, which is conscious, without a second, formless and bodiless, has been imagined for the good of seekers.

A **sadhaka** (seeker) is one who is still mastering life, still purifying themselves. A **sadhu** is one who has mastered life, who is auspicious in every aspect, on whom nothing leaves its colour. The seeker must receive sadhana from a Guru who has himself reached the **destination**.

## Putting a Finger on the Map Does Not Take You to Kashi

The seeker cannot get there directly, so they are given a map. Suppose you put your finger on the map from Mumbai to Kashi and said, "Look, I have arrived!" Is that an achievement or madness?

Still, the map is not useless. It is what tells you where to turn and where not to.

> "Only map is also useless, but without map it's difficult."

Without a map you can wander in any direction. Blindfolded like Gandhari, spun round and round, set out to find your station, and you might arrive by coincidence once in billions, but there are infinite other places. Lakhs of lifetimes could pass.

That is why **blind faith** will not do. You have to walk with your eyes open, with the map.

## The Idol Is the Map, the Guru Is the Driver

The idol is your map. But the map does not show where the potholes are on the road. If a bridge has collapsed somewhere today, you may have to take a new route.

That is why you need a **living Guru**, today's driver, who knows which signal is working today and which is not. Each journey can bring new difficulties, so until you become a skilled driver yourself, you will need both the driver and the map.

The map is your **scriptures**: the Puranas, the texts, the Upanishads. The driver is your **Guru**. The two together take you there once. After that, travel as often as you like, go into samadhi, stay there, worship.

*There is a map in your life, but is there also a driver who knows today's road?*

## Do Not Bow to Miracles

Once you learn to bow in the right way, the rest happens by itself. Gurudev's words are:

> "Do not bow to miracles; bow properly, and miracles will happen by themselves."

## The Form Is Imagined, the Meaning Is True

The saints, Gurus and avatars who reached that state saw that we cannot see it, just as Kashi or London cannot be seen from here. So they made a map and gave it to us. This is **rupakalpana**, the imagined form.

This form of Ganapati is not real, but the meaning of Ganapati is real. It is **nirakara** (formless), pervading everything, and that is the truth. The real Kashi exists, and so does the Kashi drawn on the map. But the map is not final.

## Key Takeaways

- Worship without knowing its essence is meaningless; love of the form alone is attraction, not union.
- The idol and worship of God with form are the first step; the saints did it knowing its essence.
- Only one who has seen the form of God can describe it; so initiation from a living Guru is essential.
- Scriptures are the map and the Guru is the driver; the map alone is useless, but without it the path is hard.
- Not blind faith: walk with your eyes open.
- Do not bow to miracles; bow properly, and miracles will happen by themselves.
- The form is imagined, its meaning is true; with the map and the Guru, reach the original.
