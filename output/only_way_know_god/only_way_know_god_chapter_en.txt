# Chapter: The Map, the Guide and the Destination

## Worship Without Knowing Its Essence

Worship performed without knowing its essence has no meaning. Why? Let us understand it through an example.

Suppose you are to be married. I am speaking here of oneness, of becoming one. That is the form of Ardhanarishvara; that is why husband and wife are called each other's other half.

Now suppose you are given only a photograph from a marriage proposal, or someone brings you the idol of a girl, and says, "Just live with this photograph." Who that person is, whose photograph it is, nobody knows. Suppose I picked a photo of any random girl or boy from the internet, from someone's social media profile, gave it to you and said, "This is your husband" or "This is your wife." Would that work? Tell me that first.

No name, no address, only a photograph. Dark or fair, you do not even know. Whatever that person is, you know absolutely nothing about them. If such a photograph is placed before you, can you become one with it? Tell me honestly, can there be love, can there be a marriage with it?

And if someone does fall in love with the photograph itself, is that not madness? "I fell in love with a photograph and I was even married to the photograph": is this sentence right or wrong?

## The Path Beyond the Photograph

You have seen the photograph; now ask for the person's details. Once you have them, understand who the girl is, what she is like, what she does. Then come the questions of where she lives, who her parents are, who is in her family. Then will you go to meet them or not? Some day you will certainly go, to see where she lives.

Having found her, you will marry and bring her to your home, or if you are the girl, you will go there. Then from that union the next generation will be born. Now if you stay stuck at the photograph, will that do?

## Have We Too Stopped at the Photograph?

Now you will be made to think about this, because I have to give you knowledge. Do you not feel that all of us have stopped at the level of that photograph, and believe we are married, and married to the photograph at that?

Whose photograph it is, we do not know. What the photograph is, we do not know. We do not know the details, we do not know where they live, yet we are married! Whom do I love? I love Ganapati. Whom do I love? Lakshmi ji, Hanuman ji. Oh, but who were they, where were they, what is their real form? Never seen, never known, nothing known.

> "That is your foolishness. Believe it or not, you should know your God."

This is your foolishness. Believe it or not, you must know your God. What it is that I am asking from, that much should be understood first.

## What Did the Saints Do?

Those who attained were all saints and renunciates. Did they worship only these forms, or were these symbols for them? They had come to know the truth. Go and ask them. But who will tell you this? Only a living Guru will tell you, because those saints have gone into samadhi. Look into their life stories as well.

In fact they reach a state after which they no longer need idol worship at all. Idol worship is only the first step. Then you will understand why Ramakrishna Paramahamsa was worshipping Kali, and why our Tukaram or Namdev called out "Pandurang, Pandurang."

When did we ever say, do not worship God with form? But they did it knowing its meaning. "I am doing this," so you too should know its meaning.

## The Difference Between Attraction and Love

That is why I said, do not just keep looking at a girlfriend's photograph. If you are going to marry and you know everything about the person, all their details, then does that photograph have some meaning or not?

Search the internet for any girl's name and look at a photo. Is that real love? Is there any scope for love in it? At most there can be infatuation, just an attraction. There will be no love, and certainly no union.

If you are all with me up to here, say "yes." There is no question of "no"; I know that cannot be.

## Only One Who Has Seen Can Tell

So only one who has seen that final form can describe it, isn't it? If you set out searching yourself, will you find it? How will you describe what you have never seen?

The one who has seen a suitable girl or boy for you, a match, is the one who will describe them: "The boy is tall, six feet, he has a job, I know him, I have seen him."

In the same way, you must first go to a saint, to a Guru, and understand that knowledge: "How do you worship? First tell me how you do it." That is, you must take some proper initiation; only then does it have any meaning. The saints are not rejecting worship of God with form or of the idol. They are saying: after the photograph, go to the original as well.

## The Form Imagined for the Good of Seekers

Up to here it is fine. Now scripture has already described this:

> **cinmayasyādvitīyasya niṣkalasyāśarīriṇaḥ |**
> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā ||**
> "For the One who is pure consciousness, without a second, without parts and without a body, a form of Brahman is imagined for the good of the seekers."
> (Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7, popular reading)

That is, the form of Brahman, which is pure consciousness, without a second, without parts and without a body, has been imagined for the good of seekers.

"Sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya" means for the good of seekers. Who is a seeker, a sadhaka? One who has yet to master life, who is still on that path.

You will say a sadhu simply means a good person, that is all. But such a definition would mean that other people are not good. A sadhu is one who is good in every aspect, on whom nothing has left its colour, for whom everything is auspicious. That one is called a sadhu. A sadhu has mastered their life. And a sadhaka is one who is still purifying themselves.

Sadhana is given to the sadhaka. And the sadhaka must receive that sadhana from a Guru who has himself reached the destination.

## Putting a Finger on the Map Is Not Reaching Kashi

Seekers cannot get there directly, so they must be given some kind of map. Suppose you are given a map and told you have to go from Mumbai to Kashi. On the map you went from Mumbai to Kashi; so did you reach Kashi?

"Yes, I have arrived! Look, I even put my finger there. At least one of my fingers has reached Kashi." Is saying this some great achievement, or madness?

Yes, with the help of that map I can truly reach Kashi; where to turn, where not to turn. So is the map useless? No, the map is useful. But is only the map useful?

> "Only map is also useless, but without map it's difficult."

The map alone is useless too, but without a map the path is very hard.

## Travelling Blindfolded

If I know nothing, if there is no map at all, I could go in any direction, even south. Searching and searching for one place, lakhs of lifetimes would pass, and still there would be no guarantee of arriving.

Close your eyes, go down below the building, ask someone to spin you round and round, and then, blindfolded like Gandhari, go and show me you can reach your station. Could you ever arrive? There is a possibility, one in billions. By coincidence you might even arrive some day. But apart from that one place there are infinite other places. Only with a map, with your eyes open, can you get there.

That is why blind faith will not do.

## The Idol Is the Map, the Guru Is the Driver

This idol is your map. And there must also be a guide who shows you the way the first time. For does the map show how many potholes there are? Where on the road from Mumbai to Kashi are the potholes?

The map was made by someone who had once gone there and come back, who saw it all from the sky, from a helicopter, who plotted something from a satellite. Fine. But today a bridge on that road may have collapsed, may it not? We may have to take a new road, a detour, may we not?

So today's living Guru means today's driver. With him you must reach the destination at least once, the first time. The map is in your hands; the driver does not need it, he knows the way. He knows what the road will be like where. He will show you once which signal is working today and which is not.

Even after he has driven and shown you once, on the next trip a new signal or a new pothole may still cause trouble. So every time, until you become a perfect driver yourself, you will need a driver and a map.

> "Map is nothing but your scripture."

The map is nothing but your scriptures: your Puranas, texts, Upanishads, all of them. And the driver is your Guru. The two together take you there once. After that they say: travel as many times as you want. Go into samadhi, stay there, worship. Then bow in the right way, and miracles will happen by themselves.

## Do Not Bow to Miracles

Do you remember my first statement? Stop bowing to miracles, start bowing properly, and miracles will begin happening in your home by themselves. And this is the new statement I want to tell you:

> "Do not bow to miracles; bow properly, and miracles will happen by themselves."

## The Form Is Imagined, the Meaning Is True

So, "sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā." Brahman here represents the creator. Which creator? Those who have reached that state, the Gurus, the saints, the great ones, the avatars. What did they do? You cannot see that state. Just as you cannot see Kashi from here, cannot see London from here. So they are making a map and giving it to you. That is why it is called "rūpakalpanā," an imagined form.

So this form is imagined; it is not the real thing. This form of Ganapati is not real, but the meaning of Ganapati is real. Somewhere it is formless, pervading everything, and that is the truth.

The real Kashi, the real Chennai, the real Delhi, the real Haridwar, the real London all exist; yet they are also shown on the map. The map is not final. You have to travel with the help of the map, together with the driver; only then will something come of it.
