# Whose Choice Decides How Ganpati Comes Home?

Ganesh Chaturthi, the festival celebrating the birth of Lord Ganesha, is almost here. The moment the date appears on the calendar, what is the first thing we do? Not the singing and dancing, but booking our Ganpati, as Lord Ganesha is fondly called!

Have you ever paused to ask who decides the form of the idol you bring home?

## So Many Ganpatis, So Many Faces

Some homes welcome a Ganpati whose trunk curves to the right, others one whose trunk curves to the left. Some idols recline, while others stand.

One wears half pants, while another appears in the full dhoti and **sovale** (the traditional silk garment worn for worship) of a wealthy man. One is dressed as a politician in a long kurta, one wears the robes of a saint, and another carries a gun like a soldier in the army. Some look rich, others poor.

Each idol has its own form and its own colours. Whose choice shapes them? The sages', the shopkeeper's, or your own energy and taste?

*This year, on what basis will you choose the idol you bring home?*

## Begin Anywhere, Arrive at One Place

In the beginning, the sages probably said something like this: first see God in the form you wish to see. In the end, though, you must reach what is real.

Acharya Ji says you can start from anywhere, but you must arrive at the form the sages described.

If God is not as the scriptures describe Him, changing His form serves no purpose. We have not come here to display our artistry. The sages have already shown theirs, and our task is to follow it.

## Worship Is Not a Decorating Contest

Ganeshotsav, the ten-day Ganesh festival, is neither a decorating contest nor an art competition. What, then, is it for? It is for the journey from **nar** to **Narayan** (from the human to the Divine).

We need the description the sages gave of God, the form they saw when He revealed Himself to them. Ganpati is not a human being whom we can dress however we please. His form is a vision born of the sages' own spiritual experience.

That is why we must follow their vision, not our own imagination.

Your own idea of Ganpati is fine for the first day or the first year. From the second year on, however, the idol should take the original form handed down to us.

## The Ladder to the Formless

**Nirakar** (formless) refers to God beyond all form. That is where we must finally arrive.

The sages have seen the formless; we have not. The **sakar** (with form) image they described is therefore our ladder. Only when we worship both the form and the formless **with knowledge** can we reach that state.

What, then, do we gain by dressing Ganpati in a T-shirt and half pants, hanging a school bag on his shoulder, and then putting him in an army uniform? The scriptures describe him nothing like that.

## Why Choose Ganpati at All?

Some people shape Ganpati as Sai Baba, others in some other form entirely. The scriptures, however, describe Ganpati quite differently. If it is Sai Baba you want, why not worship Sai Baba directly?

Sai Baba may well be a form of God, and so may Dnyaneshwar, the thirteenth-century saint of Maharashtra. Nobody forbids that. But worship is not an art competition. It is something else altogether.

*Is our worship carrying us forward, or are we only showing off our creativity?*

## From Balwadi to First Grade

The first year is fine. But a child sitting in **balwadi** (kindergarten) must move up to first grade someday. At that point the child must adopt the original form: first the sages' vision, then its meaning. Only then does the real journey begin.

Acharya Ji explains that the idol is meant for the age of zero to six.

The idol is the first step of **sadhana** (spiritual practice), not the last. Every name and every form carries a symbolic meaning, and understanding it is how you move towards true worship.

## Key Takeaways

- Ganpati's form should come from the sages' description, not from the shopkeeper's or our own taste.
- You may begin with your own preference, but you must arrive at the form the sages saw.
- Ganeshotsav is not a contest of decoration or art; it is the journey from the human to the Divine.
- To reach the formless, worship the form with knowledge.
- The idol is the first step of sadhana; to move forward, understand its symbolic meaning.
