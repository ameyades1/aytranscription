# Chapter: Why Does Every Ganpati Look Different?

## Whose Choice Decides the Idol?

The idol that comes into your home: whose choice decides it? It should come from the choice of the rishis and munis. We need the very form of the Supreme that they saw. Start from anywhere, but you must reach the one whose form they described.

## What We Do at Ganesh Chaturthi

Ganesh Chaturthi comes home. What all do you do for Ganpati? Quickly recall it. First, you see on the calendar that Ganpati is coming, fifteen or twenty days later. So is the first thing singing and dancing? No, the first thing is booking the Ganpati.

Then the idol arrives. In one home comes a Ganpati with the trunk to the right, in another with the trunk to the left. One reclining, one standing. Someone's Ganpati comes in half pants, someone's in a full dhoti and sola, in a wealthy form. One comes dressed like a politician in a jabba-kurta, another in a simple form. One looks very rich, another poor. One comes in the garb of a saint, another as an army soldier holding a gun. Different forms, different colours.

But the question is: whose choice decides this form? The rishis' and munis' choice, the shopkeeper's choice, or do you pick it according to your own energy?

## Begin Anywhere, but the Destination Is One

In the beginning the rishis and munis may well have said: first see Him in the form you wish to see. But in the end you must reach what is real.

> "Start from anywhere, but you must go where the form they described is."

If God is not as He has been described, there is no point in changing the form at all. You have not come here to show off your art. They have already shown that art. You have to follow it.

## This Is Not a Decoration Competition

In the early days, for the sake of art or to fulfil your own wishes, doing this in the first year is fine. But what has been taught must be learnt, otherwise there is no meaning in it.

This is not a decoration competition. Nor is it a competition of your artistry. So what is this festival for? **To go from nara to Narayana, from man to God.** The Supreme that the rishis saw, the form they saw: it is that description you need. What will you decorate in it? He is not a human being. He is an imagined form that came from their realisation.

> "We have to go by their imagination, not by yours."

Your imagination is good for the first day, for the first year. But from the second year, the form that comes should be their imagination, the original they gave.

## The Path to the Formless

I should know that I have to go from nara to Narayana, that I have to worship the **formless (nirakar)**. They have seen the formless; I have not seen it at all. So the form they described is the one I need. I worship both, with form and without form, **with knowledge**, and only then can I reach that state.

So what is gained by dressing Ganpati in half a T-shirt and half pants, hanging a school bag on Him, and then putting Him in an army uniform? His description is not like that at all. Someone makes Ganpati in the form of Sai Baba, someone in some other form. But Ganpati's description is something else. Then why do you need Ganpati? Just worship Sai Baba directly. Where are we going astray?

Sai Baba too may be a form of the Supreme, I am not denying it. So may Dnyaneshwar. But this is not an art competition. This is something different.

## From Kindergarten to First Grade

The first year is fine. Say you are in kindergarten: some day you will go to the first grade, won't you? At that time you will take up the original form, won't you? First you adopt their imagination, then you understand what that imagination means. After that the further journey begins.

> "The idol is for the age of zero to six. That is step number one."

I will not tell lies; I am telling only what is true. Every name carries a symbolic meaning, the name Pandu too. Did people in those days see such decoration of Kali? Is this point clear?
