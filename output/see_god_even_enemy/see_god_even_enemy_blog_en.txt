# How to See God Even in an Enemy

## Is the Villain of Your Story Really a Stranger?

Every one of us meets some "villain" in life. Someone who hurts us, opposes us, wants to bring us down.

But this discourse says something startling: the villain who appeared before you in your story, you wrote his script too, and you are the one who brought him in.

> "Until you see with sākṣī bhāva, you will not see God."

**Sākṣī bhāva** (witness consciousness) means watching life's events like a spectator, without getting entangled in them from within.

## Eighty Percent Cancer and Twenty Percent Hope

Consider an example. Someone's body has cancer that has spread eighty percent, beyond even the fourth stage. Only twenty percent of the body is still healthy.

How many patients say, "Doctor, leave it, let me die"? Very few. Most say, "Do chemotherapy even now, do something." Even with only twenty percent healthy, they hope the remaining eighty will heal.

But where does this hope go when we look at the world? There everything looks bad to us; we see only **enmity** everywhere.

*Have you decided about someone that they can never change?*

## Fight, But Without Enmity

This teaching does not teach cowardice. If someone attacks, even comes to kill, the answer is clear:

If I must die, I will die by my **prārabdha** (the fruit of past karma). If I must fight, I will fight by doing the duty of fighting. But there is no **vair bhāva** (hatred, enmity) in it.

## Actor Versus Character

In a film there is a hero and a villain. Both are acting. But as long as you stay lost inside the character, there will be trouble, there will be **rebirth**.

The truth is that I was an actor. Sometimes the villain's role came, sometimes the hero's. Sometimes I laughed, sometimes I cried. I kept doing what had to be done.

Without witness consciousness we see not the actor in the villain, only the character. We ask, "Why is he doing this?" when he is acting at some director's signal, speaking only as much as a script written by someone else.

> "The villain who appeared before you in your story, you wrote his script too, and you are the one who brought him in."

## Sarvatra Bhagavat Buddhi: When God Is Seen in All

**Sarvatra bhagavat buddhi** means seeing God everywhere, in every person. Once this happens, however great the crime the other person commits, enmity does not arise.

If we have to fight, we will fight, but the way a mother strikes her son. Behind that blow too lives this feeling:

> **sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ sarve santu nirāmayāḥ**
> "May all be happy, may all be free from illness."
> (Shanti Mantra)

## Valya Can Become Valmiki

This is where the famous saying comes in: hate the sin, not the sinner. Hate the politics, not the politician.

Because **Valya can become Valmiki**. The robber Valya himself later became Maharshi Valmiki.

If yesterday's drunkard husband reforms today, what is the problem? If yesterday's corrupt politician says today, "Now I will run Ram Rajya," what is the problem? Everything is possible, because within him too is the same **Brahman** (supreme consciousness).

## It Is Not His Potential That Ends, but Your Vision

When we say, "Nothing can come of this person," it does not mean that person's potential has ended.

It means that **the Brahman-feeling within me has ended.** I have begun to feel that there is no Brahman within him.

> "I alone am the author of that story."

*What you have declared "impossible": is it his limit, or the limit of your vision?*

## The Example of the Dream

Who sees the dream? I do. So who gives power to the characters of the dream? I do. Their learning, their intellect, all their actions, all of it comes from me.

If only this much is understood, even the enemy is no longer a stranger.

## Faith: They Saw, So We Will See Too

The Varkaris walk chanting "Vitthala, Vitthala." Why? Because Tukoba saw Pandurang, Namdev saw him, Dnyanoba saw him, so we will see him too.

This is called **shraddha** (faith).

## Key Takeaways

- Hate the sin, not the sinner; there may be conflict, but no enmity.
- Without witness consciousness God is not seen; we see the character, not the actor.
- You yourself wrote the script of your story's villain and cast him.
- Every person can change, because Brahman is within them; Valya can become Valmiki.
- Saying "nothing can come of this person" is a sign that you have lost your own Brahman-feeling.
- Faith means: the saints saw, so we will see too.
