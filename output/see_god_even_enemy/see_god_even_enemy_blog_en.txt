# How to See God Even in Your Enemy

## Is the Villain of Your Story Really a Stranger?

Every life has its "villain": someone who hurts us, stands against us, or wants to see us fall.

This discourse makes a startling claim. The villain who stepped into your story came from a script you wrote yourself, and you are the one who brought him onto the stage.

Acharya Ji says that until we learn to look on as a witness, we will not see God.

**Sakshi bhava** (witness consciousness) means watching the events of your life the way a spectator watches a play, without getting tangled up in them from within.

## Eighty Percent Cancer, Twenty Percent Hope

Consider an example. Cancer has spread through eighty percent of someone's body, past even the fourth stage. Only twenty percent of the body remains healthy.

How many patients say, "Doctor, let it be, I'll just die"? Very few. Most say, "Try chemotherapy anyway. Do something." With only twenty percent of the body still sound, they keep hoping the other eighty will heal too.

Yet where does that hope go when we look at the world? There we see nothing but what is wrong, and **shatru bhava** (the attitude of seeing an enemy) everywhere we turn.

*Have you decided that someone in your life can never change?*

## Fight, but Without Hatred

This teaching does not ask you to be a coward. If someone attacks you, even sets out to kill you, the response is clear.

If I must die, I will die by my own **prarabdha** (the destiny shaped by past actions). If I must fight, I will fight because it is my duty. But I will not carry **vair bhava** (hostility, ill will) into that fight.

## The Actor and the Role

A film has a hero and a villain, and both are acting. As long as you stay lost inside the role, the trouble continues, and so does rebirth.

The truth is that I was an actor. Sometimes I was cast as the villain, sometimes as the hero. I laughed at times and wept at others, and I kept doing what the part required.

Without the witness's view, we see only the character in the villain, never the actor. We ask, "Why is he doing this to me?" In fact he is following a director's cues and speaking exactly the lines of a script someone else has written.

Acharya Ji reminds us that we wrote that script ourselves, and we are the ones who brought that villain before us.

## Sarvatra Bhagavat Buddhi: When You See God in Everyone

**Sarvatra bhagavat buddhi** (the vision of God everywhere) means seeing the Divine in every place and every person. Once this vision takes hold, hostility does not arise, however grave a wrong the other person commits.

If you have to fight, you will fight, but the way a mother strikes her son. Even behind that blow lies one wish:

> **sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ**
> **sarve santu nirāmayāḥ**
> "May all be happy, may all be free from illness."
> (Shanti Mantra)

## Valya Can Become Valmiki

This is where the well-known saying comes from: hate the sin, not the sinner. Hate the corruption in politics, not the politician.

Valya can become Valmiki. The bandit Valya later became Maharshi Valmiki, the sage who composed the Ramayana.

If the husband who drank yesterday reforms today, where is the problem? If yesterday's corrupt politician declares today that he will now govern by **Ram Rajya** (the ideal, righteous rule of Shri Rama), where is the problem? Anything is possible, because the same **Brahman** (the supreme consciousness) lives within him too.

## Your Vision Runs Out, Not Their Potential

When we say, "Nothing can be done about this person," it does not mean their potential has come to an end.

It means that **my own sense of Brahman within them has faded.** I have begun to believe that Brahman does not dwell in them.

Acharya Ji says we must remember that we ourselves are the authors of that story.

*When you call something "impossible," is that their limit or the limit of your vision?*

## The Parable of the Dream

Who sees the dream? I do. So who gives the characters in the dream their power? I do. Their learning, their intelligence and everything they do all come from me.

Once this sinks in, even an enemy stops being a stranger.

## Shraddha: They Saw, So We Will See Too

The **Warkaris** (pilgrims of Maharashtra's devotional tradition who walk to Pandharpur) chant "Vitthala, Vitthala" as they walk. Why? Because Tukoba saw Panduranga, the Lord of Pandharpur. Namdev saw Him, and Dnyanoba saw Him too. These saints saw Him, so we will see Him as well.

This is called **shraddha** (faith).

## Key Takeaways

- Hate the sin, not the sinner; conflict may come, but hostility need not.
- Without witness consciousness you cannot see God; you see the role, not the actor.
- You wrote the villain's script in your story and chose him yourself.
- Every person can change, because Brahman dwells in them; Valya can become Valmiki.
- Saying "nothing can be done about this person" shows that your own sense of Brahman has faded.
- Faith means trusting that what the saints saw, we will see too.
