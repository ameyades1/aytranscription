# Chapter: God Even in the Enemy

## Hate the Sin, Not the Sinner

Hate the sin, not the sinner. There may be conflict, but there is no enmity in it. The villain who appeared before you in your story, you wrote his script too, and you are the one who brought him in. I alone am the author of that story.

> "Until you see with sākṣī bhāva, you will not see God."

## The Cancer Patient and Hope

Suppose there is cancer in your body. The cancer has spread eighty percent, meaning it has gone beyond even the fourth stage. Your body is already in bad shape, isn't it? Only twenty percent remains healthy.

So how many patients say, "Doctor, eighty percent is already gone, what is the use of saving the remaining twenty? Leave it, let me die"? Rather, how many say, "Do chemotherapy even now. O God, save me somehow. Doctor, do something!" Even with only twenty percent healthy, the hope remains in their mind that the remaining eighty will also heal.

Yet in the world everything looks bad to us; all around we see only enmity.

## Fighting, but Without Enmity

If another religion attacks me, even comes to kill me, so be it. If I must die, I will die by my prārabdha. If I must fight, I will fight by fulfilling the duty of fighting. But there is no enmity in it.

## The Actor and the Character

I am explaining this with so many examples. Among the actors in a film there is a villain and there is a hero, and both are acting. As long as you stay lost inside the character, there will be trouble, there will be rebirth.

The truth is that I was an actor, quietly. Sometimes the villain's role came, sometimes the hero's. I was doing what had to be done; sometimes laughing, sometimes crying.

Until you see with sākṣī bhāva, you will not see God. Until then, in the enemy of the film, the villain, you will not see the actor, only the character. Why is he doing this? He is doing it at some director's signal. His script was written by someone else, and he was speaking only that much.

> "The villain who appeared before you in your story, you wrote his script too, and you are the one who brought him in."

You are also the one who cast him and placed him before you. Only this much needed to be understood.

## Sarvatra Bhagavat Buddhi

What happens when sarvatra bhagavat buddhi arises, that is, when God is seen in everything? It is said that however great the crime the other commits, enmity still does not arise. Yes, if we must fight, we will fight, but not out of enmity. It will be the way a mother strikes her son. Even in that blow only his good is wished, with this feeling:

> **sarve bhavantu sukhinaḥ sarve santu nirāmayāḥ**
> "May all be happy, may all be free from illness."
> (Shanti Mantra)

With this feeling, he too is set right.

## Valya Becomes Valmiki

After this comes the saying: hate the sin, not the sinner. Hate the politics, not the politician. Because Valya can become Valmiki. This potential is in every person.

Until yesterday that man was bad; today he suddenly reformed, so what is your problem? If yesterday's drunkard husband has become good today, what is the problem? Until yesterday that politician was bad; suddenly something awoke in him and after his coronation he says, "Now I will run Ram Rajya," so what is the problem? He changed, and so everything is possible. This possibility exists because within him is that same Brahman.

## It Is Not His Potential but My Vision That Ends

As long as this stays in my understanding, love can arise. When the belief comes, "Nothing can come of him, his potential is finished," it does not mean his potential has ended. It means the Brahman-feeling within me has ended, that I now feel there is no Brahman within him.

> "I alone am the author of that story."

Because I create everything. I alone am the scriptwriter. I am the one who cast him in that role.

## The Example of the Dream

Take the example of a dream; do you understand now or not? I alone see the dream. So who gives power to the characters of the dream? I do. Their learning, their intellect, whatever actions they perform, all of it I alone do, don't I? Only this much needs to be understood.

## Shraddha

The Varkaris go to Pandharpur chanting "Vitthala, Vitthala." Why do they go? Because Tukoba saw Pandurang, Namdev saw him, our Dnyanoba saw him, so we will see him too. This is called shraddha.
