# From Illness to Liberation: The Four Steps of the Spiritual Journey

## Can a Healthy Mind Live in a Sick Body?

People say that a healthy mind lives in a healthy body. If the body is unwell, the mind cannot stay well either.

The reverse is just as true. If the mind is unwell, the body will not stay well for long.

The scriptures say that we have descended, step by step, from the Atman (the Self) all the way down to the body. Now we have to nourish this very body and, slowly and repeatedly, learn to step out of it. One thing is clear: when the time comes for **samadhi** (deep meditative absorption), **a sick body will not do.**

## Four Steps on the Path of Practice

Acharya Ji lays out the entire journey as a simple sequence: from illness to health, from health to enjoyment, from enjoyment to siddhi, and from siddhi to liberation.

1. **From illness to health:** first, the body and mind become healthy.
2. **From health to enjoyment:** you truly experience what life has to offer.
3. **From enjoyment to siddhi:** you grow toward strength, capability and vastness.
4. **From siddhi to liberation:** **moksha** (liberation), which lies even beyond spiritual powers.

The first step matters most. Nobody attains moksha while lying sick.

## Say "I Am Not the Body" While You Are in Pain

The feeling "I am not this body; this body is mine" is said to be enough for liberation. It sounds very easy.

Acharya Ji, however, puts a blunt question to us. Break even a fingernail, and then, in the middle of that pain, try saying, "I am not this body. I only came here to play."

And that is just a fingernail. What happens when cancer spreads through the stomach, the lungs or the liver, when every moment brings pain, or when a heart attack strikes? At such times a person becomes completely bound to the body.

*Does my belief that "I am not the body" hold only when I am comfortable, or does it stand firm in pain as well?*

## Only the Healthy Can Truly Enjoy Life

Think about it: what pleasures can a sick person enjoy?

Someone struggles their whole life so that one day they can eat fine food. Then diabetes arrives, and the doctor forbids sweets. A little later salt goes too, because now the heart has started giving trouble.

They worked hard to buy a beautiful sofa, and now their spine is the problem. The doctor tells them to sleep on a flat, firm surface, not on the sofa. They own large houses and bungalows, yet they cannot move around and enjoy them. As Acharya Ji says, if we are not healthy, enjoyment slips out of reach.

## When the Mind Itself Is Sick

The body is only half the story. A person may own a huge bungalow and still be unable to sit in it at peace, because the mind is full of afflictions and anguish.

Has that person really enjoyed the bungalow? No. Everything needed for enjoyment is there, yet the real enjoyment is zero. Until both body and mind are healthy, pleasure never becomes real.

*Am I truly able to enjoy what I already have?*

## After Enjoyment, What? Become Vast

Suppose you have become someone who has tasted all of life's pleasures. Is that the final stop?

Acharya Ji says that you should no longer live as an ordinary person. Understand instead that your true nature is infinite, so infinite powers lie within you.

First, though, you have to lay the foundation: **a pure heart, a clear mind, a steady mind and a vast mind.** Only then should you think of becoming great.

If you want to open a hospital, why stop at one small clinic? Why not a whole chain? If you want to give discourses, why speak to just four people? Reach the whole world. Thinking big is our very nature.

## What Are Siddhis?

A **siddhi** (a special spiritual power) can take a physical form, where you become strong and powerful, or a spiritual one.

We sing of Hanuman:

> **aṣṭa siddhi nava nidhi ke dātā**
> **asa bara dīna jānakī mātā**
> "He is the giver of the eight siddhis and the nine treasures; Mother Janaki (Sita) granted him this boon."
> (Hanuman Chalisa, Chaupai 31)

Every form of God performed some miracle or other, which shows that they held these powers.

The siddhis are many: **vak siddhi** (the power by which one's words come true), **sankalpa siddhi** (the power by which one's resolve comes true), **anima** (becoming tiny), **laghima** (becoming weightless), **garima** (becoming large or heavy), **parakaya pravesh** (entering another body), and flying through the air, which we call levitation.

## Why Do You Need Strength?

Even so, only a healthy person can become powerful. Picture a leader with no strength and no voice, who sits around taking medicine every day. Would that work?

A spiritual person, too, needs more than discourses. If someone casts black magic on them, they need protection, don't they?

Our ancient sages carried no weapons. Yet people were afraid to face them, because a single curse from them carried real force.

So become strong, whether in body, in spirit, or at least in intellect, so that nobody in your field can stand up against you.

## The Power to Resist: Mahakali and Bhairavi

When the power of **Mahakali** (the fierce, all-devouring form of the Divine Mother) and the power of **Bhairavi** (another of her formidable forms) come to you, others should also hold you in some awe. This is never about spreading terror. It exists so that nobody can take unfair advantage of you.

This is **pratikar shakti** (the power to resist). Become a master in your own field, and become truly powerful.

## A Warning: The Misuse of Siddhis

Once siddhis arrive, people start using them to do all kinds of things for others. Someone who gains the power of healing often sets up shop with it.

Because of this **vaishya bhava** (a merchant's mindset), the illnesses of the people they treat come back to rest on them, and they fall sick once more. Such a fall is unacceptable, because they misused their powers.

## Liberation Lies Beyond Siddhis

Liberation stands higher than any siddhi, so you must first reach the state of moksha. Use your powers right away, and they will bring about your downfall.

The scriptures compare siddhis to excrement, something to be discarded.

Once a seeker becomes **jivanmukta** (liberated while still alive), however, they can use these powers rightly. Shri Krishna, Shri Rama, and every Guru and avatar used siddhis. Sant Dnyaneshwar Mauli, the thirteenth-century saint of Maharashtra, made a wall move through the air.

## Why Do Great Gurus Seem to Fall Ill?

After liberation, energy flows back and forth between the Guru and others. That is why even a liberated Guru can fall ill. The illness, however, is not their own; it belongs to others.

This is why we see Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Ramana Maharshi with cancer, and Swami Vivekananda with diabetes. After attaining liberation, they took the karma of others upon themselves. Such masters attain moksha even in this state of illness. An ordinary seeker cannot.

## The Halahala Poison: The Guru Is Shiva

Taking on people's karma after liberation is drinking the **halahala** (the poison Lord Shiva drank to save the world). Only then do you become Shiva. Acharya Ji explains that the jivanmukta Guru is Shiva, and Shiva is the Guru.

If the Guru can expel the illnesses that come, well and good. When there are too many, they must be endured according to karma and **prarabdha** (the fruit of past deeds that must be lived out in this life).

## How an Ordinary Person and a Sadguru Face Illness

The moment illness strikes, an ordinary person rushes about: "I need medicine, it hurts, doctor, please do something!" One test follows another.

A **Sadguru** (a true, realised Guru) does not refuse medicine. But they know whose burden brought this illness. They took this poison consciously, so they do not weep over it. If medicine helps, good; if it does not, that is fine too. They understand that this is prarabdha, and they know how long it will last.

*When pain enters my life, whose response does mine resemble?*

## Key Takeaways

- A healthy mind lives in a healthy body, and a healthy mind keeps the body healthy.
- The spiritual path climbs four steps: from illness to health, health to enjoyment, enjoyment to siddhi, and siddhi to liberation.
- Nobody attains liberation while sick; in pain, "I am not the body" does not hold.
- Without a healthy body and mind, real enjoyment is zero, however much you own.
- Build a pure, clear, steady and vast mind first, then strive to become great and powerful.
- Siddhis give protection and the power to resist, but using them before liberation leads to a fall.
- The jivanmukta Guru drinks the halahala of others and bears illness calmly as prarabdha.
