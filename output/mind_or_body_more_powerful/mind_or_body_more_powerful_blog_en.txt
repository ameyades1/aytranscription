# The Pen or the Sword: Which Comes First?

Have you ever wondered why our tradition places **Kali worship** first of all? In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji of the Antar Yog Foundation offers an answer that is both simple and startling: first the body, then the intellect.

## The First Answer to "Who Am I?"

Mahakali is the goddess of self-defence and of **sharirabodh** (awareness of the body).

Be honest for a moment. When someone asks, "Who am I?", what is the first answer that comes to mind? My name is such-and-such, I am dark or fair, tall or short.

Acharya Ji points out that the body is the first thing we see.

The Atman (the Self) comes later; the body is what we notice first. That is why Mahakali comes first.

*When you describe who you are, what picture comes to your mind first?*

## Is the Pen Really Mightier Than the Sword?

The British left us the saying, "The pen is mightier than the sword." Acharya Ji suggests that you open your **Upanishads** and see what they say.

According to the Upanishads, if you have to choose, the body must first be strong and well nourished. The intellect comes after.

Why? Imagine someone with a brilliant mind who becomes a scientist and discovers a new formula. Then a thug with no brains to speak of walks up and holds a gun to his head. Or you write a poem, and someone else steals it and walks away with it. It all happens in an instant.

An intellectual cannot get by on intellect alone. He needs strength too, and not merely physical strength, but **strength of every kind**.

## Football, Fasting and Vedanta

"A healthy mind lives in a healthy body." Swami Vivekananda said that you should play football first, and then you will understand Vedanta. The Chandogya Upanishad makes the same point:

> **annamayaṁ hi somya manaḥ**
> "O gentle one, the mind is made of food."
> (Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4)

The sage Aruni Uddalaka told his son Shvetaketu to fast for fifteen days and report what happened. His body grew weaker, he began to feel dizzy, and in the end he could not recite even a single mantra.

The lesson is clear: **the first strength is physical strength.** And it is vital to **protect** whatever you have. That is why Kali worship is the first worship, and why the very first episode of the sacred story belongs to Kali.

## Think Big, but Secure It First

Suppose you have started a company. You will naturally want it to grow and spread. **Shri Vidya** (the sacred tradition of worshipping the Divine Mother) tells you, "Think big."

But what does thinking big lead to? Competition, opposition and enemies are bound to follow.

Acharya Ji explains that what you need first is not a sword but legal protection.

Copyrights, trademarks and patents are the **Kalishakti** (Kali's protective power) of today. And as an organisation grows, it has to hire guards as well.

*Have you made arrangements to protect what you have built?*

## Even Non-Violence Needs Guardians

When the sages had to perform a **yajna** (a sacred fire ritual), they said to Shri Rama, "Rama and Lakshmana, take your bows and arrows and stand guard there, because demons will come."

Acharya Ji says that even the work of non-violence needs armed people standing around us.

These are the **Kshatriyas** (the protectors and warriors of society): the police, the army and security guards. Society needs them. And this is Kali.

## Key Takeaways

- Mahakali is the goddess of body awareness and self-defence, so her worship comes first.
- According to the Upanishads, the body must grow strong first and the intellect after.
- Strength should be of every kind, not merely physical.
- "annamayaṁ hi somya manaḥ": the mind depends on food and the body.
- Think big, but protect what you create first.
- Even the work of non-violence stays safe only with guardians.
