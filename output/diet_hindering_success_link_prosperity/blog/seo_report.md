## 1. Summary

A short talk on food and the money that buys it. Acharya Ji says a sattvic diet is not the whole story: the effect of a meal also depends on how the money behind it was earned. He goes through several mixes (black money with sattvic food, honest money with tamasic food by mistake, rajasic earning with sattvic food on a doctor's advice). He asks whether someone who lives by theft and plunder can get the same fruit from sattvic food. He then turns it round: a hardworking, sattvic person whose food is tampered with is protected to some degree by devotion, sattva guna and dravya-shuddhi (purity of wealth), as Meerabai digested poison through her devotion. The main intent is informational with an ethical edge ("does halal/haram earning affect food", "jaisa ann waisa mann", "is sattvic food enough"). There is a smaller emotional layer for honest people who are struggling ("why do good people suffer"). Search pages on sattvic food deal with ingredients and Ayurveda. Very few explain that the purity of earnings is part of the meal, so a clear answer here can stand out. The video title promises a link between diet and success or prosperity, but the blog does not make that claim (see section 10). Titles and meta are therefore built on honest earnings and sattvic food, not on "diet for success".

**English (55 words):** According to Acharya Upendra Ji, sattvic food alone is not enough. The money that bought the food also decides what it does for you. A sattvic meal paid for with stolen or unjust money does not bear the same fruit, while the purity of honestly earned money (dravya-shuddhi), together with devotion and sattva guna, protects a person.

## 6. FAQ

**1. क्या केवल सात्विक भोजन करना काफ़ी है? / Is eating sattvic food enough?**
नहीं। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि भोजन के साथ उसे खरीदने वाला धन भी असर डालता है; दोनों मिलकर फल तय करते हैं।
No. Acharya Ji says the money that bought the food also has an effect; the food and the money together decide the result.

**2. क्या बेईमानी की कमाई से बना सात्विक भोजन भी फल देता है? / Does sattvic food bought with dishonest money still bear fruit?**
आचार्य जी पूछते हैं कि जो दूसरों का हक छीनकर कमाता है, क्या उसे वही फल मिलेगा; भोजन सात्विक रहता है, पर धन की अशुद्धि साथ चलती है।
Acharya Ji asks whether someone who earns by taking what belongs to others can receive the same fruit: the food stays sattvic, but the impurity of the money travels with it.

**3. द्रव्य-शुद्धि क्या है? / What is dravya-shuddhi?**
इस प्रवचन में द्रव्य-शुद्धि का अर्थ है धन की पवित्रता, यानी मेहनत और सत्संग से, ईमानदारी से कमाया धन।
In this talk, dravya-shuddhi means the purity of one's wealth: money earned honestly, through hard work and in good company.

**4. अगर किसी सात्विक व्यक्ति को धोखे से तामसिक भोजन खिला दिया जाए तो? / What if a sattvic person is made to eat something tamasic by someone else?**
आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि उसकी भक्ति, सत्त्वगुण और द्रव्य-शुद्धि उसे कुछ हद तक बचाएगी।
Acharya Ji says the person's devotion, sattva guna and purity of wealth will protect him to some degree.

**5. मीराबाई ने विष कैसे पचा लिया? / How did Meerabai survive the poison?**
आचार्य जी के अनुसार मीराबाई ने विष को अपनी भक्ति से पचा लिया; भीतर की शुद्धि बाहर की अशुद्धि पर भारी पड़ सकती है।
According to Acharya Ji, Meerabai digested the poison through her devotion: purity within can overpower impurity from outside.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Video title promises "diet and success / prosperity".** The talk says the source of money affects what food does for you; it never says a diet brings success or wealth. Recommendation: do not reuse "diet for success" or "food for prosperity" in the WordPress title, meta or social cards (none of ours do). When the YouTube description is next revised, consider "Your Food and the Money Behind It". The slug (`diet_hindering_success...`) is only the folder name and is not used publicly.
2. **फलश्रुति / phalashruti glossed as "the fruit of an action" (from the Sanskrit review, Minor).** In standard usage phalashruti is the closing passage of a stotra or text that states the merit of reciting it. Recommendation: apply the review's gloss ("यहाँ: कर्म का फल ...") or use फल / phala. Keep "phalashruti" out of keywords, FAQ and snippets (done).
3. **सात्विक vs सात्त्विक (from the review, Minor).** Gita Press spells सात्त्विक (Gita 17.8), and the blog already writes सत्त्वगुण. Recommendation: the body can follow the review. The Hindi title, meta and keywords keep "सात्विक", the common search spelling, which is fine for queries.
4. **Key Takeaways equate द्रव्य-शुद्धि with the money itself (from the review, Minor).** Recommendation: use the review's wording ("मेहनत और सत्संग से आए धन की पवित्रता, यानी द्रव्य-शुद्धि..." / "The purity of money earned..."). The FAQ and direct answer in sections 5 and 6 already use "purity of wealth". The review also notes that in Dharmashastra द्रव्यशुद्धि usually means purifying objects. Present it as the speaker's term, not as a scriptural definition.
5. **Meerabai description only in English (from the review, Minor).** Recommendation: add "श्रीकृष्ण की अनन्य भक्त संत-कवयित्री" to the Hindi (section 4) so both versions match.
6. **Meerabai and the poison: no health claim.** The story is a traditional account of devotion, which the blog presents as Acharya Ji's illustration. Recommendation: never phrase snippets, FAQ or social cards as if devotion protects against poisoning or replaces medical care. Keep "protects to some degree" (कुछ तो बचाएगी), as the talk says.
7. **Unused supporting sources.** "जैसा अन्न वैसा मन" (a proverb) and the Manusmriti line on अर्थशौच (usually cited as 5.106, unverified per the review) are not in the talk. Recommendation: target the proverb only as a secondary query. Do not quote either one in the post or the schema as the talk's source unless the editor adds it with a verified reference.
