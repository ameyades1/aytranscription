# Your Plate Also Serves Up How You Earned It

## Is Food Just Food?

We often assume that once we eat a **sattvic** (pure, light, calming to the mind) diet, the job is done. Acharya Upendra Ji brings a new dimension to this.

What you eat is also tied to the way you earn your money. The source of the money that bought your food helps decide what that food does for you.

So is sattvic food enough? According to Acharya Ji, it is not. Honest earnings and sattvic food work together. Sattvic food bought with unjust or stolen money does not bear the same fruit, while the purity of money earned through hard work and satsang, along with devotion and sattva guna, protects a person to some degree.

## When Money and Food Combine in Different Ways

Acharya Ji lists a few situations:

- You earn black money and cook a sattvic meal at home with it. The result will be different.
- Your money comes from honest hard work, earned through satsang and among good people, but you mistakenly eat **tamasic** (heavy, dulling) food. That is another combination altogether.
- You earn with a **rajasic** (restless, craving-driven) mindset, then follow your doctor's advice and eat sattvic, Ayurvedic food. Here, too, the outcome may differ.

In other words, the food on your plate and the money that paid for it work together to shape the effect.

*Where did the money for the food on your plate come from?*

## Unjust Money, Pure Food

Suppose someone steals, harms people, and brings home money through plunder. With that money he may eat the purest sattvic food, whether he steals it, gets it through some shady deal, or comes home and cooks it himself with a pure heart.

Acharya Ji asks: will such a person receive the same **phalashruti** (the fruit of an action)? He has earned by taking what rightfully belonged to others. The food may be just as sattvic, but the impurity of the money travels with it.

## When Wrong Is Done to a Pure Person, Does Devotion Protect Them?

Now look at the reverse. A person works very hard, lives a sattvic life, and behaves well in the world. Because of **prarabdha** (destiny shaped by past actions), he has little money, or none at all.

Then some wicked person slips something harmful into his food and makes him eat something tamasic. What happens then?

Acharya Ji says that his devotion, his **sattva guna** (the quality of purity and harmony), and his **dravya-shuddhi** (the purity of one's wealth) will protect him to some degree.

## How Did Meerabai Digest the Poison?

Meerabai, the saint-poet devoted to Shri Krishna, is the proof. She was given poison, a poison her body could not have digested. Yet, as Acharya Ji explains, she digested it through her devotion.

Purity within can overpower impurity that comes from outside.

*Is your inner purity strong enough to protect you?*

## Key Takeaways

- The effect of food depends not just on what you eat but also on the money that bought it.
- Sattvic food bought with unjust or stolen money does not bear the same fruit.
- Money earned through hard work and satsang, which is dravya-shuddhi, protects a person.
- Devotion and sattva guna can overcome tamasic influences from outside, as Meerabai digested poison.
