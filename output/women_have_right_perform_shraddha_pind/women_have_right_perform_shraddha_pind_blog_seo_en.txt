# Can Women Perform Shraddha and Pind Daan?

When **Pitru Paksha** (the fortnight each year set aside for honouring one's ancestors) comes around, questions begin to stir. Who are the **pitras** (departed ancestors)? What do **shraddha** (rites of remembrance for the departed) and **tarpan** (offering water and food to the ancestors) really mean?

One question among them draws a great deal of debate: can women perform shraddha and tarpan? Do they also have the right? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji answers it on three grounds together: the scriptures, history and direct experience.

The short answer is yes. According to Acharya Ji, the Garuda Purana and the Vishnu Purana place no restriction on women performing shraddha; the right comes from the scriptures themselves. The scriptures permit the rite but do not require it of every woman, and women who are spiritually advanced and steady of mind are asked to perform it.

## Can Women Perform Shraddha? The Answer Is Yes

Acharya Ji says that women can certainly perform shraddha. Nobody handed them this right at some later date. The scriptures gave it to them from the start.

Read the Garuda Purana or the Vishnu Purana, or any text that speaks of shraddha, and you will not find a single line forbidding women from performing it. Women do have this right, Acharya Ji affirms, and it comes from the scriptures themselves.

## Why, Then, Is It Not Required of Every Woman?

The point here is a subtle one. The scriptures permit women to perform shraddha, but they do not insist on it. No scripture says that every woman must do it.

The reason lies in a woman's emotional nature. During the rite, she may be overtaken by **shmashan vairagya** (the detachment that rises on seeing death up close). That could create real difficulty at home, where young children and the whole family depend on her.

Women who have advanced far on the spiritual path and who are steady of mind, however, received a clear instruction: please do perform it. For them, no restriction applies.

## Mother Sita Performs the Rite

History offers a beautiful example. Mother Sita herself performed the shraddha of King Dasharatha, the father of Shri Rama.

She poured her whole heart into the rite, and all the ancestors appeared before her eyes. She watched them arrive in the form of brahmins, accept the meal and depart.

There are many such examples. Even so, the authors of the scriptures never force the rite on anyone.

## Why a Steady Mind Matters

The second reason concerns the monthly cycle. There is a science behind which days of the month suit **sadhana** (spiritual practice) and why women set it aside for those five days. During that time, the mind can swing up and down.

Whether you are performing **deva karma** (rites offered to the gods) or **pitru karma** (rites performed for the ancestors), you need complete focus and steady feeling, so that not even an accidental mistake creeps in. This rule is a precaution, not a prohibition.

*When you perform a sacred act, is your mind truly present in it?*

## The Order the Scriptures Lay Down

The Garuda Purana and the Vishnu Purana set out an order of those who may perform shraddha: the son, the grandson, the great-grandson, then other members of the family, cousins, and relatives on both the mother's and the father's side.

And if there is no son in the family, the texts state plainly that the women of the household should perform the rite.

## What We See in Antar Yog

In Antar Yog, around 200 to 300 people perform the shraddha rite together at one time. About 60 to 70 percent of them are women.

They perform it with such devotion that they receive the **phalashruti** (the direct fruit of a practice) at once. Men receive the fruit too, but women are receiving just as much, and often more. This alone shows that no such restriction ever existed.

## The Atman Has No Gender

In the wider, male-dominated society, women are not freely allowed to do this. People assume it is a man's job. Acharya Ji, however, reminds us of a basic truth: the **Atman (the Self)** is neither male nor female.

Our ancestors are our own parents and grandparents. All they need is someone to set them free, and that leaves no room for any line between men and women.

*Could you be the one who leads your ancestors towards liberation?*

## What the Ancestors Ask For

The Vishnu Purana describes the ancestors' longing in these words:

> **api dhanyaḥ kule jāyādasmākaṃ matimānnaraḥ |**
> **akurvanvittaśāṭhyaṃ yaḥ piṇḍānno nirvapiṣyati ||**
> "Will someone blessed and wise be born in our line, who will set aside greed for wealth and offer the pinds (rice-ball offerings) for us?"
> (Vishnu Purana 3.14)

Notice that the verse never uses the word "son". The ancestors simply hope for a wise and blessed descendant, and that descendant can just as well be a daughter.

## Press the Switch, and the Light Comes On

Acharya Ji offers a simple example. Press a switch, and the fan or the light is bound to come on. It makes no difference whether a pauper or a king presses it, a renunciate or a thief, someone old or someone young.

In the same way, the sadhanas of Antar Yog, such as Ganesh Vidya, Shri Vidya, Saraswati Diksha, Pitru Mukti, healing and Shiva Sadhana, bring quick results to most people. This is why Acharya Ji invites everyone to come and experience them first-hand.

## Key Takeaways

- Scriptures such as the Garuda Purana and the Vishnu Purana give women the right to perform shraddha; they impose no restriction.
- The scriptures permit the rite but do not require it of every woman, so that shmashan vairagya in a tender heart does not unsettle the family.
- The texts ask women who are spiritually advanced and steady of mind to perform shraddha.
- Mother Sita herself performed King Dasharatha's shraddha and saw the ancestors with her own eyes.
- The rule about the five days of the monthly cycle protects a steady mind; it is not a prohibition.
- Where there is no son, the texts clearly ask the women of the household to perform shraddha.
- The Atman is neither male nor female; the ancestors simply long for a wise and blessed descendant.
