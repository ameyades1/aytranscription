## 1. Summary

Every Pitru Paksha, many families, especially daughters with no brother, wives and widows, ask whether a woman may perform shraddha, tarpan or pind daan. The search results are split. Some ritual sites say women may not, others give a short "yes, if there is no son" answer, and a few cite the Garuda Purana without context. In this talk Acharya Ji gives a clear "yes" on three grounds: the scriptures, tradition (Mata Sita's offering for King Dasharatha) and direct experience (60 to 70 percent of those performing the rite at Antar Yog are women). He also explains why the texts permit the rite without requiring it of every woman, and reads the Pitri Gita verse of the Vishnu Purana (3.14) as asking only for a wise, blessed descendant. The main intent is **informational with a practical edge** ("can women…", "is it allowed"). A smaller group searches emotionally, after losing a parent and having no son in the family; they need a calm, accurate answer. Searches peak before and during Pitru Paksha (Ashvin Krishna Paksha, usually September), so publish or refresh the post 4 to 6 weeks earlier. The opportunity is good: the question is common, the H1 already asks it, and a well-sourced answer can beat thin pages. Accuracy matters, though: the Sanskrit review found three major issues (section 10), and the snippet and FAQ below are written so they stay correct whether or not the blog is fixed first.

## 6. FAQ

For the WordPress page only. Answers come from the talk; Acharya Ji's own readings are attributed to him, and the scriptural points follow the Sanskrit review.

**Hindi**
1. **क्या स्त्रियाँ श्राद्ध कर सकती हैं?** हाँ। आचार्य जी के अनुसार गरुड़ पुराण और विष्णु पुराण में स्त्रियों पर श्राद्ध का निषेध नहीं है, और विष्णु पुराण स्त्रियों द्वारा क्रिया करने का स्पष्ट विधान करता है।
2. **तो हर स्त्री के लिए श्राद्ध अनिवार्य क्यों नहीं?** शास्त्रों ने अनुमति दी है, आग्रह नहीं किया। आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि श्राद्ध के समय श्मशान वैराग्य जाग सकता है, और घर-परिवार स्त्री पर निर्भर रहता है। जो स्त्रियाँ आध्यात्मिक रूप से उन्नत और मन से दृढ़ हैं, उन्हें आचार्य जी अवश्य करने को कहते हैं।
3. **घर में बेटा न हो तो श्राद्ध कौन करे?** विष्णु पुराण पुत्र, पौत्र, प्रपौत्र और फिर परिवार के अन्य संबंधियों का क्रम बताता है, और कहता है कि जब कोई करने वाला न रहे तो स्त्रियाँ यह क्रिया करें। आचार्य जी के अनुसार बेटी भी पितरों के लिए यह कर सकती है।
4. **पितर अपने वंशज से क्या चाहते हैं?** विष्णु पुराण (३.१४) की पितृ गीता में पितर कामना करते हैं कि कुल में कोई मतिमान, धन्य मनुष्य जन्मे जो धन में कृपणता किए बिना उनके लिए पिंडदान करे। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि वह वंशज बेटी भी हो सकती है।

**English**
1. **Can women perform shraddha?** Yes. According to Acharya Ji, the Garuda Purana and the Vishnu Purana do not forbid women from performing shraddha, and the Vishnu Purana explicitly provides for women to perform the rites.
2. **Then why is it not required of every woman?** The scriptures permit it but do not insist. Acharya Ji explains that the rite can stir shmashan vairagya, and the household depends on her. To women who are spiritually advanced and steady of mind, his advice is clear: do perform it.
3. **Who performs shraddha if there is no son?** The Vishnu Purana gives an order: son, grandson, great-grandson, then other relatives, and says that when no one is left to perform it, women should perform the rites. Acharya Ji adds that a daughter can do it for her ancestors.
4. **What do the ancestors ask of their descendants?** In the Pitri Gita of the Vishnu Purana (3.14), the ancestors hope for a wise and blessed person in their line who will offer the pinds for them without being miserly. Acharya Ji reads this as a descendant who may just as well be a daughter.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"If there is no son, the women should perform it" (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The Vishnu Purana (3.13) places women after all the male relatives of both lines ("कुलद्वयेऽपि चोच्छिन्ने स्त्रीभिः कार्याः क्रिया नृप"), not directly after the son. The blog's sentence and takeaway contradict the list just above them. This is exactly the practical query many readers bring ("पुत्र न हो तो श्राद्ध कौन करे"), so a wrong answer here would be quoted by snippets and AI overviews. FAQ 3 and the snippet follow the text ("when no one is left to perform it"). Apply the review's correction before publishing, and don't use "no son? daughters must do it" wording in titles or social posts.
2. **"The verse never uses the word son" (review issue 2, Major, not yet applied).** The verse says नरः ("a man / a person"). Acharya Ji's reading (any wise descendant, son or daughter) is legitimate as his interpretation, but the blog hides the word. FAQ 4 attributes the reading to him. Apply the review's correction; never write "the Vishnu Purana says a daughter can perform shraddha" in any SEO text.
3. **Mata Sita's shraddha told as "history" (review issue 3, Major, not yet applied).** The story merges the Gaya tradition (Sita offers a sand pinda for Dasharatha) with a Padma Purana account in which Rama performs the shraddha and Sita sees the ancestors in the brahmins. Neither is in Valmiki or the Ramcharitmanas. The video title ("The Story of Mata Sita") makes this a likely search entry point, so the recommended titles leave Sita out until it is fixed; title options using "Sita's Example" are marked "only after flag 3". Apply the review's correction (attribute to the Gaya tradition / Padma Purana).
4. **"Not a single line in any text" (review issue 4, Minor, not yet applied).** Dharmashastra traditions differ on women performing shraddha, and the absolute claim invites easy rebuttal. The snippet and FAQ 1 narrow it to the two Puranas named and attribute it to Acharya Ji.
5. **"Spiritually advanced women were told: do perform it" (review issue 5, Minor, not yet applied).** This is Acharya Ji's guidance, not a cited scriptural instruction, and the English takeaway adds "The texts ask…" which the Hindi lacks. FAQ 2 attributes it to him. Apply the correction.
6. **Verse reference and translation (review issues 6 and 7, Minor).** The reference gives only the chapter (3.14; the verse is probably 3.14.22, unconfirmed), and "धन का लोभ छोड़कर" / "set aside greed for wealth" should be "without being miserly with wealth" (वित्तशाठ्य). FAQ 4 uses the corrected sense and cites only the chapter.
7. **Terms (review issues 8 and 9, Minor).** फलश्रुति is the promised fruit (or the passage stating it), not "the direct fruit"; "pitras" should be "pitris". No SEO text uses either.
8. **Five days of the monthly cycle conflicts with another post.** This blog says there is a science behind setting sadhana aside for five days each month; `women_continue_sadhana_during_their_monthly` teaches that a woman never has to give up her sadhana, and treats the rule as social in origin. Both are Acharya Ji's words in different contexts (here, focus during a pitru karma; there, personal daily practice), but a reader moving between the two posts will see a contradiction. The editor should make the context explicit in this blog (for example, "for a rite like shraddha"). Until then the two posts are not cross-linked, and no SEO text mentions menstruation.
9. **Experience claims.** "60 to 70 percent are women", "women receive the fruit at once, often more than men" and "the sadhanas bring quick results to most people" are Acharya Ji's observations at Antar Yog, not verifiable results. Keep them attributed; don't put "instant results" or comparisons between men and women into titles, metas or social snippets.
10. **Gender framing.** "A woman's emotional nature" as the reason the rite is not compulsory is Acharya Ji's explanation and is presented respectfully in the blog, but out of context (a snippet or a quote card) it could read as a stereotype and draw criticism away from the post's main, empowering point. Lead every SEO text with the "yes, women have the right" message, and quote the reason only together with its context (the family's dependence on her).
