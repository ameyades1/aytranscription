## 1. Summary

A two-minute exchange in which Acharya Ji puts one open challenge to every "hardcore" meat-eater of any religion: before eating others, eat yourself. Cut a small, visible piece of your own body every day, cook it any way you like, slaughter it the halal way or recite a mantra over it, and eat it. No life is asked for, only a daily piece. The challenge is rhetorical; the closing reflection gives its point: pain we would not bear even a piece of ourselves, we should not inflict on another being. The main intent is **informational / reflective** ("मांसाहार क्यों नहीं करना चाहिए", "non veg kyu nahi khana chahiye", "why not eat meat"), with a navigational layer from the video's title ("hardcore non-vegetarian try this"). Ranking pages for these queries are mostly health listicles, religious debates and vegan activism; this post's distinct angle is a short, sharp empathy test with no health or scriptural claims, from a living teacher. It is thin (about 300 words), so it is best positioned as a punchy entry point that links on to the fuller food talks (`eat_to_heal_not_to_harm_spiritual`, `non_veg_food_remains_body_truths`), not as a pillar. It is evergreen; interest rises during Sawan, Navratri and Chaturmas, when many people give up meat.

**English (55 words):** Acharya Upendra Ji puts an open challenge to every hardcore meat-eater: before eating others, show that you can eat yourself. One small, visible piece of your own body every day, with any spices, the halal way or with a mantra. The challenge is meant to make us think: if we cannot bear that pain ourselves, how can it be right to inflict it on another being?

## 6. FAQ

1. **आचार्य जी मांसाहारियों को क्या चुनौती देते हैं? / What challenge does Acharya Ji give meat-eaters?**
   पहले खुद को खाकर दिखाओ: रोज़ अपने शरीर का एक छोटा, दिखने लायक टुकड़ा काटकर पकाओ और खाओ। मसाले, पकाने का तरीका, हलाल या मंत्र, सब की छूट है। / Eat yourself first: cut, cook and eat a small, visible piece of your own body every day, with complete freedom over spices, cooking, halal slaughter or mantras.
2. **क्या मंत्र बोलकर काटने से मांस शुद्ध हो जाता है? / Does reciting a mantra make meat pure?**
   ब्लॉग इसे एक प्रचलित मान्यता के रूप में रखता है। आचार्य जी कहते हैं, ठीक है, मंत्र बोलकर अपना ही टुकड़ा काटो; मंत्र से चुनौती आसान नहीं होती। / The blog treats this as a popular belief. Acharya Ji's answer: fine, recite the mantra and cut a piece of yourself; the mantra does not make the challenge any easier.
3. **क्या यह सचमुच अपने शरीर को काटने के लिए कहा गया है? / Is this a literal instruction to cut yourself?**
   नहीं। यह सोचने के लिए एक प्रश्न है: जो पीड़ा हम अपने लिए एक टुकड़े जितनी भी नहीं सह सकते, क्या वह किसी और जीव के लिए उचित है? / No. It is a question to reflect on: if we cannot bear even a piece's worth of pain ourselves, is it right to inflict it on another being?
4. **यह चुनौती किसके लिए है? / Who is the challenge for?**
   हर उस व्यक्ति के लिए जो स्वाद ले लेकर मांसाहार करता है, चाहे वह किसी भी धर्म का हो। / For everyone who eats meat with relish, whatever their religion.

Use as on-page FAQ only if the editor publishes it; no FAQPage schema is given (see section 8). FAQ 3 states the rhetorical intent outright, which the blog only implies; publish it only together with review issue 3's framing sentence.
