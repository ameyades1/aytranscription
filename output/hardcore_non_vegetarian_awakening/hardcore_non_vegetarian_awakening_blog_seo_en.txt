# Eat Yourself First

Have you ever stopped to think that the flavour on your plate comes at someone else's expense? In this short exchange, Acharya Upendra Ji puts a direct and uncomfortable question to every meat-eater. He gives no sermon and builds no long argument. He simply lays down an open challenge.

Why not eat meat? Acharya Ji answers with a challenge for meat eaters rather than an argument: before eating others, eat yourself first. One small piece of your own body every day, with any spices, the halal way or with a mantra. If we cannot bear that pain ourselves, how can it be right to inflict it on another being?

## The First Question: Leave the Young Ones Alone and Start With Yourself

Forget about the young ones, Acharya Ji says. First, show that you can eat yourself!

From tomorrow, give up the chicken fry, the brain fry, all of it. Let the poor chicken wander about a little longer. In its place, cut off just a small piece of your own body.

## An Open Question for Those Who Love the Taste

This question is for anyone who eats meat with great relish, savouring every bite. It is meant for every hardcore meat-eater, whatever their religion.

Cut off a small piece of yourself every day. Add salt, oil, ghee, curd, any vegetable in the world, whatever you like. Toss it, chop it, slaughter it the halal way. You have complete freedom.

## What If You Recite a Mantra While You Cut?

Some people believe that meat becomes pure if you recite a mantra while cutting it. Fine, says Acharya Ji, recite the mantra as you cut.

He is not even asking you to take a life. Recite the mantra, cut off a small piece, and eat it. That is all.

*Could you actually do it? Pause for a moment and answer honestly.*

## Hardcore Non-Vegetarian? Could You Do It Every Day?

This is an open challenge to every person on earth. It is not a one-day stunt; it means one piece, every single day.

How big the piece is remains your choice, as long as it is large enough to see. Nobody is asking for as much as a chicken or a goat, or even as much as you eat in a day. Just cut off a sliver of your finger each day, fry it and eat it.

Who among you could manage that, Acharya Ji asks. The challenge stands open.

*If you cannot bear even a piece's worth of pain yourself, is it right to inflict it on another living being?*

## Key Takeaways

- Acharya Ji's first question: do not eat others; show that you can eat yourself first.
- The question applies to every hardcore meat-eater, whatever their religion.
- You have complete freedom over spices, cooking style, halal slaughter or mantras.
- No one asks you to take a life, only a small, visible piece of your own body.
- It is a daily rule, not a one-day show: who could keep it up?
