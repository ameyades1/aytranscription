# Eat Yourself First

Have you ever thought that the taste on your plate comes at someone else's cost? In this short exchange, Acharya Upendra Ji puts a direct, piercing question before every meat-eater. No sermon, no long argument, just an open challenge.

## The First Question: Leave the Children, Start With Yourself

Acharya Ji says:

> "Leave the children aside, eat yourself and show me!"

From tomorrow, give up chicken fry, brain fry, all of it. Let the poor chicken roam about a little. Instead, cut off this small a part of your own body.

## An Open Question to the Lover of Taste

This question is for the one who eats meat with great relish, savouring every bite. And it is for every hardcore meat-eater, whatever religion they belong to.

Cut off this small a piece of yourself every day. Salt, oil, ghee, curd, every vegetable in the world, add whatever you like. Turn it this way and that, chop it, make it halal. You have complete freedom.

## Cut It While Chanting a Mantra, and Then?

It is said that if something is cut while chanting a mantra, it becomes pure. Acharya Ji says, fine, then cut it while chanting a mantra.

> "I am not even asking you to take a life."

Just this small piece, cut with a mantra, and eat it.

*Would you be able to do it? Pause for a moment and think honestly.*

## Not a One-Day Show, a Daily Rule

This is an open challenge for every person on Earth. Not a one-day stunt, one piece every day.

How big the piece is, is your choice. It just has to be visible. Not the size of a chicken or a goat, he never said that. Not even as much as you eat every day. Cut off this small a bit of finger every day, fry it and eat it.

> "So who will be able to do it, tell me? This is an open challenge."

*Pain we cannot bear for ourselves even to the extent of one small piece, is it fair to inflict it on another living being?*

## Key Takeaways

- Acharya Ji's first question: not others, first eat yourself and show me.
- The question is for every hardcore meat-eater, whatever their religion.
- Spices, cooking style, halal or mantra, complete freedom in all of it.
- No taking of life, only a small, visible piece of your own body.
- Not a one-day show but a daily rule: who will be able to do it?
