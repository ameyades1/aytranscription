# Chapter: The Essence of True Freedom and Democracy

## India's Long Name

In today's age, our India has a long introduction. Do you know by how many names our Constitution describes India? One after another, many qualifiers have been added: we are a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic, a country run by a federal government. Our name is that long.

## Why Did We Have to Declare Sovereignty?

The word "sovereign" means that no one else rules over us; the rule is our own. No foreign or alien power governs us. But consider: why did this word have to be added to our name at all? Because at one time someone else did rule over us. A country to which this never happened has no need to carry such a name. So we had to declare to the world:

> "We are free. We are a sovereign country."

## Democracy: Of the People, By the People, For the People

Second, we have democracy. Democracy means that the entire system of the country is in the hands of the people. This government is of the people, by the people and for the people.

How much of "for the people" remains now, and how much of "of the people", is hard to say. In English we say "by the people", but what has definitely remained is "buy the people", that is, purchasing the people. A "U" has been added in the middle, because you have forgotten yourself.

> "You have forgotten what your strength was. The Guru has to come to remind you of it."

This system has truly become a mockery, a game. Buying the people has become the reality. Someone is given something through some scheme, but it is your own money; no one pays from their own pocket. It is the king's duty to truly spend for the public, not for personal gain, but for the good of all of us.

## Acharya Chanakya's Warning

Acharya Chanakya used to say that the day democracy becomes very popular, when people come to like it very much, understand that the dark age of Kali Yuga has arrived. These are not my words; they are Acharya Chanakya's words.

Because in the true sense, in a democracy the people themselves choose who their king will be. When everyone's vote is equal, the question arises: who is doing the choosing?

He said that democracy, if it exists, is meant for the learned. If there are two learned people standing, and other learned people choose which of them can do better, then that vote has some value. Those standing should be capable, the options should be good, and those choosing should also be wise. When there is such a committee or assembly and an election is held in it, Chanakya says, that is where democracy truly works.

## Do We Know Our Constitution?

If more than half the people here have no understanding of politics, no understanding of economics and the economy, what then? How many of you know about your Constitution? How many have read it? It was taught in school, but in reality no one actually read it.

So how did you decide whether the government is running according to it or not?
