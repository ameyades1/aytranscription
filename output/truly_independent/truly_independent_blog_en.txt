# Are We Really Free?

## A Long Name with a Deep Meaning

Have you ever noticed how long a description of India our Constitution gives? A sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic, and a country governed as a federal union.

Every one of these words has a story behind it. Let us look at two of them through Acharya Ji's eyes.

## Why Did We Have to Call Ourselves "Sovereign"?

**Sovereign** means that we govern ourselves and no one else rules over us. No foreign power holds the reins.

Yet a question arises: why did we need to add this word to our name at all? Because there was a time when others did rule over us. A country that has never lived through that has no need to announce that it is free and sovereign.

## By the People or Buy the People?

The second word is **democracy**, which places the entire machinery of the country in the hands of its people. It promises government of the people, by the people, for the people.

How much of "for the people" and "of the people" survives today is hard to say. With a smile, Acharya Ji observes that "by the people" has clearly turned into "buy the people". A "U" has crept into the middle because you have forgotten yourself. You have lost sight of where your strength lay, and the Guru has to come to remind you.

The system has turned into a game. The money handed out through government schemes is your own; no one pays for them out of their own pocket. A ruler's **dharma** (sacred duty) is to spend for the good of the people, not for personal gain.

*Do you remember where your real strength as a citizen lies?*

## Chanakya's Warning

Acharya Chanakya, the ancient strategist and author of the Arthashastra, once warned that the day democracy becomes hugely popular, you should know that the darkest depths of **Kali Yuga** (the age of moral decline) have arrived. Acharya Ji makes it clear that these words are Chanakya's, not his own.

Why would Chanakya say this? In a democracy, the people choose their ruler. When every vote counts equally, everything depends on who is doing the choosing.

According to Chanakya, democracy is meant **for the learned**. It needs capable candidates and good options in front of the voters, and voters wise enough to choose well. Only when an assembly like that holds an election does democracy work in the true sense.

## Have You Read Your Constitution?

Now ask yourself honestly. If more than half the voters have no grasp of politics, economics or how the economy works, what is an election really worth?

How many of us have actually read our Constitution? We were taught it in school, but who truly read it?

*If you have never read the Constitution, how can you judge whether the government is following it?*

## Key Takeaways

- The word "sovereign" reminds us that we were once ruled by others, which is why we had to declare our freedom.
- Democracy means government of the people, by the people, for the people, yet today it is turning into "buy the people".
- The money handed out through schemes belongs to the people; a ruler's dharma is to spend it for the public good.
- According to Acharya Chanakya, democracy is meaningful only when both the voters and the candidates are learned.
- True freedom begins with remembering your own strength and knowing your Constitution.
