## 1. Summary

A short talk (a three-minute clip) that turns the equality debate around: women were never merely equal to men, says Acharya Upendra Ji, but at least a thousand times more powerful. This is why the tradition calls woman **Shakti** (as Parvati, Lakshmi and Saraswati are Shakti), and why the label **abala** misses the point: the power is not physical. The second half turns that power into responsibility: a mother holds a thousand times her child's strength, and Gandhari's failure to use hers while Duryodhana was young is the warning. The main intent is informational ("स्त्री को शक्ति क्यों कहा जाता है" / "why is woman called Shakti"), with an emotional and parenting side (a mother's role in her child's upbringing). The opportunity: most pages for these queries are generic "nari shakti" essays or Women's Day quotes; a short post that ties the Shakti idea to a concrete duty (the mother's responsibility, the Gandhari example) and refuses to blame women offers a clear, authentic angle. The post is short (about 400 words), so it will rank best on narrow question queries, not on the broad "nari shakti" head term. It overlaps strongly with `gyan_sarovar_mount_abu_women_address` (see section 7, cannibalization).

## 6. FAQ

1. **स्त्री को शक्ति क्यों कहा जाता है? / Why is woman called Shakti?**
   हमारी परंपरा स्त्री को शक्ति कहती है, जैसे पार्वती, लक्ष्मी और सरस्वती शक्ति हैं; आचार्य जी के अनुसार यह शरीर के बल की नहीं, किसी गहरे स्तर की शक्ति है। / Our tradition calls woman Shakti, as Parvati, Lakshmi and Saraswati are Shakti; according to Acharya Ji, this power is not physical but belongs to a deeper level.
2. **क्या स्त्री अबला है? / Is a woman abala, the weak one?**
   समाज उसे अबला इसलिए कहता है क्योंकि शरीर से वह बलवान नहीं दिखती; आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि उसकी शक्ति शरीर सौष्ठव की भाषा में नहीं है। / Society calls her abala because her body does not look strong; Acharya Ji says her power cannot be measured in muscle.
3. **माँ का सबसे बड़ा उत्तरदायित्व क्या है? / What is a mother's greatest responsibility?**
   बच्चे के पास माँ से हज़ार गुना कम शक्ति होती है; उसे लाड़ में न बिगाड़ना, समय पर ज्ञान देना और स्वयं राजसिक-तामसिक न रहना। / A mother holds a thousand times her child's power; she must not spoil the child, must teach at the right time, and must not herself stay rajasic and tamasic.
4. **गांधारी से क्या सीख मिलती है? / What is the lesson of Gandhari?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार दुर्योधन के बचपन में गांधारी के पास उससे हज़ार गुना शक्ति थी, पर उन्होंने उसका उपयोग नहीं किया। / According to Acharya Ji, Gandhari had a thousand times Duryodhana's strength while he was a child, but did not use it.
5. **क्या यह स्त्री को पापी कहना है? / Does this call women sinners?**
   नहीं; आचार्य जी स्पष्ट करते हैं कि स्त्री को पापी नहीं कहा गया, उस पर उत्तरदायित्व बहुत है, और यह उसकी शक्ति की पहचान है। / No; Acharya Ji makes clear that a woman is not called a sinner: she carries great responsibility, and this is a recognition of her power.

Use as on-page FAQ only if the editor publishes it; no FAQPage schema is given (see section 8). FAQ 4 should follow the review's correction (flag 1): "while he was a child", never "Gandhari never used her power".

## 10. Authenticity flags

Sanskrit review (tools/review/divine_power_every_woman_holds.md, verdict "Minor issues"); none of these is applied yet:

1. **"Gandhari never used hers" (review issue 1, Minor).** In the Mahabharata Gandhari does counsel and rebuke the adult Duryodhana; the speaker's point is about his childhood. Apply the review's takeaway wording ("while Duryodhana was young" / "दुर्योधन के बचपन में"). The FAQ (section 6, question 4) already uses the narrower wording; never put "Gandhari failed" or "Gandhari never used her power" in a title, meta or social card.
2. **Gandhari context only in English (review issue 2, Minor).** Add the context line to the Hindi or accept it as a translator's note. Not used in SEO text.
3. **Hindi sentence reads the reverse of its meaning (review issue 3, Minor).** "स्त्री को पापी कहकर यह नहीं कहा गया है" should become the review's wording. This sentence is the post's main safeguard against a misreading (flag 7), so fix it before publishing.
4. **"इसीलिए / This is why" reverses the tradition (review issue 4, Minor).** The goddesses are Shakti in their own right; apply the review's takeaway wording. The direct answer and FAQ (sections 5 and 6) already use "as Parvati, Lakshmi and Saraswati are Shakti", not a causal link.

SEO-specific:

5. **"A thousand times more powerful" is the speaker's teaching, not scripture.** Titles, metas, snippet and FAQ attribute it to Acharya Ji ("आचार्य उपेंद्र जी के अनुसार", "according to Acharya Ji") and never present it as a scriptural fact or a measurable claim.
6. **No gender-war framing.** Don't target or headline "women are superior to men" or "men vs women". The talk reframes the debate rather than taking a side in it; such titles would be clickbait and would misrepresent the tone.
7. **No guilt or fear selling.** "The sin of that child's entire life falls on her" must not become a hook ("Mothers, your child's sins are on you"). Readers who are mothers, especially anxious ones, should meet the post's balance: the blog itself says this is responsibility, not blame. The title uses "A Mother's Thousandfold Power", not "sin". The review notes that no scriptural basis is claimed for this point; keep it that way in all SEO text.
8. **Short source.** The video is a three-minute clip and the post is about 400 words. Don't stretch it with claims the talk does not make (for example about Navratri, Women's Day or specific Shakti sadhanas) to reach a seasonal peak. It can be promoted around Navratri and International Women's Day (8 March) as is, without changing the text.
