# Chapter: The Secret of Visarjan: From Name and Form to Non-Duality

## Ten Days of Festival and Its Forgotten Purpose

What did we do in these ten days? In the days between, we only played loud music and looked at decorations. Then on the tenth day we said that this idol is no longer needed. All our talk in these ten days was that your Ganpati is big and ours is small, that this decoration is good. If this is all we did, what was the benefit of this symbol?

> "First understand at least this much: what the festival was, and what we are doing."

## What the Rishis Said About the Tenth Day

On the tenth day we did what the rishis had said: now there is no need for this idol. It meant that every person should dissolve this embodied form, this name and form (*nama-rupa*).

Just as your nose ring, your ring and your chain are all melted down into one gold, in the same way that name and form was offered into flowing water, into a river, into the sea. Flowing water is a symbol of the Ganga, and Ganga means the ever-flowing **Ganga of knowledge**. This name and form was to be offered into that Ganga of knowledge; we were to take a dip in that Ganga of knowledge.

In these ten days, when the Ganga of knowledge flows, you will see thousands of idols on the banks of the river and the sea, that is, thousands of names, forms and bodies, because each was the symbol of its own home. All of them will dissolve into the same sea, into the same Ganga of knowledge. One day everyone will dissolve into that same state of knowledge, bliss, dispassion, truth, complete truth, beyond the gunas, the formless.

## The Same Handful of Clay for Everyone

What is common to all these idols? **Clay.** After the immersion, clay taken out of the water is placed on your seat, on the low stool that in Marathi you call a *paat*, and given to you to take home.

Now think: does the same thing go to every home or not? Whether the cloth was black or red, the idol fair or dark, tall or short, an idol of the Guru or a simple one, in a woman's form or one in half pants, whatever it was; however your Ganpati looked when it went, when it came back did everyone's take exactly the same form or not? Just a heap of clay.

In reality this means: what was the material it was made of? That material is one and the same for all of us: that Paramatma. On the tenth day you were to bring that Paramatma home. From that day on you should have seen **non-duality (Advaita)** everywhere.

Your Ganpati was just the same. Even if it was twenty-two feet tall, you get only that much clay. Do they give it twenty-two kilos of clay? It too will get only a handful. That huge form was only a symbol. Take your small idol, and you too will be brought just as much clay. No one will say: your idol was small, so I will weigh out one gram for you, one spoonful, on a scale. Everyone will get exactly the same.

> "Do they give it twenty-two kilos of clay? It too will get only a handful."

This difference was to dissolve; the knowledge of this was to come in these very ten days.

## The Purpose Lost in Competition

But what was to be done, we never did in the Ganpati festival at all. So what was the use of passing ten days? Your Ganpati is big, ours is small; this decoration is good; we got first prize, we got second; your music is good, ours is good. We were not supposed to do all this nonsense. **We were to embrace non-duality and move ahead.**

That purpose was left far behind. The thing that mattered was never learned. Is it crystal clear now?

## The Place of the Clay in the Home

After bringing the clay home, we are told to spread it all over the house. In earlier times houses were made of clay. Then it was said: mix it into the plaster of the walls, so that in your home this truth will always be remembered.

And what do we do today? We bring the clay home and think, "Oh, this is rubbish. Mother, what do I do with it?" Up to the house we carried it saying "Ganpati, Ganpati," and now we flushed it straight down the bathroom drain. Two moments ago it was Ganpati, and now we threw it away as rubbish.

Do you understand what it really was? And what have we made of the festival of that Ganpati? First understand at least this much: what the festival was, and what we are doing.

## Our Duty to Come Out of Our Mistakes

The scriptures say that you have full freedom to err, but one thing always keeps going on in the heart: what you did is a sin. It is your duty to come out of it before it harms you or others.

Here in Antar, something different from all other institutions will be taught. Teach children fully from childhood what Ganpati means.
