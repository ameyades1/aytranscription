## 1. Summary

Every year on Anant Chaturdashi millions of families immerse their Ganpati, and searches for "visarjan kyon karte hain", "Ganpati Visarjan meaning" and "what to do with visarjan clay" peak in the days just before and after. Most ranking pages give puja-vidhi, muhurat times or the eco-friendly-idol angle. This talk offers what they lack: visarjan as offering the idol's **nama-rupa** (name and form) to the "Gyan Ganga" (the Ganga of knowledge), and the handful of clay that comes back, the same for the biggest pandal and the smallest home idol, as a lesson in **Advaita**. It also criticises the competitive side of the festival (prizes, "my Ganpati is bigger") and the habit of washing the returned clay down the drain. The main intent is **informational** ("meaning", "why"), with a practical side ("what to do with the clay after visarjan"). The post is short (about 750 words) and belongs to the Ganesh Series (Ep 12). Refresh it 4 to 6 weeks before Ganesh Chaturthi; its own peak is Anant Chaturdashi, ten days later.

**English (58 words):** According to Acharya Upendra Ji, Ganpati Visarjan means offering the idol's name and form to flowing water, the Ganga of knowledge, just as different ornaments melt into the same gold. The handful of clay that comes back is the same for everyone, whatever the size of the idol: it stands for the one Supreme Self and teaches Advaita.

## 6. FAQ

Answers come only from the talk. Not on the page yet; if it is added, add a matching FAQPage block then.

1. **गणपति विसर्जन क्यों करते हैं? / Why is Ganpati immersed?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार विसर्जन मूर्ति के नाम-रूप को बहते जल, यानी ज्ञान-गंगा में अर्पित करने का प्रतीक है; एक दिन हम सब भी उसी निराकार, गुणातीत अवस्था में विलीन होंगे। / Acharya Ji explains that visarjan symbolises offering the idol's name and form to flowing water, the Ganga of knowledge; one day all of us will merge into that same formless state beyond the three gunas.
2. **विसर्जन के बाद लौटी मिट्टी का क्या अर्थ है? / What does the clay returned after visarjan mean?**
   हर मूर्ति का रूप अलग था, पर लौटती मिट्टी सबको मुट्ठी भर, बिल्कुल समान मिलती है। वह उस एक पदार्थ, परमात्मा, का प्रतीक है जिससे सब बना है। / Every idol looked different, but everyone gets back the same handful of clay. It stands for the one substance everything is made of, the Supreme Self.
3. **विसर्जन की मिट्टी का क्या करें? / What should we do with the visarjan clay?**
   परंपरा के अनुसार इसे घर में फैलाया जाता था; पहले मिट्टी के घरों में इसे दीवारों के लेप में मिलाया जाता था। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि इसे कचरा समझकर नाली में न बहाएँ। / By tradition it was spread through the home, and in mud houses it was mixed into the wall plaster. Acharya Ji asks us never to wash it down the drain as rubbish.
4. **क्या बड़ा गणपति या पुरस्कार उत्सव का उद्देश्य है? / Is a bigger idol or a prize the purpose of the festival?**
   नहीं। आचार्य जी के अनुसार बड़े-छोटे की होड़, पुरस्कार और संगीत की प्रतियोगिता व्यर्थ है; उद्देश्य अद्वैत को अपनाकर आगे बढ़ना था। / No. Acharya Ji says the race for bigger idols, prizes and better music is pointless; the purpose was to embrace Advaita and move forward.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"अंतर में" loses the reference to Antar Yog (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The Hindi reads "इसीलिए अंतर में कुछ अलग सिखाया जाता है", which reads as "inwardly"; the English says "Antar Yog teaches". Apply the review's correction ("अंतर योग में") before publishing. None of the SEO texts quote this line.
2. **"शास्त्र कहता है..." has no source (review issue 2).** No text is named for "you are free to make mistakes, but your heart knows". Attribute it to Acharya Ji, as the review suggests, or name the scripture. Don't use this line in snippets, FAQ or social cards as a scriptural statement; none of the SEO texts do.
3. **"वही किया जो ऋषियों ने कहा था" / "what the rishis prescribed" (review issue 3).** No shastric source is given for visarjan as described, and the ten-day public festival in its present form dates from the late 19th century. Titles and meta call the meaning Acharya Ji's explanation ("Acharya Upendra Ji explains"); avoid "शास्त्रों के अनुसार विसर्जन" / "scriptural rules of visarjan" headlines.
4. **"God" for परमात्मा weakens the Advaita point (review issue 5, not yet applied).** The English blog says "that substance is the same for all of us: God". The direct answer and FAQ above use "the Supreme Self"; apply the review's wording in the blog too, so the snippet and the page agree.
5. **The clay custom is a regional tradition, not an injunction (review issue 4).** The FAQ presents spreading the clay through the home and mixing it into wall plaster as "tradition" (परंपरा), as the blog does. Don't phrase the practical query's answer as a ritual rule ("must", "shastra says"), and don't add environmental or eco-idol claims: the talk does not make them.
6. **Hindi-English consistency (review issues 6 to 8, Minor).** "गुरुदेव" → "आचार्य जी"; drop the English glosses in the Hindi; "Lord Ganesha arrives" → "Ganpati arrives". These don't affect the SEO texts but should be fixed before publishing.
7. **Respectful criticism.** The talk criticises competitive pandal culture and throwing the clay away. Keep titles from turning this into an attack ("Stop doing visarjan wrong!", "Your Ganpati celebration is fake"); the recommended titles frame it as meaning, not blame.
