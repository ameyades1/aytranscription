# What the Handful of Clay Returned After Visarjan Tells Us

## Ten Days, and Then?

Ganpati came, music played for ten days, there were decorations, and on the tenth day we said that this idol is no longer needed.

But what did we talk about in these ten days? Your Ganpati is big, ours is small, this decoration is good. If that is all that remains of the festival, what was the benefit of this beautiful symbol? Acharya Ji says we should first understand at least this much: what the festival was, and what we are doing.

## Visarjan: Offering Name and Form into the Ganga of Knowledge

On the tenth day we did what the rishis had said. But what did they mean? That every person should dissolve their **nama-rupa** (name and form, that is, embodied identity).

Understand it with a simple example. Your nose ring, ring and chain all look different. But when melted, they all become one gold. In the same way, the name and form of the idol is offered into flowing water, into a river, into the sea.

Flowing water is a symbol of the Ganga, and Ganga means the **Ganga of knowledge**. Visarjan meant taking a dip in that Ganga of knowledge.

On the banks of rivers and seas you see thousands of idols, that is, thousands of names, forms and bodies, each the symbol of a home. All dissolve into the same sea. In just the same way, one day all of us will dissolve into that same state of knowledge, bliss, dispassion, truth, complete truth, **gunatita** (beyond the three gunas), the formless.

## The Same Clay in Everyone's Hands

After the immersion, clay taken from the water is placed on your seat, on the *paat*, and given to you to take home.

Now just think. The idol had black cloth or red, was fair or dark, tall or short, an idol of the Guru or a simple one, in a woman's form or in half pants. When they went, every form was different. But when they came back? Exactly the same in everyone's hands, just a heap of clay.

This has a deep meaning. This clay is the material everything is made of, and that material is one and the same for all of us: **Paramatma**. On the tenth day, that Paramatma was to be brought home. From that day, **Advaita** (non-duality, the experience of one reality in all) should have been visible everywhere. Acharya Ji asks whether anyone is given twenty-two kilos of clay; even the biggest idol gets only a handful.

Whether the Ganpati is twenty-two feet tall or the size of a palm, the clay that returns is only a handful. No one weighs it on a scale and says, your idol was small, so you get only a spoonful. Everyone gets exactly the same. This very difference of big and small was to dissolve.

*In your life too, is a feeling of "mine is big, yours is small" covering the one truth that is the same in all?*

## When the Festival Becomes a Competition

First prize, second prize, whose music is better, whose decoration is grander. Gurudev says plainly that all of this was pointless. **We were to embrace non-duality and move ahead.**

That purpose was left far behind, and the thing that mattered was never learned.

## Do Not Treat the Clay as Rubbish

Tradition said: spread this clay all through the house. Earlier, houses were made of clay, so it was mixed into the plaster of the walls, so that this truth would always be remembered in the home.

And today? The clay comes home and the question arises, "Mother, what do I do with this rubbish?" What was brought home with cries of "Ganpati, Ganpati" is flushed down the bathroom drain two moments later. As Acharya Ji puts it, two minutes ago it was Ganpati, and now we have thrown it away as rubbish.

*How did what was worshipped a few moments ago suddenly become rubbish? What changed, the clay or our vision?*

## Our Duty to Come Out of Our Mistakes

The scriptures say that you have the freedom to err, but the heart knows from within that it is a mistake. It is your duty to come out of it before it harms you or others.

That is why something different is taught in Antar: teach children fully, from childhood, what Ganpati means.

## Key Takeaways

- Visarjan is a symbol of offering name and form into the Ganga of knowledge.
- Flowing water is the Ganga, and Ganga means the Ganga of knowledge.
- The returned clay is the same for everyone: it is a symbol of the one Paramatma.
- From the tenth day, non-duality should have been visible everywhere.
- Competition over big and small Ganpatis, prizes and music is not the purpose of the festival.
- Give the clay a place in your home; do not wash it down the drain as rubbish.
- Teach children from childhood what Ganpati means.
