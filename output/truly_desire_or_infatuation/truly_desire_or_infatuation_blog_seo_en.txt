# True Kama or Mere Attraction?

In **Kali Yuga** (the present age of spiritual decline), we have made love very small. For most of us, it now means marriage, or the bond between two lovers, and little else.

The scriptures see it differently. In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji unfolds a meaning of **prema** (love) and **kama** (desire) that turns our usual thinking completely upside down.

Love vs lust: how do we tell them apart? According to Acharya Upendra Ji, kama does not mean only sensual craving; it means desire of any kind. Its higher form wishes for another's good, like a hand placed on the head in blessing or a handshake with a friend. The intention behind a touch decides whether it is kama or lust.

## Does Love End at Marriage?

The scriptures say that where love arrives, kama is bound to follow.

In Kali Yuga, our thinking stops at the point where love means marriage or romance. Was everything else, then, not love at all? Wherever true love exists, kama comes along with it on its own.

## Two Levels of Desire

Kama carries desire within it, and desire works on two levels: a lower one and a higher one.

The scriptures do not even count the lower kind. It leads towards **sakama bhakti** (devotion driven by self-interest). The higher kind is a different energy: a noble wish for another person's good, and every one of us can cultivate it.

We say love should be selfless and free of desire. Yet the scriptures say kama joins love naturally. This is not a contradiction. It is a deep mystery.

## What Is the True Meaning of Kama?

Kama means a desire of any kind. It also means **kamavasana** (sensual craving), which expresses itself through touch. Yet the true meaning of kama is not the one we find in the **Kamashastra** (classical texts on sensual love).

Look at it instead through the **Vishnu tattva** and the **Lakshmi tattva** (the divine principles of Lord Vishnu and his consort Lakshmi). When you stroke a dog with affection, there is touch, there is love, and there is a feeling that it should be well.

You meet a friend and shake hands. There is touch, and behind it a desire of the higher kind. Someone comes to bless you and places a hand on your head. That too is touch, and it too carries a wish for your good. This is what kama means.

Acharya Ji says that wherever there is love, there will be touch, and love is not confined to marriage.

*How many of the touches in your life come from nothing more than a wish for someone's good?*

## Kalki: The Avatar of Both Kama and Love

Shri Krishna said that even in this incarnation, as the **prema avatar** (the incarnation of love) alone, it was hard to make people understand. What would happen when he came as the **kama avatar** (the incarnation of desire)?

That is why the scriptures describe **Kalki** (the prophesied final avatar of Lord Vishnu) as one who must come as both the avatar of kama and the avatar of love, to show the world what kama truly is. Shri Krishna did not have to explain kama; his task was to teach love. Kalki's task will be harder still.

## Love or Lust? The Great Confusion of Kali Yuga

It is said that in Kali Yuga, the greatest problem will be kama itself. People mistake it for love and announce, "We have fallen in love."

Acharya Ji points out that it was never love in the first place. It was lust.

What such a person goes on to do cannot even be called **kama purushartha** (desire in harmony with dharma, the right way of living, and one of the four aims of human life).

*What you call love: is it really love, or only attraction?*

## The Doctor's Example

Consider a doctor who is a gynaecologist. He has to touch his patients, because that is his profession.

Should his wife then ask, "How many women have you married?" Saying that would only get in the way of his work and push the marriage towards breaking apart.

Touch will happen, but the real question is this: what is the **intention** behind it? The doctor does love his patients, though not in the sense you might imagine. His love is for his work. This, too, is what kama means.

## Key Takeaways

- The scriptures say that where love arrives, kama is bound to follow.
- Love is not confined to marriage or romance.
- Desire has two levels: the lower leads to sakama bhakti, the higher wishes for another's good.
- The true meaning of kama is not the one in the Kamashastra; every touch given with love, such as a blessing or a handshake with a friend, is kama.
- Kalki must come as both the avatar of kama and the avatar of love.
- The greatest problem of Kali Yuga is that people mistake lust for love.
- The intention behind a touch decides whether it is kama or lust.
