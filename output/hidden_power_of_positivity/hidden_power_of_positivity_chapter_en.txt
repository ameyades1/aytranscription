# Chapter: Hearing the Auspicious, Seeing the Auspicious

## The Meaning Hidden in Every Word

Every verse of our scriptures, every single word, deserves to be understood in great depth. Take, for example, this peace invocation:

> **oṁ bhadraṁ karṇebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma devāḥ |**
> **bhadraṁ paśyemākṣabhiryajatrāḥ ||**
> "O gods, may we hear the auspicious with our ears; O worthy of worship, may we see the auspicious with our eyes."
> (Rigveda 1.89.8)

This is only the preparation. Your "atha", the true beginning, has not yet started. It is spoken before that, to bring the mind to some calm.

We know that "karṇa" means the ear. So this is about the ears. "Śṛṇuyāma" means to hear. So it speaks of hearing with the ears, a prayer to the gods. And "bhadra": hear the good, something like that is being said. Abhadra means bad or wrong, so bhadra must mean good, auspicious. Thus the plain meaning of the sentence is only this: "May I always hear only the auspicious with my ears."

## Bhadra Is That Whose Result Is Auspicious

Now, what have we been taught until today? See good, do good, be good. In the same way there would be "hear good". But that is not the meaning of this mantra.

**For my ears, bhadra is that whose result is good for me.** Whether what I hear sounds pleasant or unpleasant has nothing to do with it. This is the first misunderstanding.

If the Guru's words today feel like bitter medicine, they should still be swallowed. They are bhadra to hear, because in truth their result is meant to be auspicious. If a king makes a rule and it is for your good, a signal, should that signal be heeded or not? It is good for us, even if it does not feel good. When a teacher shouts at a student, when a mother shouts at her son, that shouting is in truth bhadra for him. We should listen to it.

On the other hand, a vulgar song, a bad play, everything wrong, may well be pleasant to hear. A film song, and so much else. In our tradition we speak of **preya** and **śreya**; preya, what feels dear to me, is not called bhadra. **Only that whose result will be auspicious is bhadra.** Do you understand?

Each verse unfolds this vastly. Right now I am only giving a hint, because we have no idea of the depth of each word.

## Knowing the Future: Auspicious or Inauspicious?

Take, for instance, hearing from Nāḍī Śāstra or astrology. The scripture says:

> **heyaṁ duḥkham anāgatam |**
> "The suffering that has not yet come is to be avoided."
> (Patañjali Yoga Sūtra 2.16)

Suffering that has not yet arrived should be warded off beforehand. So one should hear in advance what is coming, so that I can take some action. Then is this hearing bhadra or abhadra? The scripture has said "heyaṁ duḥkham anāgatam", yet for many people it becomes abhadra, because they come to know the future in advance.

## The Tale of the King and the Minister

There is a story about this. There was a king, a good king, and he had good ministers. One of those ministers was fine as long as he was fine. But some people who said crooked things, astrologers of the kind called "witches" in English, came to him and said, "You will become king. You are the next king."

As long as all was going well, he was a good minister. But as soon as he heard this prophecy, he thought: if it is a prophecy, then I am bound to become king. Because of this, his mind and his wife's mind were corrupted. He thought: I will become king, but how? As long as this king is alive, I cannot be king. So they plan that he must be killed, now. And they do kill the king.

After the killing they are filled with remorse. It is as though ghosts, spirits and demons follow them everywhere, and they make their life hell. If something like this happened in reality too, tell me: was his hearing bhadra or abhadra?

## The Future Written in a Horoscope

In astrology, in Nāḍī, we sometimes hear things. If one hears and begins to plan accordingly, one has heard bhadra.

But suppose it appears in someone's horoscope: "After ten years I will serve the Guru himself; my liberation is certain." If hearing all this makes him egoistic, the matter is turned upside down. Arey, it speaks of ten years later, so what does that mean? In these ten years you must do something to reach sakhya bhakti, to reach complete self-surrender; only after that perhaps will the Guru listen to you. That is why it appears in the horoscope.

But he feels that today itself he has become something, and if from today he begins to behave that way, perhaps it will never happen after ten years at all. The Guru will send him away right now. Now, was this hearing bhadra or abhadra for him? So **the result that follows decides whether I heard bhadra or abhadra.** This much is now clear.

## Mārkaṇḍeya Ṛṣi: Bitter Words, Auspicious Result

Likewise, someone hears that his horoscope says he will have a heart attack after six years, five years. On hearing it he says, "Gurudev, such and such has happened, what should I do? Please do something for me, where did I go wrong?" And if, because of hearing it, he takes remedial measures, then that hearing was bhadra.

Just as Mārkaṇḍeya Ṛṣi was told that his life span was only sixteen years. His father told him that such a boon had been given: we would have a short-lived son. But do not worry, the son himself says to them. He heard it. Had he not heard it, perhaps he would have died at sixteen. What if his father and mother had never told him? They did tell him. Those were bitter words, but for him they proved bhadra. His life span became sixteen kalpas.

## Phalaśruti: From Nara to Nārāyaṇa

So on what does this bhadra depend? "bhadraṁ karṇebhiḥ śṛṇuyāma devāḥ", do you understand now? What is its phalaśruti? What do you do with it? What is your net outcome? **Whether your journey goes from nara to Nārāyaṇa, from the human to the Divine, is what decides whether you heard bhadra or not.**

## Seeing the Auspicious: The Yajña Bhāva in Everything

Now the second part of the mantra, "bhadraṁ paśyemākṣabhiryajatrāḥ". Bhadra means good, "paśyema" means may we see. So what is called seeing the good? "Yajatrāḥ": yaja means yajña. **Wherever I see the spirit of yajña, wherever I see sacrifice, that is seeing bhadra.**

Watching a vulgar film deliberately is out of the question, but if by mistake one is ever seen, why look at the actress's body? Look at how much yajña, how much sacrifice she must have made to maintain that body. Even in the wrong thing that came before my eyes by mistake, is there yajña or not?

You saw a road, very good. But how many poor labourers must have offered their yajña bhāva to build it, how much sacrifice. If that is not seen, nothing bhadra was seen. Any object that came into my hands: how many people laboured for it, how much yajña bhāva went into it.

When the Guru gives you this knowledge, how great a jñāna yajña it must be, come down through the Guru tradition, through the lineage of the ṛṣis, that I am receiving. If this was not seen, and with a selfish attitude one thought, "I got it, done, let's go," then he saw nothing. One who did not see the yajña has gone away having seen only abhadra.

You saw a big building; what does that mean? The hard work of so many people. Whether it is a sculpture, some art, or a painting, its yajña bhāva should be seen. How many months it took, and not only the time to make that painting, but how much yajña bhāva went into gaining mastery in that art. If this is not seen, you have not seen bhadra; you have seen abhadra.

## The Cycle of Yajña in the Gita

Do you see why one should look this way? Śrī Kṛṣṇa himself explained in the Bhagavad Gita that the Vedas arose from the Supreme, that everything comes through the Vedas, and that the main teaching in them is yajña bhāva. From yajña alone arise rain, nature and everything else, from which we receive all our facilities. If you have the Bhagavad Gita with you, look it up:

> **annād bhavanti bhūtāni parjanyād anna-sambhavaḥ |**
> **yajñād bhavati parjanyo yajñaḥ karma-samudbhavaḥ ||**
> **karma brahmodbhavaṁ viddhi brahmākṣara-samudbhavam |**
> **tasmāt sarva-gataṁ brahma nityaṁ yajñe pratiṣṭhitam ||**
> "All beings arise from food; food arises from rain; rain arises from yajña; yajña arises from action. Know action to arise from the Veda, and the Veda from the Imperishable. Therefore the all-pervading Brahman is ever established in yajña."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.14-15)

All beings are born of food. Food arises from rain, that is, from parjanya. Parjanya means not just rain but the whole of nature. Rain arises from yajña. Yajña means yajña bhāva: where everything is sāttvic and runs on sacrifice, there this happens.

Yajña arises from vihita karma. Vihita means action ordained by the scriptures; and what is not in the scriptures is rahita. Know the whole body of actions to arise from the Veda, because where are ordained actions laid down? In the Vedas. Veda means that divine knowledge which is eternal. And know the Veda to arise from the imperishable Supreme; its origin is there. This proves that the all-pervading, supreme, imperishable Paramātmā is ever established in yajña.

## The Tapasyā Behind the Splendour

So if I am to see, I must see the yajña bhāva. "bhadraṁ paśyemākṣabhiryajatrāḥ". If that is not seen anywhere, then I have seen nothing of God's creation.

If you see even the richest of men, do not look at his luxurious bungalow. If you did not see the tapasyā that he, his family and his lineage must have done behind it, the yajña bhāva, you saw abhadra. And seeing it will bring you some other pain: jealousy will arise, or you will try to copy him, to adopt his style.

## There Is Negative Yajña Too

What if you see that he must have black money? Listen, earning black money also takes a lot of yajña. But that will be negative. Just as there is yajña with mantras of dark energy too, is there not? There is. The practitioner of black magic also has his yajña. A terrorist too needs yajña bhāva to become big. It takes suicide bombers and all the rest. To set off bombs in fifty places at once will take yajña. We are not endorsing it, but even that takes yajña. Kaṁsa too, and Rāvaṇa too, performed yajñas.

So set aside talk of the physical, symbolic yajña. **Yajña actually means sacrifice.**

## The Soldier, the Sportsman and Mother's Laddoo

In your soldier who gives his life for the country, the yajña bhāva should be seen. When there is a sportsman, do not look only at today's century; see his yajña bhāva.

A film becomes a hit or a flop. If it flopped, do not just say that. So many people were behind it. That is prārabdha: sometimes a hit, sometimes a flop; sometimes a match is won, sometimes lost; sometimes an election is won, sometimes lost. What is to be seen behind it? The yajña bhāva.

The same with today's laddoo. Mother made it, and the son likes it. But how his mother raised him, how much sacrifice there was: if that yajña bhāva was not seen at all, then the son saw everything as abhadra. He never saw anything good, anything auspicious in his mother. Have you understood by now?

In the same way, whoever studies, whoever does anything, there is sacrifice at their respective level. **To see bhadra is to see that sacrifice.**
