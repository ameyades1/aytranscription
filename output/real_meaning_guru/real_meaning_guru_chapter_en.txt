# Chapter: Guru Purnima and the Real Meaning of Vyasa

## Why Is Guru Purnima Held on the Full Moon?

Let us first talk about the special occasion of Guru Purnima. Why is this festival celebrated only on the full moon? Why not on the new moon (amavasya)? Do such questions come to your mind? Whatever we do for our ancestors (pitars) is done on the new moon. So why is Guru Purnima on the full moon? Did this basic question arise in your mind?

## The Sun, the Moon and the State of the Guru

Purnima means the full moon. The sun is the symbol of the Self (atma tattva). Those who have done the Navagraha camp will know these nine planets. What does the sun symbolize? The atma. The word atma means "I", my very Self. **I am not this body.**

What happens when the complete reflection of that Self, that Supreme Lord, falls upon the moon? What does the moon symbolize? The mind, the individual soul (jivatma). When the full light of the sun is reflected upon the moon, whose state can this be? Whose state of mind is this? It is **the state of a Guru**.

> "When the full light of the sun is reflected upon it, whose state of mind is this? It is the state of a Guru."

That is why this symbol was given for the Guru, and that is why Guru Purnima falls on the full moon day.

## Vyasa Is Not a Name but a Position

Second, this same day is also called Vyasa Purnima. It is the special day of Vedavyasa. Many people do not even know what the word Vyasa means. People think it was his name, that he was some Guru, the Guru of Gurus and so on. In fact, Vyasa was not his name. His name was **Krishna Dvaipayana**. He is called Vedavyasa.

Veda means knowing, the process of knowing. What we study today as the four Vedas was almost impossible to learn all at once. First tell me, would you be able to study all of that in such detail? And it was a time when your education was not considered complete until everything was memorized. If I made a rule today that you would qualify only when everything was memorized, would that work? It would not.

So all the knowledge we had, the Puranas, the shastras, the Vedas, the Upanishads, he divided into small branches, piece by piece. **Dividing into pieces is what Vyasa means.** The one who divided the Vedas, who separated the Veda into parts, was called Vyasa. Understand it this way: Vyasa is a position.

The Vyasa for whom we celebrate Guru Purnima, Krishna Dvaipayana, is **the twenty-eighth Vyasa**. Many people do not even know what we are celebrating or what this day is. That is why I have told you this first.

## Seventeen Puranas and Still Incomplete

Vedavyasa composed the Puranas. He composed seventeen Puranas. Just as we travel across India, he travelled across all of India in his time, to understand different cultures, to understand all the methods and knowledge, to understand the different kinds of devotion, knowledge and action.

When he had composed the seventeen Puranas, there was knowledge in all of them, a great deal of essential knowledge (tattva jnana). Even so, he could not sleep. He was not content.

## The Bhagavata Purana: Becoming a Wise Devotee

His work was completed when he wrote the eighteenth Purana. Its name is the **Bhagavata Purana**. The Bhagavata Purana is essentially all about devotion (bhakti). He said that without devotion, no knowledge has any meaning.

> "It is essential to become a wise devotee."

We too must become that wise devotee.
