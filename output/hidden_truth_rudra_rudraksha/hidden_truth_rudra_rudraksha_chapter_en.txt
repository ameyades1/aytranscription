# Chapter: The Hidden Mystery of Rudra and Rudraksha

## What Does Rudra Mean?

The Charitramrit has a special section on the rudraksha, and the Shiva Purana has an entire section devoted to it. There, "Rudra" and "Rudraksha" are spoken of. So first let us understand what Rudra means. Does anyone have an idea? Who is called Rudra?

Keep in mind that **raudra** is different. The raudra form means a fiercely angry form. That is not what Rudra means.

## Aditya and Vasu: People Like God

Rudra, Vasu, Aditya: such names of deities appear. You know it is said there are thirty-three koti deities, and among them the twelve Adityas and others are described.

The twelve Adityas mean the twelve months. Basically, a person who thinks of you all the time, walks with you, and lives only for you is called **Aditya**. Aditya is the one who stays with me twenty-four hours a day, twelve months a year, works for me and lives for me.

**Vasu** means that in which we can live, that in which dwelling is possible. So the earth is Vasu. Because of the sun we can stay alive, so the sun too is Vasu. The many things by which we survive, by which our life is sustained, are called Vasu.

Such people are not the Supreme Being, but they are certainly like God.

## Rudra: The One Whose Absence Makes You Cry

The third is Rudra. The root meaning of Rudra is **rudan**, crying. But not merely crying. That whose absence makes you cry is Rudra. In other words, as long as it is there, it seems to have no importance at all.

Take one of your fingernails. These nails never seem important to us. But if one breaks by accident, torn right down to the root, there will be pain, won't there? Then you may cry.

So too with any organ inside you. Today there is no pain in it, so it doesn't even come up. Tell me, when was the last time you thought about your lungs, that they are working and that is why I am alive? When was the last time you thought about your heart, that, poor thing, it is pumping day and night? When was the last time you thought about your spleen, your gall bladder? But if, by accident, some problem arises in them, you may cry, may you not? All such things are called Rudra.

## The Example of Sachin Tendulkar

At one time our cricket team had only one "God of cricket", you know. As long as he played, there was joy, and the moment he was out, the whole team's game seemed to end midway. I am talking about Sachin Tendulkar of the 1990s. For example's sake, he can be called Rudra.

As long as he is there, there is no importance: "Come on, hit it, he's out." But the moment he is gone, you cry.

Any person, any object, as long as it is there, seems to be simply ours; we don't even bother about it. But if it leaves our life, or its use, its function, stops even a little, it will bring you to the point of tears.

## Rudraksha: The Guru's Tears

Now the rudraksha. **Rudra** means Lord Shiva, and **aksha** means the tears of the eyes. It is said that Shiva's tears fell and the rudraksha arose from them. All of that is an imaginary tale. Its real meaning is: **the Guru is Shiva, Shiva is the Guru.** So this is about the Guru.

As long as the Guru is in our life, as long as the Guru's grace flows, everything of mine goes well. That too happens by the Guru's grace. Even for an atheist, whatever is going well is going well because of some Guru's grace from a past life.

See the Bhagavad Gita, chapter nine, verses twenty and twenty-one. There Lord Krishna says clearly that a person experiences the vast heaven through past merit, and past merit arises from some Guru's grace.

> **te taṁ bhuktvā svarga-lokaṁ viśālaṁ kṣīṇe puṇye martya-lokaṁ viśanti**
> "Having enjoyed the vast heavenly world, when their merit is exhausted they return to the world of mortals."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.21)

As soon as that merit is used up, the time for crying will come again. So does the Guru also qualify to be called Rudra or not?

Have you understood the definition of Rudra? I am giving a definition entirely at the level of Vedanta. I am not teaching some general dictionary meaning, not a word-for-word translation, but the deep symbolic meaning.

## When Does the Guru Shed Tears?

So rudraksha means his tears, the Guru's tears. Do you know when the Guru will shed tears? What joy or sorrow does he have? But he is human too. So why would he cry? When he sees that the world does not follow Vedanta correctly, as is happening now.

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> "Whenever there is a decline of dharma, O Bharata."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

When the decline of dharma is visible, when everyone is seen going astray and adharma is growing, at that time the Guru feels sorrow.

## The Example of the Cancer Patient

I say this only as an example, may it happen to no one, and if you practise Shiva sadhana, Rudra sadhana, it will not happen at all. Suppose someone has cancer. Even if eighty percent of the cells in the body are damaged, it is the last stage, it has reached multiple organ failure. Even then, does anyone say, "Oh, eighty percent is damaged anyway, a hundred percent is going to go, so let me die"? Has even a single person ever said this?

He still goes to the doctor as the last hope, that this eighty percent will still be healed. Even if only twenty percent of the body is healthy, he feels that the eighty percent will be healed. He feels there is still a chance, something will happen. He may be crying, but he feels it can still happen.

Why does he feel this? Because it is my own body, and I want to live.

## The Guru's Vast "Self"

In the same way is the Guru, whose "self" has become so vast that it has spread through the whole world.

> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "Such a great soul, who knows that Vasudeva is everything, is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

When he has reached such a state, and in it he sees eighty percent of people gone bad, sees corruption, sees negativity, sees everything, what do you think? He too must feel like crying, must feel bad.

Yet he is a person who does not lose hope. He sees that some remedy is still possible. Like a patient heading towards death, the whole earth is going into decline in the Kali Yuga, yet there must still be some chance. The one who takes that chance is the Guru.

Why? Because his self is so vast. He feels: I will do something, I will stop it, I will make a resolve (sankalp), I will do sadhana, I will bring awakening, I will give shaktipat. As a last remedy, the Guru feels this. Is it making sense? When it is our own body, we try for it till the last moment. The Guru's self is the whole world.

## Rudra Is Within You Too

Rudra is within you as well. The five organs of action (karmendriyas), the five organs of knowledge (jnanendriyas), and the one mind are called Rudra. Why? Because if these too go wrong, the same crying will come.

Rudra means everything that makes you cry because of its **absence**, not its presence. Crying because of someone's presence is a different matter; you may marry, a spouse comes in front of you, and then the trouble starts! That is a different subject. Here we are speaking of crying from absence.

## The Symbolic Meaning of Wearing Rudraksha

So what was there to learn from all this? The symbolic meaning of wearing a rudraksha. What its scientific use is, is not the discussion here at all. All that comes in Purva Mimamsa, in the ritual portion of the Vedas. We do not want a worldly-level explanation. We want what Uttara Mimamsa, that is Vedanta, teaches, what knowledge teaches.

I myself do not wear a rudraksha. If needed I sometimes wear one; if there isn't one, I won't, it makes no difference. After reaching a certain level, you need no support of any kind. I am not talking about the science at all; I am talking about the symbolic meaning.

Symbolically, wearing a rudraksha means: **remembering the Guru every moment so much that before I do any wrong action, I remember the Guru first: will there be tears in his eyes?**

I am about to eat something wrong; I went to a hotel and these are tamasic, rajasic foods; immediately I should remember the Guru.

## The Third Between You and the World: The Guru

There are two things: one is you, and the other is some object, person or place you are dealing with. Between these there will always be a third, and that will be the Guru.

- Going to buy clothes: will the Guru like it?
- Going to eat food: will the Guru like it?
- Going to visit such a place: will the Guru be pleased?
- Taking some shortcut in worship: will the Guru like it?

Every time you begin to see this, "my Guru will have tears in his eyes", that is, he will be unhappy with me, the one in whose mind this feeling arises has truly worn the rudraksha. This is what Vedanta says.

## Not the Vedas, but Vedanta

The Vedas will say something else. A class on the Vedas is not going on here. Classes on the Vedas will go on everywhere else in the world. And in the end, what did the Lord say? Forget all this:

> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> "Abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

What He said is final. Does this dharma make sense?

## Conclusion: Devotion to the Guru Is the Rudraksha

I explained the definition of Rudra and explained the rudraksha. Shiva is the Guru, the Guru is Shiva. In the absence of the Guru, of the Guru's grace, one may cry. So wearing the rudraksha means feeling, with every action of mine: what will happen, will this bring a fault against the Guru? The arising of this feeling is itself wearing the rudraksha.

**As devotion to the Guru grows, the rudraksha is worn automatically.**
