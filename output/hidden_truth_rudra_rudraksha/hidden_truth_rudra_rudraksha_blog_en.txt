# The Real Meaning of Rudraksha: The Guru's Tears

The moment we hear the word rudraksha, we picture a mala or a sacred bead. The Shiva Purana has an entire section on the rudraksha. But have you ever wondered what the word "Rudra" truly means?

In this discourse, Acharya Upendra ji gives not a dictionary meaning but a deep symbolic meaning at the level of Vedanta. And this meaning touches your everyday life.

## Rudra Is Not Anger

First, let us clear up a confusion. **Raudra** and **Rudra** are different. The raudra form means a fiercely angry form. That is not what Rudra means.

Among the thirty-three koti deities, the names Rudra, Vasu and Aditya appear. Before understanding Rudra, understand Aditya and Vasu.

## Aditya and Vasu: The Foundations of Our Life

**Aditya** (the twelve Adityas, meaning the twelve months) is the one who thinks of you all the time, walks with you, and lives only for you. Twenty-four hours a day, twelve months a year.

**Vasu** (the basis of dwelling) is that in which we can live. The earth is Vasu. The sun, because of which we are alive, is also Vasu.

Such people are not the Supreme Being, but they are certainly like God.

*Who in your life lives for you twenty-four hours a day, twelve months a year?*

## Rudra: The One Whose Absence Makes You Cry

The root meaning of Rudra is **rudan** (crying). But not merely crying. That whose absence makes you cry is Rudra.

As long as it is there, it seems to have no importance. Take your fingernail. It never seems important. But if it accidentally tears down to the root, the pain can make you cry.

When was the last time you thought about your lungs? About your heart, that, poor thing, it pumps day and night? About your spleen, your gall bladder? But if even a small problem arises in them, you may cry. All such things are called Rudra.

## The God of Cricket

Acharya ji gives a lovely example: Sachin Tendulkar of the 1990s. Our team had only one "God of cricket". As long as he played, there was joy. The moment he was out, the whole team's game seemed to end right there.

As long as he is there, no importance: "Come on, hit it, he's out." But the moment he leaves, you cry.

Any person or object, as long as it is there, seems to be simply ours. But if it leaves our life, or its function stops even a little, it brings us to the point of tears.

*Who or what in your life will you value only after it is gone?*

## Rudraksha: Shiva's Tears or the Guru's?

**Rudra** means Shiva and **aksha** means tears. The story says rudrakshas arose from Shiva's tears. Acharya ji says all of that is an imaginary tale. Its real meaning is:

> "The Guru is Shiva, Shiva is the Guru."

When everything in life is going well, that too is by the Guru's grace. Even for an atheist, whatever is going well is going well because of some Guru's grace from a past life.

> **te taṁ bhuktvā svarga-lokaṁ viśālaṁ kṣīṇe puṇye martya-lokaṁ viśanti**
> "Having enjoyed the vast heavenly world, when their merit is exhausted they return to the world of mortals."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.21)

Through past merit a person enjoys the pleasures of the vast heaven, and as soon as the merit is exhausted, returns to the world of mortals. That merit was formed by some Guru's grace. As soon as it ends, the time for crying will come. That is why the Guru too is Rudra.

## Why Does the Guru Cry?

So rudraksha means the Guru's tears. But what joy or sorrow does the Guru have? Why would he cry?

When he sees that the world does not follow Vedanta correctly.

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> "Whenever there is a decline of dharma, O Bharata."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

Whenever the decline of dharma is visible and adharma grows, at that time the Guru feels sorrow.

## The Patient in the Last Stage

Acharya ji gives an example, saying may it happen to no one, and for those who practise Shiva sadhana it will not happen at all.

Someone has cancer. Eighty percent of the cells are damaged, it is the last stage, organs have begun to fail. Does anyone say, "Eighty percent is gone anyway, so let me die"? No one says that.

He goes to the doctor with a last hope. He may be crying, but he feels there is still a chance. Why? Because it is his own body, and he wants to live.

## The One Whose "Self" Is the Whole World

Now think of the Guru whose "self" has become so vast that the whole world is contained in it.

> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "Such a great soul, who knows that Vasudeva is everything, is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

When such a Guru sees eighty percent of people gone bad, sees corruption and negativity, he too must feel like crying.

But he does not lose hope. The whole earth is going into decline in the Kali Yuga, yet there must still be some chance.

> "The one who takes that chance is the Guru."

The Guru thinks: I will do something. I will stop it, I will make a **sankalp** (resolve), I will do **sadhana** (spiritual practice), I will bring awakening, I will give **shaktipat** (the transmission of spiritual energy from Guru to disciple). Just as we try for our own body till the last moment, the Guru does so for the whole world.

## The Eleven Rudras Within You

Rudra is within you too. The five **karmendriyas** (organs of action), the five **jnanendriyas** (organs of perception) and the one mind are called Rudra. If any of them goes wrong, you will cry.

Note that Rudra is what makes you cry by its **absence**, not its presence. Acharya ji says with a laugh that crying because of someone's presence, like when a spouse appears in front of you after marriage, is a different subject!

## The Real Meaning of Wearing Rudraksha

The scientific use of the rudraksha is not the subject here. All that comes in **Purva Mimamsa** (the ritual portion of the Vedas). Here the subject is **Uttara Mimamsa**, that is, **Vedanta** (the path of knowledge).

Acharya ji himself says he does not wear a rudraksha. Sometimes he wears one, and if there isn't one, it makes no difference. After a certain level, no support is needed.

Symbolically, wearing a rudraksha means: remembering the Guru every moment so much that before any wrong action, the Guru comes to mind first: **"Will there be tears in his eyes?"**

## Between You and the World: The Guru

On one side are you, on the other some person, object or place you are dealing with. Between them there will always be a third: the Guru.

Going to buy clothes, will the Guru like it? Tamasic, rajasic food in front of you at a hotel, will the Guru like it? Going to visit some place, will the Guru be pleased? Taking a shortcut in worship, will the Guru like it?

> "My Guru will have tears in his eyes": the one in whose mind this feeling arises has worn the rudraksha.

*Would the Guru have been pleased with everything you did today?*

## Not the Vedas, but Vedanta

Classes on the Vedas will go on all over the world. But in the end, what did the Lord say? Forget all this:

> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> "Abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

Abandoning all dharmas, take **sharan** (refuge) in Me alone. This is the final word.

## Summary

Shiva is the Guru, the Guru is Shiva. Wearing the rudraksha means feeling before every action: will this bring a fault against the Guru? **As devotion to the Guru grows, the rudraksha is worn automatically.**

- **Rudra** does not mean anger but that whose absence makes you cry.
- **Aditya** is the one who lives for you every moment; **Vasu** is that on whose support you live.
- **Rudraksha** means the Guru's tears, because the Guru is Shiva.
- The Guru cries when the world strays from dharma, but he does not lose hope.
- The five organs of action, the five organs of perception and the mind are the Rudras within you.
- Remembering the Guru every moment, between you and the world, is the true wearing of the rudraksha.
