# Chapter: Mahalaya Shraddha and the Truth About the Pitars

## The Question: Who Are the Pitars?

Some questions about Pitru Paksha often come to our minds, yet we do not know their right answers. Who are the Pitars? Does a person become a Pitar after death? What is the real meaning of Shraddha and Tarpan? Why are these fifteen days of Pitru Paksha, which come after Ganapati, considered so special? And when we call a place haunted by ghosts and spirits, does that too have some connection with the Pitars? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji shed light on these questions in simple language.

## The Sequence of Chaturmas: Brahmacharya, Discernment and Liberation

Namaskar. As soon as Chaturmas begins, the first festival to arrive is Raksha Bandhan. The expectation there is that a sister is not protected just like that. Only one who can himself observe brahmacharya can protect.

After that come Ganesh Chaturthi and the other festivals we celebrate, and in them my expectation is that our faculty of discernment, *vivek buddhi*, awakens.

One who observes brahmacharya, who has become strong, and whose discernment has also awakened, only such a person becomes capable of liberating his ancestors. This is the root meaning of this sequence.

## The Three Rites of Pitru Paksha

Pitru Paksha is a period of fifteen days that comes every year. In it we do three things: Shraddha, Pinda Daan and Tarpan.

**Shraddha** means to remember our ancestors with faith (*shraddha*), and to remind them that we are all happy here. Whatever Shraddha mantra the pandit ji recites, or whatever scripture you look at, its meaning is this: "We are all happy here. Wherever you go, to your next world, your next birth, or anywhere else, become free. Do not get stuck here." This is the essence of Shraddha. It is a rite performed with faith.

The second rite is **Pinda Daan**. To understand it, we must first understand the word *pinda*. What is it that we give away? It is said:

> **yathā piṇḍe tathā brahmāṇḍe**
> "As in the body, so in the cosmos."
> (Traditional scriptural saying)

As it is in the pinda, so it is in the cosmos. Pinda means this whole body of mine, and along with it my mind, my intellect, everything. This is what I have to give away, that is, offer completely; not only for the Pitars, but for liberation, for the welfare of the world. Because I am only a part of my ancestors.

It is said that as soon as any one person in the whole lineage becomes liberated, all the ancestors of his household, of his clan, become liberated. When one person in the family becomes *jivanmukta*, liberated while living, many people attain liberation. This is how it unfolds.

The third rite is **Tarpan**. Tarpan simply means giving thanks. That is why there is not only Pitru Tarpan. In Shraddha we do Pitru Tarpan, but at other times there is also Deva Tarpan and Rishi Tarpan, that is, we also thank the gods and the sages.

Our ancestors gave us this body, gave us honour and respect, wealth and property, and our clan and *gotra* in society. They raised us from childhood and gave us education. For all of this we give thanks. And these are not only our parents, but all our ancestors across many generations. This is the main rite of Pitru Paksha.

## When the Soul Gets Stuck

Our ancestors, someone's father, mother, and above them grandfather, great-grandfather, maternal grandparents, from both sides, are fine as long as they are alive. But according to Hindu belief, as soon as they leave the body, either they should attain liberation, that is, moksha, or their next birth should take place somewhere.

If neither of these happens, then the body is gone, but the *jivatma*, the individual soul, still remains. If some desires and cravings are stuck in its mind, it can become attached to an object, a person or a place.

If it becomes attached to some object, some food or anything else, it will keep coming back for it again and again. If it becomes attached to a person, then wherever you go, your Pitars will keep following you, because they are attached to you, until one of the two, you or they, lets go of that bond.

And sometimes a place, some house, is so dear to them that they do not leave it, and do not let anyone else come there either. Then that place becomes like a haunted house, a ghost bungalow. The things we hear about fall into this category.

## Do Not Live in This Misunderstanding

So these Pitars are our own ancestors, our own people, who have left this body but have neither gone to the next world, nor taken their next birth, nor become liberated. This is a very frightening state. It is not a good state at all.

We think that they were only our parents, or our grandparents; what harm would they do to us, they were simple, innocent people. The scriptures describe that in such a state, do not live in this misunderstanding.

Scriptures such as the Garuda Purana tell us that once they left this body they became *preta*; after the preta state they came into the Pitar state. After that, if you neglected them, they can be just as harmful, and can cause just as much suffering. At that time the old relationship no longer remains.

They are connected to you, and for some time they wait for you to do something for them, to make an effort for their liberation. And if you do not, that very waiting slowly turns into a curse, into a *dosha*. This is what is called **Pitru Dosha** or **Pitru Shaap**.

## No Negligence in Duties to the Gods and the Ancestors

That is why, if you read the Taittiriya Upanishad, it says:

> **devapitṛkāryābhyāṃ na pramaditavyam**
> "One should never be negligent in duties towards the gods and the ancestors."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

One should never be careless in the duties to the gods and the ancestors. These must be done with full seriousness, after studying them in depth. They cannot be done carelessly. One has to put one's whole heart into them.

Further on, the same Upanishad says:

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Regard your mother as a god, regard your father as a god, regard your teacher as a god."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

Even while they are alive, all of them are to be regarded as gods, so after they are gone too, all these rites must be performed with the same reverence.

During Pitru Paksha, most of these duties and rites take place. One has to set aside special time for them. This is not something to be done on the go, having heard a little something and done it. It must be done with one's whole mind.

## Why Mahalaya Shraddha?

The second point is that our ancestors leave at different times; someone will pass away in April, someone in May, someone in December. If I have to sit and perform Shraddha on each one's date, I will keep doing only this all year round.

Think of a young man today. His grandfather's date, then his grandmother's, his maternal grandmother's, his paternal uncle's, his maternal uncle's, all on different dates. That young man will keep doing Shraddha all year, will keep worshipping only the Pitars. In the Shrimad Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna himself says:

> **yānti deva-vratā devān pitṝn yānti pitṛ-vratāḥ**
> **bhūtāni yānti bhūtejyā yānti mad-yājino 'pi mām**
> "Those who worship the gods go to the gods, those who worship the ancestors go to the ancestors, those who worship spirits go to the spirits, and those who worship Me come to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.25)

Those who worship the gods attain the gods; those who worship the Pitars attain the Pitars; those who practise the worship of ghosts and spirits attain ghosts and spirits; and those who worship Me attain Me alone.

So by remembering the Pitars again and again, that person will himself become a Pitar. The sages did not consider this a proper destination.

That is why it is generally taught that as soon as a person leaves the body, a Shraddha is performed immediately, called **Ekoddishta Shraddha**, that is, a Shraddha with a single purpose. After that comes the monthly Shraddha, and then, when a year is complete, the annual Shraddha. If Shraddha is performed on that date like this for three years, that is fine.

After that, all the ancestors there are must have passed away on one date or another. That is why there is the provision of **Mahalaya Shraddha**, which is to be done during those fifteen days of Pitru Paksha. If the Shraddha of all the Pitars is done together, that is sufficient. This is what we call Pitru Paksha.

In this way no one's ancestor is left out either. It is done collectively, and on the day when everyone all over the world performs it, no one's ancestor remains left over. It is with this purpose that Pitru Paksha was established within Chaturmas.

## The Immediate Fruit of Sadhana

Why do I invite you to Antar Yog again and again? Because here people receive the *phalashruti*, the fruit of the practice, on this principle, here and now.

Just as science says that if you press the button, the fan or the light must come on, whether a poor man presses it or a king, a sannyasi or a thief, an old man or a young one; in the same way, the sadhanas at Antar Yog, whether the sadhana of Ganesh Vidya, Shri Vidya sadhana, Saraswati Diksha, the sadhana of Pitru Mukti or healing, or Shiva sadhana, bring their fruit quickly, to most people and in greater measure.

So do come here, take the benefit of this practice, and leave with direct experience. Thank you.
