# Why Mahalaya Shraddha Matters: The Truth About Our Ancestors

As soon as **Pitru Paksha** (the fortnight dedicated to the ancestors) arrives, questions start to stir. Who are the ancestors we honour? Does everyone become one after death? What do **shraddha** (rites performed for the departed) and **tarpan** (offerings of water to the departed) really mean?

There is one more question, usually asked in a lowered voice: do the houses we call haunted, or possessed by spirits, have anything to do with our ancestors? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji answers all of these in very simple language.

The Mahalaya Shraddha meaning, in short: a single rite honouring all of one's ancestors together during the fifteen days of Pitru Paksha. According to Acharya Upendra Ji, dwelling on the ancestors on separate dates all year pulls a person toward becoming an ancestral spirit, so all are honoured at once and no one's ancestor is left out.

## Chaturmas Is a Journey, Not Just a List of Festivals

The first festival of **Chaturmas** (the four sacred months of the monsoon season) is Raksha Bandhan, when a sister ties a protective thread on her brother's wrist. Acharya Ji points out that a sister is not protected by the thread alone. Only someone who can live by **brahmacharya** (self-restraint) is able to protect her.

Next comes Ganesh Chaturthi, whose purpose is to awaken **viveka** (discernment, the ability to tell right from wrong).

After that comes Pitru Paksha. Notice the order: the person who is self-controlled, strong and awake in discernment is the one who can help free their ancestors.

## What Is the Difference Between Shraddha, Pinda Daan and Tarpan?

Three rites fill the fifteen days of Pitru Paksha: shraddha, pinda daan and tarpan. Each of them carries a deep meaning.

## Shraddha: "We Are Happy Here, You Move On"

The name of the rite, shraddha, comes from **śraddhā** (reverent faith). It means remembering your ancestors with reverence and reminding them that all of you here are happy.

Whatever mantras the priest recites and whatever texts they consult, the essence stays the same: "We are all happy here. Go on and find freedom in your next world or your next birth. Do not stay stuck here."

## Pinda Daan: Offering Yourself

To understand the **pinda** (the body, offered symbolically as a ball of rice), recall an ancient maxim:

> **yathā piṇḍe tathā brahmāṇḍe**
> "As in the body, so in the cosmos."
> (A traditional scriptural saying)

The pinda is my whole being: my body, my mind, my intellect, everything. **Pinda daan** (the offering of the pinda) means surrendering this entire existence, not for the ancestors alone, but for liberation and for the good of the world.

Why? Because I am a part of my ancestors. It is said that when a single person in a family becomes **jivanmukta** (liberated while still alive), all the ancestors of that family are freed.

*Have you ever considered that your own spiritual growth could open the door to freedom for your entire lineage?*

## Tarpan: A Simple Thank You

At heart, **tarpan** means giving thanks. Acharya Ji puts it in the plainest words: thank you.

Tarpan is not reserved for the ancestors. During shraddha you offer it to them, but at other times there is also **deva tarpan** and **rishi tarpan**, thanking the gods and the sages as well.

Our ancestors gave us this body, our honour and standing, our wealth, our family line and **gotra** (clan lineage). They raised us and educated us. And these are not only our parents but many generations who came before them. Tarpan is gratitude to all of them.

## When the Soul Cannot Move On

In the Hindu understanding, a soul that leaves the body has two natural paths: **moksha** (liberation), or the next birth.

What if neither happens? The body is gone, but the **jivatma** (the individual soul) remains. If some **vasana** (a lingering desire) or craving is still caught in the mind, the soul can attach itself to one of three things: an object, a person or a place.

- **Attachment to an object:** It may be a favourite food or something else, and the soul will keep returning to it again and again.
- **Attachment to a person:** Wherever you go, your ancestors will follow close behind, because they are bound to you, until one of you lets go.
- **Attachment to a place:** A house may be so dear to them that they neither leave it nor let anyone else move in. That house becomes what people call a haunted house.

## The Mistaken Belief That "They Were Our Own"

We often think: "They were our parents, our grandparents, simple and good-hearted people. What harm could they possibly do to us?" Acharya Ji warns us not to hold on to this misconception.

He explains that according to scriptures such as the Garuda Purana, which describes the journey of the soul after death, a person who leaves the body first becomes a **preta** (a restless spirit of the newly departed) and then passes from that state into the state of a **pitru** (an ancestral spirit). In that state the old relationship no longer holds. If they are neglected, they can cause just as much suffering.

For a while they wait, hoping you will do something for their freedom. If you do nothing, that waiting slowly turns into affliction and curse. This is what is known as **Pitru Dosha** (the affliction that arises from neglected ancestors).

## Not a Task to Rush Through

The Taittiriya Upanishad gives a clear instruction:

> **devapitṛkāryābhyāṁ na pramaditavyam**
> "Never be careless in your duties to the gods and to the ancestors."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

You need to perform these rites seriously and with full understanding. The same Upanishad goes on to say:

> **mātṛdevo bhava | pitṛdevo bhava | ācāryadevo bhava ||**
> "Regard your mother as a god, regard your father as a god, regard your teacher as a god."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

Those you revered like gods while they lived deserve the same respect in the rites you perform after they are gone. That means setting aside dedicated time for it. You cannot listen to a little, go through the motions and be done; you have to give it your whole heart.

*How do you remember your ancestors: as a ritual to get through, or with your whole heart and attention?*

## Why Mahalaya Shraddha, Then?

Now a practical question. One relative passed away in April, another in May, another in December. Grandfather, grandmother, maternal grandmother, uncles on both sides: each has a different date. A young man who performed a shraddha on every one of those dates would spend the whole year doing nothing else.

There is also a danger in this. In the Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna says:

> **yānti devavratā devān pitṝn yānti pitṛvratāḥ |**
> **bhūtāni yānti bhūtejyā yānti madyājino 'pi mām ||**
> "Those who worship the gods go to the gods, those who worship the ancestors go to the ancestors, those who worship spirits go to the spirits, and those who worship Me come to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.25)

Acharya Ji explains that a person who keeps dwelling on the ancestors, again and again, will end up becoming an ancestral spirit themselves.

The sages did not consider this a good destination. They therefore set up a system:

1. Right after death, the **ekoddishta shraddha** (a rite performed for that one person)
2. Then the **masik shraddha** (the monthly rite)
3. Then the **varshik shraddha** (the annual rite), on the same date for three years

After that, all the ancestors are honoured together in the **Mahalaya Shraddha** (the collective rite for all ancestors) during the fifteen days of Pitru Paksha. Nobody's ancestor is left out. On the day when people all over the world perform it together, no one's ancestor remains forgotten. This is why Pitru Paksha has its place within Chaturmas.

## Press the Switch, and the Light Comes On

Acharya Ji compares sadhana (spiritual practice) to science. Press the switch and the fan or the light is bound to come on, whether a poor man presses it or a king, a monk or a thief, someone old or someone young.

In the same way, the **phalashruti** (the fruit of a practice) of the Antar Yog sadhanas comes here and now, without delay: Ganesha Vidya, Shri Vidya, Saraswati Diksha, the sadhana for Pitru Mukti (freeing the ancestors) or healing, and Shiva sadhana. Acharya Ji invites you to come, receive the benefit of these methods and leave with a direct experience of your own.

## Key Takeaways

- Chaturmas unfolds in sequence: self-restraint at Raksha Bandhan, discernment at Ganesh Chaturthi, and the liberation of the ancestors in Pitru Paksha.
- The essence of **shraddha** is telling your ancestors: "We are happy. Go and be free; do not stay stuck here."
- **Pinda daan** means offering your body, mind and intellect for liberation and the good of the world; one jivanmukta in a family frees many ancestors.
- **Tarpan** means thank you: gratitude to the ancestors, the gods and the sages.
- A lingering desire binds the soul to an object, a person or a place; this is where stories of haunted houses come from.
- The waiting of neglected ancestors slowly turns into Pitru Dosha; do not fall for the belief that "they were our own."
- Perform your duties to the gods and the ancestors wholeheartedly, never carelessly.
- Honouring ancestors on separate dates all year pulls you toward becoming an ancestral spirit yourself; after three years, honour them all together in the Mahalaya Shraddha.
