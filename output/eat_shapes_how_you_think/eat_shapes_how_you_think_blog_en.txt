# Eat the Food of the Land You Were Born In

Today, with a single click, we can order dishes from anywhere in the world. But is everything within our reach actually meant for us?

Acharya Upendra Ji offers a simple yet profound principle: **eat food that is grown locally.**

## Can Every Flavour Become Your Way of Life?

Acharya Ji says that Chinese food is for a person from China, not for you. You can enjoy it now and then, but it cannot become your way of life.

Mexican or Thai, it is fine to eat such food for a day or two in an emergency or at a celebration. But if it turns into your daily habit, then, in Acharya Ji's words, God will say, *"You will fall ill."*

## The Soil, the Earth Element and Destiny

This is about more than taste or health. Acharya Ji links it to **karma** and to **prarabdha** (the destiny shaped by your past actions).

The land where you are born holds the secret of your karma. That soil is meant for you, and so is its **prithvi tattva** (the earth element).

Whatever grows from that earth is meant for the people born on it. Living beings eat the plants that grow around them, and, as Acharya Ji explains, the qualities they eat become their own.

*Where was the food you eat every day grown? Have you ever asked yourself what qualities it is filling you with?*

## Your Region's Food Is God's Design for You

You were born in India and you live in India, so Indian food is meant for you. More specifically, the food of your own region is meant for you.

If you live in Bengal, Bengali food will suit you best. If you live in Maharashtra, the food grown there will appeal to you most.

Acharya Ji goes further: it will not merely appeal to you. **God has made that very food right for you.**

## Does This Mean Other Cuisines Are Forbidden?

No. Acharya Ji makes it clear that his first point was to develop a taste for your own local food. He never said you must not try anything else.

Feel free to sample other grains and other styles of cooking for a change of taste. That is perfectly fine.

Suppose someone asks, "I live in Maharashtra, but I love idli-sambar. Is that all right?" Acharya Ji's answer is yes, there is no problem at all.

Think of it this way: you do not belong to Maharashtra alone. You belong to the vast land called Bharat, and **you are Indian.** The food of all of India suits you.

*Is the taste of your own land still the foundation of your meals, or has it become an occasional guest on your plate?*

## Key Takeaways

- Eat **locally grown food**; let foreign cuisine be an occasional treat, not a way of life.
- According to Acharya Ji, making foreign food your daily diet leads to illness.
- The land of your birth, its soil and its **earth element** are tied to your karma and your **destiny**.
- The qualities of what you eat become your own qualities.
- The food of your own region suits you best, and, more broadly, the food of all of India suits you too.
- Trying other flavours is not forbidden; just keep your local food as the foundation.
