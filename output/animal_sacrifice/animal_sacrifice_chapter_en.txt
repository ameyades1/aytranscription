# Chapter: The True Meaning of Animal Sacrifice

## The Symbolic Language of the Scriptures

Let us look a little at the method of the Savitra Kathaka Agnichayana Mahayajna, as given in the Vedas. The question is: are the Vedas written in a coded language, or in a plain, open one? If anyone could read them and understand, what need would there be for a Guru? Is scripture a self-help book?

The scriptures that have been written are in a coded language, so that not everyone understands them easily. They are symbolic, so that they remain protected. One must go to a Guru to decode them. It is the Guru who teaches the correct meaning.

## The Mention of Animal Sacrifice in the Vedas

In that method a single sentence is mentioned: if your Savitra Chayana Yajna is to succeed, a yajna must be performed, and in that yajna an animal must be killed, it must be sacrificed.

Now what would the literal meaning be? Exactly what is happening outside: kill this, kill that, kill a goat, kill an elephant, or, whatever anyone says, kill a mouse. Sacrifice is offered physically outside, but will it do you any good? Can any good ever come to you by causing pain to another? Even in other traditions and other religions where this was done, their own scriptures say it should not have been done.

In the Savitra Kathaka, in the Vedas, it is mentioned that until there is an animal sacrifice, there will be no change of age, no awakening of the Self. Does that seem literally true to you? Or must this be coded language? It certainly must be coded.

## The Meaning of Pashu: Pasha

The word *pashu* (animal) comes from *pasha*. Pasha means that which keeps you bound. Animal life is bound to nature. It cannot go beyond nature; it stays stuck right here, in Prakriti.

The qualities within me that make me an animal are my lust, anger, greed, attachment, arrogance, envy, hypocrisy, pride, fear, grief, harshness and cruelty. All this demonic wealth, all these negative qualities make me an animal. It is this animal that must be killed, this that must be sacrificed.

## Which Yajna, Which Fire Pit?

Now the question will arise: how, and in which yajna, is this sacrifice to be made? What size should the fire pit be? A chakra fire pit, an outer fire pit or an inner one? The answer is in the fourth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, which speaks about yajna. Verse thirty-three of the fourth chapter is very important:

> **śreyān dravyamayād yajñāj jñāna-yajñaḥ parantapa**
> **sarvaṁ karmākhilaṁ pārtha jñāne parisamāpyate**
> "O scorcher of foes, the sacrifice of knowledge is superior to the sacrifice of material things. All action, in its entirety, O Partha, culminates in knowledge."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.33)

Among all yajnas, O Arjuna, scorcher of foes, the yajna of knowledge is far superior to the yajna of material things. According to Shri Krishna, Jnana Yajna is far, far higher than any Dravya Yajna.

## What Is Dravya Yajna?

Dravya Yajna means whatever you have done in life. A soldier goes to the border and performs the yajna of battle. Yajna means fire, Agni. And Agni means there must be some **burning desire** within. Into that fire, offerings are poured.

That which satisfies is called *anna*, food. What fills the stomach is the food we eat. Thoughts are the food of our mind; all thoughts are food for the mind. Whatever is seen outside is *dravya*, material. So whatever outer things you pour into the fire, whatever you do, is called Dravyamaya Yajna. And what is superior to Dravyamaya Yajna? Jnana Yajna, far superior.

## When Does Jnana Yajna Happen?

When will what is happening here be called a Jnana Yajna? When there is a Guru who has a burning desire to teach you. And first there must be a disciple who has a burning desire to learn. Then it becomes the fire.

Offerings are given into the fire, *samidha* (sacred firewood) is offered. Your samidha is made of ignorance, of darkness; that is offered first. This is why disciples in olden times went to the Guru carrying samidha. It was symbolic: "I will drop all this first." They went to the Guru to remove ignorance, but first the samidha of ego. Doing this, one then has to learn.

## Purification in the Fire

Put gold into fire and it becomes pure. Copper or any other metal: the noble metals shine and the rest, the dross, burns away. In the same way, your negative qualities should burn away when you go to the Guru, and what is positive, the charity you give, the service you do, the love you give, should become more purified and rise to higher and higher levels. This is what is expected from a true yajna.

## Every Action Ends in Knowledge

The yajna of knowledge is far superior to the yajna of material things, and all actions culminate in knowledge. It is said here with certainty: what will every single action finally turn into? Knowledge.

If your sacrifice for your family resulted in a divorce, what does that mean? That action turned into some knowledge: how not to lead a married life. Isn't that right?

If a thief goes to jail, he has learnt a lesson from that action, that he should not have done it this way. Too much food, the wrong food, and the doctor said get surgery; he learnt a lesson from that food, that he should not have eaten like that. And is all this not written in the Gita? Be honest, eat sattvic food. So every action of yours will finally end in knowledge.

## Through Bitter Experience, or Beforehand?

An old person says, doesn't he: "My hair has not turned white for nothing. I have seen twenty-five more rainy seasons than you." He says he has seen so much more. So what happened? That knowledge came to him, at least of how not to live a life.

So tell me rightly, what is better? To learn how not to live through bitter experiences, as life is ending, or to receive that knowledge in childhood itself? Which is far better?

## The Meaning of the Method

So now is the method understood? What has been said? The animal must be killed, offered into the yajna, and only then will your Atma and your sun become radiant, become awakened. Do you understand its meaning? You do not have to kill a real animal.

## At the End of Many Births

This becomes complete when we understand the final point, the nineteenth verse of the seventh chapter:

> **bahūnāṁ janmanām ante jñānavān māṁ prapadyate**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "At the end of many births, the one possessed of knowledge surrenders to Me, realising that Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

*Bahunam* means many. *Bahunam janmanam ante* means after many births, in the last birth. This is the birth of liberation; it speaks of someone at the state of Kaivalya, a Sadguru or an Avatar. After many, many births comes the last birth. He is a *jnanavan*, one possessed of knowledge; would you not call someone whose birth of liberation is certain a jnani? He is an Avatar, a Sadguru.

*Jnanavan mam prapadyate.* Shripad must also have worshipped. Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped Kali. All the other Gurus also do some worship of their own. So whom do they worship? Do they worship an idol, a picture or a yantra? Whom do these great souls, these Avatars, worship?

This is the Bhagavad Gita, so here it is "Vasudeva". If it were the Shiva Purana it would be Shiva; if it were the Datta Prabodh, Datta would come. Put the name of your own chosen deity there. *Vasudevah sarvam iti* means: all is God. Now for him, no single idol, picture, yantra or ring remains God; that is called *anya devata*, another deity.

## Sa Mahatma Sudurlabhah

*Vasudevah sarvam iti*: the very moment he realises this, *sa mahatma*, he becomes a Mahatma. His Agnichayana Yajna is complete; at that level, Savita awakens. He is a Mahatma, but what kind? *Sudurlabhah*, such a person is very rare. Will you find such people anywhere on the road? The state of a liberated, final, living Sadguru, the state of an Avatar, is indeed rare.

The translation of this verse is: in the last of many births, the person who has attained the knowledge of Truth worships Me, knowing "everything is the Supreme, everything is Vasudeva"; such a Mahatma is extremely rare.

## When Vasudeva Is Seen in All

In that state, could he ever kill an outer animal? Could a person kill another for his own liberation? Is that possible? He would then see Vasudeva in everyone. He begins to feel: "He and I are exactly the same."

> "All these Vedas are in coded language."

## Jnana Satra and Sadhana

Is it understood? What you are doing is absolutely right. You have come into the Jnana Satra, the session of knowledge. Now we will look at its application one by one, slowly. Theory alone will not help much. Knowledge does not stop one from sinning; otherwise it would not have been said, "*vinasha kale viparita buddhi*" (when destruction approaches, the intellect turns perverse).

That is why my work is to awaken the Kundalini. Sadhana is here too; do it from there as well, and you will get the same result. Why, what and how it comes, I do not know, but you will certainly get the result. Do practise sadhana. The more you do it collectively, the more you will gain.
