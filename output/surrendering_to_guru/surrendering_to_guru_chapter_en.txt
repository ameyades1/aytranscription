# Chapter: Abandoning All Dharmas and Taking Refuge in the Guru

## The Surprising Command to Give Up "All Dharmas"

> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja**
> **ahaṃ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone. I shall free you from all sins; do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

"Tyaj" means to give up, to leave behind. To it is added "pari", meaning fully, completely. Just as ātmā becomes paramātmā, so tyajya becomes parityajya. That is: instantly, completely, leave behind what has been named and come.

But what is to be left behind? "Sarva", meaning all, everything. Notice that it does not say "sarva adharma parityajya". It says "sarva dharma": leave all dharmas and come. It does not say leave all adharma and come. Is that not somewhat shocking? Is it not surprising?

## In Whose Refuge?

"Mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja", come into My refuge. Whose refuge? The Guru's refuge. For Arjuna it was Krishna; for you, Krishna will be someone else, and one who is here now, living.

We saw this yesterday too. A future guru is of no benefit to you, nor is a guru of the past who has taken samadhi. What benefits you is a living guru, now. Even a guru in samadhi sends you to some living guru. That same sadhana, that same devotion, will bring you to a living guru.

## "Mā Śucaḥ": Do Not Grieve

Look at the next part of the verse. "Ahaṃ tvā", then what will I do? "Sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi", I will free you from all your sins. "Mā śucaḥ", meaning do not weep. I will cleanse everything of yours and give you liberation.

It means your sins themselves are gone. It was because of some sin that illness came, because of some sin that poverty came, because of sin that some quarrel arose and relationships were spoiled. Right? So all of that will be set right, if you do as I say. "Mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja."

## The True Meaning of Dharma

Now the question is this, isn't it: how does one give up all dharmas? What does giving up dharma mean? Was it said to give up being Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Jain? That cannot be it. Then does giving up dharma mean giving up speaking the truth and such things? No.

The word dharma does not mean Hindu, Muslim or Christian. Dharma means karma. Not mere action, but karma. Which karma? Duty-bound action.

If you are a son, is there not a son's dharma? That is, the duties of a son. Likewise a father's dharma, a husband's dharma, a wife's dharma of devotion to her husband, as people say. For a neighbour, a neighbour's dharma. So what does dharma mean? Some do's and don'ts given to me as duties for a particular role. That much is right, isn't it?

## Drop Your Own Ideas and Come to the Guru

The verse means: giving up all dharmas, that is, all duty-bound actions, unto Me. First He says, drop whatever is your own idea and come to some guru. Ask the guru what is right for me to do and what I should not do.

Once the guru sets that direction, it is as if you have been seated on the right train. I said yesterday: if you have to go to Delhi, you should at least check whether your train is going to Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai or Delhi. Once you have caught the right train, once you have caught the right direction of action, the destination will come by itself.

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> "Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

Then your right is to action alone, never to its fruit. With firm resolve you must set the path right, and the guru will show the path. At least once, you have to take it with understanding.

The full meaning of the verse is this: giving up all dharmas, that is, all duty-bound actions, unto Me, take refuge in Me alone, the almighty Supreme Lord, the support of all. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.

## Do Not Bow to Miracles; Bow Properly

The sudden healing you saw yesterday means that some wrong deed done at some time, the fruit of some sinful karma, must have been its cause. From yesterday's healing, this much became clear: it can be healed in a single stroke.

I said that this is not a miracle, and do not bow to miracles. So what should you do? Bow properly, and a miracle will happen in your life. That is, first drop the ego.

> "Do not bow to miracles. Bow properly, and a miracle will happen in your life."

To bow properly means "na mama": not I, but You; not mine, but Yours. As this feeling grows firm, the ego will go. And as this becomes right, miracles will happen in your life overnight.

This is what the guru teaches you. It is to such a guru, one who teaches this, that you must reach.
