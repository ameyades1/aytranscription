# Why Set Aside Every Dharma and Take Refuge in the Guru

One verse of the Gita catches you off guard the first time you hear it. Shri Krishna tells Arjuna to abandon every dharma and come to Him for refuge.

He does not say every wrong, but every **dharma** (one's rightful duty). Acharya Ji opens up this verse and shows that the whole secret of **sharanagati** (complete surrender) lies hidden inside it.

The sarva dharman parityajya meaning is this: in Gita 18.66, Krishna asks Arjuna to set aside every dharma, not every wrong, and take refuge in Him alone. Acharya Ji explains that dharma here means the duty of each role, not religion, and the refuge is a living Guru: drop your assumptions and ask the Guru what is right for you.

## A Verse That Takes You by Surprise

> **sarvadharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> **ahaṁ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Abandon all dharmas and take refuge in Me alone. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

"Tyaj" means to let go. Add "pari" and it becomes letting go completely and at once. Just as a prefix turns **atma** (the self) into **Paramatma** (the Supreme Self), it turns tyajya into **parityajya**.

And what are you asked to give up? "Sarva dharma", every dharma. The verse does not say "sarva adharma", every wrong. That is what makes it so astonishing.

## Whose Refuge? A Living Guru

"Mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja" means "take refuge in Me." Here, the refuge Krishna offers is read as the refuge of the **Guru**. For Arjuna, that was Krishna. For you, Krishna will be someone else, and he will be alive today.

A Guru yet to come in the future cannot help you, and neither can one who has already taken samadhi and left the body. The benefit comes from a living Guru. Even a Guru who has left the body sends you to a living one. True sadhana and devotion will lead you there.

## Mā Śucaḥ: No More Tears

The promise in the verse is this: "I will free you from all sins; do not grieve."

Acharya Ji connects it to everyday life. One sin brought illness, another brought poverty, another led to quarrels and broken relationships. Once the sins themselves are gone, all of this will heal too, on one condition: you do as you are told.

## What Does Abandoning Every Dharma Mean? Dharma as Duty

What, then, does it mean to set aside every dharma? Does it mean giving up being Hindu, Muslim, Christian or Jain? No. Does it mean giving up telling the truth? Certainly not.

**Dharma means action.** Not mere activity, but **duty**: the action that is yours to do.

A son has his dharma as a son, a father as a father, a husband as a husband. A wife has the dharma of devotion to her husband, and a neighbour has the dharma of a neighbour. Every role carries things you ought to do and things you ought not to do. That is dharma.

*How many roles are you playing today, and who taught you what is right in each one?*

## Drop Your Assumptions, Board the Right Train

The message of the verse is to set aside all your own assumptions and come to the Guru. Ask him what is right for you to do and what is not.

Acharya Ji's example is very simple. If you want to go to Delhi, at least check whether the train is bound for Chennai, Hyderabad, Mumbai or Delhi. Once you have boarded the right train, once your actions point in the right direction, the destination will come on its own.

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> "Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

From there, what you control is the action, not its outcome. Your task is to resolve firmly and set your path right, and the Guru shows you that path.

## Bow Properly, and the Miracles Follow

During the session, people saw sudden healings take place. Acharya Ji says this is no miracle. Some old sinful deed was the cause of an illness, and it can be undone in a single stroke. Don't bow down to miracles, he says. Instead, learn to bow properly, and miracles will happen in your life.

To offer **namaskar** (a respectful greeting with folded hands) properly is to mean "na mama": not I, but You; not mine, but Yours. The firmer this feeling grows, the more your ego dissolves. And as soon as the ego goes, miracles unfold in your life overnight.

*When you bow, are you only folding your hands, or do you also mean "not mine, but Yours"?*

This is what the Guru teaches, and reaching such a Guru is true surrender.

## Key Takeaways

- Gita 18.66 asks you to abandon "every dharma", not "every wrong".
- The refuge is the Guru, and the benefit comes from a living Guru.
- When sins fall away, illness, poverty and broken relationships heal too.
- Dharma does not mean religion; it means the duty that comes with each role.
- Set aside your own assumptions and take direction from the Guru; board the right train and the destination comes on its own.
- Don't bow to miracles; bow properly: "na mama", not mine, but Yours.
