# Chapter: Not a Wish, but Strong Willpower — Manifesting Goals the Right Way


## Introduction: Merely Wanting Achieves Nothing

Today there is much talk of "manifestation" — want something in your mind, and you will get it. But in this short excerpt from a discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji gives a deeper warning: **even a light, fleeting wish is a karma, and it too bears fruit** — but often not in the way we imagined. A goal is realised through *prabal ichchha shakti* (strong willpower), not through a *passing thought*.

*Karma Yoga* — union through action; action that transforms a person from within. *Phalashruti* — the definite fruit of an action or a spiritual practice.


## Seek, Don't Merely Wish

At the very outset, Acharya Ji makes clear the difference between "seeking" and "merely wanting":

> "'Seek and it shall be given' — that is said to the seeker. **'Wish and it shall be given to the wisher' — it is not like that.** It will not happen through wishes."

> "They say, 'If wishes were horses, beggars would ride.' **It will not happen through a passing thought.** 'Yes, I am a king, I am a king' — nothing will come of that."


## A Passing Thought Is Also a Karma

Yet a fleeting wish does not go to waste either. A student of Karma Yoga must understand its reverse side too:

> "What happens through such a passing thought — that also needs to be learned. **If you want to learn Karma Yoga, you should also know its negative outcome.**"

> "If you say — it is not a strong desire, but there is a little desire — **then is that also a karma or not?**"

To explain this, Acharya Ji gives three examples.


## First Example: "There Should Be Cars Ahead and Behind Me"

> "'There should be cars ahead of me and behind me.' What is the wish? There should be cars ahead of me and behind me. **But this is not a strong desire.**"

> "Now its phalashruti will be that perhaps after some years, or in the next birth, **he may end up as a traffic constable on some very important, busy traffic signal.**"

The wish was fulfilled — there are cars ahead and behind — but in a form that teaches him how shallow that wish was:

> "And then he will meet just such a car driver, someone who picks a fight. Or he sees the huge maintenance expense of a car, and he will say — **I don't want a car.**"


## Second Example: A Dress Like the Heroine's

> "There is some big film heroine, isn't there — 'I want to wear a dress like hers, do something like her.' **There is no strong willpower, only a wish.**"

> "In the next birth she will become some heroine's washerwoman, **where she has to wash her designer dresses every day.** How much hardship there is in it, how much of a waste of time it is for her — only then will she learn this lesson, for sure."

> "And then she will tell you — 'I don't want it now.' But she did make that wish, **its phalashruti has to come, it must come.** And the purpose that will come out of it is that the thought was not a serious thought."

Acharya Ji makes clear that the fault lies not in the object of the wish, but in its lack of seriousness:

> "Now, is being a heroine a bad thing? Is having cars ahead and behind a bad thing? **But it was a passing thought, that is why the problem arises.**"


## Third Example: A Fleeting Wish to Be a King

> "Someone has no strong willpower to become a king, but a wish just came. Now he will be born somewhere — some big prime minister somewhere, **he will become a servant or a dog or something in his house, where he will see it all.**"

> "He will be born in the house of just such a king who is very troubled, who is in tension, **so that nature will tell him, 'Don't you become a king.'**"

Why does nature do this? Because one who did not do his work properly at a smaller level and wants to reach the top directly could not make a good king anyway:

> "Do you understand what nature is doing? The ideas he had in smaller times, he was not carrying them out properly; he wants to go up suddenly. **So such a man will not become a king anyway.**"

> "**That karma will take him over to such places.** He will learn the lesson that this is not to be done — so that is good. **But in reality, the public should not get such a king.**"


## Strong Willpower: The Path Opens by Itself

Now Acharya Ji shows the reverse side — where the wish is both pure and strong:

> "But it can be the reverse — the good one, whose wish is good. **He will automatically reach such a king, or some such political party, where he will keep finding people who encourage him,** and they will take him up, up, up."

> "How can that happen? That wish will remain — **it happens through strong willpower.** It is possible. Then **he will keep finding just such a path, again and again and again.**"

So keeping a big goal is not wrong in itself:

> "So if someone said, 'I want to become a heroine and nothing else,' there is no problem at all. **But if there is strong willpower, then such an agency will come** or something."


## Not Straight to the Summit: The Small Steps

But even strong willpower does not carry one to the summit in a single leap:

> "But will a big blockbuster film come directly at the start? **That has not happened even for any superstar till today.** They too — they come up from small serials, don't they? **This many people forget.**"


## Think Big — but While Erasing the Ego

Acharya Ji recalls a principle of Shri Vidya, along with a condition attached to it:

> "**It is not bad to think big. 'If you think, think big' — that is a statement of Shri Vidya.** But before that big thinking, that series of smaller willpower — a lot of it will come in this."

> "**Nature will test you many times.** One who has a very big vision, nature will bring down many times to do small tasks."

What is the purpose of these small tasks?

> "Because **a Karma Yogi has to look after other people a great deal too.** All of it happens so that the ego inside him is wiped out. So this may fructify — in small ways."

One who has to do good for many must first see and understand everyone's life from below; and small tasks erase the ego within.


## Karma Yoga Even in the Four Rotis at Home

Finally, Acharya Ji clarifies that he is speaking here of the Karma Yoga of a *great work* — but this does not mean there is no Karma Yoga in small, household tasks:

> "The Karma Yoga of a great work — and what Karma Yoga is needed to make four rotis at home? That is fine, that is good, **one should become good for the home.**"

> "I am talking about great work. But at home too one has to do well — **it is not that Karma Yoga does not happen there.** There is the small-scale kind too, that also exists."


## Summary: Key Teachings

1. **"Seek and it shall be given" — not "wish and it shall be given."** Merely wanting, or repeating "I am a king," achieves nothing.
2. **A passing thought is also a karma** — its phalashruti must come, but often in the form of a hard lesson.
3. A fleeting wish for cars → a traffic constable at a busy signal; a wish for a heroine's dress → the heroine's washerwoman; a wish to be king → a servant in a troubled king's house. **Nature shows that the wish was not serious.**
4. The fault is not in the object of the wish — **being a heroine or a king is not a bad thing; the problem is the passing thought.**
5. **With strong willpower, the path keeps opening** — the right people and the right opportunities come together by themselves.
6. The summit is not reached directly — **even superstars come up from small serials.**
7. **"If you think, think big"** (Shri Vidya) — but nature tests one with a big vision through small tasks, so that **the ego is wiped out** and one can understand everyone's life.
8. **There is Karma Yoga even in small household tasks** — the small-scale kind is Karma Yoga too.
