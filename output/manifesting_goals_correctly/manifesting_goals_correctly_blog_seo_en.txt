# Strong Willpower, Not Mere Wishing, Shapes Your Life

Have you ever wondered what becomes of each small want that crosses your mind? We assume such thoughts drift in and out and leave no trace. Seen through **Karma Yoga** (the path of selfless action), even a faint wish is an action, and it bears fruit.

Let us look at how a **wish** differs from **strong willpower**, and how nature responds to each.

Wish vs willpower: a wish is a passing want, while strong willpower is a resolve you work for wholeheartedly. In Karma Yoga, even a faint wish is an action and bears fruit, often as an experience that teaches you, "I don't want this." Wishing alone manifests nothing; a good, strong will finds its own path.

## What Does Wanting Alone Bring You?

There is a well-known saying: "Seek and it shall be given to the seeker." Those who search are the ones who find. Nobody promised that those who merely want something will receive it.

Acharya Ji points out that no one ever said "Wish and it shall be given to the wisher." Wishes on their own achieve nothing.

As the old proverb puts it, "If wishes were horses, beggars would ride."

Repeating "I am a king, I am a king" changes nothing. Still, it helps to know where such fleeting thoughts lead. If you want to learn Karma Yoga, you need to understand its shadow side as well.

## Is a Faint Wish Also Karma?

Yes, it is. Suppose someone thinks, "I'd love to have cars all around me." This is no burning desire, just a passing fancy.

Its **phalashruti** (the fruit or outcome) may be that, a few years later or in the next life, this person stands at a busy crossroads as a traffic constable. There they are, with cars in front and cars behind!

Out there, they will meet drivers spoiling for a fight, or they will see how much it costs to keep a car running. Eventually they will say it themselves: "I don't want a car after all."

## Wanting to Be Like a Film Star

Take another person, who wishes to dress like one of today's leading film actresses and to live a little like her. Again, there is no strong will behind it, only a wish.

In her next life, she may become that actress's washerwoman, scrubbing designer outfits every day. Then she will see how much effort they demand and how much time they swallow. And she will say, "I don't want this anymore."

Acharya Ji explains that once you have made a wish, its result is bound to come.

Notice that there is nothing wrong with being an actress, or with having cars all around you. The trouble is that the thought was never serious. It was a **passing thought** and nothing more.

*Which of your wishes keep drifting through your mind without your ever having worked wholeheartedly for them?*

## Dreaming of a Throne Without Preparing for It

Here is a third example. Someone simply gets the wish to become a king, but has no strong will behind it.

Such a person might end up as an aide to a powerful prime minister, a servant in his household, or even his dog, watching everything from close by. Or they might be born into the home of a king who suffers greatly and lives under constant strain. In this way nature tells them, "Don't become a king."

Why does nature do this? Because this person was not doing their own small tasks well, and wanted to leap straight to the top. Someone like that would never become a king anyway. Their actions will carry them to places where they learn what not to do.

And the truth is, the people should never end up with a king like that.

## How Does Strong Willpower Open the Path?

The reverse happens too. When someone's wish is both good and strong, they find their own way to the right place, perhaps a political party. There they keep meeting people who encourage them and lift them higher and higher.

This is the miracle of **strong willpower**. The path keeps opening up on its own.

## Think Big, but Be Patient

If someone says, "I am determined to become an actress," there is no problem with that. With strong willpower, opportunities will come. But will a big hit arrive with the very first film? That did not happen even for the biggest stars. They too worked their way up through small television serials, a fact many people forget.

**Shri Vidya** (a path of worship of the Divine Mother) teaches that if you are going to think, think big. Thinking big is not wrong. But before any great goal comes a whole chain of smaller acts of will, and nature tests you again and again.

The wider your vision, the more often nature brings you down to do humble work. Why? Because a **karmayogi** (one who walks the path of Karma Yoga) has to care for others as well. All of this serves to dissolve the **ego** within.

*Are you giving the small tasks on the way to your big dream the same wholehearted effort?*

## Even Four Rotis at Home Are Karma Yoga

Karma Yoga in great undertakings is one thing. But you should also do your best when you make four rotis at home. It is not as if Karma Yoga is missing there. It works on a small scale too, and we will explore that later.

## Key Takeaways

- Wanting alone brings nothing; those who seek are the ones who find.
- Even a faint wish is an action, and its fruit is bound to come.
- Passing wishes often turn into experiences that teach us, "I don't want this."
- With strong willpower, the path keeps opening up on its own.
- Think big, but expect small steps and tests before the big goal.
- Small tasks come to dissolve the ego and help us understand others.
- Even small chores at home are Karma Yoga.
