## 1. Summary

This short talk asks the oldest question in Indian spirituality, "मैं कौन हूँ? / Who am I?", and makes it practical: we are busy all day yet do not know our real work, where we came from or where we are going. Acharya Ji explains adhyatma as the study of the Self, tells the teaching-story of the newborn who cries "ko'ham" (who am I?) with "so'ham" (I am That) as the answer, shows how toys and distraction train a child to play in maya and forget why it came, and closes with the image of driving blindfolded. The main intent is informational with a strong reflective/emotional pull (people searching "who am I spiritually", "main kaun hoon", "purpose of life"). The head term "who am I" is very competitive (Ramana Maharshi, Advaita sites, psychology pages), so the opportunity is the long tail: "ko'ham so'ham meaning", "मैं कौन हूँ कहाँ से आया", "how maya makes us forget who we are", where a short, warm, story-led answer from a living teacher can stand out. The talk sits in the Mind & Self cluster beside "first duty in life", "sense pleasures don't last" and "what decides your destiny".

## 6. FAQ

Answers are taken from the blog only.

1. **मैं कौन हूँ का प्रश्न क्यों ज़रूरी है? / Why does the question "who am I" matter?**
   हम अपने जीवन का बीच वाला हिस्सा देखते हैं, पर आरंभ और अंत नहीं जानते। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि यह जाने बिना जीना आँखों पर पट्टी बाँधकर गाड़ी चलाने जैसा है। / We see the middle of life but not its beginning or end; without knowing them, Acharya Ji says, life is like driving blindfolded.
2. **अध्यात्म का अर्थ क्या है? / What does adhyatma mean?**
   आत्मा का अध्ययन: मैं कौन हूँ, कहाँ से आया, कहाँ जाऊँगा। / The study of the Atman: who am I, where did I come from, where will I go.
3. **कोऽहम् और सोऽहम् का क्या अर्थ है? / What do ko'ham and so'ham mean?**
   कोऽहम् का अर्थ है "मैं कौन हूँ?" और उत्तर सोऽहम् का अर्थ है "मैं वही (परमात्मा) हूँ"। परंपरा में यह उपदेश आदि शंकराचार्य के नाम से कहा जाता है। / Ko'ham means "Who am I?"; the answer, so'ham, means "I am That". Tradition attributes this teaching to Adi Shankaracharya. (Uses the review's corrected gloss; see flags 1 to 3.)
4. **माया हमें खुद को कैसे भुला देती है? / How does maya make us forget ourselves?**
   बच्चे को खिलौने और बहलावे देकर हम उसे माया में खेलना सिखाते हैं, और वह भूल जाता है कि किस लिए आया था। / Toys and distractions teach a child to play in maya, and it forgets why it came.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **सोऽहम् glossed as "तू वही है, तू परमात्मा है" (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** सोऽहम् is first person, "मैं वही हूँ"; "तू वही है" is तत्त्वमसि. Ko'ham/so'ham is a likely search entry point, so a wrong gloss would be quoted by snippets. FAQ 3 uses the corrected meaning. Apply the review's correction before publishing.
2. **English so'ham gloss "You are That; you are God" (review issue 2, Major, not yet applied).** Same mix-up. No SEO text uses "You are That" for so'ham.
3. **The newborn crying "ko'ham" attributed to Adi Shankaracharya (review issue 3, Minor).** Not found in his authenticated works; the Hindi states it as his own words. FAQ 3 says "tradition attributes"; the "why does a newborn baby cry" keyword is Low priority and should not be targeted with "Shankaracharya says" wording. Adi Shankaracharya is kept as a tag because the talk is framed around him; drop it if the editor removes the attribution.
4. **Jnaneshwar's parents calming him with Gita verses (review issue 4, Minor).** Not part of the standard account; present as "it is said". No SEO text uses it.
5. **Terminology consistency (review issues 5 to 7, Minor).** कर्म missing in the English, गुरुदेव/आचार्य जी mixed in the Hindi, शिविर unglossed. No SEO effect; all SEO texts use "Acharya Upendra Ji".
6. **"Chant Om to a crying baby and it will settle down."** This is Acharya Ji's suggestion, and the blog itself adds that a baby may also cry from hunger. Don't turn it into a promise ("Om stops a baby crying") in titles, snippets or social posts.
