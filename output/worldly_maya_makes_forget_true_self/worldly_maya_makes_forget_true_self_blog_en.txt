# How the Maya of the World Makes Us Forget Ourselves

## Everyone Is Busy, But For What?

We are all caught up in our work. Office work, housework, this job, that job. The housewife says it, and so does the one who goes to the office.

But Gurudev places a simple question before us: do you truly know what your **karma** (duty, your real work in life) is? And do you really know who you are?

> "Do you truly know what your karma is? Who are you? Where have you come from? Where will you go?"

## The Simple Meaning of Adhyatma

**Adhyatma** (spirituality) means the study of the Self. Study of what? Of the Atma.

The middle part of life we can see. But where we came from and where we have to go, the beginning and the end, we simply do not know.

## What We Have Never Seen

You have seen others being born, but not your own birth. Nor have you seen your own death. With this body, both remain hidden from your eyes.

The world's population is growing. Creatures, animals, birds: everyone is being born. Someone was born a king, someone a dog or cat, someone a thief, someone a demon. Why?

And where do people go? Some in an accident, some in a bomb blast, some from illness, some by natural death. Our grandfather, our great-grandmother: where did they go?

*One day I too will go. So where will I go? And where did I come from? Have you ever thought about this?*

## A Question Before Our Quarrels

We keep complaining: my sister-in-law is like this, my son like that, my mother like this, my neighbour like that, my boss is bad.

But have you ever wondered where these people came into your life from?

## Ko'ham: Why Does a Child Cry?

Adi Shankaracharya raises this very question. When a child cries, the mother thinks through her ordinary **vasana** (desire, impression): he must be hungry. Her thinking never goes beyond eating and drinking.

Shankaracharya says the child does not cry for food and drink. In the first few days after birth, it realises: why was I born at all? It cries saying "Ko'ham, ko'ham."

> **Ko'ham**

**Ko'ham** means: who am I? And the answer is:

> **So'ham**

**So'ham** means you are That, you are the Supreme (I am That). Both are symbols, spoken by Adi Shankaracharya as a poet. Gurudev says both of these are taught in the shivir.

## The Childhood of Dnyaneshwar Maharaj

Sant Dnyaneshwar also cried as a child. His parents would carry him on their shoulders and recite verses of the Bhagavad Gita with their meaning, and he would grow calm and fall asleep.

Gurudev says, try it yourself. Recite the mantra "Om", or something like it, to a child with its meaning. The child will become calm. Yes, sometimes it may cry from hunger too; that is not denied.

## The Toy and Maya

But what do we do? We hand it a toy and amuse it with "here, cock-a-doodle-doo."

> "It learns to play in maya and forgets what it came for."

We bring it into this very world, and then it stays stuck its whole life in this **maya** (the illusion of the world).

## Why Did This Very Thought Come to You?

You think, I want to do a big business, but it isn't happening. First ask: why did the thought of business come at all? Why not of becoming a guru? Why not of becoming a king?

The thought of becoming a king came to someone, the thought of becoming a guru to someone else. The thought of drinking liquor came into someone's mind; it did not come into yours.

Someone says, I can't find a job, I am stuck in engineering, I'm not getting into an MBA. But why did this thought come to you at all? Positive and negative thinking come later.

*The goal you are chasing today: have you ever asked where that thought within you came from?*

## The Origin of a Thought

Why does everyone not get the same thought, like a syllabus? Surely it has some origin. Without knowing it, how will there be progress?

## A Blindfold and a Car

Imagine: you don't know the road, you don't know the map, you don't even know where you are standing. There is a blindfold over your eyes, you are driving, and you don't know the destination.

What is the use of driving such a vehicle?

We should know where we have come from. If we went the wrong way, we should turn and come back. And we should also know where the goal is.

*Think truthfully and tell me: should this be known, or not?*

## Key Takeaways

- **Adhyatma** means the study of the Self: who am I, where did I come from, where will I go.
- We are busy with our work, but do not know what our real karma is.
- According to Shankaracharya, a newborn cries asking "Ko'ham" (who am I), and the answer is "So'ham" (you are That Supreme).
- Through toys and distractions, the child learns to play in maya and forgets the purpose of its coming.
- Without knowing the origin of why a particular thought arises in our mind, there is no real progress.
- Without knowing where we came from and where we have to go, life is like driving a car blindfolded.
