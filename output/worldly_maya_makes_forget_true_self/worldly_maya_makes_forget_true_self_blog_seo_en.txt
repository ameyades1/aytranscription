# How the Maya of the World Makes Us Forget Who We Are

## Everyone Is Busy, but for What?

We are all tangled up in our own tasks: work at the office, work at home, this job and that one. The homemaker says it, and so does the person who commutes to an office every day.

Acharya Ji puts a simple question to all of us. Do you actually know **the real work of your life**, the duty you are truly here to fulfil? And do you really know who you are, where you came from and where you will go?

Who am I, where did I come from, where will I go? These are the questions of adhyatma, the study of the Self. We stay busy, yet from childhood we learn to play in the maya of the world and forget why we came. Living without knowing this is like driving blindfolded.

## Spirituality in Plain Words

**Adhyatma** (spirituality) means the study of the Atman (the Self). What are you studying? Your own true self.

We can see the middle stretch of life clearly enough. Its beginning and its end, where we came from and where we are headed, remain a complete mystery to us.

## What We Have Never Seen

You have watched others being born, yet you never saw your own birth. You will not see your own death either. In this body, both events stay hidden from your eyes.

The world's population keeps growing. Insects, animals, birds, every kind of creature is being born. One arrives as a king, another as a dog or a cat, one as a thief, another as a demon. Why?

And where does everyone go? Some leave in an accident, some in a bomb blast, some through illness, and some die a natural death. Our grandfathers and great-grandmothers, where did they go?

*One day I will go too. Where will I go, and where did I come from in the first place? Have you ever stopped to wonder?*

## A Question Before the Quarrels

We keep complaining: my sister-in-law is like this, my son is like that, my mother is impossible, my neighbour is difficult, my boss is terrible.

Have you ever asked how these people came into your life at all?

## Ko'ham (Who Am I?): Why Does a Baby Cry?

Adi Shankaracharya raises exactly this question. When a baby cries, the mother thinks through her ordinary **vasana** (desire shaped by deep impressions) and decides the child must be hungry. Her thinking never goes beyond food and milk.

Shankaracharya says the baby is not crying for food. In the first few days after birth, it senses something: why was I born at all? It cries out, "Ko'ham, ko'ham."

> **ko'ham**
> "Who am I?"
> (Traditional teaching attributed to Adi Shankaracharya)

The question has an answer:

> **so'ham**
> "I am That. You are That; you are God."
> (Traditional answer to ko'ham)

Adi Shankaracharya spoke both of these as a poet, as symbols. Acharya Ji explains that both are taught at the **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching).

## The Childhood of Sant Jnaneshwar

Sant Jnaneshwar, the great thirteenth-century saint of Maharashtra, also cried as a baby. His parents would carry him on their shoulders and recite verses of the Bhagavad Gita to him along with their meaning, and he would grow calm and fall asleep.

Try it yourself, Acharya Ji says. Chant "Om", or something like it, to a baby and explain what it means. The child will settle down. Of course, sometimes a baby does cry from hunger too; nobody denies that.

## The Toy and the Illusion

What do we actually do? We push a toy into the baby's hand and coo, "Here, look, coochie-coo!" Acharya Ji points out that the child learns to play inside illusion and forgets why it came.

We draw the child into this very world, and then it spends its whole life stuck in this **maya** (the illusion of the world).

## Why Did This Particular Thought Come to You?

You think, "I want to build a big business, but it isn't happening." First ask yourself why the idea of business came to you at all. Why not the idea of becoming a Guru? Why not becoming a king?

One person dreamed of becoming a king, another of becoming a Guru. Someone else felt the urge to drink, and you never did.

Another person says, "I can't find a job, I'm stuck in engineering, I can't get into an MBA." Yet why did this goal occur to you in the first place? Whether your thinking is positive or negative is a question that comes much later.

*The goal you are chasing today: have you ever asked where that thought came from within you?*

## The Root of a Thought

Why doesn't the same thought come to everyone, like a fixed syllabus? It must have an origin. How can you make progress without knowing it?

## Blindfolded at the Wheel

Picture this. You don't know the road, you have no map, and you can't even tell where you are standing. You are driving with a blindfold over your eyes, and you have no idea where you are going.

What good is driving like that?

We need to know where we came from. If we have taken a wrong turn, we should turn around and come back. And we need to know where our destination lies.

*Think about it honestly and tell me: shouldn't you know these things?*

## Key Takeaways

- **Adhyatma** is the study of the Atman: who am I, where did I come from, where will I go.
- We stay busy with our tasks but do not know the real work of our life.
- According to Shankaracharya, a newborn cries out "ko'ham" (who am I?), and the answer is "so'ham" (I am That, the Divine itself).
- Toys and distractions teach a child to play in maya and forget why it came.
- Real progress needs an understanding of where our particular thoughts come from.
- Living without knowing where we came from and where we are going is like driving blindfolded.
