# Chapter: The Maya of the World and Knowing Oneself

## Do You Know Who You Are?

We are all entangled in our own work. One says, I have to do this office work; another says, I have to do that work; someone has to look after the housework. The housewife says the same, and so does the one who goes to the office. Everyone repeats the same thing: I have this work, I have that work.

My question is this: do you truly know what your karma is? And do you really know who you are? Where have you come from? Where will you go? Do you know this?

## The Meaning of Adhyatma

What we call *adhyatma* (spirituality) means study. Study of what? Of the Atma, the Self. The study of the Self is adhyatma.

So do you really know who you are? Where have you come from, and where do you have to go? The middle part you can see, but what of the beginning and the end?

## The Mystery of Birth and Death

You must have seen many people being born in front of you. Yet you have not seen your own birth, nor have you seen your own death. Isn't that right? With this particular body, you have not seen these two things. But others being born, you see all the time. People talk about the world population, which means something or other keeps increasing. Creatures, animals, birds: we see all of them being born too.

Has the question ever arisen: where did this one come from? Why was it born? Someone as a king, someone as a dog or a cat, someone as a thief, someone as a demon: why were they born like that?

And when they go, where do these people go? You have seen many people die. Some in an accident, some in a bomb blast, some by natural death, some from illness. Where did they go? Our grandfather, our great-grandmother: have you ever thought about it? And since they went, I too will go someday, so where will I go: have you thought about this? And where did I come from: have you ever thought about that?

## The Question Behind Our Quarrels

People keep quarrelling: my sister-in-law is like this, my son is like that, my mother is like this, my neighbour is like that, my boss is bad. Have you ever wondered where that neighbour, that boss or that wife came into your life from?

## Ko'ham: The Cry of the Child

Adi Shankaracharya asks this very question. When a child cries in infancy, the mother immediately thinks according to her own *vasana* (desire). What will her vasana be? The ordinary vasana: eating and drinking. Her thinking does not go beyond that at all. So for the child too, she says: he must be hungry.

Shankaracharya says the child does not cry for your eating and drinking. In the first few days after birth, it immediately realises: why was I born at all? So when it cries, it cries "Ko'ham, ko'ham."

> **Ko'ham**

Ko'ham means: who am I? Who am I? You will learn both of these during the shivir. When the child asks, "Who am I?", it has to be given the answer:

> **So'ham**

That is, you are That, you are That; you are the Supreme. These two are symbolic. Adi Shankaracharya said this in the role of a poet.

## The Childhood of Sant Dnyaneshwar

Dnyaneshwar Maharaj was also small once, and he too cried. Then his parents would carry him on their shoulders and explain verses of the Bhagavad Gita to him with their meaning, and even at that tender age he would grow calm and fall asleep.

You too should try it. Recite the mantra "Om" in front of children, or something like it, with its meaning. The child will become calm. It does not cry for food. Yes, sometimes it may cry for food too; I am not denying that.

## Lost in Maya

But you give the child a toy, you amuse it saying "here, cock-a-doodle-doo", and so it learns to play in maya and forgets what it came for. You bring it into this very world, and then it stays stuck in it its whole life.

> "It learns to play in maya and forgets what it came for."

## Why Did This Thought Come at All?

Some of you think: I want to do a big business, but my business is not growing. First think: why did the thought of business come to you at all? Why did the thought of becoming a guru not come into your heart? Why not the thought of becoming a king? Yet did the thought of becoming a king come into someone's heart or not? And did the thought of becoming a guru come into someone's heart or not? Into someone's mind came the thought of drinking liquor; it did not come into yours.

Have you ever reflected on this? Someone says, I don't get a job, I have got stuck here in engineering, I am not getting into an MBA. But why did this thought come to you at all? Did you ever think about that first? Why did this very thought come to you? After that we will talk about positive and negative thinking.

## The Origin of a Thought

Why did this thought come first? Where is its origin? Why does everyone not get the same thought, like a syllabus? So surely this too must have some origin. Must it not?

## A Journey Without a Destination

Without knowing that origin, how will you progress? You don't know the road, you don't know the map, so how will you take your car anywhere? You don't know the destination, so where will you reach? What will become of such a car?

You don't know where you are standing. There is a blindfold over your eyes, you are driving the car, and you don't know the destination. So what is the use of driving such a vehicle?

> "There is a blindfold over your eyes, you are driving the car, and you don't know the destination."

At the origin, where I have come from: this should be known. If you have gone the wrong way, you should take a detour and come back. And should you know where the goal is, or not? Think truthfully and tell me: should you know it, or not?
