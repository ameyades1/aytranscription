# Why Do We Struggle Despite Our Best Efforts? The Secret of Acting with Understanding

## Are You Aiming Your Life at the Right Goal?

Every day we do so much. But have you ever stopped to ask what a particular event has taught you?

Acharya Ji says every action should come with three questions: What is this event teaching me? What is it about to teach me? What did I learn today? If your attention is not on these questions, all you end up with is suffering.

If you are aiming at anything other than knowledge, struggle is guaranteed.

## Only Two Paths: Learn First, or Learn the Hard Way

That really is all there is to it. You will learn either way. The only question is how.

The first path is **action guided by knowledge**: you understand first, then act. The second is discovering the truth through your mistakes. But that discovery comes wrapped in **bitter experience**.

Either life forces the truth on you, or you embrace it willingly. Embrace it, and you also find the happiness and bliss you were looking for in all the wrong places. Wait for life to force it on you, and suffering comes along with it.

A lesson that **satsang** (spiritual gathering and teaching) could have taught you at twenty becomes a crisis at sixty, because you never took it to heart. Had it sunk in earlier, sixty could have been a happy age too.

That is why Acharya Ji reminds us that every action should teach us something.

## Reflect Before You Act

Sooner or later, nature will teach you anyway. So why not reflect first? Before you act, ask yourself: why am I doing this?

There are three ways to do this. Ask the Guru. Think it through yourself beforehand. Or, at the very least, once you have acted, consider what needs to change before your next step.

## Not Pleasure, but the Highest Spiritual Wisdom

Acharya Ji is clear: the ultimate aim of life should be the highest spiritual knowledge, not enjoyment and pleasure.

Pleasure and pain cannot be the goal at all. Happiness does not live in objects, in achievements or in actions. Shloka after shloka has tested and confirmed this. Let there be just one aim: how do I reach that supreme understanding?

*What are you working so hard for today: pleasure, or understanding?*

## When Understanding Comes Too Late

Most people don't live this way. So pleasure and pain keep visiting their lives, but the insight never arrives. Then hardship comes, and only then does it all make sense. Nature has done its work. But by then it is too late.

Acharya Ji gives an example. At twenty-two, someone marries in a hurry, swept up in love at first sight. By forty or forty-five, the marriage is ending in divorce. Now they may finally see what ideal, **selfless love** is, but can they go back to twenty-five? Can they wipe away the stigma? Something has already broken.

This doesn't mean a good life is impossible afterwards. Anything is possible. But how much better it would have been to see this clearly at twenty-five.

This is **Karma Yoga** (the path of selfless action): act, but see clearly first. Pleasure and pain are merely outcomes, not the goal.

## Good Times, Bad Times: When Do We Learn?

Good and bad experiences both teach us. But which teaches more?

In good times we hardly stop to think; if anything, ego creeps in. Very few people think, "I gave in charity, and that is why this good time has come; now I must serve even more." In bad times, though, the message always gets through.

## Time Is Just Time

Maharshi Manki's Gita teaches that there is no such thing as good or bad time; there is only time. Vedanta goes further still: even time is not the final word, for something is **kalatita** (beyond time).

Time seems good or bad because we are not aware of **mukti** (liberation).

What do we call a good time? When we have money, relationships, name and fame, and all we want is to consume. A bad time is when we lose the money, the job and the relationships.

According to Maharshi Manki's Gita, though, these periods serve quite different purposes:

- **Good times:** a time to serve people, a time for Karma Yoga and **Bhakti Yoga** (the path of devotion)
- **Bad times:** a time to reflect, to study and to meditate

As Acharya Ji explains, what we call a good time was never a good time at all; it was a time to serve others.

During **Sade Sati** (Saturn's seven-and-a-half-year transit in Vedic astrology), people rush around even more and take on two extra jobs. Yet Shani (Saturn) is forcing them to sit still so that they look within. Spiritually, that is exactly what he wants, and exactly what people refuse to do.

*Is your difficult time asking you to stop and look within?*

## Why Problems Even in a Favourable Planetary Period?

Acharya Ji shares his own experience. He is in the **dasha** (planetary period) of an exalted Jupiter, yet he faces many problems. By worldly standards, he could simply sit back and relax: there is money, there is everything, there is nothing to worry about.

Instead, he has taken on the responsibility of bringing others to that same place. On this path of service, suffering will come again, and he must swallow the **halahala** (the deadly poison Lord Shiva drank to save the world). Even in service, the world shows itself as "duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam" (an abode of sorrow, impermanent). Either the world forces you to see this, or you accept it consciously.

## Selfless Service and the Promise of Yogakshema

As you walk this path, bliss comes, and then every reward follows on its own: wealth, **Shri** (prosperity and grace), **vidya** (learning), name and fame. The more selfless you become, the greater your glory and abundance.

> **ananyāś cintayanto māṁ ye janāḥ paryupāsate**
> **teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham**
> "Those who worship Me with undivided devotion, thinking of nothing else, who are ever united with Me: I Myself carry their welfare, providing what they lack and preserving what they have."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

**Yogakshema** means providing what is lacking and protecting what one already has. Here, devotion to God is read as devotion to people: God Himself looks after everything for those who wholeheartedly keep others in their thoughts, with no thought for themselves. The day this sinks in, your Karma Yoga truly begins.

## Lessons Shape Character and Destiny

Experience teaches us, and it is mostly the painful experiences that do. So you should have done what was needed before the bad times came.

These lessons shape your **character** and how you see the world, and it is character that shapes **destiny**. But be careful: if your intellect is not yet mature, you can draw the wrong conclusion. You can misread the right scripture, and you can misread Shani's message too. You might even reach a distorted conclusion like "God is in everything, except this."

Still, something always sinks in: "I should have given in charity", "I should eat less", "I shouldn't eat fried food or eat out", "I should love more", "I was so full of ego".

## The Wisdom Is Already Within

Where was all this wisdom hiding? Did someone place them inside us from outside? No. **That knowledge is already within every one of us.**

We ourselves are the **Atman** (the Self). Between us and that truth lie only layers: of ego, of ignorance, of attachment. With every action, a layer comes off.

Here lies the real difference:

- **Karma Yoga:** peeling away the layer with understanding first, then acting
- **Ordinary action:** having the layer knocked off by the blows of experience

Don't wait for experience to knock the layers off. Act with understanding.

*Which layer within you could you peel away today, before life has to knock it off?*

## Key Takeaways

- Every action should teach you something; pleasure is not the point
- You will learn either way: by acting with understanding, or through bitter experience
- Reflect before you act, or seek the Guru's guidance
- Don't put off until sixty a lesson you could learn at twenty; the past never comes back
- Good times are for service; bad times are for reflection, study and meditation
- Good and bad time are only perceptions; awareness of liberation dissolves them
- For those who serve selflessly, God Himself carries their yogakshema (Gita 9.22)
- The wisdom is already within; peeling away the layers of ego, ignorance and attachment with understanding is Karma Yoga
