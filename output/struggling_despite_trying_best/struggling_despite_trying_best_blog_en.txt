# Why Do We Struggle Despite Trying Our Best? The Secret of Action Guided by Knowledge

## Is the Aim of Your Life the Right One?

Every day we perform so many actions. But have we ever paused to ask what this event taught me?

Acharya ji says that every action should carry three questions: what is this event teaching me, what is it about to teach me, and what did I learn today. One whose attention is not on these questions ends up with nothing but sorrow.

If your aim itself is anything apart from knowledge, struggle is certain.

## There Are Only Two Paths: Action from Knowledge, or Knowledge from Action

That is the whole game. Knowledge is going to come anyway. The only question is how.

The first path: **action guided by knowledge**, that is, action done with understanding. The second path: knowledge arriving through action itself. But then it comes with **bitter experience**.

Knowledge will either come by force, or you will embrace it willingly. Embrace it willingly, and the happiness and bliss you were seeking in the wrong place will also be found. If it comes by force, sorrow comes along with it.

What could have been learned through satsang at twenty, its absence brought the problem at sixty. Had that knowledge come earlier, there would have been happiness even at sixty.

So Acharya Ji says that every action we perform should result in us obtaining some knowledge from it.

## Contemplation Before Action

Nature will give that knowledge sooner or later. So why not do **chintan** (deep reflection, contemplation) first? Before acting, ask: why am I doing this?

There are three ways: either learn it from the Guru, or reflect yourself before acting, or at the very least, after acting, consider what must be done before the next action.

## Not Pleasure, but the Highest Spiritual Knowledge

Acharya ji says clearly: high spiritual knowledge alone should be the ultimate aim of life, not pleasures and happiness.

Pleasure and pain can never be the aim. Happiness is not in objects, not in achievements, not in actions. This has been examined through many shlokas. Let there be only one aim: how will I attain that supreme knowledge.

*What you are working so hard for today, is it pleasure or understanding?*

## When Understanding Comes Too Late

Most people do not do this. So pleasures and pains keep coming into their lives, but lessons do not. Then sorrow comes, and only then does understanding arrive. Nature has done its work. But by then it is too late.

Acharya ji gives an example. At twenty-two, a marriage happened in haste, love at first sight. By forty or forty-five, the time of divorce had come. Now, even if one understands what ideal and **selfless love** is, can one return to twenty-five? Will the label that was attached be erased? Something is already damaged.

This does not mean a positive life is impossible afterwards. Everything is possible. But it would have been better if this knowledge had come at twenty-five.

This is **Karma Yoga**: act, but first take knowledge. Pleasure and pain are only outcomes, not the aim.

## Good Times, Bad Times: When Do Lessons Come?

Both good and bad experiences give lessons. But when do more lessons come?

In good times there is no time to think; ego comes in instead. Very few people think, "I gave charity, that is why this good time came; now I must serve more." But in bad times, the lesson surely comes.

## Time Is Only Time

The Manki Gita of Maharshi Manki teaches that there is no good or bad time; there is only time. And Vedanta goes further: not even time, there is something **kalatita** (beyond time).

Time feels good or bad because we are not aware of **mukti** (liberation).

What do we call good times? When there is money, relationships, name and fame, and only the desire to consume. Bad times are when money, job and relationships are gone.

But according to the Manki Gita, their purpose is different:

- **Good times:** a time for serving people, for Karma Yoga and Bhakti Yoga
- **Bad times:** a time for contemplation, for knowledge and meditation

Acharya Ji explains that what we call a good time was never a good time; it was a time for serving others.

During Shani's **Sade Sati**, people rush around even more and try two more jobs. Whereas Shani is forcibly making them sit, so that they look within. What he wanted done from a spiritual standpoint is exactly what people do not do.

*Is your difficult time asking you to pause and look within?*

## Why Problems Even in a High Dasha?

Acharya ji shares his own experience. He is running the dasha of an exalted Guru (Jupiter), yet there are many problems. From a worldly standpoint, he could sit comfortably: there is money, everything, no worry.

But he has taken the responsibility to bring other people to that state too. On this path of service, sorrow will come again, and the **halahala poison** has to be drunk. Even in service, the world appears as "duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam" (an abode of sorrow, impermanent). Either the world shows you this by force, or you embrace it consciously.

## Selfless Service and the Promise of Yogakshema

Walking this path, bliss comes, and then all the rewards follow: wealth, Shri, Vidya, name and fame, on their own. The more selfless a person is, the greater their glory and prosperity.

> **ananyāś cintayanto māṁ ye janāḥ paryupāsate**
> **teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham**
> "Those who, with undivided devotion, think of Me and worship Me, for those ever-steadfast ones, I carry what they lack and preserve what they have."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

**Yogakshema** means providing what is lacking and protecting what one has. One who, with undivided feeling, thinking of nothing else, keeps others in their thoughts, has everything looked after by God Himself. The day this is understood, Karma Yoga begins.

## Lessons Build Character and Destiny

Experiences give knowledge, and mostly it is the bad experience that teaches. So what had to be done should have been done before the bad time came.

These lessons shape your **character** and your understanding of the world, and from character comes **destiny**. But beware: if the intellect is not developed, a wrong lesson can also be learned. There can be a wrong understanding of the right scripture, and a wrong understanding of Shani's message too. Even an understanding like "God is in everything, but not in this" can arise.

Still, some lesson or other always comes: "I should have given charity", "I should eat less", "I should not eat fried food or food from outside", "I should love", "I was very egoistic".

## Knowledge Is Within

Where were these lessons hidden? Did someone put them in from outside? No. **That knowledge exists within all of us.**

We ourselves are the **Atman**. In between there are only layers: of ego, of ignorance, of attachment. With each action, one layer at a time is removed.

Here lies the real difference:

- **Karma Yoga:** removing the layer through knowledge first, then acting
- **Ordinary action:** the layer being removed by the blow of experience

The layer is not to be removed by the blow of experience. Action is to be done with knowledge.

*Which layer within you could you remove with knowledge today, instead of letting life remove it with a blow?*

## Key Takeaways

- The purpose of every action should be gaining knowledge, not pleasure
- Knowledge will come anyway: either through action guided by knowledge, or through bitter experience
- Contemplate before acting, or learn from the Guru
- Do not postpone until sixty the lesson available at twenty; the past does not return later
- Good times are for service; bad times are for contemplation, knowledge and meditation
- Good and bad time is only a notion; awareness of liberation dissolves it
- For one who serves selflessly, God Himself carries their yogakshema (Gita 9.22)
- Knowledge is within; removing the layers of ego, ignorance and attachment through knowledge is Karma Yoga
