# Chapter: Action Guided by Knowledge, Understanding Before Experience

## What Is the Aim of Life?

So what is the aim of your life? In every action, see what this event is teaching me, what this event is about to teach me, and what I learned today. One whose focus is not there, what will they get? Sorrow. If your aim itself is anything apart from knowledge, sorrow is certain. This is the importance of knowledge in action.

## Only Two Paths: Action from Knowledge, or Knowledge from Action

That is why it is said: act with knowledge. Knowledge will come anyway. Either knowledge will come from action, or you must perform action guided by knowledge. That is the whole game. While acting, only knowledge is meant to come, but then it will come with bitter experience.

Which of the two is better? Action guided by knowledge is better. After that, you still have to act, and knowledge was going to be gained anyway. It will come either by force, or you will take it willingly. If you take it willingly, the happiness and bliss you were searching for in the wrong place will also be found. If it comes by force, you will get sorrow as well as the experience.

The knowledge to come was the same. What could have been learned through satsang at the age of twenty, its absence caused the problem at sixty. Had that knowledge come earlier, there would have been happiness even at sixty.

> "Every action that you perform should result in you obtaining some knowledge from it."

## Contemplation Before Action

That knowledge will come on its own sometime, through nature. So why not contemplate instead? And do it before acting: why am I doing this? Either get it known from the Guru, or, once the action is done, at least reflect on what must be done before the next action.

## Not Pleasure, but the Highest Spiritual Knowledge

Gaining high spiritual knowledge must be the ultimate aim of your life, not pleasures or happiness. Why? As I said, pleasure and pain can never be the aim of your life. They do not give lasting happiness anyway. Happiness is not in objects, not in achievements, not in actions. We examined all this through so many shlokas. So the aim itself should be the highest spiritual knowledge. How will I attain it: that should be the ultimate aim.

## When It Is Too Late

Most people do not do this. That is why pleasures and pains keep coming into their lives, but lessons do not. Then usually sorrow begins, and after that they understand on their own that they got the lesson. Nature did what it had to do. But by then it is too late. One cannot go back into the past.

For example, by the age of forty or forty-five the time of divorce has come, and at twenty-two one had married in haste, love at first sight. Now at forty, even if the knowledge has come of what ideal love was, what selfless love was, can you go back to twenty-five after that? Can the label that was attached be removed? Society, people, the sanskaras that were lost: can they come back now? Something is damaged, that much is certain.

I do not say that a positive life cannot happen or carry on after this. Everything is possible, but it would have been better if this knowledge had come at twenty-five, would it not?

This is the explanation of Karma Yoga: act, but first take knowledge. Otherwise nature will give knowledge anyway. Pleasure and pain are an outcome, it is said. They should not be your aim.

## Good Times or Bad Times: When Do Lessons Come?

Both good and bad experiences offer valuable lessons. But when do more lessons come, in good times or in bad times? In good times there is no time even to think about what lesson it gave. Ego comes in it. Very few people think, "I gave charity, I did this, that is why good times have come; now I must do more service from this." How many people get such a lesson? But in bad times, the lesson surely comes.

## Time Is Only Time

Then we learned in the Manki Gita of Maharshi Manki that there is nothing called good and bad time; there is only time. And later, when we learn Vedanta, we understand: not even time, there is something beyond time. So good time and bad time are just your idea. Do you know why time feels good or bad? Because you are not aware of liberation (mukti).

When good times come, what do we call good times? That I have money, relationships, everything, name and fame. When there is only the desire to consume, only to take, you call it a good time. And bad time is when this enjoyment is not available: money gone, job gone, relationships all gone.

Yet what was its purpose, as told in the Manki Gita? In good times you are to serve people; that was the time for Karma Yoga and Bhakti Yoga. And what you call bad time was a period of contemplation, a time for knowledge and meditation.

But people do not do that either. During Shani's Sade Sati they try to do even more action, try two more jobs. Whereas Shani is forcibly making you sit there. From a spiritual standpoint, what he wanted you to do, people simply do not do.

> "What you call a good time was never a good time; that was a time for serving others."

## Why Problems Even in a High Dasha?

And if you served, then even there the experience of "duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam" (an abode of sorrow, impermanent) would come. Now I am running the dasha of an exalted Guru (Jupiter), yet I have so many problems. Why? From a worldly standpoint, I could be sitting comfortably, so much money, everything, no tension, and I would be very happy, would I not? The dasha itself is such that there should be no tension.

But no. Because it is a good dasha, I have taken the responsibility to take so many people into that state too, so that someday they also get such a good dasha. When I go to do this, sorrow will come again. Then the halahala poison has to be drunk. What is the need of halahala? For whom does it become "duḥkhālayam aśāśvatam"? Either the world forces it, or you have to do it consciously.

## Selfless Service and Yogakshema

It is said that while doing this, bliss will come, and then you get all the rewards. Wealth comes, Shri comes, Vidya comes, everything happens, name and fame come on their own. The more selfless a person is, the more their name, fame, wealth, everything.

> **ananyāś cintayanto māṁ ye janāḥ paryupāsate**
> **teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham**
> "Those who, with undivided devotion, think of Me and worship Me, for those ever-steadfast ones, I carry what they lack and preserve what they have."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

It says "yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham", does it not? The one who, with undivided feeling, thinking of nothing else, keeps others in their thoughts, "ye janāḥ paryupāsate", for such "nityābhiyukta" people, I will look after everything. The day this is understood, your Karma Yoga can begin in some measure.

## It Is the Bad Experience That Teaches More

So both good and bad experiences offer valuable life lessons. That is, what do they give? They give knowledge. And mostly it is the bad experience that teaches something. So what had to be done should have been done before the bad time came.

## Lessons Build Character and Destiny

These lessons develop your character and understanding of the world. After the knowledge that experience gives, next time you... if the bad time has already happened, a lesson will still come from it. But if your intellect has not yet developed, you may take the wrong lesson meanwhile, may you not? There can be a wrong understanding of the right scripture, just as there can be a wrong understanding of Shani's message.

So lessons will build your character. Your character is formed from them, and from that your destiny also develops. After that comes the understanding of the world. Sometimes the understanding may even come that God is in everything, but not in this. Or lessons like: "No, I simply have to do this", "Oh, I should have given charity", "I should eat less", "I should not eat fried food or food from outside, it is harmful", "I should love", "I was very egoistic". Some lesson or other will surely come.

## Knowledge Is Within: Removing the Layers

Where were these lessons hidden? Were they somewhere outside? Did someone put something inside from outside? That knowledge exists within all of us. We were ourselves that Atman to begin with. So what was in between? Layers. Layers of what? Of ego, of ignorance, of attachment. With each action that you do, only one layer at a time is removed.

So to remove the layer first and then act, this is Karma Yoga. And to have the layer removed by going through experience, this is ordinary action. Do you understand the difference? The layer is not to be removed by going through experience; action is to be done with knowledge.
