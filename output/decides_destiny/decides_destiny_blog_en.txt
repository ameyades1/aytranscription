# Who Writes Your Destiny? Not Karma, but Samskara

We often hear, "As you sow, so shall you reap." But do our deeds alone really make our destiny? The Bhagavad Gita gives a deep and surprising answer to this question.

Let us understand, through this discourse, where the real root of destiny lies.

## Samskara: How Much Have You Become Like the Divine?

One word comes up again and again on the path of sadhana: **samskara**. It is said that by the Guru's grace your samskaras change.

The word samskara is formed from **sam + ansha + akara**. It means how much of you has taken the form of the Supreme. In what measure you have become like God (how much you are like the Lord).

We say the same thing in everyday language. For a child who does good deeds and speaks well, we say: "He has very good samskaras; his mother gave him very good samskaras." That is, he is like God.

And for one who steals and causes trouble, we say: "Did your mother give you no samskaras at all?" That is, he is very far from being like God.

*How much of you is filled with the Divine today? What samskaras do your words and deeds bear witness to?*

## When Kundalini Awakens, Samskaras Change

When the Guru raises your **chetana** (consciousness) and awakens your **kundalini shakti**, your samskaras change.

And from here comes the most important principle:

> "Samskaras determine your destiny."

## Are My Deeds My Destiny?

In the discourse, the listeners were asked one question again and again: how many believe that their destiny is made by their past sinful and virtuous deeds, by the fruit of their auspicious and inauspicious actions?

The answer was clear. According to the Bhagavad Gita, this belief is completely wrong. **Destiny is not made by past deeds; it is made by the samskaras of those deeds.**

## The Gita's Five Causes: Five Pillars of Destiny

In the eighteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, Lord Krishna gives five causes for the accomplishment of any action.

> **pañcaitāni mahābāho kāraṇāni nibodha me**
> **sāṅkhye kṛtānte proktāni siddhaye sarvakarmaṇām**
> "These five causes for the accomplishment of all actions are declared in the Sankhya doctrine, which shows the way to end all actions; learn them well from Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.13)

> **adhiṣṭhānaṁ tathā kartā karaṇaṁ ca pṛthag-vidham**
> **vividhāś ca pṛthak ceṣṭā daivaṁ caivātra pañcamam**
> "The seat of action, the doer, the various instruments, the many distinct efforts, and destiny as the fifth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.14)

The **accomplishment of action** means your resolve bearing fruit, or the action reaching completion where you get its fruit, or else dropping that action itself. In the discourse these are explained through the examples of a cricketer and a doctor.

1. **Adhishthana (foundation, the first thought):** First there must be the foundation of a thought. "I want to become Prime Minister", "I want to rest today", or in a cricketer's mind, "I have to score a century."
2. **Karta (the doer):** You yourself must be physically present and fit to play.
3. **Karana (organs and instruments):** Just as the inner instrument is called antahkarana, the outer organs are karana. To bat, the eyes, hands and feet must be sound; hair or a crooked nose make no difference to a century. **Upakarana** is needed too, the extension of the organs: a bat and pads for the player, a stethoscope for the doctor.
4. **Vividha cheshta (the many efforts):** Even with everything in place, the action still has to be done. The player has to hit the ball; the doctor has to examine.
5. **Daiva (destiny):** The fifth and subtlest cause.

## Daiva: The One That Shapes the Other Four

Daiva means your destiny. And this very destiny also decides what you think.

Not everyone becomes an architect, a doctor or a politician. The question is, why did that particular thought come to someone? That is part of his destiny.

He made the resolve, but will he be physically able for the work? Someone may get paralysis; someone may be born without one or two organs. What facilities one gets, whether one acts or stays lazy, all this too is based on daiva.

So what is the Gita's definition of daiva? Settle it clearly in your mind:

> "The samskaras of auspicious and inauspicious deeds done earlier are called daiva."

**Destiny is not made by karma. It is made by the samskara you put into your mind after doing the karma.**

## You Gave Charity, but Whose Fruit Did You Get?

Suppose you gave a lot in charity, but brought ego along with it: "Only I can give charity to everyone."

Then what samskara was planted in the mind? That of ego. So the fruit will be not of charity, but of that ego.

*Is there sometimes an "I" hiding behind your good deeds too?*

## The Same Act, Two Different Destinies

How, after all, are sin and merit decided? Is killing someone auspicious or inauspicious? Inauspicious. Then what of our soldiers sitting on the border, who fire so many bullets? How will that be merit for them? And why was Arjuna told to fight?

The answer: it depends on the circumstances and the samskara with which the act was done. If someone was killed, was it revenge? For whom was it done? What was the thought behind it?

If someone ripped open another's stomach with a knife to rob him, that is sin. But when a doctor performs surgery, he does it to save you.

> "The act is the same; the samskara behind it, what you think about the other person, that will determine your destiny."

## Higher Kundalini, Higher Samskaras

As your kundalini shakti rises, your samskaras too become of a higher level. And higher samskaras create a higher destiny.

## Key Takeaways

- **Samskara = sam + ansha + akara:** how much of you has taken the form of the Supreme.
- When the Guru raises consciousness and kundalini shakti, samskaras change, and samskaras decide destiny.
- According to the Gita (18.13-14), the five causes of the accomplishment of action are adhishthana, the doer, the instruments, the many efforts and daiva.
- Daiva is the name of the samskaras of past auspicious and inauspicious deeds, not of the deeds themselves.
- Charity given with ego bears the fruit of ego, not of charity.
- The same act (such as surgery and robbery) becomes sin or merit by the samskara behind it.
