# Chapter: Who Shapes Destiny? Karma or Samskara

## The True Meaning of Samskara

You have heard this word again and again: samskara. On the path of sadhana, your samskaras change. But what does samskara mean?

The word samskara is formed from **sam, ansha and akara**. It means how much of your being (ansha) has taken the form (akara) of the Supreme. By how much, in what measure, you have become like God: that is the measure of your samskara.

We can understand this from daily life. A child who does good deeds, acts with good qualities and speaks well, what do we say about him? "Your son has very good samskaras. His mother gave him very good samskaras." Being given good samskaras means you are like God.

And the one who steals, causes trouble, does negative things, what is said to him? "Did your mother give you no samskaras at all?" It means you are not like the Lord; you cannot even be compared with Him; you are far away from that.

So the root of the word samskara is sam-ansha-akara: how much of my form has become filled with the Supreme.

## Kundalini, Samskara and Destiny

When the Guru raises your consciousness, awakens your kundalini shakti, your samskaras change. And samskaras alone determine your destiny.

> "Samskaras alone determine your destiny."

## A Question: What Makes Destiny?

To complete this subject of samskara, let us open the eighteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, verses thirteen, fourteen and fifteen.

First, a question. How many of you feel that your destiny is made by the sinful and virtuous deeds you did in the past? How many believe that whatever I have done is responsible for my destiny? How many believe that the fruit of my auspicious and inauspicious deeds is my destiny? I am asking again and again.

According to the Bhagavad Gita, this answer is completely wrong. The Gita says destiny is not made because of past deeds; it is made because of the **samskaras** of those deeds.

## The Five Causes of the Accomplishment of Action

The eighteenth chapter says this clearly. I cannot go into its detail today; that we teach in the shivir (retreat).

> **pañcaitāni mahābāho kāraṇāni nibodha me**
> **sāṅkhye kṛtānte proktāni siddhaye sarvakarmaṇām**
> "O mighty-armed one, learn from Me these five causes for the accomplishment of all actions, declared in the Sankhya doctrine, which shows the way to end all actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.13)

These are the five factors that determine your destiny.

The accomplishment of actions means the fulfilment of your resolve: what is to bear fruit for you, or the action ending where you receive its fruit, or else you drop that action itself.

> **adhiṣṭhānaṁ tathā kartā karaṇaṁ ca pṛthag-vidham**
> **vividhāś ca pṛthak ceṣṭā daivaṁ caivātra pañcamam**
> "The seat of action, the doer, the various instruments, the many distinct efforts, and destiny as the fifth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.14)

## The First Cause: Adhishthana

Adhishthana means foundation. First there must be the foundation of a thought: "I have to do this. I want to become Prime Minister. I have to do such-and-such. Today I want to rest a little." Whatever it is, adhishthana must be there. If someone is a cricketer, the thought comes to his mind: "I have to score a century." This is adhishthana, the first thought.

## The Second Cause: The Doer

The second cause is the doer. You yourself have to be physically present. If I want to be a cricketer and score a century, first the thought comes to my mind that I must score a century; that is adhishthana. After that I must be physically fit to play; that is the doer.

## The Third Cause: The Various Instruments

The third cause is the various kinds of karana. Karana means instruments and sense organs. Just as we call the inner instrument antahkarana, the outer organs are called karana.

Only if my eyes, hands and feet are sound will I be able to bat. Do I need my hair to be fine for that? If my nose is crooked, can I not score a century? That is, the organs needed for the particular task must be in perfect order.

Then are the supporting things, the bat and pads, also needed or not? If I am a doctor, do I need a stethoscope or not? This is called **upakarana**, the extension of my organs. Karana and upakarana: I must have these. These too will determine my destiny.

## The Fourth Cause: The Many Efforts

Even after having all this, many different kinds of effort are needed. After having everything, I still have to act. The player still has to hit the ball; the doctor still has to examine the patient.

## The Fifth Cause: Daiva

What is the fifth cause? **Daiva**. Daiva basically means your destiny. Because this very destiny also decides what I think.

Not everybody tries to become an architect. Not everyone becomes an architect, not everyone becomes a doctor, not everyone becomes a politician. The question is, why did that particular thought come to him? That is part of his destiny.

He made the resolve, but whether he will be physically able for that work may also depend on his daiva. Someone may get paralysis; someone may be born without one or two organs. Which facilities and instruments one gets or does not get is also part of destiny. Whether he will act or not, whether he will be lazy or not, all this is again based on destiny itself.

## What Is Daiva?

So what meaning does the Gita give for daiva? Settle this clearly in your mind once:

> "The samskaras of auspicious and inauspicious deeds done earlier are called daiva."

Your destiny is not made by karma. It is made by the samskara you put into your mind after doing the karma.

> "Destiny is not made by karma; it is made by the samskara you put into your mind after doing the karma."

## Charity and Ego

You gave a lot in charity, but in return you came back with ego: "Only I can give charity to everyone." So what samskara did you plant? That of ego. Then you will not get the fruit of charity; you will get the fruit of that ego.

## The Same Act, Different Samskaras

Someone killed another person. Was it revenge? For whom was it done? What was the samskara behind it, what was the thought?

Now how are sin and merit determined? If I kill someone, is it auspicious or inauspicious? Inauspicious. Then our soldiers sitting on the border fire so many bullets; how will that be merit for them? Why was Arjuna told to fight? It depends on the circumstances and on the samskara with which he did that act.

Someone ripped open another's stomach with a knife to rob him; that is sin. But when a doctor performs surgery, he does it to save you. The act is the same. The samskara behind it, sam-ansha-akara, what you think about the other person, that will determine your destiny.

> "The act is the same; the samskara behind it alone determines your destiny."
