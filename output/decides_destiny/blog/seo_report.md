## 1. Summary

The talk asks whether our past actions alone make our destiny, and answers through Bhagavad Gita 18.13-14: of the five causes behind the accomplishment of any action (adhishthana, karta, karana, vividha cheshta and daiva), the fifth, daiva, is explained as the samskaras (impressions) left by past good and bad actions, not the actions themselves. Acharya Ji reads samskara as "sam + ansh + akar" (how much of you has taken the form of God), ties the change of samskaras to the Guru raising consciousness and Kundalini, and illustrates the point with a cricketer, a doctor, charity given with ego, and the same cut made by a robber and a surgeon. The main search intent is informational with a strong emotional undertone: people searching "भाग्य कैसे बनता है", "kya karm hi bhagya hai" or "what decides your destiny" want to know whether they are trapped by the past. Ranking pages are mostly astrology and motivational content or generic "karma vs destiny" essays; a verse-based answer built on the Gita's five causes, with everyday examples, is a distinctive and honest angle. "Gita 18.14 five causes" and "daiva meaning" are low-competition long-tail entry points where a precise answer can rank.

What decides your destiny? The Bhagavad Gita (18.13-14) names five causes behind every accomplished action: the foundation, the doer, the instruments, the efforts and daiva, or destiny. **English**
1. **Do actions alone decide destiny?** According to Acharya Ji, no. Destiny comes from the samskaras of actions, the impressions they leave in the mind.
2. **What are the five causes of action in the Gita?** The foundation, the doer, the various instruments, the many kinds of effort and, fifth, daiva (Bhagavad Gita 18.14).
3. **What does daiva mean?** The discourse explains daiva as the samskaras left by past good and bad actions.
4. **What fruit does charity given with ego bring?** The impression left is ego, so the fruit comes from the ego, not from the giving.
5. **How do samskaras change?** When the Guru raises your consciousness and awakens Kundalini Shakti, your samskaras change and grow more refined.

## 10. Authenticity flags

From the Sanskrit review (`tools/review/decides_destiny.md`) and the SEO check:

1. **"The Gita says the belief is wrong" / "the Gita defines daiva" (Major, from the review).** Gita 18.14 only names daiva as the fifth cause; the definition "samskaras of past good and bad actions" comes from the Gita Press commentary and from Acharya Ji's teaching, not the verse. Recommendation: apply the review's corrections ("प्रवचन में गीता के आधार पर समझाया गया कि यह धारणा अधूरी है" / "Drawing on the Gita, the discourse explained that this belief is incomplete"). The titles, meta descriptions, direct answers and FAQ here already attribute the definition to Acharya Ji ("explains daiva as"), and avoid "the Gita says karma does not create destiny". Editor to decide.
2. **"Pillars of destiny" heading (Minor, from the review).** The five causes are causes of the accomplishment of action, of which daiva is one. Recommendation: rename the heading as in section 4; the English secondary keyword uses "five causes of action", not "five pillars of destiny".
3. **Etymology of samskara (Minor, from the review).** "सम् + अंश + आकार" is the speaker's teaching reading, not the grammatical derivation (सम् + √कृ + घञ्). The Hindi presents it as fact. Recommendation: use the review's wording; do not target "संस्कार शब्द की उत्पत्ति" or "samskara etymology" as a keyword, and the FAQ avoids the etymology.
4. **Readings of adhishthana, karta and karana (Minor, from the review).** The talk's "first thought", "physically present" and "outer senses" readings differ from the Gita Press glosses (body; doer; mind, intellect and senses). Recommendation: add the review's bridge line before the list. The direct answer uses neutral glosses (the foundation, the doer, the instruments).
5. **Missing vocative in the Hindi meaning of 18.13 (Minor, from the review).** Add "हे महाबाहो!" as the review suggests. No SEO impact.
6. **Kundalini and the Guru (SEO).** The claim that the Guru's raising of Kundalini changes samskaras and thereby destiny is Acharya Ji's teaching and is presented as such; no title or meta promises a quick change of destiny, and "change your destiny fast" style keywords are deliberately not targeted.
7. **Soldiers and violence (SEO).** The talk's point that the same act can be sin or merit depending on the samskara behind it should not be excerpted alone in snippets or social cards as a justification of killing; keep the surgeon example and Arjuna's context together.
