# Chapter: The Evolution of Dharma: From the Mantra Samhita to the Upanishads

## The First Stage: The Mantra Samhita, the Part of Praise

The first part of the Veda is called the Mantra Samhita. Mantra Samhita means offering praise to the Supreme Lord. It is praise that is written in it.

Picture it as a scene. A guest is coming to your home. You have invited him with great feeling; you have called him in. He arrives and you say, "Come, come, please sit." Then for two hours you speak only good things: "Oh, you have achieved so much! You received a degree, you had a dance competition, you did this, you did that, your business has grown so much." In this way you praise him lavishly. And then you say, "Enough praise now, please go. I have spoken well of you for two hours; now I will get back to my work." Will that do?

In the Vedas, the Mantra Samhita is this part of praise. Someone realised that I should show appreciation. The sun was up above. People of the four varnas existed even then, according to their intellect. In those days there was no business that ran all year round; there was farming, which happened only when the rains came. So there were different kinds of labour.

Those with a Brahmin intellect also had a great deal of time in those days. They would wonder how this nature runs, how faithfully it does its work. That is all they observed: how the river keeps flowing without pause. The feeling arose in them that the time has come for me to appreciate this. Their minds were calm, so they could think about such things beyond themselves.

The rishis and munis reflected in solitude. Their desires were fewer than those of others. Everyone else had work and only work: the king had to govern, the Vaishya needed trade and money, and the poor Shudra was oppressed and did all the labour. So the rishis and munis meditated upon nature: how faithfully it comes and goes, how the sun rises by its law. While praising it, compositions arose on their own, and those compositions became the Mantra Samhita portion of the Vedas.

## The Second Stage: The Brahmana Portion, Where Praise Became Action

After some time this evolved. A guest has come home, so what will merely saying "you are good, you are good" achieve? Then came the second part of the Veda, which is called the Brahmana.

The spirit of the Brahmana portion is this: a guest has come, so at least offer a laddu, give a little water, give something. He has come, so first give something, then we will talk. We will certainly praise him, but along with it something must happen first: have his hands and feet washed, tell him we have made arrangements for him here. From this feeling arose the Shodashopachara puja, the Panchopachara puja and all the other rites.

Take the sun as the example. First it was said, "O Aditya, O Surya, O Lord Surya, O Surya Deva." As the name grew, first it was Surya, then perhaps Surya ji. Then it evolved further: is this enough for Lord Surya? Come, let us offer him water. In this way something was added to the action.

Physically, of course, the sun cannot receive anything. The water does not even reach him; poured from an upper floor, it falls on the head of the person below, and there is a complaint. Then worship began in one's own home, and the sun came into an idol. The water goes neither to the sun nor anywhere else. Then came a new rite, because I cannot go to the river every day. In this way new rites kept evolving. This part of the Veda is called the Brahmana.

The name of the Brahmin varna also comes from this Brahmana portion of the Vedas. One who walks the path of Brahman is called a Brahmin; he has a single aim, to go towards Brahman. That part of the Veda concerned with action, which includes yajna, dana, tapas, upachara and puja, is called the Brahmana.

## Love Is Expressed in Action

Gradually it came to be understood that when a guest comes, praise alone will not do. Some action is needed along with it. **Love is expressed in action.**

I always say: if a wife says, "I love you very much, but I will not make tea for you," that does not work.

Shri Krishna often gave gifts to Radha. Why? The stories describe that he gave gifts such as Kusum Vihar, Radha Kund and Krishna Kund. He was a king, so he gave gifts of that level. It is said, give at your own level. Love must be in action, and that too unconditionally. If love does not reflect in action, then that love was not love. Merely saying it with the mouth is a different matter. If a king says, "I love you very much," and later imposes heavy taxes, that is not love. Love must be seen in action: he built roads, he built everything for the people; then it is real love. The teaching of the Brahmana portion of the Vedas is this clear: not mere appreciation.

About the wife I also say this: if someone sits with a japa mala and praises all day long, "Honey, honey, honey, honey," will that do? She will say that she is going to someone else. Why would that happen? Because praise alone is of no use; action is needed along with it.

In this way ritual, karmakanda, began, and it came to be called the Brahmana portion. People kept moving forward; worship too kept evolving. In this way these two parts came into being.

## Nature Worship and Ancestor Worship

After this a very great history unfolded. How many of you know it? Let me tell you a little of the history of the Brahmin and of this worship. Swami Vivekananda has described it beautifully in Bhakti Yoga, and in Karma Yoga as well.

After this, that knowledge was lost for a long time. Then the Brahmin class began performing worship, because it was in them that the evolution had happened. They understood that there is something, there is nature. Then, each in his own hut, each in his own ashram, they began saying "thank you, thank you, thank you" and performing its rites. Was their aim a bad one? They began worshipping the sun god, the river, the trees and plants, everything.

It is said that religion began with ancestor worship and nature worship. At some point the first human being must have died. Until then everything was fine. Then the question arose, "He was right here yesterday; where has he gone?" People began to wonder: he was among us, what happened to him? Then some research must have been done on it, then some action for it, and then shraddha, pinda and everything else must have begun.

At first there was only praise: "He was a good man." We still do this in shraddha today. For the person who has gone, we speak well and think well of him. Praise still happens, and action also happens: from the last rites of the body to everything else. From ancestor worship and nature worship began the worship of the devatas, because that is what could be seen. In sadhana one first goes from the form to the formless. It was said: what is seen, that alone will be worshipped, and at first this is all they could see. Until then the Veda was a process of knowing, and this much had been understood.

## When the Kings and Vaishyas Grew Curious

So was the work of the Brahmin right or wrong? At that time the aim was to give thanks, to perform its rites, and everyone was doing his own.

Seeing this, the Vaishyas and Kshatriyas grew curious: what is it these people do? "I should know; something is going on here. Good things are happening to them, they are gaining siddhis and knowledge; they are certainly benefiting." The kings came to them and began inquiring. The Brahmins told them everything: "This is how we give thanks; this is what we do."

Now the king was rajasic. He said, "I have no time; will you do this for us?" Understand that this was still the pure stage. The Vaishya too said, "I have money; you do it and I will give you dakshina." The king would say, "You do it for me, and I will do this and that for you; do it for my protection." The Vaishya would say, "Do it for wealth."

Then the rishis would say, "Oh, it does not work like that. Whatever I have received from nature, I am giving thanks for it. You will have to give your own thanks yourself." Do you understand? You will have to give your own thanks yourself. "I can teach you that knowledge, I can bring it down into you, as it passed from the Sun to Manu and from Manu to Ikshvaku. I may guide you; I may be your teacher."

But the king would say, "No, no, you do it. We have a great deal of work; we cannot do it. You want dakshina; I will give it." And they began forcing it upon them: "I will give you so many cows, so much of this."

## The Beginning of Dakshina

When the king kept pressing all this upon them, an assembly of Brahmins was held. The assembly said, "Free cows are being offered, so take them; what is lost?" This is where dakshina began; it is described in our own scriptures.

At that time they said that now we must also learn this: someone will not have the time, so can another do it on his behalf? How to do my own worship I have learnt; now how is it to be done for someone else? Then how it is to be done while meditating on his mind is given in Patanjali. Patanjali Rishi explained that by meditating on another, there will be some exchange of my tapas. This is where the whole theory we speak of begins: meditating on him, giving my tapas, taking on his poison. Then they started that knowledge. "Come, it is being given for free. He is taking it; he himself is doing nothing."

For some time this went well. The Brahmins did it for themselves and for others too. The king was happy, the Vaishya was happy, and the rest of the serving class was happy too, that at least someone had been found to do worship for them. No one learnt it; only the Brahmins knew it. Since then this ritual has been going on.

## When Worship Became Mechanical

Then a time came. One generation of Brahmins passed away, and the generation of those kings passed too. The Brahmin two or three generations later began doing the worship mechanically; that is all he had learnt. The king had learnt that dakshina must be given to the Brahmin. The Brahmin forgot for whom he used to perform the worship. The king forgot why he used to give the dakshina. After the third and fourth generation, both had become mechanical: one did mechanical worship, the other gave mechanical dakshina.

After this, those of less developed intellect and consciousness, the Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras, saw that he merely offers this many flowers, does this, does that; I can do this too. Not the purpose of the Atma Tattva, only the outer action. They began doing it themselves, and the business of the Brahmins began to end.

All this is described in our scriptures, and Swami Vivekananda has explained this entire matter. When you read him on how the history of dharma evolved, you will understand this. The Brahmins said, "This has now become a problem; our business is going. They have learnt it themselves, and what did they learn? Only to offer flowers mechanically. They too have started doing the same." Then they said, "This will not do. Now this intellect has stagnated, and our business is gone too. Now we have to evolve further."

## Many Gods, Many Purposes

This means that the Veda, which had come to a halt, evolved again. "No, no, keeping a single deity will not do. Now we will have many deities. Do you want a house? Then this puja. Do you want a vehicle? Then this puja. Do you want a son? Then this puja." They made the methods of worship different from one another. Now the mantras too would be somewhat different. In this way the Mantra Samhita, the rites and the Brahmana portion all changed once more.

But this was not to trap people. It was evolution; the Veda was evolving. Man had learnt. We say, don't we, that necessity is the mother of invention. This necessity arose; the question of their survival arose. The knowledge that existed had become obsolete, because others had copied it. So something new came: new deities kept arising, and for different purposes.

Yes, specialisation also came into it: this one has this mantra, that one has that mantra. It developed like a scientific discovery. And once again the business of the Brahmins began to run, because people wanted specific remedies. The intellect evolved. I am not saying this in the sense of trapping people either; the intellect genuinely evolved. For this, higher dakshina began to be asked, because there was research behind it.

This again went on for years together. Again four or five generations of both sides passed, and both forgot again. On their own so many deities came into being, so many purposes, everything kept growing. And in the end it became a multi-billion-dollar business like today's. People understood only this: unless this much is given, nothing happens. And the evolution of the Veda stopped once again.

## The Revolt of Yajnavalkya Rishi

Just then, from among them, came Yajnavalkya Rishi. He himself was from a Brahmin family, but he did not like this. He asked, "What was the purpose? Self-realisation. Why did our ancestors, the rishis and munis, begin this? To see the Supreme Lord, to know Him, to praise Him. This does not seem right to me. We must oppose it. This is becoming a way of trapping these people. Now it has become a perfect business."

But when a lobby forms, does anyone listen right away? That it should stop from tomorrow?

Yajnavalkya Rishi went to his guru and said, "All this seems wrong to me." The guru said, "Even if it seems wrong, you will have to do this, because it has now become the tradition. Now this has become a business, so it is a business. What is, is; this is how it goes." Yajnavalkya said, "I do not accept this." Then the guru said, "If you do not accept it, then return the knowledge I have given you, and go."

The story says that he vomited, that is, he threw it up. This is symbolic of his returning all that knowledge. "Now I will discover something new of my own." Then he immediately went into meditation, and he realised some higher truths: the truth is not only in this; there is something a little higher than this.

## The Division into Shukla and Krishna Yajurveda

But would that big lobby listen to him? At once he was opposed. A rule was made with the king's help, because the king too was rajasic and readily came along. Despite Yajnavalkya Rishi's knowledge being excellent, a division was made: what the Brahmins did was given the name Shukla Yajurveda, and what was Yajnavalkya's was called black, all wrong, and so Krishna Yajurveda. That is why even today there is more ritual in the Shukla Yajurveda; in the Krishna Yajurveda there are rites too, but the contemplative portion is greater in it.

Then Yajnavalkya Rishi was boycotted. Something like what is called being externed: "You are not to stay here; whatever you want to do, go and do it outside." He had to go to the forest, to the aranya. But he continued his research. The process of knowing went on. Then the truths that became known to him, he gave to society after some time, on returning. His time too had to come.

## The Aranyakas and the Upanishads

That part of the Veda is called the Aranyaka. From that very Aranyaka arose the Upanishad named Brihadaranyaka. Then there is the Chandogya Upanishad, which is an Upanishad of a middle position, a mixture of both: in it will come a little of the ritual portion, something of the body too, and of the Atma as well.

So the order is this: first the Mantra Samhita, then the Brahmana, then the Aranyaka, and beyond even this, on the basis of Yajnavalkya Rishi's knowledge, in which the Atma was known, that part became the Upanishad.

## Shri Krishna's Message: Rise Above the Three Gunas

That is why Shri Krishna said:

> **traiguṇya-viṣayā vedā nistraiguṇyo bhavārjuna**
> **nirdvandvo nitya-sattva-stho niryoga-kṣema ātmavān**
> "The Vedas deal with the realm of the three gunas; O Arjuna, rise above the three gunas, free from the pairs of opposites, ever established in sattva, free from concern for acquiring and preserving, established in the Self."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.45)

The Vedas are concerned with the objects of the three gunas; O Arjuna, rise above the three gunas. Go above all of this; learn the essence of the Veda directly. Understand this clearly.

**Nirdvandva** means beyond the pairs of opposites: cold and heat, gain and loss, victory and defeat, honour and dishonour; rise above all these. First become nirdvandva.

**Nitya-sattva-stha**: one is not to take up the actions of rajas and tamas, but to remain always established in sattva. And if you want sattva, where will you find it? With a genuine guru.

**Niryoga-kshema**: give up even the worry over your yoga and kshema, over what you are to gain and how to keep what you have gained. Your guru will then take care of that. That is why Shri Krishna goes on to say:

> **ananyāś cintayanto māṁ ye janāḥ paryupāsate**
> **teṣāṁ nityābhiyuktānāṁ yoga-kṣemaṁ vahāmy aham**
> "Those who worship Me, thinking of Me with undivided devotion, ever united with Me: their gaining and keeping I Myself carry."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

Those who worship Me with undivided devotion, contemplating Me, who are ever engaged in Me: their yoga and kshema I Myself carry. I will look after you. Shri Krishna takes you there.

## The Scripture That Says, "Do Not Read Me"

The verses just before this, around the forty-first and forty-second verses of the second chapter, say this plainly. The Veda is the only scripture in the world which calls out and says, "Do not read me."

You call it the book of the Hindus. Shri Krishna is our Lord, and the Gita our guide. And He Himself says that it is not to be read. This is the only scripture that calls out plainly and says, "Do not read me." How astonishing it is to hear this! About the Veda of Sanatan Dharma, it was said from the mouth of Shri Krishna that one who merely keeps reading it is deluded.

> "One who reads Vedanta, that too is the Veda, isn't it? Read only that much of the Veda."

He has said that out of a hundred pages, ninety-six were to be left aside, and one was to come straight to the last four pages. This means that one was to come to a guru and learn this final knowledge.
