## 1. Summary

A short talk (the video runs 5 min 46 s) that moves the Trimurti and their consorts from story to life: **Brahma–Saraswati** are the power to begin (rajas, action, guided by knowledge), **Vishnu–Lakshmi** the power to sustain (sattva and wealth), and **Mahesh–Kali / Mahakala–Mahakali** the power of tamas that one day takes everything away. Its sharpest point is about Saraswati: everyone, even a thief or a drinker, has "a Saraswati of their own", so the real question is the level and source of one's knowledge, and Saraswati is worshipped alongside Lakshmi: knowledge before wealth. The main intent is **informational** ("ब्रह्मा विष्णु महेश का अर्थ", "त्रिदेव और त्रिदेवियाँ", "देव और देवियाँ", "Brahma Vishnu Mahesh meaning", "Trimurti", "gods and goddesses"), with a **seasonal** hook at Diwali / Lakshmi Puja (early November 2026; check the panchang), so publish now and refresh 4–6 weeks before Diwali each year. Ranking pages are mostly mythology summaries ("Brahma creates, Vishnu preserves, Shiva destroys") or kids' explainers; the angle only this post offers is the three pairs as forces in one's own work, habits and losses, which is exactly what the talk says stories for children leave out.

## 6. FAQ

For the page or a WordPress FAQ block only; answers come from the talk.

1. **त्रिदेव और त्रिदेवियाँ कौन हैं? / Who are the three gods and three goddesses?** Brahma with Saraswati, Vishnu with Lakshmi, and Mahesh with Parvati or Kali (Mahakala and Mahakali). Together they stand for creation, sustenance and dissolution.
2. **ब्रह्मा के साथ सरस्वती क्यों हैं? / Why is Saraswati paired with Brahma?** Beginning anything needs action (rajas), but action alone is not enough; whatever is created with knowledge turns out far better.
3. **"हर किसी की अपनी सरस्वती" का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "everyone has their own Saraswati" mean?** Everyone, even a thief or a drinker, has reasons for what they do. What matters is whether that knowledge is self-made out of ignorance or confirmed by an authentic source.
4. **पालन के लिए विष्णु और लक्ष्मी क्यों चाहिए? / Why are Vishnu and Lakshmi needed for sustenance?** Whatever is born must be cared for, which needs steadiness (sattva) and the means to provide food, clothing and medicine (Lakshmi).
5. **महाकाल और महाकाली की शक्ति क्या है? / What is the power of Mahakala and Mahakali?** Tamas, the force that runs on its own and one day takes away people, possessions and position; Acharya Ji asks us to prepare for that day.

## 10. Authenticity flags

From the Sanskrit review (tools/review/power_of_deities_in_your_life.md, verdict "Minor issues"); none is applied to the blog yet:

1. **"Saraswati Puja before Lakshmi Puja, in every scripture" (review issue 1).** The order is unconfirmed (printed Deepavali vidhis often put the bahi-khata Saraswati puja after Mahalakshmi puja) and "every scripture" overstates it. The SEO texts therefore say only "knowledge before wealth" and that Saraswati is worshipped too; the Hindi secondary keyword is "धन से पहले ज्ञान", not "लक्ष्मी से पहले सरस्वती". Apply the review's wording; if the heading changes, update the also-read/heading notes in section 4.
2. **Rajas/sattva bridge for creation (review issue 2).** The blog never says Saraswati is sattvic, so "in creation and in sustenance alike" lacks a step. Apply the review's added clause. The direct answer (section 5) keeps to what the blog says.
3. **ब्रह्म भाव → ब्रह्मा-भाव (review issue 3).** ब्रह्मभाव means oneness with Brahman. Not used in SEO text.
4. **सत्वगुण → सत्त्वगुण (review issue 4).** The SEO texts already use सत्त्वगुण; the blog should match.
5. **Swami Samarth and the chillum (review issue 5).** The English adds a narrator's statement of fact that the Hindi does not make. Apply the review's shorter wording. Keep the saints' names out of all SEO text, snippets and social cards.

SEO-specific:

6. **"Power of Deities" in the video title.** It could be read as a promise of boons or miracles. The talk is about inner powers in one's own life; titles, meta and social cards must not promise wealth, protection or results from worship.
7. **Diwali hook.** Use the Lakshmi Puja season for refreshing and promotion, but never present the puja order as a ritual instruction; the talk gives no vidhi.
8. **Shiva or Kali.** The video thumbnail shows Shiva with Parvati, while the blog's emphasis is Mahakala–Mahakali and tamas. "Dissolution" (संहार) is the talk's word; avoid "destroyer" framings that make Kali a fear figure, and keep Parvati in the alt text as the thumbnail shows her.

## 12. SEO checklist

### Keywords
- [x] Hindi and English each have a focus keyword
- [x] The focus keyword isn't used by another post (no cannibalization)
- [x] 2–4 secondary keywords per language
- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best

### Category and tags
- [x] One category, from the approved 14
- [x] 3–6 tags per language
- [x] No tag repeats a category name
- [x] Existing tags reused; no near-duplicates
- [x] Each tag can be shared by at least 3 posts
- [x] No broad labels that only restate a category
- [x] Series tags named exactly as the series
- [x] `festivals` only when the post is about the festival itself; Pitru topics in `pitru`

### Structured data (in this report, not on the page)
- [x] One JSON-LD block per language: BlogPosting, Person, Organization, VideoObject (if there is a video), BreadcrumbList
- [x] Hindi and English versions linked as translations

### Content trust
- [x] Titles, metas and snippets faithful to the talk
- [x] No unsupported scriptural claims
- [x] Health promises, fear-selling and attacks on other traditions flagged

### Not covered
- Live broken links (internal links only work once the linked post is published)
- Canonical and Open Graph tags (SureRank adds these itself)
