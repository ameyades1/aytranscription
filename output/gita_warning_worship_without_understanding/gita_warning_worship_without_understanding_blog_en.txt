# Marriage to a Photo or Union with God

## Do You Know Your God?

We worship every day. We offer flowers, perform aarti, chant mantras. But have you ever paused to ask what the one you worship **actually is**?

Acharya Ji says that we should know our God.

This discourse begins with that one simple but piercing question. And today you will be made to think about exactly this.

## The Final Goal: The Formless

All of us ultimately have to reach the **nirākāra** (the formless, that which has no form). The final worship is of the formless alone.

But the formless cannot be seen. Even those who went there and returned could not describe it. So whatever definition seemed nearest to them, they poured into the form of the murtis.

In other words, every **murti** is a sign, a finger pointing toward the formless.

## Every Murti Is a Code

Look closely:

- **Saraswati** is the goddess of knowledge, and she is with Brahma ji, the creator.
- **Brahma ji** is shown as an old man with a beard, because he is aged in wisdom.
- He has **four faces**, because knowledge of all four directions is needed.
- The other deities are shown with sculpted, powerful bodies, to convey youth and strength.
- But **Ganapati** has a rounded belly. That too is deliberate.

Each deity's form was designed according to the qualities we need from it. Height, colour, how many hands and feet, clothing, the colour of the clothing, what is offered to it: all of these hold hidden signs for reaching the formless.

*Do you understand even one sign in the form of the deity you worship?*

## The Gita's Warning

Now comes the sternest part of the discourse.

If this meaning is not understood, what will clinging to the symbol give you? Worship every murti in the world your whole life, and still you will gain nothing except another birth. And even that birth may be one step lower.

The speaker says clearly that this is not his own statement but that of the Bhagavad Gita itself: Shri Krishna tells us that it leads to **adhogati** (a spiritual downfall).

Worship and rituals performed without knowing the **marma** (the essence, the inner meaning) have no meaning.

## A Question: Would You Marry a Photo?

To explain this, an unusual example is given.

The goal of spirituality is **ekatva** (oneness), becoming one. Like the form of **Ardhanarishvara**, like husband and wife being called each other's **ardhāṅga** (other half).

Now suppose someone gave you a photo of some unknown girl or boy from the internet and said, "This is your life partner. Just live with this photo."

No name, no address. Dark or fair, nothing known. Can you become one with that person? Can there be love or marriage with them?

And what if someone falls in love with the photo itself? Acharya Ji asks whether it is right or wrong to say that one fell in love with a photo and even got married to the photo.

## The Path Beyond the Photo

A real relationship is built like this:

1. See the photo.
2. Then ask for the biodata: who is she, what does she do?
3. Then learn more: where does she live, who are her parents, who is her family?
4. Then go to meet them.
5. Then marriage, and from it the next generation.

Now if you stay stuck at the first step, the photo, will that work?

## Have We Stopped at the Photo?

This is where the example becomes a mirror held up to us.

"I love Ganapati." "I love Lakshmi ji." But who was Hanuman ji? Where was he? What is his true form? Never seen, never known.

Acharya Ji says that this is our foolishness, whether we accept it or not.

The words are harsh, but the intention is compassion. The speaker says he does not want to give just a mantra or two; he wants to give **vidyā** (knowledge). The mantra will come at the end, in **sādhanā**.

*The one you ask from every day: do you really know who that is?*

## So Is Murti Worship Wrong?

Not at all. This is said clearly: "When did we ever say, do not worship **sākāra** (God with form)?"

Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped Kali. Tukaram and Namdev called out "Pandurang, Pandurang." But the difference is that **they did it knowing the meaning.**

The saints who attained knew these were symbols. They reach the step where murti worship is no longer needed at all. Murti worship is **the first step**.

And who will tell you this? A **living guru**, because those saints have gone into samādhi. It can also be seen in their life stories.

## Attraction Versus Love

Looking at a photo is not wrong. If the marriage is settled and all the references are known, the photo has meaning.

But a photo found by searching any name on the internet? At most it can bring **infatuation**, just an attraction. It will not be love. And **union** certainly not.

## Only One God

The discourse pauses on a big question. God is one. In Hinduism too there is only one God, "that one same single Lord".

Then what are these **thirty-three koti** deities?

*If God is one, why so many forms? Pause and reflect on this once.*

## Key Takeaways

- The final goal is the formless; murtis are signs for reaching it.
- The shape, colour, hands and clothing of every murti are thoughtfully designed symbols.
- Worship without knowing the essence, in the Gita's words as the speaker conveys them, leads to a downfall.
- Loving God without knowing God is like marrying an unknown photo.
- The saints also worshipped form, but knowing its meaning; murti worship is the first step.
- Only a living guru can reveal this meaning.
- Without knowledge there is only attraction, not love or union.
- God is one.
