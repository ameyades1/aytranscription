# Liberation Does Not Come After Death

## Does Liberation Come After Death?

Many of us have long believed that moksha (liberation) is something attained after death. A whole life passes, then the body falls away, and only then comes freedom.

Acharya Upendra Ji challenges this belief directly. He says it is a misconception, a falsehood.

> "Liberation is not attained after death. Before dying, I have to become liberated while living."

**Jivan mukta** means one who is already liberated while still in the body.

## What Is Possible After Death?

So can nothing at all happen after death? It can; there is a method for it too. But you cannot do it by your own strength.

Only one who has become free while living can liberate your **pitars** (ancestors). Only such a one can bring a ghost or spirit under control and set it free. After death, you can do nothing on your own.

*If liberation after death is not possible by my own strength, can I keep postponing it beyond today?*

## Death: The Body Went, the Living Remained

It is very important to understand these two words, death and liberation, precisely.

In spiritual language, death simply means: **the living remained, but the body went.** The body is gone, but the being still exists. And its **kamanas** (desires) and **vasanas** (deep latent impressions) still remain.

## Liberation: The Body Remained, the Living Ended

Liberation is its exact opposite: **the body remained, but the living ended.**

Here "the living ended" means worldly living ended. No interest in the world remains. The rest of life is lived with **sakshi bhav** (witness consciousness), for the work of others or for one's own **kartavya karma** (duties).

It is a state of deep witnessing and bliss. If there is something to do, he can enjoy it too; if not, he simply passes the time.

## Like One Who Has Woken from a Dream

Acharya Ji explains this through the example of a dream. Suppose you have woken from a dream, but you wished the dream to go on, so it goes on. Now you may enjoy it if you like, or lie quietly. The choice is yours.

But when you know that everything is you, that it is all imagination, would you feel any desire to show someone magic or the miracle of a **siddhi** (supernatural power)?

He also makes clear that this is not about being half awake. People wake from an unfinished dream and say, "Oh, what an interesting dream! That heroine was just about to put the wedding garland around my neck, I was just about to win the lottery," and go back to sleep.

That is not this state. This is the **state of complete wakefulness**, where the dream remains only an option. Such a person can play at miracles if he wishes, but he has no need of them.

*Is my life like an unfinished dream that I keep wanting to return to?*

## Moksha Means the Decay of Moha

Acharya Ji opens up the word moksha: **moksha means the decay of moha**, the destruction of attachment.

> "The body went but the living remained: that is death. The body remained but the living ended: that is liberation."

## Key Takeaways

- Liberation happens while living, not after death.
- Liberation after death is not possible by one's own strength; only one who is free while living can bring it about.
- Death: the body went, but the living (desires, latent impressions) remained.
- Liberation: the body remained, but worldly living ended.
- The liberated one lives as a witness, for others or for duty, like one fully awake from a dream.
- Moksha means the decay of moha (attachment).
