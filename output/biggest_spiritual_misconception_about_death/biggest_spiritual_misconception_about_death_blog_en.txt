# Liberation Does Not Come After Death

## Does Liberation Come After Death?

Many of us have grown up believing that **moksha** (liberation) is something we receive after death. First a whole lifetime passes, then the body falls away, and only then, at last, comes freedom.

Acharya Upendra Ji challenges this belief head-on. He calls it an illusion, a falsehood: liberation does not arrive after death. Before death comes, we have to become free while still alive.

A **jivanmukta** (one liberated in life) is someone who has already become free while still living in the body.

## What Can Happen After Death?

Does that mean nothing at all can happen after death? Something can, and there is a method for it. But you cannot do it by your own strength.

Only someone who has become free in this life can free your **pitrs** (departed ancestors). Only such a person can bring a restless spirit under control and set it free. Once you have died, you can do nothing for yourself.

*If I cannot free myself after death, can I really keep putting it off?*

## Death: The Body Goes, the Living Remains

You need to understand these two words, death and liberation, very precisely.

In spiritual language, death means something simple: **the living remains, but the body is gone.** The body no longer exists, yet the being itself lives on. Its **desires** and its **vasanas** (deep-rooted tendencies) still remain.

## Liberation: The Body Stays, the Living Ends

Liberation is the exact opposite: **the body remains, but the living has ended.**

Here, "the living has ended" means that worldly living has ended. The liberated person has no interest left in the world. They live out the rest of their life in **sakshi bhava** (witness consciousness), working for others or carrying out the **duties that fall to them**.

This is a state of deep witnessing and bliss. When there is something to do, they can enjoy doing it. When there is nothing, they can let the time pass quietly.

## Like Someone Who Has Woken from a Dream

Acharya Ji explains this with the example of a dream. Suppose you have woken up from a dream, yet you wished for it to go on, and so it continues. Now you can enjoy it if you like, or simply lie there in silence. The choice is yours.

But when you know that you yourself are everything, and that all of it is imagination, would you still want to impress anyone with magic or the miracle of a **siddhi** (a supernatural power)?

He also makes clear that this has nothing to do with half-waking. People come out of a dream halfway and say, "What an exciting dream! The heroine was just about to place the wedding garland around my neck, and I was just about to win the lottery." Then they go straight back to sleep.

That is not the state he means. This is **complete wakefulness**, where the dream has become merely an option. Someone in this state can play at miracles if they wish, but they no longer have any need for them.

*Is my life like an unfinished dream that I keep wanting to slip back into?*

## Moksha Means the End of Attachment

Acharya Ji opens up the word itself: **moksha means moha-kshaya**, the wearing away of **moha** (attachment). Clinging comes to an end.

He sums it up this way: when the body is gone but the living remains, that is death. When the body remains but the living has ended, that is liberation.

## Key Takeaways

- Liberation happens while you are alive, not after death.
- After death, you cannot free yourself; only someone liberated in life can do it for you.
- Death: the body goes, but the living (desires and vasanas) remains.
- Liberation: the body remains, but worldly living ends.
- A liberated person lives as a witness, for others or for duty, like someone fully awake from a dream.
- Moksha means the wearing away of attachment.
