## 1. Summary

A short talk (video 4:53) in which Acharya Upendra Ji explains that every liquid we drink divides into body fluid, urine and **prana**; why hospitals, hosts and hotels all give fluid first; why the prana carries the nature of what was drunk; how the **Pranamaya Kosha** stands above and governs the Annamaya Kosha; the maxim "yatha pinde tatha brahmande" (body and earth both about seventy percent water); and the Moon as karaka of water and mind, with Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4. The main intent is informational ("what is pranamaya kosha", "how does water become prana"), with a secondary wellbeing intent from the video title "Water Is Your First Medicine". The opportunity is the Pranamaya Kosha / water-and-prana query space, where most results are either thin yoga glossaries or generic hydration health pages; this talk links an everyday habit (offering water first) to the kosha teaching and to a cited Upanishad passage. The video-title phrase "first medicine" is not in the body and implies a health promise, so it is kept as a secondary keyword only (see section 10).

## 6. FAQ

1. **प्राणमय कोश क्या है? / What is the Pranamaya Kosha?**
   HI: योग में बताए गए शरीर के कोशों में प्राणमय कोश प्राण-ऊर्जा का शरीर है। आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि यह अन्नमय कोश (भोजन से बना स्थूल शरीर) से ऊपर है और उसे नियंत्रित करता है।
   EN: Among the koshas (sheaths) described in yoga, the Pranamaya Kosha is the body of life energy. Acharya Ji explains that it stands above the Annamaya Kosha, the physical body made of food, and governs it.

2. **जो पानी हम पीते हैं, वह कहाँ जाता है? / Where does the water we drink go?**
   HI: हर तरल के तीन भाग होते हैं: एक भाग तरल रहकर रक्त तक बनता है, एक मूत्र बनकर निकलता है, और एक भाग प्राण बनता है। (यह विभाजन छान्दोग्य उपनिषद् ६.५.२ से मेल खाता है।)
   EN: Every liquid divides into three: one part stays fluid and even forms blood, one leaves as urine, and one becomes prana. (This division agrees with Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.2.)

3. **थके हुए अतिथि को पहले पानी क्यों देते हैं? / Why do we offer water first to a tired guest?**
   HI: थकने पर प्राण चला जाता है, और प्राण के लिए तरल चाहिए। इसीलिए घर, समारोह और होटल, सब जगह पहले जल या कोई पेय दिया जाता है।
   EN: When we are tired, prana has drained away, and prana needs fluid to return. That is why homes, celebrations and hotels all offer water or a drink first.

4. **क्या जो पीते हैं उसका असर मन पर पड़ता है? / Does what we drink affect the mind?**
   HI: हाँ। प्राण के साथ जो पिया उसका गुण भी आता है। छान्दोग्य उपनिषद् (६.५.४) कहता है कि मन अन्नमय है, और आचार्य जी जोड़ते हैं कि जल भी, प्राण के माध्यम से, मन को प्रभावित करता है।
   EN: Yes. The prana carries the nature of what was drunk. The Chandogya Upanishad (6.5.4) says the mind is made of food, and Acharya Ji adds that water too, through prana, shapes the mind.

5. **"यथा पिण्डे तथा ब्रह्माण्डे" का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "yatha pinde tatha brahmande" mean?**
   HI: जैसा शरीर में है, वैसा ब्रह्मांड में है। शरीर लगभग सत्तर प्रतिशत जल है और पृथ्वी लगभग इकहत्तर प्रतिशत, यह उसी का उदाहरण है। (यह एक पारंपरिक योग-उक्ति है।)
   EN: As it is in the body, so it is in the cosmos. The body is largely water, and about seventy-one percent of the earth is water too. (This is a traditional yogic maxim.)

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Health claim in the title "Water Is Your First Medicine" / "जल आपकी पहली औषधि है".** The body never calls water a medicine or promises a cure; the talk is about prana and the mind. A searcher with a medical need could read the H1 as a health promise. Recommendation: keep the H1 (it is the video's title), but use the Pranamaya Kosha title tag and keep "medicine" out of the meta description and snippets; do not add cure or treatment claims. If the editor wants the phrase in the body, add a line that it is a spiritual point, not medical advice.
2. **"(पारंपरिक योग-सूत्र वाक्य)" (Hindi, from the Sanskrit review, Major).** Reads as the Patanjala Yogasutra, which does not contain "यथा पिण्डे तथा ब्रह्माण्डे". Correct to "(पारंपरिक योग-उक्ति)", matching the English "A traditional yogic maxim". Do not attribute the maxim to a named text in FAQ or schema.
3. **Chandogya bridge (review, Minor).** "और केवल अन्न से नहीं, जल से भी" / "And not of food alone, but of water as well" reads as if the Upanishad says water forms the mind; the next words of the mantra say "आपोमयः प्राणः" (prana is made of water). Suggest quoting the fuller line and marking the extension as Acharya Ji's. The FAQ above already words it as "Acharya Ji adds".
4. **Excerpt marking (review, Minor).** "अन्नमयं हि सोम्य मनः" is part of 6.5.4; mark it "(छान्दोग्य उपनिषद् ६.५.४, अंश)" / "(Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4, part)" unless flag 3 is applied.
5. **IAST (review, Minor).** "annamayaṁ" should be "annamayaṃ" to match the site convention.
6. **"Smallest" sheath (review, Minor).** The Annamaya Kosha is the grossest / outermost sheath (Taittiriya 2.1–2.2), not the "smallest". Since the focus keyword is Pranamaya Kosha, this sentence will be read closely; suggest "सबसे स्थूल" / "the grossest (outermost)". The FAQ avoids "smallest".
7. **"Seventy percent of the body" (review, optional).** Adult body water is usually given as about 55–60 percent. Not scriptural; optional softening to "लगभग साठ-सत्तर प्रतिशत" / "roughly sixty to seventy percent". The meta and snippets avoid the figure.
8. **Alcohol.** "मदिरा से भी प्राण आता है" / "Alcohol also brings prana" is immediately qualified (its nature comes with it). Snippets and social cards must not quote the first half alone, which could read as endorsing alcohol.
