# Control Your Mind Before It Controls You

Does a spiritual life mean giving up entertainment altogether? Acharya Upendra Ji says no. The real question is not what you watch, but **who holds the key to your mind**.

In this discourse he explains a simple yet profound idea: true control means you can pull your mind out of something as easily as it settles into it.

## Is It Wrong to Watch a TV Serial?

Acharya Ji makes it clear that he is not a "no, never, not at all" kind of Guru. Watch a Bollywood film, follow a TV serial, enjoy the ordinary entertainment that astrology links to Venus, the planet of pleasure and the arts. None of it is a problem.

There is just one limit: do not watch anything vulgar or obscene. The Guru will not permit that.

And whatever you watch, give it your whole attention. You have the ability to absorb your mind in something, and that ability is a strength.

## Sattvic Intellect: Choose First, Then Focus

An important word comes in here: **dhriti** (steadfastness, the power to hold firm). When the **sattvic buddhi** (the pure, discerning intellect) joins hands with dhriti, it makes a choice before watching anything. Is this right for me to watch? Will it corrupt my mind?

> **dhṛtyā yayā dhārayate manaḥ-prāṇendriya-kriyāḥ**
> **yogenāvyabhicāriṇyā dhṛtiḥ sā pārtha sāttvikī**
> "The **unwavering** steadfastness with which a person, through yoga, holds steady the workings of the mind, the life breath and the senses, that steadfastness, O Partha, is sattvic."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.33)

## When Your Child Comes to You in Tears

Now imagine you are lost in a TV serial and your child comes to you crying: "Mummy, I have to do this homework," or "Mummy, I've hurt my foot."

Will you forget that you are their mother, their father? Your child's pain matters far more than your serial. The **viveka buddhi** (the intellect that discerns right from wrong) makes this decision.

Acharya Ji says that you should be able to withdraw your mind as easily as you put it into something.

Otherwise you end up saying what no parent ever wants to say: "Let your crying go on, let your foot keep bleeding. My serial ends in half an hour, and I'll come then."

*Does your mind leave something as easily as it gets absorbed in it?*

## A "Seven-Hundred-Rupee Investment"

Acharya Ji paints another scene. You are at the cinema with your wife, watching a three-hour film. The ticket cost four hundred rupees and the popcorn three hundred, so you have already spent seven hundred.

Just then news arrives that someone has had an accident, or that someone has died. And the mind says: "I've invested seven hundred rupees. If I leave now, I won't follow the film later. If he's dead, he's dead. It can wait."

Watching the film was not wrong. But now **a greater duty** has arrived. The sattvic intellect grasps at once the difference between **what you must do and what you must not**.

## Is Your Mind the Master and You the Slave?

When your power of holding firm becomes sattvic, your mind stays under your control. You can turn it towards God and towards higher duty whenever you wish, and withdraw it whenever you wish.

What does the opposite look like? You see something in the market and you simply have to go after it. You see alcohol and off you go. You look at someone, lust awakens, and you follow at once. This is **pravritti** (the habitual pull of the senses).

At that point, Acharya Ji explains, the mind becomes the master and we become its slaves.

Control must stay in your hands, he says. Real concentration means your mind can settle within and step out again just as easily.

Moving your mind away and back again like this is not a **rajasic** (restless, desire-driven) tendency. It is a **sattvic** (pure, balanced) one, because it serves a higher duty. It means leaving the place where bondage is greater and going where **liberation** (moksha) lies.

*Who is steering your mind today: you, or your habits?*

## The Prime Minister and the World Cup

Now take a bigger example. The country has reached the World Cup final. The Prime Minister is the chief guest at the ceremony and is to present the trophy. He would never attend a small three-nation series, but this is a special occasion.

He has watched four overs when the phone rings: "Sir, there has been a bomb blast in the neighbouring state."

Should he stay seated at the match now? However grand the event, a king's first duty is to save the lives of his people. He has to pull his mind out of the game **in a single stroke**.

## Protection Comes Before Entertainment

Organising a sporting event is part of a king's work too. There he is offering his people **ranjan** (entertainment). But now he must provide **rakshan** (protection), and protection ranks higher.

Acharya Ji says that a person with this quality can become a good sustainer, a true Vishnu.

Those who have this disposition become a good **dharta** (one who upholds and holds things together). They become good sustainers and protectors, carrying the qualities of **Lord Vishnu**, the preserver of the world.

## Key Takeaways

- Entertainment is not forbidden; just stay away from anything vulgar or obscene.
- The sattvic intellect first chooses what to watch and what to avoid.
- True control means withdrawing your mind as easily as it settles into something.
- When a higher duty arises, pull your mind away at once, in a single stroke.
- If your mind drives you, the mind is the master and you are the slave.
- Moving the mind away and back for a higher duty is sattvic, not rajasic.
- This quality makes a person a good dharta, sustainer and protector.
