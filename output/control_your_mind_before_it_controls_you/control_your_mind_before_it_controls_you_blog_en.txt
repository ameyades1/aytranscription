# Control Your Mind Before It Controls You

Does spiritual life mean giving up entertainment completely? Acharya Upendra Ji says no. The real question is not what you watch, but **who holds the key to your mind**.

In this discourse he explains a simple yet deep point: being able to withdraw the mind from something as easily as it gets absorbed in it is true control.

## Is Watching a Serial Wrong?

Acharya Ji says clearly that he is not a "No, not at all, never" kind of Guru. Watch a Bollywood film, watch a serial, enjoy ordinary entertainment connected with Shukra; there is no problem.

There is just one limit: do not watch what is extremely obscene and filthy. The Guru will not permit that.

And whatever you watch, watch it wholeheartedly. The ability to absorb the mind is within you; it is a power.

## Sattvic Intellect: First the Choice, Then the Focus

An important word comes in here: **dhriti** (steadfastness, the power of holding). When the **sattvic intellect** (pure, discerning intellect) joins with dhriti, it chooses before watching anything: is this right to watch? Will it spoil my mind?

> **dhṛtyā yayā dhārayate manaḥ-prāṇendriya-kriyāḥ**
> **yogenāvyabhicāriṇyā dhṛtiḥ sā pārtha sāttvikī**
> "O Partha, the unwavering steadiness by which, through yoga, one holds the activities of the mind, the life breath and the senses, that steadiness is sattvic."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.33)

The **unwavering** (avyabhicharini, never faltering) power of holding by which a person, through yoga, keeps hold of the activities of the mind, prana and senses is sattvic dhriti.

## When the Child Comes Crying

Now imagine you are immersed in a serial and your child comes crying: "Mother, I have to do this homework," "Mother, I have hurt my foot."

Will you forget that you are the child's mother, the child's father? The child's pain is far more important than your serial. This decision is made by the **discerning intellect**.

Acharya Ji says that just as easily as we absorb the mind, we must know how to withdraw it.

Otherwise we will say what no one wants to say: "Let your crying go on, let your blood flow, my serial will end in half an hour, then I will come."

*Does your mind leave something as easily as it gets absorbed in it?*

## The Seven Hundred Rupee "Investment"

Acharya Ji paints another scene. You are in a theatre with your wife watching a three-hour film. Four hundred for the ticket, three hundred for popcorn, seven hundred rupees already spent.

Then news comes that someone has had an accident, or someone has passed away. And the mind says: "It is a seven hundred rupee investment, I will not understand the film later. If he died, he died, it's fine."

Watching the film was not wrong at that time. But now **a greater duty** has arrived. The sattvic intellect grasps this difference between **duty and non-duty** at once.

## The Mind the Master, You the Slave?

When the power of holding becomes sattvic, the mind stays under your control. You can place it in the Lord and in the higher duty whenever you wish, and withdraw it whenever you wish.

What is the opposite? You see something in the market and you simply have to go. You see liquor, so you must go. You see someone, lust awakens, and you go after them at once. This is **pravritti** (habitual pull).

Acharya Ji explains that then the mind has become the master and we have become its slave.

Acharya Ji says control must be in your hands. The mind staying within and coming out just as easily, this is the real power of concentration.

And this repeated withdrawing and placing of the mind is not the **rajasic** (restless, desire-driven) tendency. It is the **sattvic** tendency, because it is for a higher duty. Leaving where there is more bondage and going where there is **moksha** (liberation).

*Who is running your mind today: you, or your tendencies?*

## The Prime Minister and the World Cup

Now a bigger example. The country has reached the World Cup final. The Prime Minister is the chief guest at the ceremony; the World Cup is to be presented by his hands. He does not come for a small triangular series, but this is a special occasion.

He watched four overs, and a phone call came: "Sir, there has been a bomb blast in the neighbouring state."

Is it proper for him to keep sitting at the match now? However big the event, in a king's duty saving the lives of the people comes above all. The mind must be withdrawn from there, **in a single jolt**.

## Protection Above Entertainment

Organising sports is also the king's work. There he is giving the people **ranjan** (entertainment). But now he must give **rakshan** (protection), and protection is higher.

Acharya Ji says that a person of this quality can become a good maintainer, a good Vishnu.

The one in whom this tendency lives becomes a good **dharta** (upholder, one who sustains). He becomes a good sustainer and protector, with the qualities of **Vishnu**.

## Key Takeaways

- Entertainment is not forbidden; just do not watch what is obscene and filthy.
- The sattvic intellect first chooses what to watch and what not to.
- Withdrawing the mind as easily as it gets absorbed is true control.
- When a higher duty arises, withdraw the mind at once, in a single jolt.
- If the mind runs you, the mind is the master and you are the slave.
- Withdrawing and placing the mind for a higher duty is the sattvic tendency, not the rajasic one.
- This very quality makes a person a good upholder, sustainer and protector.
