## 1. Summary

The blog answers a very common seeker's question: "I have heard discourses and read the scriptures, so why does nothing change inside me?" Acharya Ji's answer is the classical Vedantic sequence of shravana (listening), manana (reflection) and nididhyasana (sustained practice that turns belief into experience), with a clear test for when each step is complete: shravana when you can repeat the Guru's teaching point by point, manana when no doubt remains, and nididhyasana only with tapasya and qualification (the doctors and the MBBS degree). It ends with three tests for whether a spiritual experience is genuine: scripture, earlier masters established in samadhi, and a living Guru. The search intent is informational ("shravana manana nididhyasana meaning", "निदिध्यासन क्या है") with a strong emotional/problem layer ("why is my sadhana not working", "साधना का फल क्यों नहीं मिलता") and a practical question ("are my meditation experiences real?"). Ranking pages for the term are mostly academic Vedanta explainers that define the three words but rarely say *when each step is complete* or how to test experiences; that practical, Guru-centred angle is what only this talk offers. The video title ("missing step... spiritual success") is the hook; the focus keyword follows the term itself, which has the clearest intent.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi (≈50 शब्द):** श्रवण, मनन और निदिध्यासन साधना की तीन सीढ़ियाँ हैं। श्रवण तब पूरा होता है जब आप गुरु की बात बिंदुवार दोहरा सकें; मनन तब, जब कोई संदेह शेष न रहे; और निदिध्यासन जाने और माने हुए को तपस्या से अनुभव में बदलता है। आचार्य उपेंद्र जी कहते हैं, किसी एक को अधूरा छोड़ने से साधना रुक जाती है।

**English (≈55 words):** Shravana, manana and nididhyasana are the three steps of sadhana. Shravana (listening) is complete when you can repeat the Guru's teaching point by point; manana (reflection) is complete when no doubt remains; and nididhyasana turns what you know and believe into experience through tapasya. Acharya Upendra Ji teaches that leaving any one step half done stalls your practice.

## 6. FAQ

Answers are taken from the blog only.

1. **श्रवण कब पूरा होता है? / When is shravana complete?** जब आप गुरु की बात बिंदुवार या शब्दशः दोहरा सकें, या उनका मूल आशय सौ प्रतिशत आपका हो जाए; इसके लिए ग्यारह या सौ बार भी पारायण करना पड़े तो करें। / When you can repeat the Guru's teaching point by point or word for word, or its essence has become fully your own; do as many readings as that takes.
2. **मनन का अर्थ क्या है? / What does manana involve?** अन्य ग्रंथ पढ़ना, दूसरों के अनुभव देखना और संदेह गुरु से पूछना; मनन तब पूरा होता है जब कोई शंका शेष न रहे। / Reading other texts, looking at others' experiences and taking doubts to the Guru; it ends when no doubt remains.
3. **विश्वास है, पर अनुभव क्यों नहीं होता? / I believe, so why don't I experience it?** यहीं निदिध्यासन आरंभ होता है; अनुभव के लिए वैसी ही तपस्या और योग्यता चाहिए, जैसे चिकित्सक को एमबीबीएस की उपाधि। / That is where nididhyasana begins; experience needs the same tapasya and qualification, as a doctor needs an MBBS degree.
4. **ध्यान में प्रकाश या सुगंध का अनुभव सच्चा है? / Is seeing light or smelling fragrance in meditation real?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार ये तन्मात्रा या मन की कल्पना हो सकते हैं; हर अनुभव सत्य नहीं होता। / Acharya Ji says these may be tanmatra or the mind's own imagination; not every experience is real.
5. **अनुभूति सत्य है, यह कैसे जानें? / How do I know an experience is genuine?** तीन कसौटियाँ: परंपरा के शास्त्र में उसका वर्णन, पूर्व के समाधिस्थ गुरुओं के अनुभव से मेल, और जीवित गुरु का प्रमाण। / Three tests: the tradition's scriptures describe it, it matches earlier masters established in samadhi, and a living Guru confirms it.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Nididhyasana is glossed only as "putting it into practice" (Sanskrit review, item 1).** In Vedanta it is unbroken, one-pointed meditation on the truth heard and reasoned out; "जीवन में उतारना" is Acharya Ji's practical reading. Recommendation: apply the review's correction. Until then, the direct answer, FAQ and meta describe nididhyasana as turning what is known into experience (both senses), and never offer a dictionary definition of the term that leaves out meditation.
2. **Tanmatra is glossed as "subtle sense impressions" (review, item 2).** The tanmatras are the subtle elements (sound, touch, form, taste, smell). Recommendation: apply the review's wording; FAQ 4 keeps "may be tanmatra or the mind's own imagination" as Acharya Ji's statement and adds no definition. Do not target "tanmatra meaning" as a keyword from this post.
3. **"The scriptures say seven or eleven parayanas" (review, item 3).** No single scriptural rule was found. Recommendation: change to "tradition" as the review suggests, or confirm the text Acharya Ji meant. The FAQ says "as many readings as it takes" and does not cite a scripture for the counts.
4. **"श्रवण से ही श्रुति शब्द आता है" (Hindi only, review, item 4).** Both come from the root √श्रु; the English is already correct. Recommendation: apply the review's Hindi correction.
5. **The sequence's source is not named (review, item 5).** Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.4.5 ("आत्मा वा अरे द्रष्टव्यः श्रोतव्यो मन्तव्यो निदिध्यासितव्यः") is the classical source. Recommendation: the editor may add it; until then titles, meta, schema and FAQ do not quote the verse or name the Upanishad, and the tags avoid "Upanishads". "Advaita Vedanta" is used as a tag because the three steps are the standard Vedantic method; drop it if the editor prefers tags only for what the blog names.
6. **"Spiritual success" and healing.** The video title promises "spiritual success" and the doctors' story concerns healing. Recommendation: no title or snippet promises healing or results from a practice; the doctors' story is used only as Acharya Ji's point about qualification and tapasya, and the link on "methods of healing" points to a related talk, not to a claim.
7. **Visions of light, God or fragrance.** The blog says these may be imagination. Recommendation: in social posts keep this as a gentle caution, attributed to Acharya Ji, not as mockery of seekers' experiences; the "seeing light during meditation" keyword stays Medium because the blog answers it only briefly.
