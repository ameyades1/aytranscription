# Chapter: Shravan, Manan and Nididhyasan: Three Steps to Realisation

## The Three Practices of Sadhana

It is said that there are three practices we must undertake: shravan, manan and nididhyasan. So what is the first practice? Shravan, listening.

From shravan comes shruti. Our Vedas are called shruti. Notice that nowhere is there any mention of reading or writing. The guru spoke to the disciple, and the disciple received it. It was received by hearing, and so it is called shruti.

## Shruti, Smriti and Purana

In the sankalpa of our worship we say:

> **śrutismṛtipurāṇoktaphalaprāptyarthaṃ**
> "For attaining the fruit declared in the Shruti, Smriti and Puranas"
> (Sankalpa formula of worship)

Here smriti means that which has remained in memory, whether it is the Manusmriti or any other smriti. Certain events have already taken place, and on their basis we apply certain rules, just as laws and regulations come to be made. What we saw earlier is called smriti. And purana means that which existed before and still applies today.

## Is Listening Alone Enough?

So we were speaking of the Vedas. Does mere listening get the work done? First one must listen; that is shravan. And then what must be done on top of it? Manan.

But first tell me, where does shravan end? You did listen. Some guru explained the Bhagavad Gita to you, so you did shravan. But will a hundred out of a hundred points settle in your mind the very first time? If you have retained only ten percent, then only ten percent of shravan has happened.

That is why the scriptures say: do at least eleven parayanas, do seven parayanas. Why is this said? Because until then, shravan itself has not happened. And when shravan itself is not complete, on what will you do manan?

Each time you read the same thing again, your level of consciousness rises. You go up to the next level, and then the one after that.

## When Is Shravan Complete?

So the question arises: when is my shravan complete? When I can repeat what my guru has said, point by point or word by word. When I have received the guru's words in full, or if not the words, then when the essence of what the guru meant has become a hundred percent mine, then my shravan is complete.

> "If it takes eleven parayanas, then eleven; if it takes a hundred parayanas, then a hundred. Then my shravan is complete."

## Manan: The Journey Out of Doubt

Now do you know what question will arise next? I can repeat my guru's words exactly as they are, every point, but at once a problem comes up: "Guruji, I know what you want to say, but I do not believe it." I do not believe that this sadhana yields so much fruit, that doing this brings about that, and doing that brings about this.

So for this it is said: reflect a little on what you have heard.

What does manan mean? To study other texts, to go to the library, to look at the experiences of others and see what fruit they received, and then to put your doubts to the guru. Doing all this is manan.

Now when will the process of manan end? When no doubt remains in your mind. When I can say that I now have no doubt: yes, doing this sadhana brings this fruit, doing this brings about that, Ganapati is just so, I no longer have any doubt. Then understand that my manan too is complete.

## Nididhyasan: From Belief to Experience

Now a second problem arises. Do you know what it is? "I believe what you say, but I do not experience it." I am not getting the anubhuti, the inner experience. Swamiji got it, everyone else got it, that great soul got it, that king got it. How will I get it?

This is where nididhyasan begins. Nididhyasan means: now, when will you bring it into your life? Will you be able to practise it the way those who achieved it did?

## The Doctors' Question and the Importance of Qualification

We started all the techniques of healing, and now I teach them all as well. When the healing work began, some doctors came. They came and said, "Are you going to show this as a miracle, or can all of us do it too? We are intellectuals. If there is a process and you tell us, can we repeat it?"

So I said, yes, certainly. You too can practise it. But you will have to do tapasya like mine. You will have to gain the qualification in it, and then you too will be able to do it.

Just as you have a doctor, an MBBS. Can anyone practise medicine? Yes, anyone can. But when? When they have earned the degree. Not "I can practise any way I like."

> "Will sadhana too have a qualification or not? That has to be attained."

People think they too will become accomplished just by doing sadhana any which way. But sadhana too has its qualification, and it has to be earned.

## Is the Experience Real or Imagination?

So the first is shravan, the second manan and the third nididhyasan. Now the question is: do I too get the same anubhuti?

That experience may also be an illusion, that is, an imagination of your own mind. Some people, the moment they close their eyes, feel, "I saw light, I saw God, some fragrance came." None of this is anything; it is called tanmatra.

## Three Tests of True Experience

The anubhuti you have had, did some rishi or muni have it before? It must match with that. Your living guru must certify it. And the living guru will be following some tradition, so it must be described in the scriptures. When all three match, only then is that anubhuti accepted as true.

Which three tests did I give?

1. **There is proof of it in the scriptures.**
2. **Earlier gurus established in samadhi have reached that state**, and I am comparing my experience with it.
3. **My living guru has certified it.**

> "When these three things are there, then my anubhuti is true."

Whether it is the experience of a small healing, or great siddhis are to come, or you have attained liberation, whether it is truly so or not is known by these same three tests.
