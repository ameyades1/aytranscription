## 1. Summary

The story of Ganesha's birth (Parvati forming a boy while bathing, Shiva beheading him, the elephant head) is one of the most retold Hindu stories, and searchers ask both "what is the story" and "who is Ganesha really". Most ranking pages retell the Purana or give a body-part list. This talk decodes the whole story as one inner process: the all-pervading Ganesha is Omkara, Brahman; the Ganesha of the Muladhara is viveka, discernment; Parvati's "grime" is a part of Prakriti (the three gunas) from which maya and the intellect arise; Shiva's absence is forgetting the Self, his return is the Guru and satsang; the battle is liberation against maya; the elephant head gives discernment its marks. The main intent is **informational** ("who is the real Ganesha", "asli ganesh kaun hai", "meaning of the birth story"). Searches peak around Ganesh Chaturthi (Bhadrapada, late August to September; the video was posted as a Ganesh Chaturthi special on 2025-08-26); refresh the post 4 to 6 weeks before. The opportunity is the question the H1 itself asks, *who* Ganesha really is, answered through the birth story; ganesh_have_elephant_head_mouse keeps the "why elephant head" query (see cannibalization in section 7).

## 6. FAQ

Answers come only from the talk. Not on the page yet; if it is added, add a matching FAQPage block then.

1. **असली गणेश कौन है? / Who is the real Ganesha?**
   जो सर्वत्र व्याप्त है, वह ओंकार, परम सत्य है: वही ब्रह्म वाला गणेश है। मूलाधार से जुड़े गणेश हमारे भीतर की विवेक बुद्धि हैं, इंद्रियों का स्वामी बनने की बुद्धि। / What pervades everything is Omkara, the ultimate reality: that is the Ganesha who is Brahman. The Ganesha of the Muladhara is the discernment within us, the intelligence that masters the senses.
2. **क्या गणेश सच में पार्वती के मैल से बने? / Was Ganesha really made from Parvati's grime?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार यह प्रतीक है। "मैल" गंदगी नहीं, देवी का ही एक अंश है जो उस समय उन्हें नहीं चाहिए था: प्रकृति के तीन गुण, जिनसे माया और बुद्धि बनी। / Acharya Ji explains it as a symbol. The "grime" is not dirt but a part of the Goddess she did not need at that moment: the three gunas of nature, from which maya and the intellect arise.
3. **शिवजी ने गणेश को क्यों नहीं पहचाना? / Why did Shiva not recognise Ganesha?**
   यह गणेश शिव-पार्वती के मिलन से नहीं, प्रकृति के अंश से जन्मे, प्रकृति की प्रथम विकृति; इसलिए ओंकार उन्हें नहीं पहचानता। / This Ganesha was not born from the union of Shiva and Parvati but from a part of Prakriti, its first distortion, so Omkara does not recognise him.
4. **हाथी का सिर किसका प्रतीक है? / What does the elephant head stand for?**
   विशालता और विवेक के गुण: सूप जैसे कान व्यर्थ बातें उड़ा देते हैं, सूँड अच्छे को चुनती है, स्मृति श्रेष्ठ है, और विशाल आकार बताता है कि बुद्धि इंद्रियों से ऊपर है। / Vastness and the marks of discernment: winnowing-basket ears blow away useless talk, the trunk picks out the good, memory is the finest, and the huge body shows the intellect standing above the senses.
5. **"श्री गणेश करना" का अर्थ क्या है? / What does "doing the Shri Ganesh" mean?**
   शुरुआत करना। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि जीवन की शुरुआत ही विवेक के बिना नहीं हो सकती। / To make a beginning. Acharya Ji says life itself cannot truly begin without discernment.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"एक आँख" / "The single eye" (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** Neither the elephant nor Ganesha is described with a single eye; the speaker likely meant the small eyes or the single tusk (Ekadanta). Check the audio and apply the review's correction before publishing. Kept out of the FAQ, snippet and alt text.
2. **"Every creature's head is tried" (review issue 2, Minor).** The Gita Press Shiva Purana has the gods bring the head of the first creature they meet in the north. Don't repeat the "every creature" detail in SEO texts as part of the scriptural story.
3. **Partial verses (review issues 3 and 4).** "वासुदेवः सर्वमिति" (Gita 7.19) and "सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म" (Chandogya 3.14.1) are parts of verses; mark them as such in the blog. If quoted in a snippet, keep the reference with "part".
4. **Atharvashirsha reference (review issue 5).** No mantra number is given and "a Vedic hymn" is loose; don't add a number or "Vedic" in schema, FAQ or titles until checked against the Gita Press text.
5. **"Vinayaka" gloss (review issue 6).** The English blog glosses Vinayaka as "remover of obstacles"; it means the supreme leader. Not used in SEO texts; fix in the blog.
6. **"Inertia" for जड़त्व (review issue 7).** The English "inertia" suggests tamas only; the FAQ above says "a part of Prakriti, the three gunas" instead.
7. **Hindi closing question unclear (review issue 8).** Not used in SEO texts; apply the review's Hindi wording.
8. **Symbolic readings are Acharya Ji's teaching.** That the "grime" is the three gunas, Shiva's absence is forgetting the Self and the battle is satsang against maya is his interpretation, not a scriptural statement. All SEO texts attribute it ("Acharya Upendra Ji explains"). Avoid "The scriptures say Ganesha was never a boy" or "Shocking truth about Ganesha's birth".
9. **Respect for devotees.** The talk questions the literal story and answers devotees' objection gently ("मैल का अर्थ गंदगी नहीं"). Titles must not mock the story or say "the story is false"; the post's own conclusion is that discernment lets every Purana story open up as a symbol, not that the stories are fiction.
