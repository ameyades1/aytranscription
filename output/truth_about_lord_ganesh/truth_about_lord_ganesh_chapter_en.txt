# Chapter: Who Is the Real Ganesha?

## Beyond the Ganesha with a Trunk

Who is the real Ganesha? Do you still feel that somewhere out there sits a Ganesha with a trunk, eating modakas? Well, day by day, at least this much is becoming clear: he is not like that. Then what did that form mean? It was symbolism, a representation through form.

## Ganesha's Address: Muladhara or Everywhere?

So where is his real address? The scripture says:

> **tvaṃ mūlādhārasthito'si nityam**
> "You eternally abide in the Muladhara chakra."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha)

But I also say that God is not caught in some loop that he must stay in one particular chakra. For on one side it is said:

> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti**
> "Vasudeva is everything."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

He pervades everywhere. And it is also said:

> **sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ brahma**
> "All this is indeed Brahman."
> (Chandogya Upanishad 3.14.1)

So how did that which is everywhere become special in the Muladhara chakra? Did we clarify this or not? Think and tell me.

## Two Ganeshas: Omkara and Discernment

What is called all-pervading is **Omkara**, the ultimate truth, the ultimate reality. And what is called Ganesha is the **discerning intellect** (viveka buddhi) within me: the Vinayaka intellect, the intellect that becomes master of the ganas. Until control comes over the Muladhara chakra, that intellect does not manifest. Is this much understood?

The Ganapati of the form of Omkara, the real Ganapati, is all-pervading. "Sarvam khalvidam brahma": he is the Ganesha who is Brahman. I myself have to reach there. And this discerning intellect within me is the beginning of that journey. That is why we say "to do the Shri Ganesha", meaning to begin. Life itself cannot begin without it.

## Ganesha from Parvati's Dirt: Prakriti's First Creation

Now understand how the discerning intellect is formed. At the top are Purusha and Prakriti. Below them, the very first creation, the special or first creation, comes from your Prakriti, from Parvati.

The story says that Ganesha arose from the dirt of Parvati's body while she was bathing. You remember that story, don't you? First he was a son, then Shiva cuts off his head, and after that an elephant's head is placed on him. All of this is symbolic. Do you think Parvati did not bathe for many years, and only then did enough dirt come off to produce a boy? That simply cannot be. It is all symbolism.

That dirt means inertia, the part that is Maya, that is, **sattva, rajas and tamas**. There will be a great deal of it, so much that the whole universe can be created from it, Ganapati can be created from it. Your intellect was created from it. Is this clear?

## The Real Meaning of "Dirt"

Here "dirt" does not mean the filth we usually call dirt. People with a devotional bent will ask: did the Goddess not bathe? So let us answer them too.

When you apply soap, do you come out completely dirty every day? Is the body completely dirty every day? One who bathes daily, who is healthy, clean, who has done no heavy work, who is pure, still bathes as a rule, doesn't he? But when you pour water with soap and shampoo, do you later use whatever flows away? Everything that has flowed off is called dirt.

It may be good or bad. It may be with fragrant ubtan, or the oil of an abhyanga bath. The water that flows after touching the body, we even call tirtha at the time of abhisheka. The description is like that. Her dirt means what left her body while touching it during the bath. There is no need to always think of sweat or filth.

And how could there be so much dirt? This whole story is symbolic. A part of her came out which she did not need at that moment, just as we clean everything.

The real Prakriti and Purusha are in the state of the ultimate divine Omkara. The dirt of that Shakti is sattva, rajas and tamas. That is, Maya began from within it, and from there the first intellect was created.

## Shiva Going Out: Forgetting the Self

Then the story moves on. Shiva does not even recognise the boy. You know that story, don't you? Until then he had not become Ganapati, the master of the ganas; he had not even been named. He had been told, "Do not let anyone in." There is a description like that. Then Shiva arrives. He had gone out, and in the meantime this creation took place.

In the same way, only when I have forgotten the Self (atma tattva) do I desire to create the world and Maya. Otherwise only the thought of liberation would arise. That is why Shiva's going out is necessary; only then can something be made from this dirt. From it, Ganapati, that is, your discerning intellect or your intellect, becomes active in order to create.

## Shiva's Return: When the Guru Comes

When Ganapati had arisen, when he was born, Shiva returns. This means that sometimes you come to satsang, you have an encounter with some Guru, you meet them in person, and they give you some guidance. Even then you do not believe their words; you do not understand them either.

The boy says, "I am Parvati's son," and Shiva does not even know that he has become a son. Is this concept coming through? Parvati knows, that is, the inert part, Prakriti, knows; Shiva does not even know that this has happened.

## The Fierce Battle: The Path of Liberation and the Part of Maya

After this a fierce battle takes place. On one side is your path of satsang, of liberation, and on the other the part of Maya. Note that this Ganesha was not born from the union of Shiva and Parvati; he is said to be born from Parvati's dirt. Understand this difference. He is the **first distortion** (vikriti) of your Prakriti. That is why Omkara does not even know of him.

When Shiva comes, there is a fight. After the fight, when the boy is killed, Prakriti comes forward again. Parvati says, "Oh, what have you done! He was our own son." That is, I myself created him, he is my own form, and so he is a part of you, because I am yours.

## The Elephant's Head: The Qualities of Discernment

After this Parvati says, "Quickly, do something." That is, the intellect made only of dirt will not do. Then the heads of all creatures are sought and tried, but none fits. This means that for such a pure one, some special intellect is needed; only then will it match. If one has to imagine, to use the intellect, the best is the elephant. The elephant represents vastness, and his body too became vast like that.

Look at the qualities attributed to him:

- **Ears:** like a winnowing basket, blowing away what is bad. One should not be easily swayed by what one hears; useless things should blow away and useful things should remain.
- **One eye:** the power of concentration.
- **Trunk:** drawing out only the good from among bad and good.
- **Memory:** the finest memory, learned.
- **Vastness:** the biggest of all creatures, just as the intellect stands above all the senses.

The elephant was the only creature found with so many such qualities, and his head was placed there. Thus he became Ganapati, and he was told, "From now on, become the master of the ganas (Ganadhipati)."

## Understanding the Stories as Symbols

If there is such a discerning intellect, all our stories too begin to be understood as imagination and symbol. Has this understanding begun?
