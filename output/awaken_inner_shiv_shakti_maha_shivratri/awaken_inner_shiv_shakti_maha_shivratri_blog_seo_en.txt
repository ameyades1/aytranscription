# This Maha Shivratri, Discover the True Secret of the Shivling

What does the Shivling represent? In Acharya Ji's reading, Lord Shiva's form represents our physical body, while the Shivling represents the linga sharira, the invisible subtle body within us. After death the body remains, but the unseen power that drove its actions, the jivatma, departs. Beyond even this is the unborn Purushottama, whom the scriptures call aling.

## As You Pour Water on the Shivling, Do You Know Whom You Worship?

On **Maha Shivratri** (the Great Night of Lord Shiva), we perform **abhishek** (the ritual bathing of a sacred image) on the **Shivling** (the rounded stone emblem of Lord Shiva). Yet a simple question remains: what does the Shivling stand for?

If someone asked you why the Devi is shown with eight or eighteen arms, could you answer? Have you ever read the Shiva Purana or the Linga Purana?

People of other faiths often laugh and ask whether we worship parts of the body. Then they ask how the formless could ever take on a form, if the Shivling is a symbol of the formless. Acharya Ji says this confusion exists because we do not know our own scriptures.

## What Does the Shivling Represent? Shiva in Form and the Linga Sharira (Subtle Body)

Lord Shiva has a **sakar** (with form) aspect: the ascetic with matted locks, with the Ganga flowing through his hair. This form represents our own physical body.

And the Shivling? It represents the **linga sharira** (the subtle body), the invisible part of us that the eyes cannot see.

## What Changes After Death?

Acharya Ji offers a simple example. Imagine two people: one did good all their life, the other did harm. Both have died, and both bodies lie before you.

Can the good person's body now do anything kind? Can the other's body do anything wicked? No. The ears, nose, hands and feet are all still there, yet nothing happens.

So what was the unseen power that drove one person to noble deeds and the other to wrongdoing? That power is the **jivatma** (the individual soul).

Acharya Ji explains that what you could see was the body with form. The formless part within us has a symbol of its own, and that symbol is the Shivling.

*When you do something good or bad, have you ever wondered who within you is really doing it?*

## Aling: The One Who Was Never Born

Beyond even the subtle body lies another state. The scriptures call it **aling** (beyond every mark or sign). This is the wholly formless one, called **Purushottama** (the Supreme Being), who stands beyond both Purusha and Prakriti.

That one was never born at all. It is **ajanma** (unborn).

## The Three-Leaved Bilva: A Resolve to Go Beyond Three

We offer the Shivling a **bilva** leaf (the sacred leaf of the bael tree), which grows in three parts. But what do these sets of three mean?

- **The three gunas** (the qualities of nature): **sattva** (purity), **rajas** (activity) and **tamas** (inertia). Rising above them means becoming **gunatrayatita** (beyond the three gunas).
- **The three bodies:** the physical body; the subtle body, which holds the mind and the energy of prana; and the causal body, which holds the **vijnanamaya kosha** (the sheath of the intellect) and the **anandamaya kosha** (the sheath of bliss). The aim is to go beyond all three.
- **The three times:** past, present and future. Going beyond them, you become **kalatita** (beyond time).

When you offer the bilva leaf, you are offering up all three of these triads.

## Beyond Time: What If You Miss Your Flight?

Acharya Ji points out that Shri Krishna says in the Bhagavad Gita that those who stay stuck in time will be reborn, while those who move beyond it no longer worry about time.

This is no excuse for carelessness. You still have to catch your flight on time. But if you miss it, you should not let stress take over.

Perhaps that plane was going to crash. Perhaps some hardship was waiting for you at the other end. And even if hardship does come, it will carry a lesson. The airport does not shut down because you missed one flight.

Acharya Ji says that those who understand this gradually move beyond time.

*The last time something did not go to plan, how did your mind react?*

## Triputi: The Devotee, God and Devotion

**Triputi** (a triad) means experiencing three things as separate. I am the devotee, He is God, and what I am doing is devotion.

The same pattern runs through everything: the seer, the seen and the act of seeing; the knower, the known and knowledge; the lover, the beloved and love.

As long as these three feel separate, rebirth will continue. To offer all three and go beyond them is the true offering.

Acharya Ji says we have performed plenty of rituals. Now it is time to turn to knowledge, and beyond knowledge to its **phalashruti** (the fruit it bears).

## Shiva and Parvati: Purusha and Prakriti

Parvati is the daughter of the mountain, which makes her **jada** (inert matter). Our mind, intellect and ego all belong to **mula prakriti** (primordial nature).

Lord Shiva is **Purusha** (the conscious Self). The scriptures describe our body as a city, a **pur**, with nine gates such as the ears and nostrils. The one who dwells in this city is the Purusha.

> **puri śete iti puruṣaḥ**
> "The one who rests within the city is the Purusha."
> (Traditional etymology of the word Purusha)

And the very first creation, the **prathama kriti** (first act of creation), is **Prakriti** (nature).

## Lord Shiva's Family Lives Within You

- **Ganapati:** the first task is to think. You can become the lord of the **ganas** (attendants) only when you have **viveka buddhi** (a discerning intellect).
- **Kartikeya:** the commander of Lord Shiva's family. The body, the five senses and the mind together are called **Shadanana** (the six-faced one).

## "Shi" and "Va": The Balance Hidden in Shiva's Name

Lord Shiva symbolises the union of Purusha and Prakriti. That is why the Shivling, too, is a symbolic form of their meeting.

The word Shiva has two parts:

- **"Shi"** points to the feminine principle within you and represents the left side of the body.
- **"Va"** points to the masculine principle within you and represents the right side of the body.

Acharya Ji suggests a small experiment. Say "Shi" on its own and notice where its vibration arises. Then say "Va" and notice again.

And **Shivaa** means Parvati; its meaning also holds within it liberation, or **moksha**.

## Shiva Means Well-Being, Shiva Means Health

Shiva means the auspicious one, the one who brings well-being. The name also describes an all-pervading state of bliss.

There is another beautiful meaning: perfect health. The Hindi word **swastha** (healthy) literally means **sva mein sthit** (established in the Self), and Acharya Ji says this state, too, is called Shiva.

*This Maha Shivratri, will you only pour water on the Shivling outside, or will you also recognise the Shiva within?*

## Key Takeaways

- Lord Shiva's form represents our physical body; the Shivling represents the formless subtle body within us.
- After death the body remains, but the unseen power that drove its actions, the jivatma, departs.
- The unborn Purushottama, beyond Purusha and Prakriti, is called aling in the scriptures.
- The three-leaved bilva is an offering to go beyond the three gunas, the three bodies and the three times.
- Going beyond time means freedom from the stress of time, not carelessness.
- As long as the devotee, God and devotion feel separate, rebirth continues.
- Lord Shiva is Purusha and Parvati is Prakriti; Ganapati is the discerning intellect, and Kartikeya commands the senses and the mind.
- "Shi" is the feminine principle and "Va" the masculine; Shiva means auspiciousness, well-being and being established in the Self.
