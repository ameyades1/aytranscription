# Chapter: The Secret of the Shivalinga and the Union of Shiva and Shakti Within

## The Question of Maha Shivratri: Whom Do We Worship?

If someone asked you how the Goddess comes to have eight arms, or eighteen arms, could you answer? You worshipped the Shivalinga, so whom did you actually worship? Today we are performing abhisheka on the Shivalinga. You have come here for exactly this, Maha Shivratri. If I asked anyone what the Shivalinga symbolizes, what answer would I get? Has any of you ever read the Shiva Purana or the Linga Purana?

People of other religions ask, "Oh, you worship parts of the body?" They ask this, and they laugh as well. Then we say that it is not only that; there is something beyond it. Then they ask: if it is a symbol of the formless, how did the formless acquire a form? It does have some form, after all. This problem exists because our own people do not know their own scriptures.

## Shiva With Form and the Linga Sharira

Shiva has a form with attributes (sakara): with matted locks, the Ganga flowing from them. This form is a symbol of our own gross body. And the Shivalinga that we see is a symbol of the linga sharira, the subtle body.

Understand this through an example. There are two people, one good and one bad. If asked who is the good one, everyone will say, "I am." So suppose you are good and the other is bad. All your life you did good deeds and he did bad deeds. Then both of you died. Your body lies here, and so does his.

Now my question is: can your body do any good deed now? Can his body do any bad deed? The answer is no. So was there some power, invisible, which was getting good work done through you and bad work through him? There certainly was an invisible element within us. Otherwise, after death the body is the same; ears, nose, hands, feet, all are just as they were.

> "What was visible was the form; and the formless part within us, its symbol is what we call the Shivalinga."

That invisible element is the jivatma, the individual soul, which cannot be seen.

## Alinga: Purushottama, the Unborn

When we speak of the completely formless, called Purushottama, who is beyond both Purusha and Prakriti, our scriptures name it **Alinga**. It was never born at all; it is unborn.

We do not properly know our own scriptures, and that is why difficulties arise. Many people of other religions come to me, and I explain to them the meaning of each of our symbols, one by one.

## The Meaning of the Three-Leaved Bilva Patra

You offered a bilva leaf with three leaflets. What are these threes that you offered?

First, sattva, rajas and tamas, the three gunas. To go beyond these three gunas, that is, to become **gunatrayatita**.

Second, my three bodies:
- The **sthula sharira**, this physical body.
- The **sukshma sharira**, which holds my mind and my prana energy.
- The **karana sharira**, which is my vijnanamaya kosha and anandamaya kosha.

One must go beyond even these. I offered all three of these threes.

Third, my three times: past, present and future. To go beyond these three and become **kalatita**, beyond time, as we will be right now in this moment. To be beyond time means to have no bondage to time, to the clock.

## Becoming Kalatita: Beyond Time

Shri Krishna said in the Gita that those who are caught in time will be reborn; those who have gone beyond time have no worry about time.

This does not mean you may start a program on time or not, as you please. You must catch your aeroplane on time. But if you miss it, there should be no tension. Perhaps that plane was going to crash. Or perhaps the poor plane was not going to crash, yet some trouble was waiting for you there. And even if trouble does come, there will be some lesson in it. The airport does not shut down.

> "Whoever understands this gradually becomes beyond time."

## Going Beyond the Triad

In the same way, one must go beyond the **triputi**, the triad. Devotee, God and devotion: as long as these three feel separate to us, "I am someone's devotee, I am the one who worships, I am doing devotion," this is the triputi. The seer, the seen and seeing; the knower, the known and knowing; the lover, the beloved and love. As long as these three feel separate, there will be rebirth.

To go beyond this, to offer up all three, that is the meaning of this offering. We do not even know from our scriptures what we were meant to do. We have done plenty of ritual. Now let us come to knowledge, and beyond knowledge, to its fruit (phalashruti).

## Purusha and Prakriti: Shiva and Parvati

Parvati is the daughter of the mountain, that is, inert matter. Whatever is within us, mind, intellect, ego, is called Prakriti, Mula Prakriti, primordial nature. Shiva is called Purusha.

Understand the meaning of Purusha. This body of ours is called a town. A town is called a "pura". It is a pura with nine gates: the ears, nose and so on. The one who sleeps, who dwells, within such a pura is the Purusha.

> **puri śete iti puruṣaḥ**
> "The one who rests in the city (of the body) is the Purusha."
> (Traditional etymology of the word Purusha)

And the first creation (prathama kriti) that arose in it is called Prakriti, Mula Prakriti. That is Parvati, the daughter of the Himalaya, inert. Until it is explained in this language, it will not be understood.

## The Symbolic Meaning of Shiva's Family

Then, when I did my first act, the act of thinking, that work of thinking belonged to Ganapati. I will become the lord of the ganas only when I have discerning intellect (viveka buddhi).

Likewise, Kartikeya in Shiva's family is the commander of the army. My body, my five senses and the mind, together they are called **Shadanana**, the six-faced one.

## The Meanings of the Word Shiva

Many meanings come from the word Shiva. One meaning is the one who is all-pervading and in a state of bliss.

Lord Shiva is the symbol of the union of Purusha and Prakriti, as I just told you. That is why the Shivalinga, too, is a symbolic form of male and female, linga and yoni, that is, of Purusha and Prakriti.

The word Shiva is made of two parts: "Shi" and "Va".

**"Shi"** indicates the feminine principle within you and represents the left side of your body. Try it sometime: say only "Shi" and see where its vibration arises. It should come in the right place.

**"Va"** indicates the masculine principle within you and represents the right side of your body. Try saying "Va".

Then come Shiva and Shivaa. Shivaa means Parvati, and liberation (moksha) is also contained in its meaning.

Further, Shiva means auspicious, beneficent, the one who brings about your welfare. You will find many definitions. Another meaning is to be in good health. To be *swastha*, healthy, means **to be established in the Self (swa)**. That too is called Shiva. As I said, Shiva has many meanings.
