# The True Lakshmi Puja: The Art of Awakening Divine Wealth

Every year we perform **Lakshmi Puja** (the worship of Goddess Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth and prosperity). We light lamps, offer prayers, and quietly wish for money in our hearts.

But have you ever stopped to ask what Lakshmi really is, and what the true meaning of Lakshmi Puja is? In the sixteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna describes a set of qualities he calls **daivi sampatti** (divine wealth), and those qualities are the true Lakshmi. Those three verses do not mention money anywhere.

## How Can You Tell Whether Your Lakshmi Puja Is Working?

The test is simple. Your Lakshmi Puja is succeeding to the extent that these qualities of divine wealth have taken root in you.

> **abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ |**
> **dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam ||**
> "Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadfastness in knowledge and yoga, charity, control of the senses, sacrifice, study of the scriptures, austerity and straightforwardness."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.1)

## Where Does Fear Come From?

The first quality is **abhaya** (fearlessness), the complete absence of fear.

Fear arises only when there is an "other": an object, a person, a place or a job. The moment you say "my job", there are two of you, yourself and the job. Lose the job, and fear sets in.

When the sense of **advaita bhava** (non-duality) dawns, the understanding that everything is God and all of this is my own creation, the mind says: if this goes, let it go; something else will come. That does not mean you become careless.

*What is the "other" in your life that you are most afraid of losing?*

## What Are the Qualities of Divine Wealth? More Gems in the First Verse

- Purity of heart, and unwavering steadiness in the practice of meditation
- **Sattvic dana** (charity given in the spirit of goodness), since charity comes in three kinds
- **Dama** (restraint), keeping the senses under control
- Worship of God, the deities and one's elders and teachers, along with noble rites such as **agnihotra** (the Vedic fire offering)
- Study of the Vedas and scriptures, both learning them yourself and teaching them to others
- Singing the names and glories of God
- Bearing hardship to follow your own dharma (your rightful duty and path)
- Simplicity of body, senses and heart

And this is just the first verse. This, too, is Lakshmi.

> **ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam |**
> **dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam ||**
> "Non-violence, truthfulness, freedom from anger, renunciation, peace, refraining from slander, compassion for all beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty and steadiness of mind."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.2)

## Does Non-Violence Mean You Never Fight?

No. **Ahimsa** (non-violence) means causing no one pain through thought, word or deed. Yet the classical means of dealing with others, **sama, dana, danda** (persuasion, inducement and punishment), include punishment too. The condition is that even the punishment must serve the good of the person receiving it.

Maa Mahishasuramardini, the Divine Mother who slew the buffalo demon Mahishasura, killed him for his own redemption. Shri Krishna killed the tyrant Kansa for his redemption as well. All that is required is a heart free of hatred.

## Knowing How to Speak Is Also Lakshmi

Speak what is true, speak what is pleasant, speak what is beneficial: **satyaṁ vada, priyaṁ vada, hitaṁ vada**.

Saraswati, the goddess of learning, grants knowledge. But the art of knowing when, how and in what manner to speak, and of sustaining that skill over time, comes from Lakshmi.

## How Do You Respond Without Anger?

Do not become angry even with someone who has wronged you. That does not mean you let them off without a lesson. You can respond with a calm mind, after making a plan, weighing their strengths and faults, and measuring your own strength.

Chandragupta, the founder of the Maurya empire, was taught exactly this: do not fight until you have the strength. First become a warrior, then go into battle. Acharya Ji says the point is to move forward with a plan, not to strike today in anger.

The Lord says, **kāma eṣa krodha eṣa rajo-guṇa-samudbhavaḥ** (it is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of rajas) (Bhagavad Gita 3.37). When **rajas** (the quality of passion and restless activity) grows, desire grows with it. If the desire goes unfulfilled, anger follows; if it is fulfilled, ego follows.

Rajas is itself a form of Lakshmi, but it needs Saraswati beside it. Only then does it turn into merit, and only then does it become divine wealth.

## Why Is a Mother's Anger Different?

This does not mean you should never be angry with your child. When the time comes, you have to scold. But that scolding carries love and the wish that the child improves. Even when a mother strikes her child, she never becomes the child's enemy.

## Slander Versus Constructive Criticism

Do not indulge in **ninda** (slander, speaking ill of others). Constructive criticism is a different matter, as long as it serves the good of the other person.

Does the daughter-in-law of the house avoid her share of the work? Perhaps she grew up in a different environment, pampered and indulged. If you keep telling her "your mother never taught you any values" until her sense of self is reduced to nothing, it is like committing **brahmahatya** (the gravest of sins, traditionally the killing of a Brahmin) against her. The fault falls on you instead. If you want to show her the value of work, do the work yourself and set the example; that is what succeeds.

## Is Your Compassion Truly Selfless?

You need compassion for all beings with **no motive behind it**. Scattering grain for pigeons so that a debt gets cleared, or feeding a cow or a dog so that **Shani** (Saturn, believed to bring hardship when unfavourable) is appeased, is a transaction, not compassion.

*The next time you give, pause for a moment and ask yourself: is some expectation hiding behind this?*

Staying free of attachment while living among the objects of the senses, and **gentleness**, are further gems of this same verse.

## Modesty: Discernment with Two Eyes

You should feel a sense of shame about conduct that goes against either society or the scriptures.

Suppose people on some beach are shamelessly doing whatever they please, and you say, "Everyone was doing it, so I did too." There, the scriptures should make you feel ashamed. Or suppose a puja is going on while a DJ blares and chaos reigns in the background. There, the regard of society should make you feel ashamed. The instinct to get up and leave such a place is also divine wealth.

## Hours Lost to the Screen

**Freedom from pointless activity** is also divine wealth. Needless TV serials, gossip, and hours spent watching short videos are all pointless activity, unless they teach you something.

*How many hours did you spend today that taught you nothing and did no one any good?*

> **tejaḥ kṣamā dhṛtiḥ śaucam adroho nātimānitā |**
> **bhavanti sampadaṁ daivīm abhijātasya bhārata ||**
> "Radiance, forgiveness, fortitude, purity, freedom from malice and absence of pride: O Bharata, these are the qualities of one born with divine wealth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.3)

## Tejas: A Voice That Transforms Lives

**Tejas** (spiritual radiance) is the power of great souls whose influence leads even sensual, base-natured people to give up injustice and turn to noble deeds.

A single sentence transformed Valmiki, the robber who became the sage-author of the Ramayana. When Chanakya spoke, the whole village listened. After the Ahar **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching) on food, many people gave up eating meat, and after the Brahmacharya Shibir, many young men gave up promiscuity. That is tejas.

This radiance comes from practising **brahmacharya** (celibacy and mastery over one's energies). First comes tejas, then **medha shakti** (the power of a sharp, retentive intellect).

## Who Is Truly Steadfast?

**Dhairya** (fortitude) means holding on to something for a very long time. "Dhi" means intellect, and a person whose intellect is steady is called **dhira** (the steadfast one). The one who keeps patience all the way to **samadhi** (the state of complete meditative absorption) is the truly steadfast person.

## Standing Your Ground Without Enmity

- **Shaucha** (purity): outer cleanliness matters too
- **Adroha** (freedom from malice): holding no enmity towards anyone, while still using persuasion, inducement and punishment to secure your rightful due
- **Amanita** (absence of pride): not the conceit of "I deserve to be revered", but the feeling "God is working through me"

## This Is Mahalakshmi

Put simply, these three verses together are called **Mahalakshmi** (the Great Lakshmi).

In all these years of worship, is this what we ever asked for? And where in these verses is money mentioned? Lakshmi is the consort of Shri Krishna himself, for he is Lord Vishnu, and he is the one explaining all this to Arjuna.

The Lord goes on to say that hypocrisy, arrogance, pride, anger, harshness and ignorance are the marks of **asuri sampada** (demonic wealth).

> **daivī sampad vimokṣāya nibandhāyāsurī matā |**
> **mā śucaḥ sampadaṁ daivīm abhijāto 'si pāṇḍava ||**
> "Divine wealth leads to liberation, while demonic wealth leads to bondage. So do not grieve, O Arjuna, for you are born with divine wealth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.5)

## It Already Lives Within You

If you are a seeker on the spiritual path, these qualities already live somewhere within you. When you go to the Guru, all that is needed is to lift the veil that covers them.

It is hard to change someone who has no urge to give at all. But the wish to give must have awakened in you at some point, or you would not have come as far as Lakshmi Puja today. As Acharya Ji points out, people of all three kinds perform Lakshmi Puja out in the world, but not everyone's worship bears fruit.

## Key Takeaways

- The divine wealth of the Gita's sixteenth chapter is the true Mahalakshmi, and it makes no mention of money.
- Fearlessness comes from a sense of non-duality, but it never means carelessness.
- Non-violence and freedom from anger are not weakness; respond calmly, with a plan and without hatred.
- Saraswati grants knowledge, but the art of speaking well is Lakshmi.
- Keep criticism constructive; crushing someone's sense of self is a fault.
- Let compassion be free of motive, not a transaction.
- Pointless activity, such as hours of short videos, drains divine wealth.
- Radiance comes from brahmacharya; fortitude is the mark of a steady intellect.
- These qualities already live within the seeker; the Guru helps bring them to light.
