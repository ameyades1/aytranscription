# Chapter: Divine Wealth Is the True Mahalakshmi

## What Is Divine Wealth

All the divine qualities the Lord describes in the sixteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita are together called **daivī sampatti**, divine wealth. And this divine wealth is what we call Lakshmi. These qualities are described in three verses. Let us understand them one by one and see how far they have entered us, because to the extent these qualities come into us, to that extent our Lakshmi worship is being done properly.

> **abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhir jñāna-yoga-vyavasthitiḥ**
> **dānaṁ damaś ca yajñaś ca svādhyāyas tapa ārjavam**
> "Fearlessness, purity of heart, steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, charity, self-control, sacrifice, study of scripture, austerity and straightforwardness."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.1)

## Fearlessness: The Complete Absence of Fear

The first quality is **abhaya**, the complete absence of fear. When does fear disappear? When the feeling of non-duality (advaita) arrives, when one experiences that everything is God.

As long as there is an "other", there can be fear. There can be love too, but because there is an other, it can also frighten me. That other may be an object, a person, a place or a job. The moment we say "my job", there are two: I and the job. If the job goes, there is fear. Fear comes only when there is an other.

When the feeling of non-duality comes, that all this is my own creation, the mind says: fine, if this has gone, it has gone; another will come. But remember, "let it go if it goes" does not mean becoming careless.

## Purity of Heart, Charity and Restraint of the Senses

Then come purity of the inner being, constant firm steadiness in the yoga of meditation for the sake of knowledge, and **sattvic charity**. Charity is of three kinds, and of these, sattvic charity is part of divine wealth.

Then **restraint of the senses**, that is, control over the senses. Worship of God, the gods and the gurus, and the practice of noble acts such as the agnihotra. Study and teaching of the Vedas and scriptures, that is, both reading oneself and teaching others. Singing the name and qualities of God, bearing hardship to fulfil one's own dharma, and simplicity of the inner being along with the body and senses.

All these qualities are divine wealth. And this is only the first verse. This is Lakshmi.

> **ahiṁsā satyam akrodhas tyāgaḥ śāntir apaiśunam**
> **dayā bhūteṣv aloluptvaṁ mārdavaṁ hrīr acāpalam**
> "Non-violence, truthfulness, freedom from anger, renunciation, peace, absence of slander, compassion for all beings, freedom from craving, gentleness, modesty and steadiness."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.2)

## Non-Violence: Punishment Without Hatred

Not causing pain to anyone in any way through mind, speech or body is non-violence. But this does not mean one must never fight. Among sāma, dāma and daṇḍa (persuasion, inducement and punishment), there is punishment too, but even in that punishment the good of the other is hidden.

Mother Mahishasuramardini killed Mahishasura, but for his liberation. Shri Krishna killed Kamsa, also for his liberation. Such punishment is acceptable. The only condition is that there is no hatred in the mind.

## Truth: Speech That Is True and Pleasant

The next quality is speech that is true and pleasant. Its essence is: speak the truth, speak pleasantly, speak what is beneficial.

> **satyaṁ vada. priyaṁ vada. hitaṁ vada.**
> "Speak the truth. Speak pleasantly. Speak what is beneficial."

If you have mastered this art, that itself is Lakshmi. Knowledge is given by Saraswati, but the manner of speaking, sustaining it continuously and knowing when to say what, this discernment comes from Lakshmi.

## Freedom from Anger: Responding with a Calm Mind

Not being angry even with one who has harmed you. Not being angry even with one who has wronged you. But this does not mean you must not teach him a lesson. You can respond after planning with a calm mind. Decide how to respond on merit, and also see whether you have the strength or not.

Look at the example of Chandragupta. As long as he did not have the strength, he was told not to fight: first become a warrior, then go and fight. And for this too, a calm mind is needed. Not jumping in today in anger; one must plan.

Why does anger come? The Lord says:

> **kāma eṣa krodha eṣa rajo-guṇa-samudbhavaḥ**
> "It is desire, it is anger, born of the quality of rajas."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.37)

Desire and anger are born of rajas. When rajas grows a great deal, much desire will arise and much anger too. If the desire is not fulfilled, anger; if it is fulfilled, ego. That is why rajas is itself a form of Lakshmi, but it must have the support of Saraswati. Only then does it become merit, only then does it become divine wealth.

The reason for not being angry is that forgiveness can also be an option, and if one must fight, a calm mind is still needed.

## A Mother's Anger: Full of Love

This does not mean you can never be angry with your son. The scripture says:

> **lālayet pañca varṣāṇi daśa varṣāṇi tāḍayet**
> "Pamper for five years, discipline for ten years."
> (Chanakya Niti)

When the time comes, one has to scold too. But in the midst of that shouting there is love, there is the feeling "may he improve", there is no enmity. When a mother hits her son, does she become his enemy? Even on that day she is not his enemy. When it is needed, such anger is acceptable.

## Renunciation, Peace and Steadiness of Mind

Renouncing the pride of doership in actions, that is, there should be no ego. Withdrawal of the inner being, meaning the absence of restlessness of the mind.

## Absence of Slander: Not Condemnation but Constructive Correction

Not slandering anyone. This does not mean never making any comment. You can offer constructive criticism, in which the other person's good lies. But if your words hurt them so that they fall, go down, there is no benefit.

Suppose the daughter-in-law of the house does not do any work. Fine, she has come; perhaps she came from a royal family and has that nature, or was raised with pampering at home. Now your household's culture is different, and there is a lot of work in your home. In such a case, if you keep saying "you are like this, you are like that, your mother gave you no values" and reduce her personality to zero, that is like committing brahmahatyā on her. You will gain nothing from it; on the contrary, you will incur sin. Instead, show her the importance of work, show it by doing it yourself. That will work.

Not slandering means not criticising someone in order to bring them down. And it is also said that the critic's house should be kept close.

## Compassion: Kindness Without a Motive

**Compassion without a motive** towards all beings. A motive means some self-interest, some expectation that "this will benefit me". I fed the pigeons this much so my debt will be cleared; I gave the cow this much, gave the dog this much so Saturn will be pacified; give the pigeons this much and I will get married. None of this is compassion. Compassion must be shown, but without any motive.

Not having attachment to sense objects even when the senses come into contact with them, and **gentleness**, these too are part of divine wealth.

## Modesty: Towards Both Society and Scripture

There should be shame at conduct that goes against society and scripture. Suppose you go to some beach, like Goa or Miami, where people are shamelessly doing anything. There, shame before society is surely needed. The second is shame before scripture. If someone says, "Everyone was doing it, so I did it too", then shame before scripture should arise.

On the other hand, somewhere everything looks good on the surface, worship is going on, that is, something in line with scripture is happening, but behind it DJ music is playing and everything is in disorder. There, something wrong is happening from society's point of view. When something is right by one and wrong by the other, I should feel shame, I should get up and leave. This tendency that awakens within is also called divine wealth.

## Absence of Pointless Activity

Then it is said, **absence of pointless activity**. Unnecessary serials, gossip, sitting for hours watching short videos without reason, two hours, four hours, all this is pointless activity. Of course, if it has some purpose and some knowledge is coming from it, that is different.

That was the second verse. All this too comes under Lakshmi.

> **tejaḥ kṣamā dhṛtiḥ śaucam adroho nāti-mānitā**
> **bhavanti sampadaṁ daivīm abhijātasya bhārata**
> "Radiance, forgiveness, fortitude, purity, absence of enmity and absence of pride: these, O Bharata, belong to one born with divine wealth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.3)

## Radiance: The Power of Words to Transform

The first quality of the third verse is **tejas**, radiance. What is tejas? Tejas is the name of that power of great souls by whose influence even people attached to the senses and of low nature usually stop their unjust conduct and engage in noble actions as they say.

Valmiki's life changed immediately on hearing a single sentence; that is the radiance of speech. When Chanakya spoke, the whole village listened; that is tejas.

Recently we taught a diet camp, and many people said, "We have given up non-vegetarian food." We taught a brahmacharya camp, and many young men said, "Our promiscuity has also stopped." That is tejas.

Where does this tejas come from? **From the practice of brahmacharya**. First tejas comes, then the power of intellect (medhā) and so on.

## Forgiveness and Fortitude

After tejas come **forgiveness** and **fortitude**. Fortitude means holding on to something for a very long time. Who is a dhīra, a steadfast person? "Dhī" means intellect. One whose intellect is sound and steady is a dhīra. One who held on with fortitude and reached samādhi is called a dhīra.

## Purity, Absence of Enmity and Absence of Pride

External purity is also needed. Then, having no enmity towards anyone. But fighting is also necessary: for one's rightful due, persuasion, inducement and punishment must be used, only without enmity.

And the absence of the pride of being worthy of worship. Not the feeling "I am someone great, to be worshipped", but rather "God is working through me", the God-centred feeling.

The Lord says, O Arjuna, these are the marks of a person born with divine wealth.

## This Is Mahalakshmi

In simple language, what is this divine wealth called? **Mahalakshmi**. The three verses we have just read, this is basically what Lakshmi is.

So consider: in all the worship we have done over so many years, did we ever ask for this? And where in these verses is money mentioned? Lakshmi is Shri Krishna's own consort, he is Vishnu himself, and it is he who is telling this to Arjuna.

## Demonic Wealth: The Path of Bondage

Right after this the Lord says:

> **dambho darpo 'bhimānaś ca krodhaḥ pāruṣyam eva ca**
> **ajñānaṁ cābhijātasya pārtha sampadam āsurīm**
> "Hypocrisy, arrogance, pride, anger, harshness and ignorance, O Partha, belong to one born with demonic wealth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.4)

O Partha, hypocrisy, arrogance, pride, anger, harshness and ignorance are the marks of a person born with demonic wealth.

> **daivī sampad vimokṣāya nibandhāyāsurī matā**
> **mā śucaḥ sampadaṁ daivīm abhijāto 'si pāṇḍava**
> "Divine wealth is held to lead to liberation and demonic wealth to bondage. Do not grieve, O Pandava, for you are born with divine wealth."
> (Bhagavad Gita 16.5)

Divine wealth is held to be for liberation, and demonic wealth for bondage. Therefore, O Arjuna, do not grieve, for you are born with divine wealth.

## The Lakshmi Hidden Within the Seeker

If you are a seeker, these qualities are already somewhere within you. You only need to go to the guru and bring them out, unlock them, remove the veil that has fallen over them.

But one who has demonic wealth within, who has no inclination to give at all, is very hard to change. Within you, at least a little of all this was there; the wish to give in the form of wealth must have arisen at some time, which is why you have come here today for Lakshmi worship.

Outside, all three kinds of people are doing Lakshmi worship, but not everyone's worship bears fruit.
