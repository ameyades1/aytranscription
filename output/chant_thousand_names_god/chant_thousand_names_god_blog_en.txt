# A Thousand Names of the One Divine

## Why Repeat the Same Thing a Thousand Times?

Most people who recite the Ganapati Atharvashirsha go straight to its **phalashruti** (the description of the fruits of recitation). There it says: recite it a thousand times.

But the mind asks: why the same thing a thousand times? What is its fruit? We make children say "*kachcha papad, pakka papad*," but that is a game. Why so much repetition here?

And then comes the **Sahasranama** (a hymn of a thousand names): the Ganesha Sahasranama, the Vishnu Sahasranama, the Devi Sahasranama. Why take a thousand names of one God?

> "There is no benefit in saying something imaginary a thousand times, if it is not seen in real life at all."

## You Have So Many Names Too

Think about yourself for a moment. One name is on your birth certificate. At home your mother calls you something else, like Pintu or Pinky. Your friends have their own name for you. At the office someone calls you by your surname.

Someone says "Doctor Sahab," someone says "Indian." Someone recognizes you as the thin one, the bald one or the one with glasses. To someone you are a brother, to someone a husband, to someone a son, to someone a father.

But are these different persons? No. Many names, one person. And still you never reach a thousand names.

*How many people called you by how many different names today? Did any of those names make you a "different" person?*

## Vasudevah Sarvam: Everything Is That One

The real meaning of the Sahasranama is hidden in this verse of the Bhagavad Gita:

> **bahūnāṁ janmanām ante jñānavān māṁ prapadyate**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "After many births, the one of wisdom takes refuge in Me, realizing that Vasudeva is all. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

After many births, in that final birth in which liberation is to come, the wise one takes refuge in Me. With what understanding? "Vasudevah sarvam," that is, everything is the Divine (All is God). Such a person is a mahatma, and such a mahatma is very rare.

One who worships Vishnu should see Vishnu in every person. One who worships the Devi should see the Devi everywhere.

## The Sahasranama Describes This Very World

Open any Sahasranama and look. Among Ganapati's names: You create obstacles, You remove obstacles. In the Devi Sahasranama you will find nearly the same names. You are the Guru, You are the one who labours, You are the sustainer, You are the destroyer, You alone are the creator.

Do we not see people doing all these works every day? Teachers, labourers, those who nurture, those who build. Seeing them, we should remember **Brahman** (the Supreme Reality): "Ah, this is what I was meant to see everywhere."

The Sahasranama is recited to awaken exactly this feeling. If it is not seen in life at all, saying it a thousand times will do nothing.

## Om Tat Sat: In Giving, See the Divine, Not the Cow

Shri Krishna said that charity must be given in the spirit of "Om Tat Sat."

> **oṁ tat sad iti nirdeśo brahmaṇas tri-vidhaḥ smṛtaḥ**
> "Om Tat Sat is declared to be the threefold designation of Brahman."
> (Bhagavad Gita 17.23)

Om means that same Supreme Being. I fed fodder to a cow. Krishna says, you did wrong. Giving to the cow was not wrong, but how did the word "cow" come into your mind? Om should have come there, that is, the Divine in the form of a cow.

I fed a beggar. Wrong again. Why did you see a beggar, and not Shanidev? And if you gave food to Shanidev with love, but so that you would be blessed with a son, that too is wrong. Nothing for me. His stomach is to be filled with **nishkama bhava** (without any selfish motive).

*When you help someone, who do you see in front of you: a person in need, or the Divine?*

## There Is No Other Here

Then the question comes: why should I help others? Just because selfless service passes the time pleasantly?

The answer: there is no other here at all. Neither a second nor a third. This is the **Advaita bhavana** (the feeling of non-duality).

It comes when one understands that all of this was my own imagination, it was only I. This is the **state of Kaivalya**: kevalo'ham, I alone am. Just as on waking from sleep we realize the dream was our own.

## Your Body Never Sends a Bill

As long as I feel my body to be one undivided whole, no merchant's attitude enters it. The hand scratches the nose, scratches the ear, but asks no price.

It does not say, put up a plaque with my name here, or "I'm doing it free today, but remember, you'll have to serve me too, or I won't do it next time."

Hand, nose, eyes, hair are all separate. Yet one shared feeling joins them all: my hand, my ear, my nose.

> "That common something called 'mine' which binds, when you feel the same for the whole world, at that moment the time of your realization has arrived."

The day the whole world feels as much "mine" as your own body, know that the time of **sakshatkar** (Self-realization) has come.

## You Are the Author of the Story

Creation is **nirakar** (beyond form), yet we see it as **sakar** (in forms). That is why the Sahasranama links countless forms to the One.

One may disagree with the scene, not with the seer (Hate the scene, not the seer). There is no **vair bhava** (enmity) in it. The villain who appeared in your story: you wrote his script, and you brought him in yourself.

> "Until you look with the witness consciousness, you will not see God."

## Key Takeaways

- The purpose of the Sahasranama is not rote repetition a thousand times, but recognizing the one Divine in every form.
- Just as you are one despite your many names, God's thousand names belong to one Truth.
- Gita 7.19: "Vasudevah sarvam," everything is the Divine; the one who understands this is a rare mahatma.
- Give in the spirit of "Om Tat Sat" and without desire: see not a cow or a beggar, but the Divine.
- Serve because there is no other at all: this is Advaita and Kaivalya.
- The parts of the body keep no accounts with each other; when you feel this toward the whole world, realization is near.
- Without seeing as the witness, God is not seen.
