# Chapter: The Secret of the Sahasranama, Seeing the One in All

## Why Say the Same Thing a Thousand Times?

Most people who recite the Shri Ganapati Atharvashirsha go straight to its phalashruti, the passage describing its fruits. There it is written: recite it a thousand times. But a question arises in the mind: why would anyone say the same thing a thousand times? You were told to say the same thing again and again. Why should I? What is the fruit of it?

We tell children, don't we, "Say *kachcha papad, pakka papad*." That is only a game. But why must this be done a thousand times?

That is mere repetition (avartan). I am speaking of the Sahasranama. Sometimes we are told to recite a Sahasranama: the Ganesha Sahasranama, the Vishnu Sahasranama, the Devi Sahasranama. Why take a thousand names?

## You Too Have Many Names

Have you ever considered that you yourself have many names? One is the name on your birth certificate. At home your mother probably calls you something else, a name like Pintu or Pinky. Your friends call you something else. At the office someone calls you by your surname, and that has become a new name. So there are already four or five names.

Someone addresses you by your profession, "Doctor Sahab." Someone may call you an Indian. If a Marathi person or a Hindi speaker comes along, your identity will differ again: the thin one, the bald one, the one with glasses. One name will come from your work.

But are these different persons? Someone will call that same person brother, someone husband; to one he is a son, to another a father. All the names are different, but the person is one. Even so, do you ever reach a thousand names?

## Vasudevah Sarvam: Everything Is the Divine

The meaning of the Sahasranama is ultimately what is said in the nineteenth verse of the seventh chapter of the Shrimad Bhagavad Gita:

> **bahūnāṁ janmanām ante jñānavān māṁ prapadyate**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ**
> "After many births, the one of wisdom takes refuge in Me, realizing that Vasudeva is all. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

After many births, in the final birth, that is, the birth in which liberation is to come, the wise one understands this and takes refuge in Me (prapadyate). With what understanding? "Vasudevah sarvam," meaning everything is the Divine. Such a person becomes a mahatma, and such a mahatma is extremely rare.

What did that mahatma understand? That everything is the Divine. So if he worships Vishnu, then in all the people he sees here, he should see only Vishnu everywhere. If he is a worshipper of the Devi, he should see only the Devi everywhere.

## The Sahasranama Describes the Work of This World

Look at any Sahasranama, such as the Sahasranama of Ganapati. It describes the very different works that go on in this world. You are like this, You create obstacles, You remove obstacles. In this way a thousand different names arise.

Take out the Sahasranama of the Devi as well and look: you will find more or less the same names there too. You are the Guru; such names will all appear in it. Check in any of them. You are the one who labours, You are also the Guru. You are the sustainer, You are also the destroyer, and the one who creates is also You.

This means: do we not see people outside doing all these works? Seeing them, I should remember that Brahman: "Ah, this is what I was meant to see everywhere." The Sahasranama is recited to awaken this feeling.

> "There is no benefit in saying something imaginary a thousand times, if it is not seen in real life at all."

## Om Tat Sat: The True Spirit of Giving

What did Shri Krishna say about giving in charity? It must be given in the spirit of "Om Tat Sat."

> **oṁ tat sad iti nirdeśo brahmaṇas tri-vidhaḥ smṛtaḥ**
> "Om Tat Sat is declared to be the threefold designation of Brahman."
> (Bhagavad Gita 17.23)

What does Om mean? That same Supreme Being. But you do not see Him at all. I fed fodder to a cow. Shri Krishna says, you did wrong. Giving to the cow was not wrong, but how did the word "cow" even come into your mind? Om should have come there. Om means the Supreme Being who is present in the form of the cow.

I fed a beggar. He says, wrong. How did you see a beggar? Why did you not see Shanidev? I gave Shanidev, especially the food that was His own share, with love. Fine, but for what? So that I may be blessed with a son. That too is wrong. Nothing for me; his stomach is to be filled in a spirit free of desire (nishkama bhava).

## There Is No Other Here

Now the question arises: why should I do this? Do I give charity and serve others because selfless action passes my time pleasantly? What is this? Why must I help another?

The answer is what is said there: there is no other here at all. Neither a second nor a third. This is called the feeling of Advaita, non-duality. It comes when one understands that all of this was my own imagination, it was only I. This is the state of Kaivalya: kevalam, kevalo'ham, I alone am, and all of this is my imagination. Just as the truth of a dream is understood on waking from sleep, so it is understood here.

## The Example of the Body: One Common "Mine"

I always say that as long as this body feels to me like one whole, undivided unit, the merchant's attitude (vaishya bhava) does not enter it. When the hand scratches the nose or scratches the ear, it does not ask for payment. Nor does it say, put up a nameplate here saying I did this, or I am giving it free today, but remember, you will have to serve me too, and if you don't, I won't do it next time.

Do the parts of your body do this to one another? They do not. Because there is a feeling of non-duality among them. The hand is separate from the nose, the nose is separate from the eye, the eye is separate from the hair. Yet there is some common element that says: my hand, my ear, my nose.

> "That common something called 'mine' which binds, when you feel the same for the whole world, at that moment the time of your realization has arrived."

When you feel this "mine," which binds everything together, for the whole world, know that the time of your Self-realization has come. This much is certain. And all of this is my own imagination.

## See as the Witness

This much has become clear: what it means when we recite the Sahasranama and the like. The creation I see appears to me in form (sakar). It is formless (nirakar), but I see it in form.

Hate the scene, not the seer. There is no enmity in it. The villain who appeared before you in your story: you wrote his script yourself, and you brought him in yourself. I alone am the author of that story.

> "Until you look with the witness consciousness, you will not see God."
