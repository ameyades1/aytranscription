# Sattva, Rajas and Tamas: Three Cords That Bind Us and Also Move Us

We have all heard that nature has three **gunas** (fundamental qualities): **sattva** (purity, clarity), **rajas** (activity, passion) and **tamas** (inertia, darkness). But have you ever paused to ask what the word "guna" actually means?

Acharya Ji begins with this question, and the answer changes the way you see the whole teaching.

In short, the three gunas are cords of maya, and even sattva binds. Yet life needs all three: rajas to act, sattva to gain knowledge and tamas to rest. To balance the three gunas is to let the right guna lead at the right time, while the goal remains liberation.

## What Does Guna Mean: a Quality or a Cord?

We usually take guna to mean a quality or a characteristic. In the Sanskrit dictionary, however, guna means **a cord** or a string.

Acharya Ji makes it clear that in the context of the Gita, "quality" is only the secondary meaning. The primary meaning is cord. Sattva, rajas and tamas are three cords, and with them **maya** (the illusion of the world) ties us down.

## Can a Good Quality Also Bind You?

This comes as a surprise: **even sattva binds.** Whether a chain is made of gold or of ordinary rope, what good is it if it keeps you tied up?

Knowledge, happiness, dispassion, awareness, **viveka** (discernment) and intelligence all belong to sattva. Yet if any **vasana** (the seed of desire) remains within, you are bound to be born again. Rajas is bondage, tamas is lethargy, and through it all a person keeps chasing happiness, over and over. That is the real problem.

Practise devotion and serve the Guru; that is good. But your goal should be **liberation** (mukti), not another birth.

*Could my good qualities also be keeping me bound?*

## Should You Give Up All Three Gunas, Then?

No. Acharya Ji says plainly that no single guna is right on its own, whether it is sattva alone, rajas alone or tamas alone.

- To act, you need **rajas**.
- To gain knowledge, you need **sattva**.
- To rest, or to bring something to an end, you need **tamas**.

None of us does the same thing all day. When you sit down to learn, sattva makes it work. If sleep overtakes you at that moment, tamas has arrived at the wrong time. Sleep is right at night, and staying awake is right now. Sitting, working and every other activity runs on the energy of rajas.

Acharya Ji explains that we need all three gunas together. The real art lies in understanding which guna should lead in which place and at which time.

*Which guna took the lead at the wrong time in my day today?*

## Three Goddesses, Three Colours

Acharya Ji links these gunas to three forms of the Devi:

- **Mahasaraswati: sattva, white.** The colour of sattva is white, which is why Saraswati is dressed in white.
- **Lakshmi: rajas, red.** "I am working myself to the bone," "My blood is boiling": the rushing about, the pressure, the rising blood pressure. This is the rajasic energy of action, and its colour is red.
- **Kali: tamas, black.** The colour of tamas is black, which is why Kali is dressed in black.

## Key Takeaways

- In Sanskrit, guna primarily means a cord; the three gunas are the cords of maya.
- Even sattva is a bond; if any vasana remains, another birth follows.
- The goal is liberation, not merely becoming good.
- Life needs all three gunas; the right guna must lead at the right time.
- Mahasaraswati (white) represents sattva, Lakshmi (red) rajas, and Kali (black) tamas.
