# Chapter: Three Gunas, Three Cords

## The True Meaning of the Word Guna

Three gunas have been described: sattva, rajas and tamas. But what does the word guna mean? Think about it in your own mind: what do you understand by the word "guna"? Even an elementary student of Sanskrit should know this.

In everyday speech, guna means quality, nature, characteristic. But if you open a Sanskrit dictionary, the word guna means **a cord**, a rope. These are three cords. In the Gita, guna does not primarily mean quality; quality is a secondary point. The main meaning is cord. With these three cords, Maya binds us.

## Sattva Guna Is Also Bondage

It has also been said that sattva guna binds too. Whether the chain is made of gold or is an ordinary cord, if it keeps you bound, what is the benefit of that cord?

Knowledge, happiness, dispassion, consciousness, discernment and intellect are sattva guna. But what is the use of knowledge if desire remains? If desire remains, there is birth again. Rajo guna is bondage, tamo guna is laziness. A person seeks happiness again, seeks happiness over and over; this is the problem.

"Guru, Guru, Guru": practise devotion, that is fine; serve the Guru. But if even behind that there is only birth again, then beyond it there must be **liberation**.

## All Three Gunas Are Necessary

Sattva alone is not right, rajas alone is not right, tamas alone is not right either. If you must act, you need rajo guna. If you want to gain knowledge, you need sattva guna. If you want to rest, to destroy tamas, to dissolve, you need tamo guna.

Do we do just one thing all day? If sattva guna is present at the time of knowledge, things will work. Now if sleep comes at that very moment, that is tamo guna, and since it came at the wrong time, it is wrong. Sleep at night, and stay awake now, that is good. But sitting, doing work, all that is going on, is the power of rajas.

> "We require all three together."

We only need to understand which guna should be dominant, in which place and at which time.

## Three Goddesses, Three Colours

Mahasaraswati is the goddess of sattva guna. The colour of sattva is white, so Saraswati's garment is white.

Rajo guna is when a person says, "I am working with my blood and sweat." Running around, they say, "My blood is boiling." This happens in action: pressure rises, blood pressure on the heart goes up, the rushing goes on. This is the power of rajas, and its colour is red. The colour of Lakshmi, connected with action, is red; her garment always stays of one colour.

In the same way there is Kali. The colour of tamas is black, so Kali's garment is black.
