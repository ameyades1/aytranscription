# The Real Meaning of Shastra: A Rulebook or a Map for Life

## What Exactly Is a Shastra?

We hear the word **shastra** (a body of authoritative teaching on a subject) all the time, but what does it actually mean? Acharya Upendra Ji opens it up in very simple terms.

A shastra is a text that lays out **what to do and what not to do**: the do's and don'ts of a subject. Whether it does this point by point or at length is secondary.

Acharya Ji asks what a shastra really contains, and gives the answer himself: guidance on what you should do and what you should avoid.

## Who Writes It?

Not just anyone can write a shastra. Its author is an **authority**, someone who has gained mastery and real experience in that field.

In other words, a shastra is not anyone's opinion. It is guidance drawn from lived experience.

*When you make decisions about your life, whose word do you trust: someone who has lived it, or someone who only has an opinion?*

## Dharma Shastra: Even Giving Follows Rules

Those who have lived **dharma** (the righteous way of life) and reached its higher levels tell you what to do and what to avoid, so that your practice of dharma bears fruit and others are uplifted too.

Take **dana** (charitable giving). How to give, what to give, how much, when, and whether to give in the spirit of "oṁ tat sat" (an offering made to the Absolute, without claim to it): the shastra spells out all of this.

> **deśe kāle ca pātre ca**
> **tad dānaṁ sāttvikaṁ smṛtam ||**
> "A gift given at the right place and the right time, to a worthy recipient, is considered pure (sattvic)."
> (Bhagavad Gita 17.20)

Put simply, **give to those who deserve it, not to those who will misuse it.** These are clearly defined rules, given by Shri Krishna in the Gita and by Lord Shiva in the Shiva Purana.

Giving is only one part of dharma. Forgiveness and much else also fall within the Dharma Shastra, so that you are uplifted first, and then society as a whole.

## Artha Shastra: Prosperity in the Service of Dharma

Just as there is a Dharma Shastra, there is also an **Artha Shastra**, the science of wealth: how to trade, how to produce, how to earn. Authorities such as Chanakya taught it, as did Lord Dattatreya, the sage Agastya and others in the Shri Vidya tradition.

The deeper meaning of artha lies in seven things: **property, respect, society, a wife, time, self-interest and health.** The same science also teaches you how to let go of things like self-interest.

> **sukhasya mūlaṁ dharmaḥ |**
> **dharmasya mūlam arthaḥ ||**
> "The root of happiness is dharma, and the root of dharma is wealth."
> (Chanakya Sutras 1–2)

Becoming wealthy is therefore a proper subject of the shastras, but **as a means to live by dharma.** How to support a family, build assets, give to the public, offer **Guru dana** (giving in support of the Guru's work), and act with **yajna bhava** (a spirit of sacred offering) for the good of the earth: all of this is part of it.

*Does any part of what you earn reach dharma, society and the Guru's work?*

## Kama Shastra: Why Write a Science for What Even Cats and Dogs Know?

**Kama** (desire, including sexual desire) is also one of the **purusharthas** (the four aims of human life). Acharya Ji asks: even dogs and cats know the act itself, so what is there to teach? Why write books about it?

Because this too has its shastra. Its authority will be either someone who understands procreation as the scriptures prescribe it, or someone fully established in **brahmacharya** (celibacy and self-restraint).

The sage Vatsyayana declared that he wrote his text in a state of meditation, after a lifetime of unbroken celibacy and after mastering both dharma and artha. He did not write it to inflame desire, but:

- to use it as a path toward **liberation** (moksha)
- to bring worthy children into the world
- so that desire does not become an obstacle in **sadhana** (spiritual practice)

As Acharya Ji points out, the purpose of this science is not to stir up desire in people. It is to move through desire toward freedom.

It contains rules for everything from the physical act to the workings of the mind: whether or not to commit adultery, what kind of thoughts to hold, and whether it is good or bad for a king to be ruled by women. It serves your own good and the good of society.

## From Liberation to Science: Every Field Has Its Shastra

In the same way there is a **Moksha Shastra**, the science of liberation. Physics, chemistry, mathematics, electricity and astrology each have a shastra of this kind too. When a scientist explains a field, that explanation also comes with things to do and things to avoid.

Now the word shastra makes sense.

## Key Takeaways

- A shastra sets out what to do and what not to do.
- An experienced authority writes it, not someone with a mere opinion.
- The Dharma Shastra teaches that even giving should follow place, time and the worthiness of the recipient, in the right spirit.
- The Artha Shastra teaches how to earn wealth in the service of dharma: "dharmasya mūlam arthaḥ".
- The Kama Shastra exists not to inflame desire but for liberation, worthy children and sadhana free of obstacles.
- From liberation to science, every field has its own shastra, serving both your good and the good of society.
