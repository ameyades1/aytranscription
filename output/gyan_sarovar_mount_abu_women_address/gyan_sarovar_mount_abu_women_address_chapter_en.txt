# Chapter: Woman, a Thousand Times the Power

## The Conference at Mount Abu

On 3 July 2025, in the quiet valleys of Mount Abu, the Brahma Kumaris organisation held a grand national conference on "Women's Empowerment through Spirituality". More than 500 influential women from every corner of the country took part. Sadguru Acharya Upendra Ji, founder of the Antar Yog Foundation, was invited to the event as a special guest.

After the auspicious lighting of the lamp, a senior Brahma Kumari sadhika introduced Acharya Ji. She said that Acharya Ji has served his motherland selflessly and continuously since 2005, that he does not merely teach the scriptures but lives by them, and that his highest aim is to make India healthy, prosperous and a spiritual guide, and to establish it once again as the Vishwa Guru, the teacher of the world. Describing his life of service as a burning lamp in the darkness of maya, she recalled Arjuna's proclamation:

> **na dainyaṃ na palāyanam**
> "No helplessness, no retreat."
> (Arjuna's famous proclamation, Mahabharata tradition)

That is, neither weakness nor flight: always moving forward on the path of duty with firm resolve. Acharya Ji then shared his thoughts on women's empowerment through the knowledge of Vedanta, in simple and clear words.

## Not Equal, but a Thousand Times More Powerful

> "A woman is never equal to a man. She is at least a thousand times more powerful."

Read the Durga Saptashati. There the Goddess is saluted in both forms, the utterly gentle and the utterly fierce:

> **atisaumyātiraudrāyai natāstasyai namo namaḥ**
> "Salutations again and again to Her who is supremely gentle and supremely fierce."
> (Shri Durga Saptashati 5.13)

But what happens to a woman today? Her fierce form often comes out in front of her husband, while her gentle form is what the outside world sees. However much injustice she faces outside, she bears it, when she was not meant to bear it.

## One Thing India Lacks: The Ardhanarishvara State

We have all the knowledge and all the powers India needs to become the Vishwa Guru. Only one thing is missing: India has not yet reached the **Ardhanarishvara** state, the union of the masculine and feminine as one. Our society is still male-dominated. How many women have become Prime Minister in our country? Very few, only one. So why not more?

That is why I said: come, let us begin the work. This change will come only through the empowerment of women. But how will it happen? For that, we must first understand the powers that lie within a woman.

## The Power of Intuition

Imagine a bus or a train. You enter the compartment and find that all the window seats have been taken by men. The journey is only twenty minutes long. Even then a woman has so much **intuition** that she senses at once: "This one does not look quite right, that one seems a bit like a goon." And from among them all, she goes and sits exactly beside the one who seems decent. This power of intuition is natural to her.

## Dhriti, Smriti and Medha

**Dhriti** means the power of retention: holding on to something for a very long time.

When a man asks me, "Where did I go wrong?", I always tell him: go and just tell your wife that she once made a mistake. Then watch. She will tell you every mistake that you, your mother and your family made, and when each one was made. And at exactly the right time! This is **smriti** (memory), this is **dhriti** (retention), and this is **medha** (intelligence). At the right moment, right at some function, she knows exactly what to say and how to say it.

## Mother, Guru and Mother India

> "Until you understand a mother, you cannot understand the Guru. And until you understand the Guru as Mother, you cannot understand Mother India."

If India is to become the Vishwa Guru, consider this: apart from India, all the other countries of the world are made up of people of other faiths. When will they listen to us? What will they listen to? Are we ready, right now, to understand them as a mother would?

## The Echo of the Conference

This extraordinary address on the power and divinity of woman left every woman present spellbound. They felt that in Acharya Ji's eyes a woman is not ordinary but deeply worthy of reverence and immensely powerful. Seeing this feeling alive in Acharya Ji himself, the audience's respect for him grew naturally.

After the session, many women met Acharya Ji personally to express their gratitude. They found his words practical and logical, and found solutions to problems in their own lives. They also showed interest in learning more about the Antar Yog Foundation and its initiatives.
