# As You Come to Me, So I Come to You

Have you ever noticed that whatever we give comes back to us in the same form? In the Gita, Shri Krishna says:

> **ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "In whatever way people approach Me, in that same way I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

But does this simply mean "tit for tat"? This discourse explores that question in depth.

## The Person Before You Is Brahman Too

The whole universe is **Brahman** (the all-pervading Absolute), and Brahman is Lord Shiva himself.

So if you abuse the person standing in front of you, you have abused Brahman. That person will not wave a lamp in your honour. They will abuse you right back.

This is the first and most obvious meaning. If you shout at home, the people around you will shout too.

## When to Raise Your Voice, When to Stay Silent

The real question is whether you have **bodha** (inner understanding): the sense of when shouting was right and when it was not.

If the Guru is shouting at you, the right response is silence, not shouting back. When a king raises his voice, he has a reason: you have evaded your taxes and refuse to pay them. He has every right to be angry.

When a father scolds his son, the son will naturally dislike it. Yet whether the scolding was fair is something only inner understanding can settle. Lord Ganesha is the one who grants this understanding, this **viveka** (discernment).

*The last time someone scolded you, did you stop to ask what lay behind it?*

## Why the Guru Feels Harsh at First

At first, the stern face of the Guru never feels pleasant. You can count on that.

Even Arjuna does not like Shri Krishna at first, with all his conditions: do this, do that. Chandragupta, too, does not warm to his teacher Acharya Chanakya in the beginning.

The meaning becomes clear later, once Lord Ganesha awakens within.

## Does God Take Revenge?

Is God's justice then like that of an ordinary person, who strikes back because you struck first? Is He some street thug? No.

God may take on an outward form that mirrors your behaviour, but His intent always remains that of the Supreme Lord: your **uddhara** (uplift and liberation).

Acharya Ji puts it this way. It is as if God says, "You will not learn from sermons; you only learn when you are kicked. Then I will teach you with a kick."

God speaks whatever language you understand. In the very form you came to destroy Him, He acts to lift you up and set you free.

## Maa Durga: A Weapon in Her Hand, Compassion in Her Heart

Maa Durga fights the demon, then throws her weapons aside, and the battle turns into hand-to-hand combat. The gods ask her, "You could kill him in an instant with a mere resolve, so why all this? Why do you let him touch you? He insults you, calling you 'wicked Durga'. Why do you listen to it?"

Her answer changes everything. She says, "I am a mother. I do not see him as an enemy at all."

She gives him a first, a second and a third chance to mend his ways. The word "wicked" does not even register with her. He keeps saying "Durga, Durga, Durga", and so, without knowing it, he is practising **nama-smarana** (remembrance of the Divine Name).

She lets him touch her so that he receives **sparsha diksha** (initiation by touch, in which the energy of the Guru or the Goddess flows through contact). This is his last chance to at least attain freedom. The longer he stays in her awareness, the more his sins are washed away.

## The Secret Hidden in the Aarti

We have sung the **aarti** (a devotional hymn sung while waving a lamp before the deity) "Durge Durghat Bhari" again and again, even during the Ganesh festival. But have we ever read the feeling behind it?

My Durga took up weapons, but to uplift the very one she fought. This is what "as you come to Me, so I come to you" means. It holds no spirit of revenge and no enmity. The two are worlds apart.

*When you answer someone harshly, is the feeling inside you revenge, or their good?*

## A Mother's Way: Letting Go of the Fruit

Acharya Ji says: act, and let go of your insistence on the fruit.

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Regard your mother, your father and your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2)

Tell me, how often does a mother send a bill for everything she does for you? What a mother does is the true renunciation of the fruit of action. The scriptures call this the highest state.

## Key Takeaways

- The whole universe is Brahman; how you treat the person before you is how you treat Brahman, and it comes back to you.
- Inner understanding and discernment, granted by Lord Ganesha, tell you when to respond and when to stay silent.
- The Guru's stern side feels unpleasant at first, as Shri Krishna did to Arjuna and Chanakya to Chandragupta; understanding comes later.
- God's justice is never revenge; His outward form may mirror yours, but His intent is always your uplift.
- Maa Durga does not see even her enemy as an enemy; as a mother, she frees him through chances, remembrance of her name and initiation by touch.
- Act and let go of the fruit; giving without keeping accounts, as a mother does, is the highest state.
