# In Whatever Form You Worship Me In That Form I Respond

Have you ever noticed that what we give comes back to us in the same form? In the Gita, the Lord says:

> **ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "As people approach Me, so do I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

But does this simply mean "tit for tat"? This discourse opens up the depth of that question.

## The Person Before You Is Also Brahman

Throughout the universe there is only **Brahman** (the all-pervading Absolute); it is Shiva alone.

So if you abuse the person standing before you, you have abused Brahman. Then he will not perform your aarti; he too will abuse you in return.

This is the first, direct meaning: if you shout at home, the other person will shout too.

## Where to Shout, and Where to Stay Silent?

The real question is whether we have **bodh** (inner understanding): where was shouting right, and where was it not?

If the Guru is shouting before you, you should not shout back but stay silent. If the king is shouting, there is a reason: you have evaded taxes, you do not pay them. He has the right to shout.

If the father scolds, the son will not like it; that is natural. But whether the scolding was right is decided by understanding. That understanding, that **vivek buddhi** (discernment), is given by Ganapati.

*The last time someone scolded you, did you pause to think about the reason behind it?*

## At First the Guru Does Not Feel Pleasant

In the first moment, the Guru's stern form never feels pleasant; that is certain.

Even Arjuna at first does not like Krishna; He sets so many conditions: do this, do that. Even Chandragupta at first does not like Acharya Chanakya.

Later it is understood. When Ganapati awakens within, then it is understood.

## Does God Take Revenge?

So is God's justice like that of an ordinary person: you hit me, so I hit you? Is He some thug? No.

God's outer form may mirror your behaviour, but His feeling is always that of the Supreme Lord: your **uddhar** (upliftment and liberation).

Acharya Ji explains that God says, in effect, that since you do not understand through speeches but only by being kicked, He will teach you by kicking.

God speaks the language you understand. The very form through which you came to destroy Him is the form through which He works for your upliftment.

## Goddess Durga: Weapons in Hand, Compassion in Heart

Durga fights, then throws away her weapons, then hand-to-hand combat takes place. The gods ask: "You could kill him in one stroke by mere resolve, so why all this? Why let him touch you? He insults you by calling you 'wicked Durge', why do you listen?"

The Goddess's answer changes everything: she says that she is a mother, and she does not see him as an enemy at all.

She gives him one, two, three chances to reform. Her attention does not go to the word "wicked" at all; he says "Durge, Durge, Durge", so he is doing **naam-smaran** (remembrance of the divine name).

She lets him touch her so that his **sparsha diksha** (initiation by touch, in which the energy of the Guru or Goddess flows through touch) may happen. It is a last chance, so that at least he may be liberated. The more he stays in the Goddess's mind, the more all his sins are washed away.

## The Secret Hidden in the Aarti

We sang the aarti "Durge Durghat Bhari" again and again, during the Ganapati festival too. But have we ever read this feeling in it?

My Durga held weapons, but for the upliftment of the one before her. This is "in whatever form you worship Me, in that form I respond". It is not a feeling of revenge, not enmity. There is a difference of earth and sky between the two.

*When you answer someone harshly, is the feeling within one of revenge or of their good?*

## A Mother's Way: Letting Go of Insistence on Fruits

Acharya Ji says that we should do our work and give up insisting on the fruit.

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Treat your mother as God, your father as God, your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2)

Tell me, how much of a bill does a mother charge for her service? What a mother does is itself the **renunciation of the fruit**. This state is called the highest.

## Key Takeaways

- Brahman is everywhere; how you treat the person before you is how you treat Brahman, and it comes back to you.
- Where to react and where to stay silent is decided by understanding and discernment, which Ganapati gives.
- The Guru's stern form is unpleasant at first, as Krishna was for Arjuna and Chanakya for Chandragupta; understanding comes later.
- God's justice is not revenge; His outer form may mirror yours, but His feeling is always your upliftment.
- Goddess Durga does not see even an enemy as an enemy; as a mother she frees him through chances, name-remembrance and initiation by touch.
- Do your work and give up insisting on the fruit; giving without keeping accounts, like a mother, is the highest state.
