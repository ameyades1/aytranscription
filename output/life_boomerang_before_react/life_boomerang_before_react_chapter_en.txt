# Chapter: In Whatever Form You Worship Me, In That Form I Respond

## The Person Before You Is Also Brahman

The Lord says:

> **ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "As people approach Me, so do I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

In simple words: in whatever form you worship Me, in that form I respond.

Throughout the whole universe there is only Brahman; it is Shiva alone. So if you are abusing the person standing before you, it means you have abused Brahman. Then he too will not perform your aarti; he too will abuse you in return.

## Where to Shout and Where Not To: A Question of Understanding

The first meaning is easy to grasp. If you shout at home, the other person will shout too. Now understanding must arise: where was shouting right, and where was it not?

Two people are shouting at each other, but if it is the Guru shouting before me, then I should not shout back; there we should have stayed silent. If the king is shouting, there must be some reason: you have evaded taxes, you do not pay your taxes. So he has the right to shout.

If the father scolds, the son will not like it; that is true. But whether he should have scolded or not depends on understanding. That understanding, that **discerning intellect (vivek buddhi)**, is given by Ganapati.

## At First the Guru Does Not Feel Pleasant

That much is understood. But in the first moment, the Guru's stern form will certainly not feel pleasant. Even Arjuna at first does not like Krishna; He sets so many conditions: do this, do that. Even Chandragupta at first does not like Acharya Chanakya. Later it is understood. When Ganapati awakens within, then it is understood.

## God's Justice Is Not the Justice of Revenge

So does "in whatever form you worship Me, in that form I respond" mean that the justice of Krishna, of God, is like that of an ordinary person: you hit me, so I hit you? Is He some thug? No.

The Lord says: My outer form will be the same; whatever you do, I will do. But My feeling is always that of the Supreme Lord. That is, I will only want your **upliftment (uddhar)**.

> "But you do not understand through speeches; you understand only by being kicked, so by kicking I will teach you."

As the saying goes, some will not listen to words, only to blows. So the language you understand, the very thing you came to do to Me, I will do too; but through it I will uplift you. You came to destroy Me through that form; through that same form I will work for your upliftment.

## The Example of Goddess Durga: The Feeling of a Mother

The example of this is Goddess Durga. Durga fights, then throws away her weapons, then hand-to-hand combat takes place. Then the gods ask: "You could kill him in one stroke by mere resolve, so why do all this? Why do you let him touch you? That wicked one keeps calling you 'Durge', why do you listen to your own insult?"

Then what did the Goddess say?

> "Because I am a mother. I do not see him as an enemy at all."

She says: I give him one chance, two chances, three chances so that he may reform. He says "wicked Durge" so many times, but I pay no attention to the "wicked". He says "Durge, Durge, Durge", and I do this so that he remembers My name.

And why does she let him touch her? Because his **initiation by touch (sparsha diksha)** will take place; My energy will flow into him. I give him a last chance so that at least he will be liberated. The more he stays with Me, in My mind, the more all his sins will be washed away.

## The Feeling Hidden in the Aarti

Have we ever read this feeling of the Goddess? We sang the aarti "Durge Durghat Bhari" again and again, during the Ganapati festival too. Now the feeling has come of how my Durga was: she held weapons, but for the upliftment of the one before her.

This is "in whatever form you worship Me, in that form I respond". It is **not a feeling of revenge**, it is **not enmity**. Understand this clearly; there is a difference of earth and sky between the two.

## Giving Up Insistence on Fruits: The Highest State

> "Do your work; give up insisting on the fruit."

Just this:

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Treat your mother as God, your father as God, your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2)

Tell me, how much of a bill does a mother charge for her service? What a mother does is itself renunciation of the fruit. This state, He has said, is the highest.
