# Rigid Mind or Flexible Intellect: Who Holds Your Destiny

## Can Destiny Be Changed?

Acharya Upendra Ji says that a person of **medha** (deep intelligence grounded in the scriptures) thinks, because all the scriptures are seated within them. They have followed the scriptures, and **tej** (radiance) has come into them.

Such a person knows a secret, which Acharya Ji puts this way: destiny is pre-written, but it can also be written anew.

## "We Are Just Like This": The Mark of Durmedha

Now what does a person of **durmedha** (a dull, closed intellect) say? "We are just like this. In our home it has always been this way. Nobody has ever changed. Show me another ideal, then perhaps I will change."

Acharya Ji says such people stay far from the Supreme, far from the Guru. **Ganapati** is not even near them, because Ganapati means the **discerning intellect** and the **steady intellect**.

And this is where life's obstacles begin. The world will keep changing anyway, and every person's intellect will be different anyway. Obstacles come because we are rigid, because we dig in.

*Is there a line in your life too, "I am just like this", that you have been hiding behind?*

## One Room, Two Truths

Acharya Ji gives a simple example. Two people stand in different places in a room and talk to each other.

The first says, "Look, there is a wall, a door, a cupboard, clothes at the back, tiles on the floor." The second says, "No, look, there is a window, a tree outside, that curtain."

So who is right? Both. The first is seeing the inside of the room, the second what lies outside the window and the door. **Each one's point of view is correct.**

## When Both Dig In

The problem begins when both say, "Nature cannot change, I will accept only my own view." Then a quarrel is certain. In Acharya Ji's words, these two will never meet in this world.

There is only one way out: change your nature for a while and stand in the other's place. Only then will you see that he was right, and so was I.

## When Will You See the Other's Life?

This example is not limited to a room. When a poor person says, "Sir, I have to come by train every day, my wife does this at home," or the house help says, "Sir, my husband comes home drunk and kicks me," we, in our attitude, in the pride of our money, say, "How can you not come? We also go to the office."

Acharya Ji asks: have you lived her life? Does anyone kick you? Does she have as much money as you? How much insult she has to bear!

Her intellect works from her angle, and yours from your angle. Saying "this is just your nature" so easily is refusing to see the other's life.

*When did you last see someone as "just making excuses", and did you truly look at their circumstances?*

## To Rise, You Must Become Flexible

Whoever wants to rise, whoever wants to become a **dharta** (one who upholds and holds everyone together), a king, an emperor, or a manager in a high position, must be flexible. They will have to take on every kind of nature. As Acharya Ji says, one can run on one goal, but one cannot run on one nature.

Let the goal stay one, the purpose of the Supreme, but let your nature adapt to the situation.

## God in All, but Treatment on Merit

Acharya Ji's principle is to see God in all, but treat each on merit.

**Bhagavad-darshan** everywhere (seeing God in every being), but not the same conduct with everyone. I will see God even in a dog, but I will treat it the way that is fitting.

God is in the poor too; there is **Shani Dev**. The treatment will only be good, just the method differs. Volunteers must be placed there, because Shani is controlled by **Mangal** (Mars), and Mangal means the police, the volunteers, order. Then even people of lower consciousness can eat their meal properly.

But if a saint or sadhu of high standing comes, there is no need to make him stand in a line; his honour and his time are different. Good food for all, for the saint, for the poor, for the dog too. **God in each one, but a fitting treatment for each one.**

## There Is Not Just One Way to Love

With gentle humour Acharya Ji asks: am I a dog, that I should lick everyone the same way? Perhaps that is why the word **chatukar** (flatterer, literally "one who licks") came about: someone who knows only one way with everyone.

There is not just one way to love. True intelligence knows what is right where. The more you want to become **Ganapati**, the lord of the ganas, the higher you want to rise, the more flexible your intellect must become.

## Key Takeaways

- Destiny is pre-written, but the person of medha knows it can be written anew.
- Saying "we are just like this" is the mark of durmedha, which keeps us away from the Guru and from discernment.
- Many obstacles in life come from our own rigidity, because the world and people will always differ.
- Two people in the same room can see different truths, and both can be right.
- Only by standing in another's place and seeing their life do we understand them.
- To rise, keep one goal but a flexible nature.
- See God in all, but treat on merit.
