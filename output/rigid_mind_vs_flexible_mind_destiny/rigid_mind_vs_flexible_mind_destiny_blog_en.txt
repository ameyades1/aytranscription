# A Stubborn Mind or a Flexible Intellect: Who Holds Your Destiny?

## Can You Change Your Destiny?

Acharya Upendra Ji says that a person of **medha** (a deep intellect grounded in the scriptures) thinks things through, because the scriptures have settled within them. Such a person has lived by those teachings, and a **tej** (an inner radiance) has awakened in them.

They know a secret, which Acharya Ji puts this way: destiny is already written, yet you can write it again.

## "This Is Just How We Are": The Mark of a Closed Mind

What does a person of **durmedha** (a dull, closed intellect) say instead? "This is just how we are. This is how things have always been done in our family. Nobody here has ever changed. Show me some other role model, and maybe I will."

Acharya Ji says that such people live far from God and far from the Guru. **Ganapati** (Lord Ganesha, lord of the ganas, the hosts of beings in his care) does not come near them either, because Ganapati stands for **viveka** (discernment) and a steady, settled intellect.

This is where the obstacles in life begin. The world will keep changing, and every person is bound to think differently. Obstacles arise because we are rigid and refuse to budge.

*Is there a sentence in your own life, "I'm just like this," that you have been hiding behind?*

## One Room, Two Truths

Acharya Ji gives a simple example. Two people stand in different spots in the same room, talking to each other.

The first says, "Look, there's a wall, a door, a cupboard, clothes hanging at the back, and tiles on the floor." The second says, "No, look, there's a window, a tree outside, and that curtain."

Who is right? Both of them. The first is describing what lies inside the room, while the second is looking out through the window and the door. **Each is right from where they stand.**

## When Both Refuse to Budge

The trouble begins when both insist, "A person's nature can't change, and I'll stick to what I see." A quarrel is then certain. As Acharya Ji puts it, these two will never come together in this world.

There is one way out. Set your own nature aside for a while and stand where the other person stands. Only then will you see that they were right, and so were you.

## When Will You Look at Their Life?

The example reaches well beyond a room. A poor man says, "Sir, I have to come by train every day, and my wife does this at home." The woman who works in your house says, "Sir, my husband comes home drunk and kicks me." In our pride and the arrogance of our money, we snap back, "How can you not come? I go to the office too, don't I?"

Acharya Ji asks: Have you lived their life? Does anyone kick you? Do they have the money you have? Think how much humiliation they must swallow!

Their mind works from their angle, and yours works from yours. Dismissing someone with "That's just your nature" is a way of refusing to look at their life.

*When did you last write someone off as "full of excuses," and did you truly see the situation they were in?*

## To Rise Higher, Learn to Bend

Anyone who wants to rise, to become a **dharta** (one who holds and supports everyone), a king, an emperor or a senior manager, has to be flexible. They must be able to take on every kind of temperament. Acharya Ji says you can walk through life with one goal, but you cannot get by with one fixed nature.

Keep your goal single, the purpose God has given you, and let your nature adapt to each situation.

## God in Everyone, Conduct According to Each

Acharya Ji's principle is this: see God in everyone, but treat each person according to their capacity.

Practise **bhagavad-darshan** (seeing God in every living being) everywhere, yet do not treat everyone in the same way. I will see God even in a dog, but I will treat the dog in the way that suits a dog.

God lives in the poor too; there you meet **Shani Dev** (Saturn, the planet linked with the poor and the labouring). Treat them well, but in a different way. You will need volunteers to keep order, because Saturn is governed by **Mangal** (Mars), and Mars stands for the police, volunteers and discipline. With that order in place, even people of a lower awareness can eat their meal in peace.

If a revered saint or sadhu of high standing arrives, you need not make them wait in line; their honour and their time call for something different. Everyone gets an excellent meal: the saint, the poor and the dog alike. **God is in every one of them, and each deserves the conduct that suits them.**

## Love Has More Than One Form

With a touch of gentle humour, Acharya Ji asks: am I a dog, to lick everyone the same way? Perhaps that is how the word **chatukar** (a flatterer, literally "one who licks") came about, for someone who knows only one way to treat everyone.

Love has more than one form. True intelligence knows what is fitting in each place. The more you aspire to become a Ganapati yourself, a leader of the many in your care, and the higher you wish to rise, the more flexible your mind must become.

## Key Takeaways

- Destiny is already written, but a person of medha knows it can be rewritten.
- Saying "This is just how we are" is a sign of durmedha, and it keeps you far from the Guru and from discernment.
- Many obstacles in life come from our own rigidity, because the world and its people are bound to differ.
- Two people in the same room can see two different truths, and both can be right.
- You understand others only when you stand in their place and look at their life.
- To rise higher, keep one goal and a flexible nature.
- See God in everyone, but treat each person according to their capacity.
