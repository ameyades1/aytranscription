## 1. Summary

"पितृ दोष" is one of the most searched pitru terms in Hindi, but the results are dominated by astrology sites that sell remedies (upay, puja, gemstones) and lean on fear. This talk answers the question differently. Acharya Ji separates **pitru dosh** (the burden from the ancestors' deeds), **pitru rin** (the debt we owe them) and **pitru labh** (the benefit their good deeds leave us), sets a first rule that your progress is in your own hands, and then shows why one shraddha is not enough: the karma of seven generations, on both the mother's and father's side, across seven lifetimes, a burden "forty-nine times over". The main intent is **informational** ("what is pitru dosh", "why shraddha every year"), with an **emotional** group of readers who suspect their troubles come from their ancestors. The post's opportunity is to be the calm, non-fear answer to "पितृ दोष क्या है" that does not sell remedies. Searches peak before and during Pitru Paksha (Ashvin Krishna Paksha, usually September), so publish or refresh it in August. The H1 is long and poetic; the `<title>` should lead with the keyword.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi (about 50 words)**
आचार्य उपेंद्र जी के अनुसार पितृ दोष पूर्वजों के कर्मों से आया बोझ है, और पितृ ऋण पूर्वजों के प्रति हमारा कर्ज़। हर मनुष्य पर माता और पिता दोनों ओर की सात पीढ़ियों और पिछले सात जन्मों के पितरों का कर्म आता है। इसीलिए एक बार का श्राद्ध पर्याप्त नहीं, शास्त्र हर वर्ष पितृ कर्म करने को कहता है।

**English (about 55 words)**
According to Acharya Upendra Ji, pitru dosh is the burden carried over from the deeds of one's ancestors, while pitru rin is the debt we owe them. Every person carries the karma of seven generations, on both the mother's and the father's side, across seven lifetimes. That is why a single shraddha is not enough, and the scriptures ask for ancestral rites every year.

## 6. FAQ

For the editor's use; not added to the page or to seo.json.

1. **पितृ दोष और पितृ ऋण में क्या अंतर है? / What is the difference between pitru dosh and pitru rin?**
   पितृ दोष पूर्वजों के कर्मों से आया बोझ है; पितृ ऋण वह कर्ज़ है जो हम पर पूर्वजों के प्रति है। आचार्य जी एक तीसरी बात भी बताते हैं, पितृ लाभ: पूर्वजों के अच्छे कर्मों का फल भी मिलता है।
   Pitru dosh is the burden from the ancestors' deeds; pitru rin is the debt we owe them. Acharya Ji adds a third, pitru labh: the good deeds of ancestors bring benefit too.
2. **एक बार श्राद्ध कर दिया, तो हर साल क्यों? / If I have done shraddha once, why every year?**
   आचार्य जी मानते हैं कि यदि कोई पितर मुक्त हो गया तो वह मुक्त है। पर हमें पता नहीं कि पितर कहां-कहां हैं और नए पितर कैसे बनते रहते हैं; पितरों का वृक्ष सात पीढ़ियों और सात जन्मों तक फैला है।
   Acharya Ji agrees that an ancestor who is freed stays free. But we do not know where all our ancestors are, and new ones keep joining; the tree spans seven generations and seven lifetimes.
3. **क्या मेरी हर समस्या पितृ दोष के कारण है? / Is every problem in my life caused by pitru dosh?**
   नहीं। आचार्य जी का पहला नियम है: आपकी प्रगति आपके अपने हाथ में है। आपके अपने कर्म भी हैं; पितरों की बात उसके बाद आती है।
   No. Acharya Ji's first rule is that your progress is in your own hands. You have your own karma; the question of the ancestors comes after that.
4. **क्या पिछले जन्मों के पितर भी हमारे हैं? / Are the ancestors of past lives also ours?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार हां। पिछले जन्मों के पितर भी हमारे ही थे, चाहे वे किसी भी धर्म के रहे हों, और सात जन्मों का सब कुछ हम साथ ले जाते हैं।
   According to Acharya Ji, yes. The ancestors of past lives were ours too, whatever their faith, and we carry everything from seven lifetimes with us.
5. **"उनचास गुना" का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "forty-nine times over" mean?**
   सात पीढ़ियां गुणा सात जन्म। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि यह आरेख ध्यान और समाधि अवस्था में दिखता है, किसी पुस्तक में नहीं मिलता, और यह भी अधूरा है।
   Seven generations times seven lifetimes. Acharya Ji says this diagram is seen in meditation and samadhi, not found in a book, and that even it is incomplete.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Muslim and Christian names attributed to "शास्त्र" / "The scriptures" (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The names are Acharya Ji's own illustration, as the next paragraph says; scripture only teaches rebirth into different families (Gita 2.22, 4.5). Apply the review's correction. In snippets, FAQ and social cards, never quote the names out of context or as "scripture says"; FAQ 4 above keeps it to "whatever their faith", attributed to Acharya Ji.
2. **Shraddha glossed as "the annual rite" in English (review issue 2, Minor, not yet applied).** Shraddha is also done in Pitru Paksha, on amavasya and other occasions. Since "why perform shraddha every year" is a secondary keyword, use the review's broader gloss so the post doesn't define the term too narrowly.
3. **"98 percent of people suffer from pitru dosh".** This is Acharya Ji's statement, not a scriptural or measured figure. Never put "98%" in a title, meta description or social card; it would read as a statistic and as fear-selling. If used, attribute it ("आचार्य जी कहते हैं" / "Acharya Ji says").
4. **"Forty-nine times over" and the seven-lifetimes diagram.** The blog itself says this is seen in meditation, not found in any book. Keep every SEO text attributed to Acharya Ji and never write "scriptures say 49". The seven-generation idea has support in the sapinda rule (Manusmriti 5.60, per the review), but the post does not cite it; add the citation only if the editor wants it.
5. **Fear-driven "pitru dosh" searches.** Most "पितृ दोष" results sell remedies and list "symptoms". This post offers neither, and its first rule is that your progress is in your own hands. Don't target "remedies", "symptoms", "upay" or "lakshan" in titles or meta, and don't suggest that a reader's troubles are proof of pitru dosh. The FAQ (question 3) states the first rule plainly.
6. **Hindi spelling and grammar (review issues 3 to 5, Minor, not yet applied).** "श्रृंखला" → "शृंखला", "गहरे छाप" → "गहरी छाप", "श्राप" → "शाप". The FAQ and snippet above avoid these words.
7. **"गुरुदेव" and "आचार्य जी" used alternately in Hindi (review issue 6, Minor, not yet applied).** All SEO texts use "आचार्य जी" / "आचार्य उपेंद्र जी" for consistent entity naming in search and AI answers; apply the same in the blog.
