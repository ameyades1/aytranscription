# Chapter: Red Flowers and Durva — The Secret of the Symbols in Ganapati Worship


## Introduction: The Message Hidden in the Offerings

Two things are offered in a special way in the worship of Ganapati — red flowers and durva grass. We usually offer them simply as tradition. Acharya Upendra ji explains that both are deep symbols: the red flower stands for the seeker's effort, and durva for their humility and surrender.


## 1. Why Red Is Dear to Ganapati

Acharya ji points to the meditation verse of the Ganapati Atharvashirsha, in which Ganapati is described again and again with the word "rakta" (red):

> **raktaṁ lambodaraṁ śūrpakarṇakaṁ raktavāsasam |**
> **raktagandhānuliptāṅgaṁ raktapuṣpaiḥ supūjitam ||**
> "Red in colour, with a large belly, with ears like winnowing fans, clad in red garments, his limbs anointed with red sandalwood paste, and well worshipped with red flowers."
> — Ganapati Atharvashirsha

> "He likes that red-coloured flower. ... It says rakta, rakta, rakta. That means this is rajas shakti."

*Rajas*, or rajoguna, is the quality that gives action, energy and movement. According to Acharya ji, **the colour red is the symbol of Ganapati's rajas shakti — the energy of action.**


## 2. The Puranic Story and Its Real Meaning

Acharya ji gives the Puranic story like this:

> "After killing the asuras, he smeared their blood all over his body — that is what the Purana says."

But he does not leave the story at its literal meaning:

> "But in reality, it means — I should be ready to work with blood and sweat; only then will you become Vinayaka."

*Vinayaka* is a name of Ganapati — the leader who removes obstacles. Acharya ji's point is that **whoever worships Vinayaka must also be ready to work with full effort.**


## 3. Do Not Fear the Struggle

When the "asuras" of life — difficulties and obstacles — appear, what should the seeker's attitude be? Acharya ji says:

> "To kill the asuras, I will not live in fear."

> "If ever I too go into battle, the only question is whether I will win or lose; both heads are going to be broken, both are going to bleed."

> "That means some shakti is certainly going to be used. Symbolic of that — rajas shakti."

In any struggle, strength will certainly be spent — that much is sure. The only question is who wins. **The red flower is the symbol of this courage and this use of strength.**

> "So all of that is the sadhana of Ganapati. That is why the red flower is symbolic."


## 4. Durva: Beyond the Threes

Now Acharya ji comes to the second symbol, durva. He recalls the lines of the Ganapati Atharvashirsha:

> **tvaṁ guṇatrayātītaḥ tvaṁ avasthātrayātītaḥ |**
> **tvaṁ dehatrayātītaḥ tvaṁ kālatrayātītaḥ ||**
> "You are beyond the three gunas, beyond the three states, beyond the three bodies and beyond the three times."
> — Ganapati Atharvashirsha

> "Durva means, as they say — gunatrayatita, dehatrayatita, kalatrayatita, avasthatrayatita — so there are three, three, three things; durva is the symbol of those three things."

These words mean:
- **Gunatrayatita** — beyond all three gunas: sattva, rajas and tamas
- **Dehatrayatita** — beyond all three bodies: gross, subtle and causal
- **Kalatrayatita** — beyond all three times: past, present and future
- **Avasthatrayatita** — beyond all three states: waking, dreaming and deep sleep

And the feeling with which durva is offered:

> "I keep offering them one by one; as many as I can, I am offering."

**Offering durva is the symbol of laying all these threes, one by one, at Ganapati's feet.**


## 5. The Tree Breaks, Durva Bends

The second message of durva lies in its nature:

> "Durva is like our grass; it is a variety of grass."

> "A big tree breaks because of its ego; and this (durva) — it is said that even when the wind comes, it bends."

In a strong wind, the big, stiff tree breaks, but durva bends and survives. **Ego breaks; humility endures.**

> "So if that is my state, then the energy matches with Ganapati."


## 6. The Science of Energy

Acharya ji says there is not only a feeling behind durva but also a reason in energy:

> "There is a scientific reason for it too — we call it energy, don't we — if you count an energy level like Ganapati's, then by science this (durva) matches it."

That is, **the energy of durva matches the energy of the Ganapati principle**, which is why it suits his worship.


## Summary: Key Teachings

1. **The red flower is the symbol of rajas shakti** — the energy of action that is dear to Ganapati.
2. The real meaning of the Puranic story of the asuras' blood is **readiness to work with blood and sweat** — "only then will you become Vinayaka."
3. Strength will be spent in any struggle; **do not live in fear before obstacles.**
4. **Durva is the symbol of going beyond the threes** — gunatrayatita, dehatrayatita, kalatrayatita, avasthatrayatita — which we offer one by one.
5. **A big tree breaks from ego; durva bends in the wind** — this humble state is what brings us in tune with Ganapati's energy.
6. At the level of energy too, **durva matches the Ganapati principle.**

In Ganapati's worship, red flowers and durva are offered together: one is the resolve of full effort, the other of full humility. May both walk together in the seeker's life too — that is the message of these symbols.
