# Why Lord Ganesha Loves Red Flowers and Durva Grass

All of us offer red flowers and **durva** (a sacred grass with slender blades) when we worship Lord Ganesha. Have you ever wondered what deeper meaning lies behind these offerings? Acharya Upendra Ji opens up the symbolism hidden in this tradition.

## What Does the Colour Red Tell Us?

Lord Ganesha loves red flowers, and red stands for blood. The word for it, "rakta", returns again and again in his meditation verse:

> **raktaṁ lambodaraṁ śūrpakarṇakaṁ raktavāsasam |**
> **raktagandhānuliptāṅgaṁ raktapuṣpaiḥ supūjitam ||**
> "Red in colour, with a large belly and ears broad as winnowing fans, dressed in red, his body anointed with red sandal paste, and worshipped well with red flowers."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha, Dhyana Mantra)

This insistent red points to **rajas shakti** (the energy of action and movement).

## An Old Story, a Lesson for Today

The **Puranas** (ancient texts of sacred stories and legends) tell how Lord Ganesha, after slaying the demons, was covered all over in their blood. Yet the real meaning of the story lies in our own lives. Acharya Ji says that you must be ready to pour your blood and sweat into your work; only then will you become a **Vinayaka** (one who rises above all obstacles, a name of Lord Ganesha).

Worship alone does not make you a Vinayaka. You need to be ready to work hard, with complete dedication.

## Do You Shy Away from Struggle?

When you face the demons in your life, you cannot live in fear. If you ever have to step onto the battlefield, the only question left is whether you will win or lose.

In any battle, both sides get hurt and both sides bleed. Every fight is bound to cost you some of your strength. The red flower stands for that very energy, rajas shakti.

*Which battle in your life are you backing away from out of fear?*

## Durva: Offering Everything That Comes in Threes

Durva represents the sets of three that the scriptures describe:

- **Gunatraya**: the three gunas (qualities of nature)
- **Dehatraya**: the three bodies
- **Kalatraya**: the three times (past, present and future)
- **Avasthatraya**: the three states (waking, dreaming and deep sleep)

As you offer the durva, hold this feeling in your heart: "One by one, I am offering all of these to Lord Ganesha, as much as I can."

## Bending Is What Saves You

Durva is an ordinary grass, yet it carries a great lesson. A mighty tree snaps because of its pride, while durva bows down whenever the wind blows.

When you learn to bend like this, your energy comes into harmony with Lord Ganesha. Acharya Ji explains that there is a science of energy behind this as well: durva matches the energy level of Lord Ganesha.

*Is the "mighty tree" inside you waiting for a storm to break it?*

## Key Takeaways

- The red flower stands for **rajas shakti**: the readiness to act with all your effort.
- The story of Lord Ganesha covered in the demons' blood means pouring your blood and sweat into your work.
- Do not fear struggle; every fight is bound to cost some strength.
- Durva symbolises offering the three gunas, the three bodies, the three times and the three states.
- The humility of the bending grass brings your energy into harmony with Lord Ganesha.
