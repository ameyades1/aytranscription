# Six Hours of Sadhana in Ten Minutes Through Bija Mantras

## Can Spiritual Practice Come in a Capsule?

To understand a **bija mantra** (seed mantra), take a simple example. When you eat spinach, your body gets iron. Yet an iron tablet or tonic gives you the same result.

For vitamin D, you can sit in the sun or take a pill, and the effect is the same. A large meal and a small capsule do the same job.

In just this way, an ordinary person cannot rewrite a lifetime of **samskaras** (deep impressions carried in the mind), nor keep lofty thoughts alive in the mind every hour of the day. That is why the Guru hands them a small **mantra**, a capsule of sorts.

## Which Mantra Are You Repeating Every Day?

The word **mantra** means that which protects the mind as you dwell on it.

> **mananāt trāyate iti mantraḥ**
> "That which protects you when you contemplate it is a mantra."
> (Traditional etymology)

Whatever you hold in your mind again and again turns into your mantra, often without your noticing. Keep thinking "I am poor, I am poor," and that becomes the mantra that keeps you poor. Keep repeating "My husband is no good," and he will never change.

Acharya Ji explains that anything we have held in the mind constantly, such as "I am poor," becomes a mantra in its own right.

A true mantra is one that **frees** the mind from its problems.

*Which sentence did you repeat to yourself most often today? Is it binding you or setting you free?*

## Bija Mantras: The Power Hidden in a Dot

A bija mantra carries a dot, the **anusvara** (the nasal sound marked by a dot above a letter), as in lam, kam and cham. A **sadhana** (spiritual practice) built from such syllables is called **bija mantratmak sadhana** (practice based on seed mantras).

These syllables are known as **nada** (silent sound). Ordinary reasoning cannot grasp their meaning, because they work through the power of sound itself.

## Arjuna and Shri Rama Followed the Same Path

Everyone knows that in the Mahabharata, Shri Krishna gave Arjuna the teaching of the Bhagavad Gita. Before that, though, he had already made it clear: "I will not wield my discus. You must fight this battle yourself."

To fight, you need three things: **strength, wealth and wisdom**. That is why Arjuna was asked to recite the **Durga Saptashati** (seven hundred verses in praise of Maa Durga), and only then did he win the war.

The same was true of Shri Rama. When Ravana carried Mother Sita away to Lanka, Shri Rama turned to the Durga Saptashati as his practice, and through its power he triumphed over Ravana.

## Not an Option but a Necessity

Perhaps you need to rise above great troubles at home or in the nation, or you wish to awaken the **Kundalini shakti** (the dormant spiritual energy). Either way, the Durga Saptashati is not optional. It is essential.

## A Six-Hour Recitation: How Realistic Is It Today?

A single reading of the Sanskrit Durga Saptashati takes at least three hours, and that is when the pronunciation is correct. With **samput** (mantras added before and after each verse), it stretches to five or six hours.

When, then, would anyone perform a **Shatachandi** (a hundredfold recitation of the text) or a **Sahasrachandi** (a thousandfold recitation)? And when would the rest of life's work get done? After observing many institutions and speaking with Brahmin priests, it became clear that this approach could not continue.

## The Answer for Kali Yuga: A Tantric Practice of Seed Mantras

Tradition holds that in **Kali Yuga** (the present dark age), the **yantras** (sacred geometric diagrams) and **tantras** (esoteric ritual methods) suited to this age are what will work. With this in mind, **Gurudev** (the revered Guru), in a state of meditation, composed a **tantrokta** (prescribed by the tantras) bija mantratmak sadhana for the seven hundred verses of the Durga Saptashati.

According to Gurudev, he did not borrow this practice from anyone; it came to him through divine inspiration. Hardly anyone today even understands bija mantras, and the ability to compose them is rarer still.

Acharya Ji points out that other Gurus give initiation, but they cannot create a mantra.

Such a composition does not spring from a passing thought. It comes from a **siddhi** (a spiritual attainment). The result is a very short mantra that should still bring the full **phalashruti** (the fruit promised by a practice).

## Here and Now

Ten or twenty minutes of this practice should yield the fruit of six hours of worship. That is its promise.

Acharya Ji says that he believes in the here and now.

*Have you kept putting off your sadhana for lack of time? What if ten minutes were enough?*

## Key Takeaways

- A bija mantra works like a capsule: small, yet it delivers the full result.
- The thought you hold in your mind again and again becomes your mantra.
- A true mantra frees the mind from its problems.
- Arjuna and Shri Rama both practised the Durga Saptashati before their victories.
- The Durga Saptashati is essential for overcoming great problems and awakening the Kundalini.
- The tantric bija mantra sadhana was composed to give the fruit of a six-hour recitation in ten to twenty minutes.
