## 1. Summary

Acharya Ji describes three kinds of disciples by what they ask the Guru for: the tamasic one wants something done for them right now, the rajasic one wants only peace of mind, and the sattvic one asks "where did I go wrong?". Only that question brings knowledge, and only knowledge resolves karma for good; outer remedies, like medicine that does not change the habit behind an illness, let the problem return. The talk is illustrated with Nachiketa (Katha Upanishad), Arjuna's curse turning into a blessing, and Bhagavad Gita 2.47. The main intent is informational with a strong self-reflection angle ("what kind of disciple am I?", "what should I ask a Guru?"). The opportunity: "types of disciples" and "शिष्य के प्रकार" queries are served mostly by short listicles and generic quotes; a clear three-gunas framework tied to the one question a seeker asks is a distinctive, snippet-friendly answer. The Nachiketa boons passage has a critical accuracy issue (flag 1), so the boons, pitru dosha and Shri Vidya are kept out of titles, keywords and snippets.

4. **क्या शुभ कर्म का अशुभ फल मिल सकता है?** नहीं। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि शुभ कर्म का शुभ और अशुभ कर्म का अशुभ फल निश्चित है; अन्याय लगे तो देखिए कि कर्म ही कहां गलत था।

**English**
1. **What are the three types of disciples?** Tamasic, rajasic and sattvic. A Guru can tell which one you are from a single sentence you speak.
2. **What should you ask a Guru for?** Knowledge, not a remedy. The sattvic disciple asks, "Where did I go wrong?"
3. **Why do outer remedies let problems return?** Because the effect is removed from the outside while we keep doing the same thing inside; without understanding, nothing changes.
4. **Can a good action bring a bad result?** No. Acharya Ji says good actions bring good results and wrong actions painful ones; when life feels unfair, look honestly at where the action itself went wrong.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Nachiketa's three boons (critical, from the Sanskrit review).** The blog says Nachiketa was freed from pitru dosha and received Shri Vidya. In the Katha Upanishad the boons are: his father's anger calmed (1.1.10), the knowledge of the Nachiketa fire (1.1.13-19), and the knowledge of the Self (1.1.20 on). Recommendation: apply the review's correction, which states the text and then frames the pitru dosha / Shri Vidya link as Acharya Ji's reading. Until then, keep these terms out of titles, keywords, FAQ and snippets (done here), and do not add pitru dosha or Shri Vidya internal links on that passage.
2. **Gita 2.47 Hindi meaning (major).** "फल देना तुम्हारे वश में नहीं" changes "मा फलेषु कदाचन" and differs from the English. Recommendation: "तुम्हारा अधिकार केवल कर्म करने में है, उसके फलों में कभी नहीं।" (Gita Press sense).
3. **Context of Katha Upanishad 1.1.5.** Nachiketa reflects at home on hearing his father, not on the way to Yama. Recommendation: "पिता के वचन सुनकर नचिकेता मन में यही सोचता है ..." / "on hearing his father's words, Nachiketa reflects ...", and frame "nothing wrong can happen to me" as Acharya Ji's interpretation.
4. **Half verses not marked.** Both 1.1.5 and 2.47 quote only the first half; the Hindi meaning of 1.1.5 drops "एमि". Recommendation: add "पूर्वार्ध" / "first half" and the review's Hindi meaning.
5. **Nachiketa's age.** "Twelve" is not in the Upanishad (it says कुमार). Recommendation: "आचार्य जी के अनुसार बारह वर्ष का" / "twelve, as Acharya Ji tells it". For SEO, don't put "12-year-old Nachiketa" in titles or snippets.
6. **Sattva's four states and spelling.** The list ज्ञान, सुख, वैराग्य, चैतन्य is the speaker's teaching, not a scriptural list (the Gita links sattva with sukha, jnana and prakasha, 14.6, 14.9, 14.11). Frame it as "according to Acharya Ji". Gita Press spelling is "सात्त्विक / सत्त्वगुण"; the keywords and meta here already use "सात्त्विक".
7. **Medical sensitivity.** "However much chemotherapy you go through, the cancer will keep growing" could read as advice against treatment. Recommendation: the review's wording ("outer treatment alone is not enough; understanding must come along with it"). The FAQ and direct answer here avoid any medical claim.
8. **Speaker naming in Hindi.** "गुरुदेव" is used for both the speaker and any guru, alongside "आचार्य जी". Recommendation: use "आचार्य जी" for the speaker for consistent entity naming (helps AI search attribution); "कठोपनिषद्" consistently.
9. **SEO pressure: pitru dosha and Shri Vidya.** These are high-volume queries and would be tempting to target from this post, but the post's claim on them is the flagged one (1). Recommendation: don't target them here; the dedicated talks already cover them.
