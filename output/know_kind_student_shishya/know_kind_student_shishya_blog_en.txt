# What Kind of Disciple Are You? Three Ways to Approach the Guru

When life turns painful, most of us go to someone for help: a Guru, an astrologer, a guide of some kind. But have you ever stopped to notice *what you actually ask for* when you get there?

Acharya Ji says there are three kinds of disciples: **tamasic, rajasic and sattvic**. A Guru can tell which kind you are from a single sentence you speak.

## The First Disciple: "Please Do Something for Me"

The **tamasic** (driven by inertia) disciple comes to the Guru and pleads, "Gurudev, please do something for me." Or else asks, "What should I do?"

This person is still stuck at the level of the body and outward action. They believe only when something is visibly "done". They want a miracle cure, some remedy, and they want it right now.

Acharya Ji points out that every Guru feels, deep down, that such a person should be sent away. Even so, the Guru still offers an answer.

## The Second Disciple: Just Give Me Peace of Mind

The **rajasic** (restless, driven by desire) seeker has no interest in solving the problem. Whatever is going to happen can happen. All they want is peace of mind.

They ask, "There is fighting at home. Should I go and sit in another room? Should I chant a mantra? Is there something I should do?" That is the whole extent of their search.

*Pause and ask yourself: the last time I went to a guide, was I one of these first two?*

## The Third Disciple: "Where Did I Go Wrong?"

In the **sattvic** (pure, driven by clarity) disciple, knowledge begins to dawn. **Sattva guna** (the quality of purity and light) brings four states with it: **knowledge, happiness, vairagya** (dispassion) and **chaitanya** (awakened awareness).

Such a disciple goes to the Guru and says only this: "Gurudev, I am going through hardship, which means the fault must lie somewhere in my past actions and the **samskaras** (deep impressions) they left behind. Please tell me where I went wrong."

They do not blame the world, and they do not keep count of other people's mistakes. Their journey begins with **acceptance**: the mistake is mine.

## Knowledge, Not Medicine, Dissolves Karma

The moment the Guru shows you your mistake, you understand it, and the karma resolves **for good**. A temporary medicine or a passing treatment only lets the problem come back.

Acharya Ji gives the example of a doctor. After a heart bypass, or with insulin taken day after day, either the dose keeps rising or a heart attack eventually comes. The illness returns.

Why? The doctor cut away the effects of your actions from the outside, while inside you went on doing exactly the same things. Nothing changed, because you never learned anything.

However much chemotherapy you go through, Acharya Ji says, the cancer will keep growing. If it is to leave you, understanding has to come.

## Nachiketa: The Ideal Disciple at Twelve

The Katha Upanishad tells the story of Nachiketa, a boy of only twelve whose own father gave him away to Death. It was a terrible thing to happen.

Even so, as Nachiketa goes to **Yamaraja** (the lord of death), he holds on to one thought: if I am a true disciple, nothing wrong can happen to me. Even if the outcome looks bad, some good must be hidden inside it.

> **bahūnām emi prathamo bahūnām emi madhyamaḥ |**
> "Among many I go as the first; among many I go as one of the middle."
> (Katha Upanishad 1.1.5)

Nachiketa reasons that he is, at the very least, a middling disciple; of that much he is sure. And with that faith, no harm can come to him.

## When a Curse Becomes a Blessing

The celestial nymph **Urvashi** once cursed Arjuna to lose his manhood. Yet during the Pandavas' year of living in disguise, that very curse turned into a blessing: it let him stay hidden as **Brihannala**, a eunuch dance teacher at the king's court.

When hardship falls on a good, sattvic person, it too turns into a blessing at the right time.

## The Unfailing Law of Karma

Where there is action, there will be a result. Wrong actions bring painful results, and good actions bring good ones; this is certain. God never commits the injustice of rewarding a wrong deed or punishing a good one.

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |**
> "You have a right to your actions alone, never to their fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

Why worry about the result, then? Get the action right first.

## When Does Something Really Feel Like a Problem?

Acharya Ji offers a simple reality check. When a bad deed brings a bad result, it doesn't feel like a problem. When a good deed brings a good result, it doesn't either.

The trouble begins when we feel we did something truly good and still got a painful result. That is when we feel life has treated us unfairly. But once you see that the action itself was wrong, you can correct it, and the problem will go. It really is that simple.

*Is there an "injustice" in my life where I have never honestly looked at my own part in it?*

## What a Sattvic Question Brings

The sattvic disciple asks, "What does this mean? Where did I go wrong in understanding it? Or was what I did wrong in the first place?" They ask for knowledge, not a remedy.

Nachiketa went to Yamaraja in exactly this spirit, and he received three boons. He was freed from **pitru dosha** (an affliction linked to one's father and ancestors). He received the knowledge of **Shri Vidya** (the sacred tradition of the Divine Mother), which brings power, wealth and **viveka** (discernment). And he was taught the knowledge of liberation, **moksha** (freedom from the cycle of birth and death).

## Key Takeaways

- The tamasic disciple says "do something for me" and wants an instant remedy.
- The rajasic disciple wants peace of mind, not a solution.
- The sattvic disciple asks "where did I go wrong?" and never blames the world.
- Outer treatment is temporary; without knowledge, karma returns.
- Good actions bring good results and wrong actions painful ones; your right is to the action alone.
- For a good person, even a curse becomes a blessing at the right time, as it did for Arjuna.
- Like Nachiketa, those who ask for knowledge are freed from pitru dosha and receive Shri Vidya and the knowledge of liberation.
