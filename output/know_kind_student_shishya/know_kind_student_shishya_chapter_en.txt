# Chapter: What Kind of Disciple Are You? The Tamasic, Rajasic and Sattvic Disciple

## The Three Kinds of Disciple

Disciples are of three kinds: those who come from a sattvic, a rajasic or a tamasic disposition. Choose for yourself which of these you are. Any guru can recognise what kind of disciple you are.

## The Tamasic Disciple: "Please Do Something for Me"

If you go to a guru, or to an astrologer, and on your way out you say, "Gurudev, please do something for me," or "What should I do?", such a person is called tamasic. This is the first disciple. Every guru feels deep within that this one should be sent away, yet outwardly he will still give an answer.

Why? Because this person is still stuck on the body, stuck on doing something. Unless some action is performed, he does not believe. He says, "Do something for me, solve my problem, give me a sure-shot remedy, give me a solution, and give it at once."

## The Rajasic Disciple: Seeking Only Peace of Mind

The second seeker is called the rajasic seeker. He does not want a solution to the problem. Let happen whatever is to happen; let happen whatever is to happen to the enemy. He will say, "I need something for my peace of mind. There is a quarrel at home; should I go and sit in another room? Should I chant some mantra? Should I do something?" That is all he seeks.

This is the second kind of disciple, the rajasic disciple. Check for yourself that you are not one of these first two.

## The Sattvic Disciple: "Where Did I Go Wrong?"

The third is called the sattvic disciple. In sattva guna, knowledge dawns. Knowledge, joy, detachment and awareness: these four states come in sattva guna.

The sattvic disciple goes to the guru and says only this: "Gurudev, suffering is coming. If suffering is coming, it means that it must be the fault of my past karma and its impressions. Please tell me where I went wrong."

He does not blame the world. He does not say where the other person went wrong, nor does he ask, "What should I do?" He asks, "Where did I go wrong?" That is, first comes acceptance, that the mistake is mine.

## Temporary Relief and Permanent Resolution

As soon as the guru points out the mistake, that is, as soon as knowledge is gained, your karma is resolved permanently. With a temporary medicine or a temporary healing, the problem comes back.

The doctor treats you, doesn't he? When the doctor has treated your heart, done a bypass, done everything, or keeps giving insulin, either the dose keeps increasing, or an attack comes again. The illness relapses.

Why does this happen? The doctor has cut your karma from the outside, but on the inside you keep doing the same karma. There is no improvement; knowledge has not come at all. So that punishment will certainly return after some time. That is why it relapses. However much chemotherapy you do, the cancer keeps growing.

We will also talk later about how healing comes. But this much is certain: if you want to be released from karma, knowledge must come.

## Nachiketa: An Example of the Best Disciple

So go to any guru the way the Katha Upanishad describes. When Nachiketa goes to Yamaraj, he is only a twelve-year-old boy. At twelve, one should have such understanding!

His father had given him away to Death, that is, an inauspicious event had taken place. Even so, he says only this: "If I am a good disciple, nothing wrong can happen to me. Even if the result looks wrong, the wrong is not mine. In what looks wrong, something good must be hidden." He knows he is a good student; he is sattvic.

## Arjuna's Curse That Became a Boon

Just as Arjuna was good. Arjuna had been cursed by Urvashi that he would become impotent. Later that same curse became a boon. During the year of living in disguise he became Brihannala. That much of the Mahabharata everyone knows.

So when a person is good, even his curse turns into a boon at the right time. However much suffering comes to a good, sattvic person, some good lies hidden in it.

## "At Least I Am a Middling Disciple"

Nachiketa knew that he was a good disciple. He says that at least he is a middling disciple; of that much he is sure.

> **bahūnām emi prathamo bahūnām emi madhyamaḥ**
> "Among many I go as the first; among many I go as the middle."
> (Katha Upanishad 1.1.5)

And if he is sure of this, then nothing bad can happen to him. Even if something bad appears today, some good must be hidden in it. This is the state of the sattvic disciple. He goes to the guru and says only this: "Where did I go wrong? Or what fruit will this bring in the future? Just tell me that much."

## The Unfailing Law of Karma and Its Fruit

Understand an important point. You performed some action, auspicious or inauspicious; will it bear some fruit or not? Where there is karma, there will be the fruit of karma. And the fruit will be according to whether the karma was auspicious or inauspicious.

Will it ever happen that inauspicious karma gives an auspicious fruit, or auspicious karma an inauspicious one? God does not commit such injustice. Inauspicious karma gives inauspicious fruit and auspicious karma gives auspicious fruit; this is certain.

Whether it is Shripad Shrivallabh Datta, or anyone else, or Shri Krishna himself, who says of himself "I am Krishna himself", he says:

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> "Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

Do your karma. Your right is in doing the action; giving its fruit is not within your power. It is not your choice. Then why worry about it? First do your karma rightly.

## When Good Karma Seems to Bring a Bad Fruit

I must have done some karma or other. I am giving you a reality check. If you believe you have done very well, the fruit too will be good. If the fruit has turned out bad, look for the answer: perhaps I did bad karma. If you get the answer that yes, I did bad karma, correct it and it will go. It is very easy.

When you did bad karma and got a bad fruit, it does not feel like a problem. When you did good karma and got a good fruit, it does not feel like a problem either. Do you know when the problem comes? When you feel you have done very auspicious work and the fruit was inauspicious. Then you feel you have suffered an injustice.

## The Sattvic Disciple's Question and Its Fruit

In such a situation, the sattvic disciple goes to the guru and asks just this: "What could this mean? For what reason does this happen?" He tries to gain knowledge. "Where did I go wrong in interpreting this? Or was my karma itself wrong?"

When Nachiketa goes to Yamaraj in this spirit, he receives three boons. His pitru dosha (ancestral affliction) is removed. After that he receives the knowledge of Shri Vidya, which brings power, wealth and wisdom, and he also receives the knowledge of liberation.

This is the sattvic disciple.
