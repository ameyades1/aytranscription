# Chapter: Vṛtti and Pravṛtti: The Yoga of Steadying a Changing Mind

## What Is Yoga?

Yoga has been defined like this:

> **yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ**
> "Yoga is the stilling of the modifications of the mind."
> (Patanjali Yoga Sutra 1.2)

What is yoga? The *nirodha* of the *vṛttis* of the *citta*. Your mind, intellect and ego all come within the *citta*. Below the *citta* is the *mahat-tattva*, that is, the intellect; that *vṛttis* do not arise in it, this is yoga. *Nirodha* means to stop, to come to rest. So what is to stop? It is the *vṛtti* that is asked to stop.

## Vṛtti: The Mind and Intellect Changing Again and Again

*Vṛtti* means the changes that happen in the mind or the intellect. Now what does change mean here? It does not mean "do not learn anything new". It means that today there is one belief, tomorrow another, the day after a third; a person does not hold on to anything steadily.

You went to the market to buy some things, and there you saw four more things, so the mind says, "Wow, I will take this too, I will take that too." Now your mind has become that. You went to scold someone, you went to do some work; on the way you saw another girl, and you went after her. You forgot what work you had set out to do. A person can do all of this, can't he? He forgot his very purpose. So what is all this? Is this not a modification of the mind and intellect?

Before marriage he said, "I will love you like this and like this, I will do this, I will do that." Later he forgot it all. Something new came in: "Oh, there is so much work." Then why did you give the promise at all? This became your *vṛtti*.

## Pravṛtti: When a Vṛtti Becomes One's Nature

And when this *vṛtti* happens *prakarṣeṇa*, that is, intensely, it is called *pravṛtti*. When it is said, "His *pravṛtti* is to steal," what does it mean? As soon as he sees some money or some object, he simply has to steal it. His mind changes automatically, instantly.

He sees a woman, and it is said, "His *pravṛtti* is to harass women." What does it mean? As soon as he sees a woman, his mind changes at once. Someone has a *pravṛtti* for drinking liquor. Is it becoming clear now?

Did you understand the difference between *vṛtti* and *pravṛtti*? *Vṛtti* means a change of the mind. Everything was going fine until now; as soon as something was seen, the mind changed immediately. And if it has become a *pravṛtti*, an intense *vṛtti*, then understand that it has become his habit. He was fine a moment ago, he saw something and changed at once; this is the *pravṛtti* of stealing. Someone has a *pravṛtti* of speaking the truth. Whoever's *pravṛtti* it is, it is a modification of the intellect, and the intellect changes accordingly at once.

## Steadiness Is the Practice

So what have we been told? Not to change, to keep the mind steady in one place. If you have a *pravṛtti*, it too should be kept just as steady. Then it should not keep changing again and again.
