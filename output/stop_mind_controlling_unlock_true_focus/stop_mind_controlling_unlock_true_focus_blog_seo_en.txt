# A Mind That Chases Every New Thing: Vritti, Pravritti and the Yoga of Steadiness

Have you ever set out to do one thing and come home having done something else entirely? This small, familiar experience connects to the deepest principle of yoga. Let us understand, in the words of Acharya Upendra Ji, why the mind keeps changing, and why steadying it is what yoga is.

In short, the chitta vritti nirodha meaning is this: yoga is the stilling of the vrittis, the changes that keep arising in the mind and intellect. When a vritti grows strong, it becomes a pravritti, a habit. The practice is to hold the mind steady in one place.

## What Is Yoga, Really?

Maharshi Patanjali defined yoga in a single line:

> **yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ**
> "Yoga is the stilling of the modifications of the mind."
> (Yoga Sutras 1.2)

**Chitta** (the field of consciousness) holds everything: the mind, the intellect and the ego. Acharya Ji explains that at the root of chitta lies **mahattattva** (the cosmic intellect), that is, buddhi. Yoga means no vrittis arise there.

**Nirodha** (stilling) means to stop, to come to rest. And what must come to rest? The vritti.

## What Is a Vritti? A Mind That Keeps Shifting

**Vritti** (a modification of the mind) refers to the changes that keep arising in the mind or intellect. Keep in mind, though, that this does not mean you should never learn anything new.

It means holding one belief today, another tomorrow and a third the day after. The mind never holds on to any one thing steadily.

Acharya Ji gives a simple example. You go to the market to buy something, notice four other things there, and the mind says, "Oh, let me get this too, and that as well." In that moment, the mind has turned into those things.

You set out to scold someone, or to get a task done, and on the way you notice some girl and start following her. You forget the very task you left home for.

When you have forgotten your purpose itself, Acharya Ji asks, what is all this? Aren't these the mind's modifications?

Here is one more example that many may recognise in their own lives. Before marriage, you promised, "I will love you like this, I will do this for you, I will do that." Afterwards you forgot all of it, because something new came along: "Oh, there's so much work to do."

Acharya Ji asks why you made the promise in the first place. This forgetting became your vritti.

*How many times today did you abandon something you had decided to do because something new caught your eye?*

## Pravritti: When a Fluctuation Becomes a Habit

Now take it one step further. When a vritti starts occurring **prakarshena** (forcefully, with great intensity), it is called a **pravritti** (a deep-set tendency).

When people say, "Stealing is in his nature," they mean that the moment he sees money or some object, he cannot help but steal it. His mind changes instantly, on its own.

In the same way, some people have a tendency to harass women: they see a woman and their mind changes at once. Others have a tendency to drink.

Acharya Ji explains that once a vritti becomes a pravritti, a powerful vritti, you should understand that it has become that person's habit.

A pravritti is not always bad, however. Some people have a tendency to speak the truth. Whatever the tendency, it is still a change in the intellect, and the intellect shifts instantly to match it.

*Which tendency within you transforms you the moment you see a certain thing?*

## Steadiness: The Real Practice of Yoga

What, then, is the **sadhaka** (spiritual seeker) asked to do? **Do not keep changing; hold the mind steady in one place.**

And if you do have a tendency, hold that steady as well. It should not keep shifting again and again. This is the first step towards chitta-vritti-nirodha.

## Key Takeaways

- Yoga is defined as **yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ**: the stilling of the mind's modifications.
- Chitta holds the mind, intellect and ego; nirodha means coming to rest.
- **Vritti** is the constant shifting of mind and intellect: one belief today and another tomorrow, a forgotten purpose, a broken promise.
- When a vritti grows strong, it becomes a **pravritti**, a habit that changes the mind the instant something appears.
- A pravritti can be good or bad, but every pravritti is a change in the intellect.
- The essence of **sadhana** (spiritual practice) is **steadiness**: not letting the mind shift, and holding it still in one place.
