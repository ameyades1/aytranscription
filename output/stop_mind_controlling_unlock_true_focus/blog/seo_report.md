## 1. Summary

A short, focused teaching on Patanjali's definition of yoga, योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः (Yoga Sutra 1.2). Acharya Upendra Ji explains chitta, nirodha and vritti through everyday examples (going to the market for one thing and coming back with four, forgetting a promise made before marriage), then shows how a strong vritti hardens into a pravritti, a habit that changes the mind the moment its object appears, and ends with the practice: hold the mind steady in one place. The main intent is informational ("chitta vritti nirodha meaning", "योग की परिभाषा"), with a strong emotional and practical edge from people asking why their mind keeps wandering or changing. The definition query is served mostly by dry word-by-word glosses on yoga sites; the opportunity is the one angle this talk owns: vritti explained as the mind forgetting its own purpose, and the vritti → pravritti step that explains habits. The broader "how to control the mind" and "steady mind" queries already belong to two other talks, so this post targets the sutra itself.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi (49 words):** चित्त वृत्ति निरोध का अर्थ है चित्त में उठने वाले परिवर्तनों का रुक जाना। महर्षि पतंजलि योगसूत्र १.२ में इसी को योग कहते हैं। आचार्य उपेंद्र जी समझाते हैं कि वृत्ति वह है जब मन आज एक बात, कल दूसरी पकड़ ले और अपना उद्देश्य भूल जाए; योग का अभ्यास है मन को एक जगह स्थिर रखना।

**English (55 words):** Chitta vritti nirodha means the stilling of the modifications of the mind. In Yoga Sutra 1.2, Maharshi Patanjali defines this stilling as yoga itself. Acharya Upendra Ji explains a vritti as the mind's constant shifting: one belief today, another tomorrow, a purpose forgotten. When a vritti grows strong it becomes a pravritti, a habit. Yoga is holding the mind steady.

## 6. FAQ

**1. योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः का अर्थ क्या है? / What does yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ mean?**
चित्त की वृत्तियों का निरोध ही योग है (पातंजल योगसूत्र १.२)। चित्त में मन, बुद्धि और अहंकार आते हैं, और निरोध का अर्थ है रुक जाना। / Yoga is the stilling of the modifications of the mind (Yoga Sutra 1.2). Chitta holds the mind, intellect and ego; nirodha means coming to rest.

**2. वृत्ति क्या है? / What is a vritti?**
आचार्य जी के अनुसार वृत्ति मन और बुद्धि का बार-बार बदलना है: आज एक मान्यता, कल दूसरी, किसी काम से निकलना और बीच में उद्देश्य भूल जाना। इसका अर्थ यह नहीं कि नया सीखना छोड़ दें। / In Acharya Ji's explanation, a vritti is the mind and intellect shifting again and again: one belief today and another tomorrow, setting out for one task and forgetting it on the way. It does not mean you should stop learning new things.

**3. वृत्ति और प्रवृत्ति में क्या अंतर है? / What is the difference between vritti and pravritti?**
जब कोई वृत्ति प्रबल रूप से (प्रकर्षेण) होने लगे, तो वह प्रवृत्ति, यानी आदत बन जाती है, जो कोई वस्तु दिखते ही मन को तुरंत बदल देती है। / When a vritti starts occurring with great intensity (prakarshena), it becomes a pravritti, a habit that changes the mind the instant its object appears.

**4. क्या हर प्रवृत्ति बुरी होती है? / Is every pravritti bad?**
नहीं। किसी की सत्य बोलने की प्रवृत्ति भी होती है। पर अच्छी हो या बुरी, हर प्रवृत्ति बुद्धि का परिवर्तन ही है। / No. Some people have a tendency to speak the truth. Good or bad, every pravritti is still a change in the intellect.

**5. साधक को क्या करना चाहिए? / What should a seeker do?**
मन को बदलने न दें, एक जगह स्थिर रखें; और जो प्रवृत्ति है, उसे भी बार-बार बदलने न दें। यही चित्तवृत्तिनिरोध की ओर पहला कदम है। / Do not let the mind keep changing; hold it steady in one place, and keep even your tendencies from shifting again and again. This is the first step towards chitta-vritti-nirodha.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Mahattattva "beneath" chitta (from the Sanskrit review, Minor).** The blog says chitta includes buddhi, then that buddhi (mahattattva) lies "beneath" chitta, which reads as contradictory; in Sankhya order mahat is the root of the inner instrument. Recommendation: the editor applies the review's correction ("चित्त के मूल में" / "at the root of chitta", attributed to Acharya Ji). The direct answer, FAQ and meta above avoid the mahattattva sentence entirely.
2. **Name of the text (from the review, Minor).** Hindi cites "पतंजलि योगसूत्र १.२" (Gita Press title: पातञ्जलयोगदर्शन; review suggests "पातंजल योगसूत्र १.२"), and the English drops Patanjali ("Yoga Sutras 1.2"). Recommendation: apply the review's corrections. The FAQ and schema use "पातंजल योगसूत्र"; the tag stays "पतंजलि योगसूत्र" / "Patanjali Yoga Sutras" to match the existing tag on other posts, since that is how readers search.
3. **Vritti is the speaker's explanation, not the sutra's list.** Yoga Sutra 1.5–1.6 lists five vrittis (pramana, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidra, smriti); the talk explains vritti through everyday distraction and changing beliefs. Recommendation: in snippets and FAQ, attribute this as Acharya Ji's explanation (done above: "आचार्य जी के अनुसार" / "In Acharya Ji's explanation"), and never present it as the classical definition.
4. **The video promises "the secret to true focus".** The talk gives the definition and the first step (steadiness), not a concentration technique. Recommendation: titles and meta do not promise a focus method or quick results; keep "focus" only as a tag.
5. **Sensitive examples.** The pravritti examples include a tendency to harass women and following "some girl" on the way. They illustrate compulsion, not approval, but should not be lifted out of context into quote cards or social snippets. Recommendation: use the market or the promise-before-marriage example for social snippets instead.
