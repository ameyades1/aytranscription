# Chapter: The Three Kinds of Remover

## The Meaning of Hartā

> **tvameva kevalaṁ kartāsi. tvameva kevalaṁ dhartāsi. tvameva kevalaṁ hartāsi.**
> "You alone are the doer. You alone are the sustainer. You alone are the remover."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha)

That is: O Ganapati, you alone are the doer, you alone are the one who upholds, and you alone are the one who takes away.

*Hartā* means the one who removes, the one who takes something away. One who takes, who destroys, who breaks something is a *hartā*. This is the very work of Mahesh: to take away, to destroy, to break.

But the question is: for what purpose is it done? Should destruction be constructive or destructive? Remember, **violence done to establish non-violence is itself called non-violence.**

## Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj: The Sattvic Remover

Such a remover also takes things away. He too takes away someone's peace and comfort. Whose? The terrorists'.

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj launched a full attack and drove out all the Mughals. What was this? It was removal. But what did he remove? He removed the sorrow and suffering of his own people, and perhaps also removed the arrogance of those facing him, the Mughals. Behind all of this was the aim of establishing *swarajya*, self-rule.

You will say there was so much bloodshed in it. But here the need was self-defence, because we had not invited them. The Mughals or the British did not come in the spirit of "atithi devo bhava," the guest is God. They came to attack you, to snatch away your self-respect. They did not understand by persuasion or by inducement, so here killing them was the only proper course. When someone starts claiming rights over your own land, you have to drive him out.

If someone forced his way into your home, went straight into the kitchen, opened your cupboard and started taking money, would you drive him out or not?

That was the work. He too removed, but he was a sattvic remover. And when he ruled from Raigad, that same Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was the upholder, a sattvic upholder. His effort was that everyone should receive justice and respect, and women should be honoured. This he did continuously. And when he fought or undertook any work, then he was the doer, a sattvic doer.

You alone are the sattvic doer, and by your own intellect you alone are the upholder, because you held such steadfastness. Therefore it was you who removed. What did you remove? You became the remover of obstacles. He removed the obstacles of many, and his own as well.

## Who Is the Best Remover?

The one who removes obstacles is the best. Which remover is the best? The one who removes people's obstacles, and "people" includes oneself.

That is why it is said of Ganapati, "tvameva kevalaṁ hartāsi." But the remover of what? The remover of obstacles. If you can remove the obstacles of others, and along with them those of your own life, whether of health, of wealth or of relationships, then you are a true remover.

The Guru takes even the *halāhala* poison upon himself to free you from it. He removes everything, from your illnesses to whatever faults you have.

## Dushasana: The Tamasic Remover

Now look at a second example, Dushasana. One who does not rule rightly is Dushasana. He stripped Draupadi of her garment.

Do you see the difference between the two? He too is removing, but he is bringing obstacles, for his own pleasure, or his own prestige, or for whatever he wants. He too removed something, but he sought to violate a woman's modesty. He sought to plunder the dignity and honour a woman has. He is removing the wrong thing, so he is a **tamasic remover**.

And the **rajasic remover** will also do something, but he will do it for his own gain.

## The Example of Cleaning: Three Kinds of Remover

With one small example we will complete the subject of the remover. Understanding the remover is very simple.

Suppose a village has to be cleaned: a cleanliness drive, a clean city drive, a clean river drive for some river, or a garden or an area has to be cleaned. I am not talking about the thieves' kind of "cleaning out" here. You will ask, "Guruji, not that kind of cleaning?" No, here cleaning means actual cleaning. For such work there are three kinds of remover.

## The Sattvic Remover

The first is the genuine remover, as we do in Antar Yog. Sometimes, to relieve people's sorrow and suffering, everyone goes for *mahādān*, the great giving, or joins some such programme. Come, today the beach has to be cleaned.

Such a person goes with a truly selfless spirit. If he gets honour, fine; if not, it makes no difference. If someone took a photo and it appeared in the newspaper the next day, he does not care about that. His feeling is: this is my nation, my city, it should stay pure. So many people will come here, tourists will come, how bad it will look to them.

Everyone is throwing litter, so he will scold them too: "Hey, don't throw litter!" He will put up a notice board as well, because if he is an upholder he will do all of that. But he himself will also clean as part of the drive.

Did he remove all the litter or not? He removed the inconvenience people were facing, the illnesses that could have spread; he removed the obstacle. He made that beach pure. This is the **sattvic remover**.

## The Rajasic Remover

Now the second kind of people. That day some organisation arrived. Everyone is wearing the same cap, the organisation's shirt, and this second group has come bringing television crews, photographers, social media, all of it.

They have probably never picked up a broom in their lives, but that day all of them are wearing reflective strips, with big banners and boards: "Clean India, clean this, clean that." Is this a rajasic remover or not?

"Look how much litter we picked up!" A photo holding a bag: "Carrying garbage, feeling happy," and Facebook likes being counted. Oh, he has never once swept his own home in his life, but he will surely go to such a big event.

He too is a remover. He too has removed litter and given people convenience. But what is his motive? What is his purpose? Tell me. His purpose is that my photo should appear, that I should be seen on television everywhere. He removed, yes, but for whom?

## The Tamasic Remover

And what will the tamasic person be like? Suppose there is a government employee being forced to do the work, or someone on whom labour has simply landed, he will get it done somehow. He took the litter out of his own house and dumped it in front of his neighbour's house. Is this tamasic or not?

The tamasic person will finish the work any which way, half-done, in a hurry, and it will have to be done again tomorrow. The potholes in the road stay as they are and will break open again every rainy season; he will patch them up and leave them like that. The quality of his work will be the very lowest, and the problem too will go away only for a while. He has no wish at all for the problem to go. "It is how it is," that is what he has accepted. All his work stays like this, because that is his nature.

He too removed, temporarily, but he is careless and lazy: if it happened, it happened, what difference does it make? No improvement, and he will not adopt any new system. He does not even care about photos. On top of that, he will threaten you. If you go to tell him that this is not how it should be done, he will say, "You do it then, sir, do this, do that," and hurl abuse at you.

## Conclusion

So have you understood all three removers? The three kinds of remover: the sattvic, who selflessly removes the obstacles of all and of himself; the rajasic, who removes but for his own name and gain; and the tamasic, who removes half-heartedly or removes the wrong thing.
