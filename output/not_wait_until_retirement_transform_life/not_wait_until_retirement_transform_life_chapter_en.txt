# Chapter: Do Not Wait for Retirement, Begin Spirituality Today

## The Difference Between the Ganesh Gita and the Bhagavad Gita

We are reading the second chapter of the Ganesh Gita. In the Bhagavad Gita, the dialogue was between Lord Shri Krishna and Arjuna; here it is between Lord Ganapati and King Varenya. The difference between them is only this: Arjuna had to fight a war, he had to win a kingdom, and Draupadi had been insulted. That Gita was spoken in the context of honour and dignity. So where would its emphasis lie? "Rise, fight, do something even now, reclaim your honour." The Bhagavad Gita also contains the knowledge of liberation, but its greater focus is on action.

It is not that the Ganesh Gita says nothing of action. But here the teacher is Ganapati, so the emphasis is on how to use the intellect, how to recognise the Self, and how all of this can lead to liberation. King Varenya was a *mumukshu*, a seeker of liberation. He had nothing to attain; he already had everything: a wife, a kingdom, everything except a son. Then Ganapati came as his son and went away. Varenya then says, "Very well, I have seen you; I have had the vision of God."

So why the Ganesh Gita at all? It says largely what the Bhagavad Gita says, but Ganapati is the deity of the intellect, so here the intellect is given greater importance.

## The Seventh Verse of Bhaja Govindam

Adi Shankaracharya composed "Bhaja Govindam." "Bhaja govindaṁ bhaja govindaṁ govindaṁ bhaja mūḍhamate": O foolish mind, your time of death is drawing near, he reminds us again and again. In its seventh verse he says:

> **bālastāvatkrīḍāsaktaḥ taruṇastāvattaruṇīsaktaḥ |**
> **vṛddhastāvaccintāsaktaḥ pare brahmaṇi ko'pi na saktaḥ ||**
> "The child is attached to play, the youth is attached to the young woman, the old man is attached to worries; but no one is attached to the Supreme Brahman."
> (Bhaja Govindam, verse 7)

Childhood passes in play, when in the stage of *brahmacharya* all learning should have been acquired. In youth there is attachment to the opposite sex, the time of desire. Then comes the householder stage, and all its time goes in enjoyment, when by then one should have learned Karma Yoga. And spirituality? People say that is for after sixty, a task for after retirement. But in one who has never practised it, spirituality does not suddenly arrive after sixty. By then the worry of "vṛddhastāvaccintāsaktaḥ" becomes so strong that he becomes like a child again.

Its meaning is: in childhood an interest in play, in youth an attraction to young women, and in old age being surrounded by worries; but no one fixes their mind on the Supreme Brahman, the Supreme Self.

## The Illusion of "I Will Do It Later"

From birth to death, a person keeps running after false cravings. He does not consider that human life is very short. As each moment passes, he is drawn closer to death. He gets so caught up in this rush that he thinks, after finishing all these tasks, I will worship God in old age. This is the same "I will do it later" that Shankaracharya pointed out long ago.

He thinks there is plenty of time for this; right now he is busy with many kinds of work. But by the time he wakes up and wants to turn his mind to God, neither the mind nor the body cooperates. Life slips through the hands like sand from a closed fist. There is no need to wait for old age to love God; rather, the practice of contemplating God should begin in childhood itself.

## Making Children Karma Yogis from the Start

Therefore all this Vedanta should be taught in the stage of *brahmacharya* itself. At that very time the child should be made a Karma Yogi. Whether boy or girl, whether working outside or becoming a devoted homemaker, whatever it may be, the child has to be made a Karma Yogi. There is no choice other than action; action must be done. One who lives in the Himalayas does not need so much education; it is because one has to live in the world that one needs all this. The major part of life is here, in the world.

## The Three Parts of Life: Thirty-Three, Sixty-Six, Ninety-Nine

Do you know what happens to one who says, "I will do all this after sixty"? Count life in spans of thirty-three years: thirty-three, sixty-six and ninety-nine, roughly a hundred years. If we count seventy-five years, it becomes twenty-five, twenty-five, twenty-five.

The first thirty-three years belong to body-consciousness. If a person lives on the level of body-consciousness, it is fine until thirty-three, because at that time all his tasks are related to the body. He wants to travel, to play, to jump about, to explore everything new in life. Go out, go to Essel World, go into the water, go to a water park, see this country, see that country. The body has many tasks. Marriage, the fulfilment of desire, everything related to the body, he can do by thirty-three.

After thirty-three, he has already experienced all the major things. It is not I who say this, it is Shankara who says it: by thirty-three, whatever major actions a person can do are finished. After this there is only repetition of the same tasks. By thirty-three he will have worked at a job, he will have seen money and what it is like. Whatever was to be done with the body has been done.

## Life on the Level of Mind and Intellect

After thirty-three the next part of life begins. One who lived only on the level of the body and carried that from thirty-three to sixty-six finds that everything is now repetition, the same work. Then he will want to walk along with his son, then with his grandson too; he stays occupied in this. And after a certain time the body does not cooperate. Pain, troubles and quarrels begin from there.

That is why it is said that at least one who was used to living on the level of the mind will be able to live well until sixty-six. Retirement comes at around sixty. If he lives on the level of the intellect and mind, he has some work or other, some involvement. There is some research to do, enthusiasm for office work: try doing this, try doing something big, bring out a research paper, do something. On the level of the intellect there is something new, some new hobby, some new enthusiasm. But the scriptures say that by sixty or sixty-six that mind also grows old. It does happen; just as the body stops cooperating, enthusiasm too comes to an end.

## The Emptiness After Retirement

Mostly, after retirement, that is, when a person retires after the age of sixty, there is no more work for the intellect. Some retire at sixty, some at sixty-five, some at seventy, but whenever it happens, after that there is nothing left for the intellect to do. What research will he do at home? Then they cannot sit peacefully. Such elderly people keep doing something or other at home, "do this, do some work at least," and then they create disorder at home. If they were influential, they become a trouble for the household; and if they cannot speak up, the person sitting in a corner keeps growing older, because there is nothing left to do with the intellect. There was so much work at the office, and now suddenly nothing.

## What Government Data Says

If retirement happens at sixty, then by sixty-two the majority of seniors either suffer mental instability or simply die. Do you know who says this? Government data, about government employees. And how is it proven? Through pensions. After sixty-two, pensions drop abruptly. What does that mean? After sixty-two he is no longer alive, or he is suffering; something is going on. And this data is not mine, it is the government's, so it must be true. In some places it drops after sixty-five, and beyond seventy it is very little. The government hardly has to pay pensions.

Why would this happen? Because spirituality in life is zero.

## Life on the Level of the Self: The Key to a Hundred Years

One who, learning from Shankaracharya, was in the habit of living a spiritual life can live fully to ninety-nine. The Vedas say:

> **jīvema śaradaḥ śatam |**
> "May we live a hundred autumns."
> (Shukla Yajurveda 36.24)

That is, may we live for a hundred autumns, a hundred years. Such a person's life will go on for a hundred years, because even after sixty nothing changes for him, because the Self is immortal. He loved before as well; he did the same during his office years, and he did the same in childhood. Now in old age he has no worry. Whether he receives the fruit or not, he is living a life on a level above it.

## The Mark of a Karma Yogi

Therefore one who learns this from childhood will not become attached. He will do everything that others do, everything he did before; only he will not be attached. Because of this he can live well until the very end. This is the mark of a Karma Yogi: in old age he will not be consumed by worry.

> "I am telling you a science: this is how you identify whether you have practised Karma Yoga or not."
