# Why Wait Until Retirement to Turn Inward?

Many of us quietly carry a plan through life: first studies, then a job, then a family, and once all that is done, devotion to God after sixty. It sounds practical. But does life really follow the plan?

This discourse, given in the context of the second chapter of the Ganesha Gita, takes up that question directly and connects an old warning from Adi Shankaracharya to the way we live today.

## The Ganesha Gita: The Gita of the Lord of Wisdom

The Bhagavad Gita is a dialogue between Shri Krishna and Arjuna. Arjuna had a war to fight, a kingdom to win and the insult to Draupadi to answer. That is why the Gita stresses action: "Rise, fight, and reclaim your honour." It also teaches the path to liberation, but its main focus stays on action.

In the Ganesha Gita, the dialogue is between Lord Ganesha and King Varenya. Varenya had a wife, a kingdom and everything else; the one thing he lacked was a son. He had nothing left to gain. He was a **mumukshu** (one who longs for liberation).

Lord Ganesha is the lord of wisdom and intellect. So this Gita focuses on how to use your intellect, how to recognise the Atman (the Self), and how to turn everything in life toward freedom.

## Shankaracharya's Warning: Bhaja Govindam

In his hymn "Bhaja Govindam", Adi Shankaracharya reminds us again and again: "bhaja govindaṃ mūḍhamate" (worship Govinda, O foolish mind), for the hour of death is drawing near. Its seventh verse captures an entire life in a single couplet:

> **bālastāvat krīḍāsaktaḥ taruṇastāvat taruṇīsaktaḥ |**
> **vṛddhastāvac cintāsaktaḥ pare brahmaṇi ko'pi na saktaḥ ||**
> "Childhood slips away in play, youth in attraction and desire, and old age in worry. Yet no one sets their heart on the Supreme Brahman."
> (Bhaja Govindam, Verse 7)

The **brahmacharya ashrama** (the student stage of life) was the time for learning. By the **grihastha ashrama** (the householder stage), you were meant to have mastered Karma Yoga. Yet we put off spiritual life as "something for after retirement". Acharya Ji points out that if you have never practised it, it will not suddenly appear in you after sixty.

## The Trap of "I'll Do It Later"

From birth to death, we keep chasing false cravings. We forget how short life is, and that every passing moment brings us closer to death.

We assume there is plenty of time. But by the time we wake up and want to turn our hearts to God, neither the mind nor the body cooperates. Life slips away like sand from a closed fist.

You do not need to wait for old age to love God. The habit of keeping Him in your thoughts should begin in childhood.

*What are you putting off in your own life by telling yourself, "I'll do it later"?*

## Raising Every Child to Be a Karma Yogi

This is why children should learn Vedanta during the student stage itself, and why they should be raised as **karma yogis** (those who act without clinging to the results) at that very age.

Whether a boy or a girl, whether they work outside or run a home, everyone is bound to act. There is no other option. Someone living in the Himalayas may not need such preparation, but our lives will be spent in the world. That is exactly where this lesson matters most.

## Three Stages of Life: 33, 66 and 99

Picture life as three spans of thirty-three years each: 33, 66 and 99, roughly a hundred years. (If you take a lifespan of seventy-five, the same pattern becomes 25, 25 and 25.)

**The first stage, awareness of the body (up to 33):** travelling, playing, discovering new things, water parks, new countries, marriage, fulfilling desires. Nearly every experience tied to the body happens by 33. By then you have seen a job and seen money. After that, Acharya Ji says, life is a repetition of the same activities. "This is not me speaking," he adds, "this is Shankara speaking."

**The second stage, mind and intellect (33 to 66):** For those who have lived only at the level of the body, everything from here on is repetition. They do the same things with their son, then with their grandson. Then the body begins to fail, and pain, hardship and quarrels set in.

Those used to living at the level of the mind and intellect manage well until 66. They have research, enthusiasm for their work, a new interest, a new passion. But the scriptures say that by 60 or 66, even the mind grows old.

## The Emptiness After Retirement

Whether you retire at 60, 65 or 70, afterwards nothing is left to occupy the intellect. What research will you do at home?

Many people then cannot sit still. Those who once held influence start interfering in everything at home and become a burden. Those who cannot speak up sit in a corner and age faster.

Acharya Ji cites government figures: most government employees who retire at 60 suffer either mental instability or death by 62. The pension records prove it, since payouts drop sharply after 62. In some places the drop comes after 65, and beyond 70 very few people remain to draw a pension at all.

Why does this happen? Because there is no spiritual life in them at all.

*If your work and your role were taken from you tomorrow, what would remain within you?*

## The Third Stage: The Level of the Atman

Those who are used to living a spiritual life can live to 99, a full hundred years, just as the Vedas say:

> **jīvema śaradaḥ śatam |**
> "May we live a hundred autumns."
> (Shukla Yajurveda 36.24)

For such a person, nothing changes after sixty, because **the Atman is immortal**. The love they lived with as a child carried them through their working years, and the same love remains in old age. Whether results come or not, they live on a level above them.

## How to Recognise a Karma Yogi

Those who learn this from childhood do not cling. They do everything others do; they just do not get entangled in it. That is why they live well to the very end.

This is the mark of a karma yogi: **they will not be consumed by worry in old age.** It is the true test of whether you have practised Karma Yoga.

## Key Takeaways

- The Ganesha Gita is the Gita of the lord of wisdom, focused on recognising the Atman and reaching liberation.
- The seventh verse of Bhaja Govindam shows childhood, youth and old age passing in play, attraction and worry, while no one turns to God.
- Postponing spiritual life until "after sixty" is an illusion; by then the mind and body no longer cooperate.
- Life unfolds on three levels: the body (up to 33), the mind and intellect (up to 66) and the Atman (up to 99).
- The emptiness after retirement comes from the absence of spiritual life.
- Children should learn Vedanta and Karma Yoga from an early age.
- The mark of a karma yogi is freedom from worry, even in old age.
