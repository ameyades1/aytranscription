# Why Do Those Who Need Nothing Still Act?

Have you ever wondered why Shri Krishna kept acting without pause, when nothing in the three worlds remained for him to gain? And why did avatars like Shri Rama and Shri Krishna go to a **gurukul** (a traditional school where students live with their teacher) at all?

This discourse on a few verses from the third chapter of the Bhagavad Gita makes one plain point: **the great act not for themselves, but for the world.**

## Whom Does Society Look To?

> **yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ**
> **sa yat pramāṇaṁ kurute lokas tad anuvartate**
> "Whatever the great do, others follow. Whatever standard they set, the whole world goes by it."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.21)

This is why leadership is a responsibility. A leader carries the weight of others, and when leaders do not tread carefully, the damage is great.

## Nothing Left to Gain, Yet He Keeps Going

> **na me pārthāsti kartavyaṁ triṣu lokeṣu kiñcana**
> **nānavāptam avāptavyaṁ varta eva ca karmaṇi**
> "Shri Krishna says that in all three worlds there is no duty he must fulfil and nothing he has yet to attain, and still he remains engaged in action."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.22)

Acharya Ji connects this with his own life. At present he earns nothing. He could do a great deal online if he wished, and invitations to speak keep arriving. Still he has to travel, because the ashram needs this, and that, and something else. He has to set an example, he says, and he does not do it for show. This is how he lives.

He has no need for ritual worship. He knows that worshipping an idol belongs to the first stage of **sadhana** (spiritual practice), yet he does it anyway. Otherwise people would say, "Acharya Ji doesn't do any of this, so why should we?"

## Why Did Rama and Krishna Go to a Gurukul?

> **yadi hy ahaṁ na varteyaṁ jātu karmaṇy atandritaḥ**
> **mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ**
> "If God did not act with unflagging care, great harm would follow, because people walk the path He walks."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.23)

Krishna was an avatar, yet he went to study under the sage Sandipani. Rama was an avatar, yet he went to the sage Vashishtha. Had they skipped the gurukul, children today would ask, "Why should we bother going?"

*What are your children, your colleagues and your younger brothers and sisters learning from you? What standard does your conduct set for them?*

## When Ideals Collapse: Varnasankara

> **utsīdeyur ime lokā na kuryāṁ karma ced aham**
> **saṅkarasya ca kartā syām upahanyām imāḥ prajāḥ**
> "If the great were to stop acting, people would fall into ruin and **varnasankara** (the decay and confusion of the varnas, the four traditional social orders) would spread."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.24)

Acharya Ji explains this with examples from today:

- **The doctor's** profession was meant to be service. When doctors care only about earning money, they become a blend of Brahmin and Vaishya, the teacher and the merchant.
- **A teacher who teaches for money** is the same blend of Brahmin and Vaishya.
- **When a ruler** takes on the nature of a Shudra, the mix of Kshatriya and Shudra produces the corrupt politician.

All of this happens when the great abandon the ideal work that is theirs to do.

## Did You See Maa Durga in Your Mother-in-Law?

> **saktāḥ karmaṇy avidvāṁso yathā kurvanti bhārata**
> **kuryād vidvāṁs tathāsaktaś cikīrṣur loka-saṅgraham**
> "Just as the ignorant act out of attachment to their work, the wise, free of attachment, should act in the same way for the sake of **lokasangraha** (bringing people together and giving them direction)."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.25)

How do the ignorant act? "I have to look after my mother-in-law, look after my wife, look after the office." One task follows another, yet they never see God in any of it. A woman says, "I am serving my mother-in-law because I have no choice," and she has never once seen Maa Durga in her.

You cannot escape work. What you do not know is whom you are doing it for, and who you really are.

And when someone who has gained wisdom becomes proud of it, the fall is even steeper. A wise devotee was meant to practise **action filled with knowledge**.

## Not "Don't Do It" but "Do It This Way"

Do not rush to tell such people, "Give up your work, it's all illusion, let's head for the Himalayas." A Sadguru acts just as they do, but shows them a new way of doing it.

- You give in charity, so the Guru must give too, and show you **mahadaan** (the great giving).
- You run a business, so the Guru must run one too, in the way of **Shri Vidya** (the tantric path of worship of the Divine Mother).

People do not warm to a leader who only ever says "don't." Whoever says "Don't park here" must also tell you where you can park. That is good parenting: not this, but that instead.

## No Change Without an Alternative

Every day we say the corrupt should be thrown out. Yet until the next generation is ready, parliament will not sit empty. Until **sattvic** (pure and balanced in nature) people arrive who can also handle the paperwork, show a little muscle when needed and carry some **rajoguna** (the quality of drive and activity), the old guard cannot be replaced.

A Guru who only sits here and gives discourses is not a Sadguru. That is why Acharya Ji has to enter politics and go out into the world to study it. Even the mightiest kings had to seek out a Guru. Shivaji Maharaj, the great Maratha king, also needed someone to inspire him. If the Guru does not do this work, the country does not change.

Do the same work that people do, then, but not alone. Bring them along and teach them as you go: not like that, like this, and here is what happens when you do.

## Do Not Unsettle Their Minds

> **na buddhi-bhedaṁ janayed ajñānāṁ karma-saṅginām**
> **joṣayet sarva-karmāṇi vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran**
> "The wise, established in God, should not confuse the minds of those engaged in action by sowing **distrust of action** in them. Performing well the duties the scriptures prescribe, they should lead others to do the same."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.26)

Stay away from a Guru who tells you, "Child, give up your business, it's all illusion." The right teaching sounds like this: "Child, this is Shri Vidya, and through it you can become even more prosperous."

**Tyaga** (renunciation) means serving the country and staying unattached. Walking away is not the right path. First build up your strength.

*Do you want to give something up because it is hard, or because you have truly risen above it?*

## Marriage Is Not the Problem

Never teach anyone, "Don't marry, it brings nothing but trouble." Shri Rama, Eknath Maharaj and Tukaram Maharaj (the saint-poets of Maharashtra), Lord Shiva, even the Buddha: all of them married.

The trouble lies not in marriage but in how you live it. A true Guru may be celibate himself, yet he will never stop you from marrying. Sai Baba never married, but did he ever tell a landlord, a king or a queen that marrying had been a mistake?

Someone who says "Don't do this, child" may be the teacher of some sect, but not a Sadguru, and certainly not an avatar. If the next generation of a tradition is teaching this way, something has gone wrong along the line.

## A Teacher Must Be Practical

A true teacher is not like a tuition class that tells you, "Memorise this, it will come in the exam." He does the work himself and has you do it as well.

## Key Takeaways

- The conduct of the great sets the standard for society, so leadership is a responsibility.
- Even those with nothing to gain act to set an example; this is why Rama and Krishna went to a gurukul.
- When ideals collapse, varnasankara spreads: the doctor or teacher chasing money, the corrupt politician.
- The ignorant act without seeing God in their work; the wise must practise action filled with knowledge.
- A Sadguru does not just say "don't"; he offers an alternative and shows the way by doing.
- Tyaga means giving up attachment, not action; marriage and business are not the problem, how you live them is.
