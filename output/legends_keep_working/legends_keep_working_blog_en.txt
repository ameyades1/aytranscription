# Why Does One Who Needs Nothing Still Act

Have you ever wondered why Lord Shri Krishna, who had nothing left to attain in the three worlds, kept acting without pause? And why did avatars like Rama and Krishna go to the gurukul?

This discourse on a few verses from the third chapter of the Bhagavad Gita says something simple: **the great act not for themselves, but for the world.**

## Whom Does Society Look To?

> **yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ**
> **sa yat pramāṇaṁ kurute lokas tad anuvartate**
> "Whatever a great person does, others also do. Whatever standard he sets, the whole world follows."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.21)

Whatever the great do, others do the same. Whatever standard such a person sets, the whole of society begins to follow it.

That is why leadership is a responsibility. A leader is a responsible person. If he does not walk with care, great harm follows.

## One Who Needs Nothing Still Keeps Going

> **na me pārthāsti kartavyaṁ triṣu lokeṣu kiñcana**
> **nānavāptam avāptavyaṁ varta eva ca karmaṇi**
> "O Partha, there is no duty for Me in the three worlds, nor anything unattained to be attained; yet I remain engaged in action."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.22)

Shri Krishna says that He has no duty in the three worlds and nothing unattained, and still He remains engaged in action.

Guruji connects this to his own life. At this time he has no income. If he wished, a great deal could happen online, and many invitations for programs come too. Still he has to travel, because an ashram is needed, this is needed, that is needed.

> "I have to show an ideal. This is not for show; it is my way of life."

He does not need worship. He knows that idol worship is the beginning stage of sadhana, and still he does it. Otherwise people will say, "Guruji does nothing, why should we?"

## Why Did Rama and Krishna Go to the Gurukul?

> **yadi hy ahaṁ na varteyaṁ jātu karmaṇy atandritaḥ**
> **mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ**
> "If I ever failed to engage carefully in action, O Partha, people would follow My path in every way."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.23)

If the Lord did not act with care, great harm would follow, because people walk on His path.

Krishna was an avatar, yet he went to the sage Sandipani. Rama was an avatar, yet he went to Vashishtha. Had they not gone to the gurukul, children today would say, "Why do we need to go?"

*What are your children, your companions, your younger brothers and sisters learning from you? What kind of standard is your conduct setting for them?*

## When Ideals Break: Varna Sankar

> **utsīdeyur ime lokā na kuryāṁ karma ced aham**
> **saṅkarasya ca kartā syām upahanyām imāḥ prajāḥ**
> "If I did not act, these worlds would perish; I would be the cause of confusion and would destroy these people."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.24)

If the great do not act, people are ruined and **varna sankar** (the degradation of the varnas) spreads.

Guruji explains this with examples from today:

- The **doctor's** profession was one of service. When he only chases money, he becomes a mixture of Brahmin and Vaishya.
- The **teacher who works for money** is also a mixture of Brahmin and Vaishya.
- When a **king** takes on a Shudra disposition, the mixture of Kshatriya and Shudra produces the corrupt politician.

All this happens when the great abandon their exemplary action.

## Did You See Durga in Your Mother-in-Law?

> **saktāḥ karmaṇy avidvāṁso yathā kurvanti bhārata**
> **kuryād vidvāṁs tathāsaktaś cikīrṣur loka-saṅgraham**
> "O Bharata, as the ignorant act with attachment to action, so should the wise act without attachment, wishing to hold the world together."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.25)

As the ignorant act, attached to action, so should the wise, free of attachment, act in the same way for **lokasangraha** (bringing people together and giving them direction).

How do the ignorant act? "I have to look after my mother-in-law, look after my wife, look after the office." Action, action goes on, but they see God in no one. "I am serving my mother-in-law under compulsion," and they never saw Goddess Durga in her.

You cannot escape the work. But for whom you are doing it, and who you are, you do not know.

And if the one who has become wise grows proud, he falls even lower. The wise devotee was meant to perform **action filled with knowledge**.

## Not "Don't", but "Do It This Way"

Do not immediately tell such people, "Leave your work, it is all illusion, come to the Himalayas." The Sadguru shows by acting just as they do, but in a new way.

- You give charity, so the guru too must show by giving: **mahadaan**, the great giving.
- You do business, so the guru too must show by doing business, in the way of **Shri Vidya**.

People do not like a leader who only says "don't." The one who says "don't park here" must also say where to park. That is good parenting: not this, but this instead.

## No Change Without an Alternative

We say every day, throw out the corrupt. But until the next generation is ready, Parliament will not stay empty. Until sattvic people come who can do the paperwork, who can be a little tough, who have a little rajoguna, they cannot be replaced.

> "The guru who sits here giving discourses is not a Sadguru."

That is why Guruji has to enter politics, go out and study. Even the greatest of kings had to meet a guru. Shivaji Maharaj too had to be inspired. If gurus do not do this, the country does not change.

So do the work that people do, but not alone. While bringing people together, teach them: not like this, like this; and what comes of doing it.

## Do Not Unsettle the Mind

> **na buddhi-bhedaṁ janayed ajñānāṁ karma-saṅginām**
> **joṣayet sarva-karmāṇi vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran**
> "The wise should not unsettle the minds of the ignorant attached to action; performing all actions well, steadfast, he should engage them in action too."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.26)

The wise one, established in the Supreme, should not create confusion, that is, **lack of faith in action**, in those engaged in action. Performing prescribed actions well himself, he should have them do the same.

"Son, give up your business, it is all illusion": do not go to a guru who says this. "Son, this is Shri Vidya, through it you can become even wealthier": that is the right teaching.

**Tyaga** means serving the country, and not being attached. Merely giving things up is not the right way. First develop strength.

*Do you want to give something up because it is hard, or because you have truly risen above it?*

## Marriage Is Not the Problem

"Don't marry, it is too painful": do not teach this. Shri Rama, Eknath Maharaj, Tukaram Maharaj, Shiva, even the Buddha, all were married.

> "Marriage is not the problem; how you handle marriage is the problem."

A true guru may himself be a brahmachari, but will not stop you from marrying. Sai Baba did not marry, but did he tell any landlord, king or queen that they did wrong by marrying?

The one who says "don't do this, son" may be the guru of one cult, but not a Sadguru, and certainly not an avatar. If the next generation of a tradition is giving such teaching, something went wrong in between.

## The Teacher Must Be Practical

A true teacher is not like a tuition class: "Memorize this, this is what will come in the exam." He shows by doing, and has you do it too.

## Key Takeaways

- The conduct of the great becomes society's standard, so leadership is a responsibility.
- One who needs nothing still acts to show an ideal; that is why even Rama and Krishna went to the gurukul.
- When ideals break, varna sankar spreads: the doctor or teacher chasing money, the corrupt politician.
- The ignorant act but do not see God in their work; the wise must perform action filled with knowledge.
- A Sadguru does not say "don't"; he gives an alternative and shows by doing.
- Tyaga means giving up attachment, not action; marriage and business are not the problem, how you handle them is.
