# Chapter: Why the Great Keep Working

## The Conduct of the Great Is the Standard

In the Bhagavad Gita, Lord Shri Krishna tells Arjuna something of great importance. He says that society always looks to its noblest people.

> **yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ**
> **sa yat pramāṇaṁ kurute lokas tad anuvartate**
> "Whatever a great person does, others also do. Whatever standard he sets, the whole world follows."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.21)

Whatever conduct the great follow, others follow the same. Whatever he establishes as the standard, the entire community of people begins to live by it.

## Even One Who Needs Nothing Still Acts

Next, the Lord gives His own example.

> **na me pārthāsti kartavyaṁ triṣu lokeṣu kiñcana**
> **nānavāptam avāptavyaṁ varta eva ca karmaṇi**
> "O Partha, there is no duty for Me in the three worlds, nor anything unattained to be attained; yet I remain engaged in action."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.22)

O Arjuna, in these three worlds I have no duty, nor is there anything worth attaining that I have not attained; even so, I remain engaged in action.

This applies to my own life as well. At this time I have no income. If I wished, a great deal could happen online, and many invitations for programs come too. Then why do I have to keep travelling? An ashram is needed, this is needed, that is needed. Action has to be done.

In the end, worship will be of a karma yogi; meditation too will rest on a karma yogi, on Shri Krishna. You will have to act continuously, but in the right way.

## Acting with Care

> **yadi hy ahaṁ na varteyaṁ jātu karmaṇy atandritaḥ**
> **mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ**
> "If I ever failed to engage carefully in action, O Partha, people would follow My path in every way."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.23)

O Partha, if I were ever not to engage in action with care, great harm would follow, because people follow My path in every way.

Leadership is a post of responsibility. A leader is a responsible person. If he does not act with care, great harm results.

I do not need worship. I know that idol worship is the beginning stage of sadhana, and still I have to do it. Otherwise people will say, "Guruji does nothing, so why should we?"

So I have to work very carefully. I have to show an ideal. This is not for show; it is my way of life. I have no purpose of my own, no need to do it, and still I must, or else great harm will follow.

## Even Rama and Krishna Went to the Gurukul

People ask: Krishna was wise, an avatar; why did he need to go to the sage Sandipani? Rama was an avatar; why did he need to go to Vashishtha?

This is the answer. Had they not gone to the gurukul, children today would say, "Why do we need to go?" That is why they had to engage in action, because people follow their path in every way. Think how exemplary a great person must be.

## Varna Sankar: When Ideals Break

> **utsīdeyur ime lokā na kuryāṁ karma ced aham**
> **saṅkarasya ca kartā syām upahanyām imāḥ prajāḥ**
> "If I did not act, these worlds would perish; I would be the cause of confusion and would destroy these people."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.24)

If I did not act, all these people would be ruined, and I would become the cause of confusion and the destroyer of all these beings.

Confusion here means varna sankar, the degradation of the varnas. This is what is happening today. Anyone marries anyone. And it also happens that a doctor's profession is no longer a profession of service; he starts trying to make money. That is a mixture of Brahmin and Vaishya. The doctor was meant to be devoted to service; earning wealth was the Vaishya's work. Now some new caste altogether has formed.

The teacher who works for money is also a mixture of Brahmin and Vaishya. When a king takes on a Shudra disposition, a mixture of Kshatriya and Shudra, the result is the corrupt politician. When does all this happen? When the great do not perform their exemplary action.

## The Action of the Ignorant and the Action of the Wise

> **saktāḥ karmaṇy avidvāṁso yathā kurvanti bhārata**
> **kuryād vidvāṁs tathāsaktaś cikīrṣur loka-saṅgraham**
> "O Bharata, as the ignorant act with attachment to action, so should the wise act without attachment, wishing to hold the world together."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.25)

O Bharata, just as the ignorant, attached to action, perform their work, so should the wise, free of attachment, act in the same way, desiring lokasangraha.

The ignorant do act, but they do not know why they are acting. And those who have become wise, if they become proud, are said to fall even lower. The wise devotee was told to perform action filled with knowledge.

What are the ignorant attached to action like? "Oh, I have to look after my mother-in-law, look after this, look after that." Look after the wife, look after the office. They keep saying action, action. But did they see God in anyone? "I am serving my mother-in-law under compulsion," and they never saw Goddess Durga in her. At the time of death, that same mother-in-law will come before them anyway. You will do the work regardless; you cannot escape it. But for whom you are doing it, you do not know. Who I was, what I was, you do not know. Shani Dev is certainly there.

These are the ignorant attached to action, so let them act. Do not immediately tell them, "Leave your work, all this is useless, it is all illusion and attachment, come to the Himalayas."

## Lokasangraha: Showing by Doing

The Sadguru, though free of attachment, acts just as worldly people do, for lokasangraha, to gather people together. You give charity, so I too must show by giving charity. Not in your way; I must show it in a new way: mahadaan, the great giving. You do business, so I too must show by doing a business, in the way of Shri Vidya.

It is simple: people do not like a leader who only says "don't." The one who says "don't park here" must also arrange where to park. That is good parenting: not this, but this instead. As long as you give no alternative, merely forbidding achieves nothing.

## No Change Without an Alternative

You people say every day, throw out these corrupt people, drive them away. But until the next generation is ready, they will have to be kept. Parliament will not stay empty. Until the next set of sattvic people comes, people who can do the paperwork, who can be a little tough, who have a little rajoguna, they cannot be replaced. There is an alternative system, and you will have to show it by doing it.

I too have to enter politics. That is why I have to go out, study them and work. I cannot just sit here and give discourses.

Even the greatest of kings had to meet a guru. Shivaji Maharaj too had to be inspired, you know that, don't you? That has to be developed. If gurus do not do this, the country cannot change. So the guru who only sits here giving discourses is not a Sadguru. He must enter. Otherwise, by studying a little, his own liberation is certain anyway.

So the action they do, you too must do, but not alone. While gathering people, teach them: not like this, like this; not like this, like this. And explain what comes of doing it.

## Do Not Unsettle the Mind

> **na buddhi-bhedaṁ janayed ajñānāṁ karma-saṅginām**
> **joṣayet sarva-karmāṇi vidvān yuktaḥ samācaran**
> "The wise should not unsettle the minds of the ignorant attached to action; performing all actions well, steadfast, he should engage them in action too."
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.26)

The wise one, firmly established in the nature of the Supreme, should not create confusion, that is, lack of faith in action, in the minds of the ignorant who are attached to the actions prescribed by scripture. Instead, performing all scripturally prescribed actions well himself, he should have them do the same.

"Son, all this is illusion and attachment, give up your business": do not go to a guru who says this. "Son, this is Shri Vidya, through it you can become even wealthier": that is the right teaching.

Tyaga means serving the country, and tyaga means not being attached. Merely giving things up is not the right way. You have a choice, don't you? First develop strength. Getting the point? What the Lord is saying is this: do not create a lack of faith in action.

## Marriage Is Not the Problem

"Don't get married, marriage is a very painful thing": do not teach this. The great saints married. Shri Rama married, Eknath Maharaj, Tukaram Maharaj married, Shiva is married. It is everywhere. Even the Buddha was married. So marriage is not the problem; how you handle marriage is the problem. So show it by doing it well. Is it clear now?

## How to Recognize a True Sadguru

A guru has a role. The one who says "don't do this, son" is not a Sadguru. He may be the guru of one cult or one culture, but he is not a Sadguru, and certainly not an avatar. The teaching of avatars is not like this. If their next generation is passing on a distorted teaching, then something went wrong in between. That the original guru said such a thing, I will simply not accept.

A real guru never teaches like that. He himself may be a brahmachari, but he will not stop you from marrying. The Swami will not marry, but he will have many householder seekers, and they found liberation right there. Sai Baba did not marry, but did he tell any landlord, king or queen that they had done wrong by marrying? They never teach like that.

## The Practical Teacher

So he should not create confusion, that is, lack of faith in action, in the minds of the ignorant who are attached. The guru who says "don't do this" is not a Sadguru. Then what is to be done? Performing well himself all actions that are shastra-vihit, that is, set out in the scriptures, he should have them do the same.

A teacher must be practical. He cannot be like the tuition classes: "Memorize this, this is what will come in the exam." It cannot be like that.
