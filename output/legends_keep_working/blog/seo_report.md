## 1. Summary

The talk explains Bhagavad Gita 3.21 to 3.26: the great act not for themselves but for the world (lokasangraha), because society follows the standard they set. Acharya Ji draws out why Krishna, with nothing left to gain, still acts; why Rama and Krishna went to a gurukul; varnasankara as the collapse of ideals (doctor, teacher, politician); and why a Sadguru does not say "give it all up" but shows a better way to do business, give and live married life. The main intent is informational and verse-led ("lokasangraha meaning", "yad yad acharati shreshtha meaning", "Gita 3.21 meaning"), with a secondary emotional and practical audience: parents, managers and seekers who wonder whether spiritual life means leaving work and family. Verse-meaning pages are dominated by thin shloka sites that give a translation and nothing more; this post can win by tying the verses to real-life examples (leadership, parenting, the gurukul) and by the distinctive teaching that renunciation means giving up attachment, not action.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi (52 शब्द):** लोकसंग्रह का अर्थ है संसार के हित और सही व्यवस्था के लिए कर्म करना। भगवद्गीता (३.२१–३.२६) में श्रीकृष्ण कहते हैं कि श्रेष्ठ पुरुष जैसा आचरण करता है, लोग वैसा ही करते हैं। इसलिए जिसे कुछ पाना शेष नहीं, वह भी कर्म करता है, ताकि समाज को सही आदर्श मिले और लोग कर्म से विमुख न हों।

**English (55 words):** Lokasangraha means acting for the welfare and right order of the world. In the Bhagavad Gita (3.21 to 3.26), Shri Krishna says that whatever the great do, others follow. So even one with nothing left to gain keeps acting, to set the right example and to keep people from losing faith in action.

## 6. FAQ

**1. लोकसंग्रह क्या है? / What is lokasangraha?**
हिंदी: गीता ३.२५ के अनुसार आसक्तिरहित ज्ञानी भी लोकसंग्रह के लिए वैसे ही कर्म करे जैसे आसक्त अज्ञानी करते हैं, अर्थात लोगों को जोड़कर और दिशा देकर संसार का हित करे।
English: In Gita 3.25, the wise who are free of attachment should act just as the attached do, for lokasangraha: holding the world together and giving people direction.

**2. श्रीकृष्ण को कुछ पाना नहीं था, फिर वे कर्म क्यों करते थे? / Why did Krishna act when he had nothing to gain?**
हिंदी: गीता ३.२२–२३ में वे कहते हैं कि उनका कोई कर्तव्य शेष नहीं, फिर भी वे कर्म करते हैं, क्योंकि मनुष्य उन्हीं के मार्ग पर चलते हैं।
English: In Gita 3.22 and 3.23 he says no duty remains for him, yet he acts, because people follow his path in every way.

**3. राम और कृष्ण गुरुकुल क्यों गए? / Why did Rama and Krishna go to a gurukul?**
हिंदी: अवतार होकर भी वे वसिष्ठ और सांदीपनि के पास गए, ताकि आने वाली पीढ़ियाँ शिक्षा और गुरु के महत्व को न छोड़ें।
English: Though avatars, they studied under Vasishtha and Sandipani, so that later generations would not decide that learning from a Guru was unnecessary.

**4. क्या आध्यात्मिक होने के लिए काम या विवाह छोड़ना चाहिए? / Must you give up work or marriage to be spiritual?**
हिंदी: नहीं। गीता ३.२६ कहती है कि ज्ञानी कर्म में लगे लोगों की बुद्धि में भ्रम न डाले। त्याग का अर्थ आसक्ति छोड़ना है, कर्म छोड़ना नहीं; समस्या विवाह या व्यापार नहीं, उन्हें निभाने का ढंग है।
English: No. Gita 3.26 says the wise should not unsettle those engaged in action. Tyaga means giving up attachment, not action; the trouble is not marriage or business but how you live them.

**5. सद्गुरु की पहचान क्या है? / How do you recognise a Sadguru?**
हिंदी: सद्गुरु केवल "मत करो" नहीं कहता; वह विकल्प देता है और स्वयं करके दिखाता है।
English: A Sadguru does not just say "don't"; he offers an alternative and shows the way by doing it himself.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Gita 3.24 meaning (Major, from the Sanskrit review).** The verse is Krishna speaking in the first person ("If I did not act... I would be the cause of sankara and would destroy these beings"); the blog renders it as "if the great did not act" and drops the second half. Do not quote this rendering in the meta description, FAQ or snippets. Recommendation: apply the review's Gita Press correction, and keep the application to "all great people" as Acharya Ji's reading in the prose.
2. **Lokasangraha gloss.** The primary keyword is lokasangraha, and the blog glosses it as "bringing people together and giving them direction", which is the speaker's emphasis; the standard sense is the welfare and right order of the world (Gita Press 3.25). The direct answer (section 5) uses the standard sense; FAQ 1 uses the blog's. Recommendation: adopt the review's gloss, or show both, before targeting the term, since searchers will compare against Gita Press.
3. **Gita 3.22, 3.23 and 3.25 paraphrased in the third person, vocatives dropped (Minor, review).** Snippets that quote these verses should use the Gita Press first-person rendering.
4. **Gita 3.26 "engaged in action" vs "attached to action" (Minor, review).** FAQ 4 follows the blog; if the blog is corrected to "attached to action", update FAQ 4 to match.
5. **Varnasankara examples.** The doctor, teacher and politician examples are Acharya Ji's modern reading, not the Gita's meaning; the blog frames them that way. Do not put them in titles or metas as "what the Gita says", and avoid caste-framed keywords ("varnasankara meaning") as targets: the term is sensitive and the post's gloss is under correction (review item 2).
6. **Names and spellings (Minor, review).** वशिष्ठ → वसिष्ठ / Vasishtha; सात्विक → सात्त्विक; "गुरुजी" → "आचार्य जी" in the Hindi for consistency. The FAQ above already uses Vasishtha and Acharya Ji.
7. **Unverified details.** Shivaji Maharaj's inspirer is not named (do not add Samarth Ramdas in SEO copy without checking the talk); Sai Baba's never marrying rests on hagiography. Keep both out of titles, metas and FAQ.
8. **Gurus who advise renunciation.** The blog warns against Gurus who say "give up your business, it's all illusion". Keep SEO copy positive ("a Sadguru shows a better way") rather than framing it as an attack on other paths or sects.

## 12. SEO checklist

### Keywords
- [x] Hindi and English each have a focus keyword
- [x] The focus keyword isn't used by another post (no cannibalization)
- [x] 2–4 secondary keywords per language
- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best

### Category and tags
- [x] One category, from the approved 14
- [x] 3–6 tags per language
- [x] No tag repeats a category name
- [x] Existing tags reused; no near-duplicates
- [x] Each tag can be shared by at least 3 posts
- [x] No broad labels that only restate a category
- [x] Series tags named exactly as the series
- [x] `festivals` only when the post is about the festival itself; Pitru topics in `pitru`

### Structured data (in this report, not on the page)
- [x] One JSON-LD block per language: BlogPosting, Person, Organization, VideoObject (if there is a video), BreadcrumbList
- [x] Hindi and English versions linked as translations

### Content trust
- [x] Titles, metas and snippets faithful to the talk
- [x] No unsupported scriptural claims
- [x] Health promises, fear-selling and attacks on other traditions flagged

### Not covered
- Live broken links (internal links only work once the linked post is published)
- Canonical and Open Graph tags (SureRank adds these itself)
