## 1. Summary

The talk takes one verse, Bhagavad Gita 3.27 (प्रकृतेः क्रियमाणानि गुणैः कर्माणि सर्वशः), and asks who really acts: "I", or prakriti through its gunas. People reach this question in two ways. Gita readers search for the verse ("Gita 3.27 meaning", "मैं कर्ता हूं"), which is **informational**. Others come with a life problem: pride in their success, or the feeling that home and family would fall apart without them, which is **emotional / reflective**. Most ranking pages are verse-by-verse commentaries with little everyday application. This talk's angle is practical: the merit "bank balance" and the teeth example, the "I must look after everyone" excuse, the social worker and doctor who need the very thing they claim to remove, and the link from doership to pitru rina and moksha. The post is short and has one verse, so the best chance is the long-tail question "who is the real doer" / "असली कर्ता कौन है" plus the verse number.

## 6. FAQ

All answers come from the talk.

1. **असली कर्ता कौन है? / Who is the real doer?**
   गीता ३.२७ के अनुसार सारे कर्म प्रकृति के गुणों द्वारा होते हैं। हम कर्म नहीं करते, वह हमारे माध्यम से होता है। / According to Gita 3.27, all actions are carried out by the qualities of nature. We do not perform our actions; they happen through us.
2. **"मैं ही करता हूं" मानना अहंकार क्यों है? / Why is "I alone do it" ego?**
   क्योंकि श्रीकृष्ण कहते हैं कि जिसका अंतःकरण अहंकार से मोहित है, वही अज्ञानी "मैं कर्ता हूं" मानता है। / Because Shri Krishna says that only the ignorant one, whose inner being is deluded by ego, believes "I am the doer."
3. **"संपूर्ण कर्म" में क्या-क्या आता है? / What do "all actions" include?**
   सब कुछ: कार्यालय का काम, घर का काम, यहां तक कि भोजन करना भी, चाहे किसी भी तरीके से किया जाए। / Everything: work at the office, work at home, even eating, whichever way it is done.
4. **प्रकृति क्या है? / What is prakriti?**
   जो कुछ दिखता है, वह प्रकृति है, जिसे पार्वती, देवी, शक्ति और माया भी कहते हैं। इसमें पंचमहाभूत हैं: अग्नि, पृथ्वी, वायु, जल और आकाश। / Everything you can see is prakriti, also called Parvati, Devi, Shakti and Maya. Within her are the five great elements: fire, earth, air, water and space.
5. **कर्तापन का अहंकार पितरों से कैसे जुड़ा है? / How is the ego of doership linked to the ancestors?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार इस जन्म का पहला काम पूर्वजों को मुक्त करना है, और शायद उनसे भी यही भूल हुई थी: "मैं करता हूं"। / According to Acharya Ji, the first task of this birth is to free one's ancestors, and they probably made the same mistake: "I do it." (Acharya Ji's teaching; see flag 5.)

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Prapancha as "the play of five" (Minor, Sanskrit review item 1, still open).** Grammatically प्रपञ्च is प्र + पञ्च् "to spread out", the manifold world. The "five" reading is Acharya Ji's teaching device, but the English gloss "the five-fold world" presents it as the dictionary meaning. No SEO text uses this etymology. **Recommendation:** apply the review's correction ("आचार्य जी इसे प्रपंच कहते हैं" / "Acharya Ji reads the word as prapancha (literally the expanded, manifold world …)").
2. **"Everything you can see is prakriti" (Minor, review item 2, optional).** Gita 7.4 also counts mind, intellect and ego as prakriti, which supports the post's own argument. The FAQ answer on prakriti repeats the blog's wording only. **Recommendation:** add the Gita 7.4 line if the editor wishes.
3. **Hindi heading "पितर क्यों बनते हैं?" (Minor, review item 3, still open).** It does not match the English heading or the section's content. **Recommendation:** "पितर बंधे क्यों रहते हैं?". seo.json does not use this heading, so renaming it will not break anything.
4. **"वक्ता" and "आचार्य जी" mixed (Minor, review item 4, still open).** The Hindi refers to the same speaker in two ways; E-E-A-T and entity consistency are better with "आचार्य जी" throughout. **Recommendation:** replace each "वक्ता" with "आचार्य जी".
5. **Freeing the ancestors as "the first task of this birth" (could not be verified).** This is Acharya Ji's teaching, not a cited scripture. FAQ 5 attributes it to him and no title or meta uses it. Pitru rina and moksha are linked in tradition (Manusmriti 6.35), but no SEO text claims a scriptural source. **Recommendation:** keep it attributed; do not turn it into a pitru dosh or fear-based hook.
6. **The "spiritual meter" given at the Shibir.** A practice of the Foundation, not a scriptural claim. It is left out of titles, meta and FAQ so that the post does not read as a promotion. No action needed.
7. **Remarks about social workers and doctors.** The blog limits them to ordinary people, not great souls or Gurus. They are kept out of the snippets so they cannot be quoted without that limit.
