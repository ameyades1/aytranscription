# Who Is the Doer When Prakriti Does Everything

Is your success purely the fruit of your own effort? Would your home, family and work really come to a halt without you? In this discourse, a single short verse of the Bhagavad Gita raises a big question: **who is the real doer?**

## Today's Present, Yesterday's Earnings

If your present is very good, it means that at some time in the past you must have done a great deal of good. This is the bank balance of **punya** (merit).

The example is very simple. To have good teeth tomorrow, you must brush today. Medical science says the same. If your teeth are good today, you must have done something good yesterday. And had you ruined them yesterday by chewing paan and bidis, they would be bad today.

That is why the order is this: first understand this life properly, and only then will life after death make sense. And understand what came before this life, and this life will make sense.

## Who Holds the Meter of Merit?

The balance of merit can also run out. We keep spending afresh, a car, a horse, a bungalow, enjoyment, but we have no meter to count how much remains.

The speaker says this meter is given in the shivir. It is not an electronic device but a **spiritual meter**, with which you can calculate your own karma yourself. Whatever has gone wrong can be set right from here on.

*If your merit were tallied today, do you know how much would be left?*

## Why Is "I Alone Do It" Ego?

The speaker asks the listeners directly: how many of you believe that success comes only and solely from your own effort, and that nothing else matters?

> "I alone do it: if such is the understanding, it is a sentence full of ego."

Shri Krishna himself says this:

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ**
> **ahaṅkāra-vimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate**
> "All actions are in every way performed by the gunas of prakriti; yet one whose inner being is deluded by ego thinks, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

In truth, all actions are performed in every way by the gunas of prakriti, yet the ignorant one whose **antahkarana** (inner being) is deluded by ego believes "I am the doer."

"All actions" includes everything: office work, housework, even eating.

## Starting With Five Percent Faith

The speaker says plainly that the Gita is not false; at least that much belief is needed. There should be at least five percent **shraddha** (faith); only then is there any meaning in talking.

And if the understanding is "I know more than Krishna," he says lightly: then there is always a next birth, talk it over with some other guru. "Better luck next birth."

## Why Do People Become Pitras?

The first task of this birth is to free our ancestors, so that they do not have to be born again. And the speaker suggests that they too probably made the same mistake: "I do it, I have done it."

This same mistake binds us too. "How can I attend this shivir? I have to look after my father, my mother, my mother-in-law, my daughter-in-law, the neighbour, even the dog." We always have every reason in the world ready.

> "Do you think they really need you? Or is it the opposite, that you need them?"

*Think honestly: for those you consider yourself indispensable to, does everything really rest on you?*

## A Direct Blow to the Ego

The speaker warns in advance that the ego will be struck hard today, because the subject is freedom from **pitra rina** (debt to the ancestors), that is, your **moksha** (liberation).

Whoever feels "I am serving society," in truth it is he who needs prestige in society. Whoever feels "I must heal the sick," actually needs patients, because if everyone became healthy his hospital would not run.

The speaker makes clear that this is about the ordinary person and ordinary intellect, not about great souls or those who have reached the position of a guru.

## Understanding the Verse Step by Step

**"All actions"**: whether at home or outside, whatever you do, all of it.

**"In every way"**: yogic, Vedic, Puranic, worldly, or by way of science, however you act, it is all included.

**"By the gunas of prakriti"**: and this is the most important phrase.

## What Is Prakriti?

Whatever you see is **prakriti**. We also call it Parvati, Devi, Shakti and Maya.

Within this prakriti are the **pancha mahabhuta** (five great elements): fire, earth, air, water and space. Of these we are made, and of these this whole world.

## Prapancha: The Play of Five

This is the play of five, which is why it is called **prapancha**.

After a wedding people ask, "How is your prapancha going?" That is, they are indirectly asking, "How entangled are you in Maya?" Whereas one was never meant to get entangled at all. We do not even know the meaning of the words we speak every day.

## Not I, but Through Me

Actions happen through the **gunas** of prakriti, but the person deluded by ego believes, "I am the doer." Whereas in truth he never does anything at all.

> "None of us does that karma. It happens through us."

As long as there is ignorance, it seems, "This is the body, and I am giving it orders, so it is acting."

*In all that you are doing today, how much is "I" and how much is prakriti?*

## Key Takeaways

- A good present today is the fruit of past merit, and that balance can also run out.
- The spiritual meter given in the shivir helps you calculate your own karma.
- According to Gita 3.27, all actions happen through the gunas of prakriti; believing "I do it" is ego and ignorance.
- The feeling "they depend on me" is often the reverse: we are the ones who need them.
- The path to freeing the ancestors and to one's own moksha begins with recognising the ego of doership.
- Prakriti is Parvati, Shakti, Maya; this play of the five great elements is prapancha.
