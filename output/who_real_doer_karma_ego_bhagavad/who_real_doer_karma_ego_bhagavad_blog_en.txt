# Who Is the Doer When Nature Does Everything?

Is your success purely the fruit of your own effort? Would your home, your family and your work really grind to a halt without you? In this discourse, a short verse from the Bhagavad Gita opens up a big question: **who is the real doer?**

## Today's Comfort, Yesterday's Earnings

If your present is going very well, it means that at some point in the past you must have done a great deal of good. Think of it as the bank balance of your **punya** (merit).

The example is a simple one. If you want healthy teeth tomorrow, you have to brush them today, and medical science says the same. If your teeth are healthy today, you must have looked after them yesterday. Had you ruined them back then with paan and bidis, they would be in poor shape now.

That is why the order matters. Understand this life properly first, and the life after death will make sense. Understand what came before this life, and this life will make sense.

## Who Holds the Meter for Your Merit?

Your balance of merit can run out. We keep spending on new things, the car, the horses, the bungalow, the good times, yet we have no meter to tell us how much is left.

Acharya Ji explains that this meter is given at the **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching). It is not an electrical device but a **spiritual meter**, one that lets you work out the account of your own deeds. Whatever has gone wrong can still be put right from here on.

*If someone added up your merit today, would you know how much you have left?*

## Why Is "I Alone Do It" a Sign of Ego?

Acharya Ji puts the question directly to his listeners: how many of you believe that success comes purely and entirely from your own effort, and depends on nothing else?

If your understanding is "I alone do it," he says, that sentence is soaked in ego.

Shri Krishna himself says so:

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ**
> **ahaṅkāra-vimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate**
> "In truth, all actions, in every way, are carried out by the qualities of nature. Yet the ignorant one, whose **antahkarana** (inner being) is deluded by ego, believes, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

"All actions" covers everything: your work at the office, your work at home, even the simple act of eating.

## Begin With Five Percent Faith

Acharya Ji is clear that you should at least trust that the Gita is not false. Unless you bring a minimum of five percent **shraddha** (faith), there is little point in the conversation.

And if your view is "I know more than Krishna," he says with a light touch, there is always the next birth. You can take it up with some other Guru then. "Better luck next birth."

## Why Do Our Ancestors Remain Bound?

The first task of this birth is to free your ancestors, so that they need not be born again. Acharya Ji hints that they probably made the same mistake: "I do it. I got it done."

The same mistake binds us too. "How can I attend this Shibir? I have to look after my father, my mother, my mother-in-law, my daughter-in-law, the neighbour, even the dog." We always have a whole world of reasons ready.

Acharya Ji asks whether we really believe they need us, or whether it is the other way round, and we are the ones who need them.

*Be honest with yourself: are the people you feel indispensable to really resting entirely on you?*

## A Direct Blow to the Ego

Acharya Ji warns in advance that today the ego will take many blows, because the subject is freedom from **pitru rina** (the debt owed to one's ancestors), which means your own **moksha** (liberation).

Those who feel "I am serving society" are often the very people who want standing in society. Those who feel "I must heal the sick" actually need patients, because if everyone became healthy, their hospital would close.

He makes clear that this describes ordinary people with ordinary understanding, not great souls or those who have risen to the level of a Guru.

## Reading the Verse Step by Step

**"All actions"**: whatever you do, at home or outside, all of it.

**"In every way"**: whether you act in the yogic way, the Vedic way, the Puranic way, the worldly way or the scientific way, everything falls within it.

**"By the qualities of nature"**: this is the most important phrase of all.

## What Is Nature?

Everything you can see is **prakriti** (nature). We also call her Parvati, Devi, Shakti and Maya.

Within prakriti are the **panchamahabhutas** (the five great elements): fire, earth, air, water and space. We are made of them, and so is this whole universe.

## Prapancha: The Play of Five

Because this is a play of five elements, we call it **prapancha** (the five-fold world, also a common word for worldly or family life).

After someone marries, people ask, "How is your prapancha going?" Without realising it, they are asking, "How deeply are you stuck in Maya?" Yet getting stuck was never the point. We use these words every day without knowing what they mean.

## Not by Me, but Through Me

Actions arise from the **gunas** (the qualities of nature), but a person deluded by ego believes, "I am the doer," when in truth that person never does anything at all.

Acharya Ji explains that none of us performs our actions ourselves. They happen through us.

As long as ignorance remains, it feels as though "this is a body, and I am giving it orders, so it does what I say."

*In everything you do today, how much is "I" and how much is nature?*

## Key Takeaways

- A good present is the fruit of past merit, and that balance can run out.
- The spiritual meter given at the Shibir helps you take stock of your actions.
- According to Gita 3.27, nature's qualities carry out every action; believing "I am the doer" is ego and ignorance.
- The feeling that "they depend on me" is often reversed: we are the ones who need them.
- The path to freeing your ancestors and to your own liberation begins with seeing through the ego of doership.
- Nature is Parvati, Shakti and Maya; this play of the five great elements is prapancha.
