# Chapter: Why Murtis and Symbols Are Necessary

## No One Can Do Without Form

It is said that abroad, in other religions, idol worship is not accepted. But as long as we have form, they too will need some kind of image. Sakar means sa-akar, that is, with form. Have we been given a form or not? If we have a form, then we also need some form in front of us, otherwise we cannot worship at all in the beginning. Later on, in the state of samadhi, you can do whatever is to be done with the formless, but for now something with form is needed.

That is why they too need a cross in some form. If nothing else, they need the moon or stars. If nothing else, to offer a chadar they need some structure; without it, how would they do it? They also circle around in Mecca and Medina. Circumambulation is always around something. There has to be some physical thing first; only then is this possible. So whether they accept it or not, no one can do without form.

The only difference is that we gave it an artistic shape. They have simple shapes like circles; we have artistic shapes. That is the only difference. What the belief is, is not the main point. There will be a symbol, and behind that symbol there will be something. So whoever accepts it, accepts it; and those who do not will still have to accept that everyone needs form.

> "Form is absolutely essential, because you have form."

## We Are Finite, and We Must Worship the Infinite

Now the next question arises. You have form, which means you are limited. This is called saant. Saant means sa-ant, that which has an end. You end somewhere, don't you? The body will reach a boundary somewhere, won't it? Whatever is visible will also have some limitations. This means we are finite. And if you are limited, your sense organs are also limited: hands, feet, ears, nose.

What I told you in the introductory class, I will now explain in detail. If we are finite and we must worship the infinite, then the finite cannot worship the infinite directly.

Ultimately, what has been said? Become one with God. Go into the state of yoga. It is said: practise karma yoga, bhakti yoga, jnana yoga, dhyana yoga. It is all yoga, isn't it? Ultimately we must reach the state of antaryog, that is, reach the God within us. To do yoga means to connect. But the finite does not connect directly with the infinite.

## The Real Worship Is of the Formless

Whom must we really worship? The infinite, the formless. Everyone knows this much. Mahaganapati, Mahadev, Mahadev the God of gods, the final sadhana of Omkar: that is what it is. At the time of death people say, he has merged into the five elements. That is, he has gone where he had to go, into the formless. So the final sadhana was of That alone. But as long as I am alive, I cannot see It directly.

## Only Two Conditions for Yoga

Therefore, for yoga I have only two conditions.

The first is that I must bring that infinite into a finite form. Then one finite, seeing another finite, can interact with it, and through it we can reach the infinite. This is the first step.

What would the second way be? I myself must become infinite. Then I will become one with the infinite, won't I? If I myself become infinite, then the meeting can happen. But can there be two infinites? If there were two infinites, the first would have to end somewhere for the second to begin. That means there cannot be two. There will be only one infinite.

> "There cannot be two infinites, so there will be only one infinite."

That will be your state of samadhi, where you have become one, where your atma tattva has awakened into the param tattva, where the state of advaita has been reached. Either you yourself become that infinite, which is called ritambhara samadhi, or kaivalya pada, or the jivanmukta state. Go into that state where you no longer consider yourself this limited body, where you know yourself as the atma tattva, beyond the body. If you reach that state, your worship will be right; it will be accepted. And if not, you will have to bring It into a form. Do you agree so far?

## Where Did the Murtis Come From?

This is not a theory of mine. I am describing how the murti came about. Where did these different murtis come from? Why did they come? For whom were they made? Otherwise, what was the need for them? If the formless was everything, why did this come separately? Why did forms such as the elephant come about at all? That is what we are explaining.

What was the need to bring the cross or the moon? The moon and stars were fine in the sky; why did they need to be brought down? Why was there a need to join pieces of wood to make a cross? For us too, the elephant, various forms, the vahana, the mouse: why did all these need to be connected with God? There must be some reason. We are trying to know that reason.

## The Murti Comes from the Vision of the Rishis

By whose choice does the murti come? It comes by the choice of the rishis and munis. The form of the Supreme that they saw is the form we need.

> "Start from anywhere, but you must go to the One whose form they have described."
