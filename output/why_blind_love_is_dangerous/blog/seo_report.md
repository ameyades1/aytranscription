## 1. Summary

A three-minute talk on love without discernment. Acharya Ji takes the **Lakshmi tattva** (love, feeling, compassion) as a symbol: on its own it is a little reckless and loves everyone without seeing who stands in front of it. He illustrates it with Jambavati and her son Samba, whose mother kept saying "he will change tomorrow" while he caused destruction; he calls this foolish compassion and links it to **putra moha**. Lakshmi without Narayana becomes rajoguna, which ends in suffering. He then turns it on the listener: giving the whole salary to the temple while neglecting the family, or borrowing to give and show off, is the same blind love. Kali, Saraswati and Lakshmi are each incomplete alone and become useful only with devotion to the Guru; yet Lakshmi is still the most beloved, because it is better to love than to turn to stone. The main intent is emotional and relationship-driven (parents who keep excusing a child, people who give too much to the wrong person), with an informational side (what is putra moh, Lakshmi tattva). The opportunity is a short, tradition-grounded answer to "why is blind love dangerous" and "putra moh kya hai", where results are mostly generic quotes or Dhritarashtra summaries. The post must stay distinct from its sibling `too_bad_to_be_too_good_valentines`, which covers love without discernment in a romantic frame; this one owns the parental-attachment and charity angle.

## 6. FAQ

For the editor's use on the page; not added as FAQPage schema.

1. **पुत्र मोह क्या है? / What is putra moha?**
   हि: संतान के प्रति अंधी आसक्ति, जिसमें माता-पिता "कल सुधरेगा" की आशा में संतान की गलतियों को शक्ति देते रहते हैं।
   En: Blind attachment to one's child, where a parent keeps empowering the child's wrongs in the hope that "he'll change tomorrow."
2. **क्या केवल अच्छा हृदय काफी है? / Is a good heart enough on its own?**
   हि: नहीं। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि केवल लक्ष्मी तत्व, बिना विवेक के, अंधी करुणा बन जाता है और विनाश का कारण बन सकता है।
   En: No. Acharya Ji says the Lakshmi tattva alone, without discernment, becomes blind compassion and can lead to destruction.
3. **नारायण के बिना लक्ष्मी का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "Lakshmi without Narayana" mean?**
   हि: यह प्रतीक है: बिना नारायण के लक्ष्मी रजोगुण बन जाती है, और रजोगुण का अंत दुख है।
   En: It is a symbol: Lakshmi without Narayana becomes rajoguna, and rajoguna ends in suffering (compare Gita 14.16, रजसस्तु फलं दुःखम्).
4. **क्या पूरा वेतन मंदिर या गुरु को दे देना चाहिए? / Should I give my whole salary to the temple?**
   हि: नहीं। परिवार का भी आप पर अधिकार है; पहले उसका पालन पोषण करें, फिर जो बचे वह दान करें। कर्ज लेकर दान या दिखावा न करें।
   En: No. Your family has a rightful claim on you; provide for it first, then give from what remains. Never borrow to give or to show off.
5. **फिर भी लक्ष्मी सबसे प्रिय क्यों हैं? / Why is Lakshmi still the most beloved?**
   हि: क्योंकि उनमें भाव और प्रेम है; पत्थर बन जाने वाले से प्रेम करने वाला अच्छा है।
   En: Because she carries feeling and love; someone who loves is better than someone who has turned to stone.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Gandhari's curse worded as putra moha (Sanskrit review issue 1, major).** In the Mahabharata (Stri Parva 25) the curse is that the Yadu clan would destroy itself, not that it would perish through putra moha; the link to putra moha is Acharya Ji's reading. Since "पुत्र मोह" is the Hindi focus keyword, the title, meta and FAQ present putra moha as the talk's teaching only and never attribute it to Gandhari's curse. Recommendation: the editor applies the review's attributed wording before publishing.
2. **Samba "born to destroy the clan" and "drew power from Shri Krishna" (review issue 2, major).** The scriptural cause is the sages' curse in the Mausala Parva; the rest is the speaker's telling. The meta says only "साम्ब की कथा" / "Samba's story", which is accurate as a story. Recommendation: do not target "Samba" or "Yadu clan destruction" queries; apply the review's attributed wording.
3. **Jambavati example is incoherent (review issue 3, major).** Jambavati's husband is Shri Krishna, so "the husband causes her pain ... Shri Krishna tells her to let go" contradicts itself; the English addition "one of Shri Krishna's queens" makes it explicit. Recommendation: check the transcript and reword; keep Jambavati out of all SEO text. The Also read link to `stop_tolerating_toxic_love` stays valid either way, since it attaches to the general point (enduring pain in the name of dharma).
4. **Lakshmi forbidden to visit her parental home and cursed (review issue 4, minor).** No known source. Recommendation: attribute to Acharya Ji as the review suggests; do not target "Lakshmi curse" queries. The in-text link "जब जब लक्ष्मी का विष्णु से वियोग हुआ" points to a post on Vishnu and Lakshmi and makes no further claim.
5. **"First to emerge in Maa Durga" (review issue 5, minor).** Supported by the Pradhanika Rahasya of the Durga Saptashati, but unsourced in the text; note also that there Mahalakshmi is trigunatmika, not rajasic. Recommendation: name the source as the review suggests; not used in SEO text.
6. **Marathi proverb form (review issue 6, minor).** "ऋण काढून सण साजरा करणे" is the usual form. Editorial only.
7. **Charity and family.** The salary-to-temple example could be read as discouraging giving. The meta and FAQ keep the talk's exact point: provide for the family first, then give; never borrow to give or show off. No fear or guilt framing.
8. **Emotional search intent.** Readers searching "putra moh" may be parents struggling with a child. FAQ and snippet stay compassionate: the problem is love without discernment, not love itself, and the talk ends by saying it is better to love than to turn to stone.
