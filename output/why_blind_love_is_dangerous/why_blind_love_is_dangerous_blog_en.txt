# Why Blind Love Is Dangerous

Is a good heart enough on its own? We tend to assume that whoever loves everyone must be the best of us. Acharya Upendra Ji raises a searching question about that assumption.

## When the Heart Is Wide Open but the Eyes Are Shut

When the **Lakshmi tattva** (the power of love, feeling and compassion) is all you have within, it becomes a little reckless. The heart is enormous and loves everyone, yet it cannot see who is actually standing in front of it.

Consider Jambavati, one of Shri Krishna's queens. Even when the husband causes her pain, she says, "I am a devoted wife; this is my **dharma** (sacred duty)." Shri Krishna tells her to let go, but she refuses.

## The Story of Samba: "He Will Change Tomorrow"

Samba, the son of Shri Krishna and Jambavati, was born to bring about the downfall of the Yadu clan, Shri Krishna's own dynasty. Gandhari, mother of the Kauravas, had laid a curse on it. Just as the Kauravas were ruined by **putra moha** (blind attachment to one's child), this line too would perish through the same attachment.

The curse came true. Jambavati stayed caught in her love for her son. Again and again Samba drew power from Shri Krishna and used it to cause destruction, while his mother's heart kept repeating, "He'll change soon, he'll change tomorrow, he'll change in time." Acharya Ji calls this foolish compassion.

*Are you also telling yourself, about someone close to you, that "they'll change tomorrow"?*

## Lakshmi Without Narayana

Lakshmi alone, without Narayana (Lord Vishnu), turns into **rajoguna** (the quality of restless activity and attachment), and rajoguna always ends in suffering. That is why, in story after story, Lakshmi meets with pain whenever she is separated from Vishnu.

She does not know where to place her love. Even when Vishnu asks her not to go, not even to her parents' home, she goes anyway. For this reason she was cursed many times.

## A Symbol That Speaks About Us

None of this concerns a physical goddess; Lakshmi here is a symbol. Someone says, "My heart is truly good," while every other faculty stays switched off. Their whole month's salary goes straight to the temple.

Was there no love to spare for their own home and family? Is love meant only for the Guru and the temple? Your family has a rightful claim on you too. You brought it into being, so raising and providing for it is your responsibility. Give in charity from whatever remains after that.

Some people even borrow money to fund outside causes, whether for name, fame or charity. A Marathi proverb captures this: "Rin kadhun san sajara kar," which means borrowing money to celebrate a festival. Acharya Ji says we should not live like this.

## Kali, Saraswati and Lakshmi: Each Incomplete Alone

Of the three, the Lakshmi tattva on its own is the most dangerous. When Kali and Saraswati dominate, there is, symbolically speaking, quarrelling and hair-pulling. Lakshmi alone just sits there, forgiving them both.

All three, in their pure and isolated forms, are therefore of little use. They become truly useful once **divine devotion to the Guru** enters them.

*Does your compassion come with discernment?*

## Why Is Lakshmi Still the Most Beloved?

Despite the greater danger, Lakshmi remains everyone's favourite. She carries feeling, **bhava** (the heart's deep emotion), and love. As Acharya Ji puts it, someone who loves is better than someone who has turned to stone.

That is why Lakshmi is held highest among the three, and why tradition says that she is the first to emerge in Maa Durga.

## Key Takeaways

- Love without discernment becomes blind compassion and can lead to destruction.
- Attachment like putra moha keeps empowering what is wrong, in the hope that "they'll change tomorrow."
- Lakshmi without Narayana is rajoguna, and rajoguna ends in suffering.
- Provide for your family first, then give; never borrow to give or to show off.
- Kali, Saraswati and Lakshmi are incomplete alone; devotion to the Guru makes them whole.
- Even so, it is better to love than to turn to stone.
