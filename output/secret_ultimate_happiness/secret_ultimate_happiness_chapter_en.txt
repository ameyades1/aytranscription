# Chapter: Which Knowledge Liberates and Which Binds

## The Paradox of Knowledge and Liberation

Knowledge can never stop you from committing sin. This is clear. Yet the scriptures also say that the knower attains liberation. So today we must understand the difference between these two statements: which knowledge gives liberation and which knowledge keeps us stuck. That is today's topic: how to recognise and classify knowledge.

On one side is bookish knowledge, knowledge gathered on one's own. If it is not realised, or if it comes from a forbidden guru, all of it will only lead to a downfall. The other kind of knowledge comes through a guru who is a knower of the Self, established in that state, *brahmaniṣṭha* and *śrotriya*. What did Krishna say to Arjuna? Only you can see. That is, only the one who is given *śaktipāt dīkṣā* will receive real knowledge. Everyone else's knowledge will only increase sin and add births upon births. Let us see how this happens.

## Body, Mind and Intellect: Your Instruments

First understand your own system. Body, mind and intellect are your instruments. They are not you. The body is yours, the mind is yours, the intellect is yours.

Now see what stands before them. For the body, the object is the whole external world of the five great elements, everything that is seen, and also what is not seen but can be experienced. For the mind, the objects are emotions. For the intellect, the objects are thoughts. So the five elements for the body, emotions for the mind and thoughts for the intellect. We call these the external objects; in Sanskrit they are called *viṣaya*. And body, mind and intellect are your *karaṇa*, your direct means.

## The Sense of Doership Is Ego

Where there are instruments, there must also be a doer. Someone feels: I used this body to touch someone, to obtain something, to run about. With my body I perceive objects, so I become the perceiver. With my mind I feel emotions, so I become the feeler. With my intellect I think, so I become the thinker of my thoughts.

The Bhagavad Gita says clearly on this:

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ |**
> **ahaṅkāravimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate ||**
> "All actions are performed by the qualities of nature; the one deluded by ego thinks, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

All actions happen through nature; you are only the witness. And whoever thinks "I am the doer" is deluded by ego, both egoistic and ignorant. This is the state where a person takes himself to be the doer. So if you feel that you work through this body and this mind, that you thought, then according to the Gita its true name is ego. Read the third chapter of the Gita. Here I am only introducing how this happens.

## The Good I Did, the Bad Someone Else Did

There are many examples showing that you do not really act. This very thought that "I do" is ego. Notice: every good deed you do, you feel that you did it. You crossed the road safely, so you did it. You saved someone, so you saved them. I am a doctor, I saved many lives, so I saved them. You gave someone money, paid off their debt, so you paid it. A judge saved someone, delivered justice, and feels: I saved them.

Now look at the reverse. When you have an accident, who caused it? Then they say God did. You went travelling and got dengue; why did that mosquito bite only you? Then they say the municipality did not do its job there, did nothing at all, so it happened; I did not do it. That is, I never do bad deeds, I only do good ones. Think about it: I saved someone from an accident just in time, so I saved them; I had an accident, so he caused it.

> "Every person says, 'I did the good deed.' Then who is doing the bad deeds?"

This is the experience of the whole world. Everyone is doing only good deeds! Then there must be some power that does all your good and bad actions. Right now you are in the state where there is no understanding of the doer and the one who causes doing. This is the first, early stage. After that one understands that I alone am the doer and the cause of doing, but this "I" is not the body; it is the "I" of the Self. As long as you feel that you are the doer, that is called ego.

## Why Knowledge Does Not Stop Sin

Now this person thinks that if he increases his intellect, his problems will go away. He wants knowledge, so he collects spiritual lectures, spiritual courses, spiritual books, spiritual videos, and swells his intellect. What will that do? Only the intellect will grow, and along with it this "I" becomes very big: ego, the sense of "I am". Beyond this it can do nothing.

> "Knowledge does not stop you from committing sin."

Let this settle in your mind first. Sin does not stop by coming to satsang, it does not stop by doing courses, it does not stop by reading books. Rather, if you have come only to listen, twelve or sixteen more births will be added. Why would this happen? The question may have arisen in someone's mind. These are not my words, they are Krishna's: knowledge does not stop sin, knowledge adds birth upon birth; and yet, liberation for the knower. The scriptures say such seemingly contradictory things, and it is to understand them that we study Vedanta.

What is the reason? These are your instruments. The mind is greater than the body, and the intellect is greater than the mind. The human being has the most intellect, and it is given for moving towards liberation. But the intellect's own nature is to increase ego. The more you study, automatically, without doing anything, the ego grows: I am the perceiver, I am the feeler, I am the thinker. This feeling will come for certain, and you will certainly try to put others down.

## Vasana: The Master of All Three

Let me tell you why this happens. There is one more element, which we call *vāsanā*. Your horoscope, your *saṁskāras*, are just another name for it. The negative *saṁskāras* that remain are called *vāsanā*.

*Vāsanā* is not a bad word. It means tendency, the tendency to do something. The *vāsanā* for food is the tendency to eat. *Kāma vāsanā* is the tendency for sex. *Loka vāsanā* is the tendency for popularity. *Deha vāsanā* is the tendency to keep the body perfect and well-built. So *vāsanā* means the horoscope that has come according to your good and bad *saṁskāras*.

This is the real problem, because *vāsanā* is the master of all three: body, mind and intellect. However much the subordinate studies, however much this "I" studies, this *vāsanā* will trouble it. It will use the intellect only according to the *vāsanā*. And the *vāsanā* says: I will create rebirth upon rebirth. Through this intellect it adds one more *vāsanā*, then another, and births keep increasing.

So is there a different way of receiving knowledge? This way cannot be it. That is why you see people doing wrong things, being egoistic, putting others down.

## The Relation of God, Vasana and Mind

For now, understand this much: God, your *vāsanā*, meaning your negative *saṁskāras*, and your mind, meaning the *jīvātmā*, are directly connected.

Above even the *vāsanā* is Om, the divine, God. Call it Allah, call it Christ, call it Buddha, that is not my question; we are calling it Omkar. From it comes the power; it is the Self, the supreme principle. But in between you have placed a filter. What filter? The filter of *vāsanā*, of your horoscope, of your likes and dislikes, of your *rāga-dveṣa*.

The guru says many things, but you can immediately say: this cannot be. Someone will surely say that. That is your *vāsanā*, which rejects or modifies what the intellect receives and learns in its own way. Because the intellect is under the *vāsanā*, not above it. The *vāsanā* will always stay in control.

See this order: the mind is the master of the body, the intellect is the master of the mind, the *vāsanā* is the master of all three, and the master of the *vāsanā* is the Self, Omkar. This *vāsanā* sits like a middle manager. It takes the order from above but does not pass it down properly. The intellect says, I will do my own practice, and the *vāsanā* says, sit quietly, you will do exactly as I want. That is why the births of a knower can increase, and a knower can also attain liberation. We will see how.

## The Jivatma: The Wandering Soul

Mind and soul, *jīva* and *ātmā*, together are called the *jīvātmā*. This same state can be that of a ghost, and also that of an ordinary being with a deluded mind. One has a body, the other does not; that is the only difference. Both are wandering through life.

One wanders like a madman with a body. Someone may be wandering outside a liquor den for liquor, someone outside a court, and someone outside a library for the scriptures. The embodied one wanders with the body. This is called the *jīvātmā*.

## The Sadguru: One Who Has Conquered the Mind

So when will liberation come? Let us move ahead. There will be a person who has conquered; he is called the Sadguru. He has won over his own mind. He is *bhāvātīta*, gone beyond emotions. The emotions are gone, so the "I" that feels is also gone.

> **bhāvātītaṁ triguṇarahitaṁ sadguruṁ taṁ namāmi ||**
> "I bow to that Sadguru who is beyond emotions and free of the three qualities."
> (Guru Stotram)

The link of *vāsanā* has been cut for the Sadguru. The moment this link is cut, the *vāsanā* ends; when the *vāsanā* ends, the ego ends; when the ego ends, the feeling "I do" never arises in him. Now he experiences that everything happens through Omkar.

His body remains, his intellect remains, the external world remains, and thoughts remain. Of these nine, these four and the original supreme principle remain. Is the guru's body alive? It is. Does the external world still exist for him? You are here, so it does. He still has an intellect, and thoughts still arise in it, about the work of the nation, about many tasks. But now this is a state free of *vāsanā*. Now the *parā vāṇī* of Omkar will come directly into his intellect. The guru's thoughts are God's thoughts.

## Which Knower Attains Liberation

The scriptures said liberation for the knower; which knower? Listen. Whoever has become one with Omkar, the supreme principle, such a Sadguru will have no *vāsanā* left. He no longer has a mind, meaning no emotion remains; he is beyond emotion, in a state free of ego. The thoughts that arise directly in his mind and intellect will be God's. That is called *parā vāṇī*. And he expresses it through his mouth, his gestures, his body; that is called Gurvani.

> "Take the Gurvani directly into the intellect, without touching the mind, without asking questions, and silently carry it out with the body."

That one is called the surrendered disciple, the true disciple. For one who has this much knowledge, it is said: liberation for the knower. All the rest of knowledge will come from this Omkar itself. The question is: have I defeated this *vāsanā* or not? Has my horoscope been cut or not? This is what was troubling me. Rise above your *rāga-dveṣa*, your likes and dislikes, and listen silently to the guru's words; only then is liberation attained.

## Vasana Distorts Knowledge

Why? Because there is no other scope. You too could hear it from there; this Omkar is for you as well, you too are Brahman. But this *vāsanā* sits in between, and because of it your knowledge too gets distorted. That is why you do not see a wife in your wife, you see a demoness; you do not see a goddess there. You do not see a husband in your husband, you do not see Shiva; you see some demon there. Why? Because this *vāsanā* has grown so big and sits there; it is the master of all three.

And the guru says: the same voice comes to me too, but it comes from above even your *vāsanā*. That is, your master is the same one; he is the common master of us both. The message has already come to me from there; my sense of "I" has been cut. I will pass the message directly from there to you.

That is why the one who listens to the guru's remedy silently, without questioning, is the one who benefits. And there is no other way besides this.
