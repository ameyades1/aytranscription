# The Secret of Ultimate Happiness: Which Knowledge Liberates

## Why Doesn't Knowledge Save You from Sin?

Knowledge can never stop you from committing sin. And yet the scriptures say: liberation for the knower.

So are both true? Yes. We only need to understand which knowledge gives liberation and which knowledge keeps us stuck.

Bookish knowledge, knowledge gathered on your own, or knowledge from a forbidden guru, if it is not realised, leads towards a downfall. Liberating knowledge comes from a guru who knows the Self, who is **brahmaniṣṭha** (established in Brahman) and **śrotriya** (versed in the scriptures). Just as Krishna told Arjuna that only he could see, only the one who receives **śaktipāt dīkṣā** (the guru's direct transmission of consciousness) receives real knowledge. Everyone else's knowledge increases both sin and births.

## You Are Not Body, Mind and Intellect

Body, mind and intellect are your instruments, not you. In Sanskrit they are called **karaṇa** (means).

Each instrument has its own object:

- For the **body**: the five great elements, this whole world that is seen and experienced
- For the **mind**: emotions
- For the **intellect**: thoughts

Where there are instruments, there seems to be a doer too. Through the body I become the perceiver, through the mind the feeler, through the intellect the thinker.

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ |**
> **ahaṅkāravimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate ||**
> "All actions are performed by the qualities of nature; the one deluded by ego thinks, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

All actions happen through nature; you are the witness. Whoever thinks "I do" is deluded by ego. According to the Gita, this sense of doership is itself **ego**.

## The Good I Did, the Bad Someone Else Did

Just look at yourself. Crossed the road safely: I did it. Saved someone: I saved them. A doctor saved lives, a judge delivered justice, someone's debt was paid off: every time, "I".

And when there was an accident? Then God did it. Got dengue? The municipality didn't do its job.

Acharya Ji asks: if every person says they did the good deed, then who is doing the bad deeds?

Some power does all the good and bad actions. This is the early stage. Later one understands that I alone am the doer and the cause of doing, but not the "I" of the body: the "I" of the **Self**.

*Which good deed did you sign your name to today with "I did it", and which trouble did you put in someone else's name?*

## Why Studying More Grows the Ego

A person thinks that if he grows his intellect, his problems will go. So he collects spiritual lectures, courses, books, videos.

The result? The intellect grows, and with it the "I" becomes very big. Sin does not stop by coming to satsang, doing a course or reading a book. If you have come only to listen, twelve to sixteen more births may be added.

These are not my words, they are Krishna's: knowledge does not stop sin, it adds birth upon birth; and yet, liberation for the knower. Vedanta is studied precisely to understand such paradoxes.

Human beings have been given the most intellect to move towards liberation. But the intellect's nature is to increase ego. The more you study, the more "I am the perceiver, I am the feeler, I am the thinker" grows, and so does the urge to put others down.

## Vasana: The Real Boss

The reason is **vāsanā**. Your horoscope, your *saṁskāras*, especially the negative *saṁskāras* that remain, are the *vāsanā*.

*Vāsanā* is not a bad word. It means **tendency**:

- The *vāsanā* for food: the tendency to eat
- *Kāma vāsanā*: the tendency for sex
- *Loka vāsanā*: the tendency for popularity
- *Deha vāsanā*: the tendency to keep the body perfect and well-built

The problem is that *vāsanā* is the boss of all three: body, mind and intellect. However much the intellect studies, it will move only according to the *vāsanā*. And the *vāsanā* says: I will create rebirth upon rebirth.

## The Filter in Between

At the very top is **Om**, God. Call it Allah, call it Christ, call it Buddha; we call it Omkar. From it comes the power.

But you have placed a filter in between: of *vāsanā*, of likes and dislikes, of **rāga-dveṣa** (attachment and aversion). The guru says something and you immediately say, "This cannot be." That is your *vāsanā* speaking.

The order is: the mind is the boss of the body, the intellect of the mind, the *vāsanā* of all three, and Omkar is the boss of the *vāsanā*. The *vāsanā* is like a middle manager who takes the order from above but does not pass it down properly.

Acharya Ji explains that because of *vāsanā* our knowledge too gets distorted. That is why one sees not a goddess in one's wife but a demoness, and not Shiva in one's husband but some demon.

*When something true makes you say "no" at once, is that "no" coming from your intellect or from your vasana?*

## The Jivatma: The Wandering Soul

Mind and soul together are the **jīvātmā**. The only difference between a ghost and an ordinary person with a deluded mind is that one has a body and the other does not. Both are wandering.

Someone wanders outside a liquor den, someone outside a court, and someone outside a library for the scriptures.

## The Sadguru: One Whose Vasana Is Cut

The path to liberation opens through the one who has conquered the mind: the **Sadguru**.

> **bhāvātītaṁ triguṇarahitaṁ sadguruṁ taṁ namāmi ||**
> "I bow to that Sadguru who is beyond emotions and free of the three qualities."
> (Guru Stotram)

When the emotions go, the "I" that feels goes. When the *vāsanā* is cut, the ego goes, and with it the feeling "I do".

His body, intellect, thoughts and the external world still exist. But now there is no filter in between. The **parā vāṇī** (the voice that comes directly from God) of Omkar comes straight into his intellect. He expresses it through his mouth, his gestures, his body. This is **Gurvani**.

## Which Knower Attains Liberation

Acharya Ji says that we must take the Gurvani directly into the intellect, without touching the mind, without asking questions, and silently carry it out with the body.

One who does this is the **surrendered disciple** (fully surrendered disciple). For the one who has this much knowledge, it is said: liberation for the knower.

Omkar is for you too; you too are Brahman. But your *vāsanā* distorts the message. The guru says: we both have the same boss, but my link of *vāsanā* has been cut, so the message comes straight to me and I will pass it straight to you.

That is why the one who listens to the guru's remedy silently, without questioning, is the one who benefits. And there is no other way besides this.

*Can you listen to something without putting it through the filter of your likes and dislikes?*

## Key Takeaways

- Body, mind and intellect are your instruments; the feeling "I do" is itself ego.
- Bookish and self-gathered knowledge does not stop sin; it increases ego and births.
- *Vāsanā*, the tendency formed by *saṁskāras*, is the boss of body, mind and intellect, and it distorts knowledge.
- The Sadguru is beyond *vāsanā* and emotion; God's *parā vāṇī* comes straight into his intellect.
- Liberation comes to the disciple who rises above *rāga-dveṣa* and puts the Gurvani into practice without questioning.
