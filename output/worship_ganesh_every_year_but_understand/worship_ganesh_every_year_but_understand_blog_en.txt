# We Worship Lord Ganesha Every Year, but Have We Understood Him?

## The Ganesha in Every Home

Lord Ganesha lives in every home. He may sit in your own house, in a relative's, or in the long tradition of your family. Even if there is no idol at home, all of us revere him. At the very least, you accept that there is a presence called Ganpati Bappa.

We know the rituals well: the **sahasravartan** (a thousand recitations) of the **Atharvashirsha** (the Upanishadic hymn to Lord Ganesha), the daily puja, the ringing bell, the twenty-one blades of **durva** (a sacred grass offered to him) and, of course, the **modak** (the sweet dumpling he loves). Everything else may be forgotten, but never the modak!

Then comes the **visarjan** (the immersion of the idol), with singing and dancing to the chant "Ganpati Bappa Morya, pudhchya varshi lavkar ya" (Ganpati Bappa, come back soon next year). There is joy on the seashore or the riverbank, and the aarti every day. Most of us have done at least this much.

## Why, Then, Do We Need a Shibir?

This is where the real question begins. If this is all we had to do, why hold a **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching)? Where does **vidya** (true knowledge) come into it?

First, consider what Lord Ganesha stands for:

- **Wisdom and learning**: he is the lord of intelligence
- **Vighnaharta**: the one who removes obstacles
- **Vinayaka**: the supreme leader, a symbol of leadership

*You worship Lord Ganesha every year, but have you ever asked how much of his nature has entered your own life?*

## So Much Worship, Yet So Many Obstacles?

Nowhere in the world do people worship Lord Ganesha as much as in India. Think of all the modaks, all the durva, all the red flowers. By that logic, India should face no obstacles at all. The Lord should be delighted with us, and every person here should be full of discernment and clear thinking.

The reality is different. Our country ranks among the lowest in the world on the happiness index. We have everything, yet we lag behind in happiness. India is also the diabetes capital of the world. Acharya Ji asks: if everything here is so sacred and so well done, how can this be happening?

## You Have Faith, but Do You Also Have Knowledge?

The plain answer is that something is going wrong. There are three possibilities:

1. We perform the rituals without understanding them.
2. We have faith, but it may have slipped into **andhashraddha** (blind faith).
3. We have faith, but we lack **knowledge**.

The scriptures say you need both: you should be a devotee who also knows. In the Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna speaks of the **jnani bhakta** (the wise devotee), whose devotion is joined with understanding. The Gita names such a person as worthy of **moksha** (liberation).

Science, too, tells us that energy can change from one form into another. When you worship with understanding, your worship aligns with the result you seek.

*Is your worship only a tradition, or do you also understand what lies behind it?*

## What Does Abhishek Mean?

What does **abhishek** (the ritual bathing of a deity) really mean? Do you know?

If the meaning does not come to you right now, don't worry. The result is bound to come. Freedom from a crisis will begin even before the crisis arrives, and it will begin at once. Acharya Ji says you will see for yourself how this magic works.

## Key Takeaways

- Lord Ganesha is the lord of wisdom and learning, the remover of obstacles, and Vinayaka, the symbol of leadership.
- India worships Lord Ganesha more than any other land, yet suffers low happiness and widespread disease, a sign that something is going wrong.
- Worship without understanding, blind faith, and faith without knowledge all remain incomplete.
- The Bhagavad Gita asks you to become a jnani bhakta, holding faith and knowledge together.
- Worship with understanding brings sure results, and freedom begins even before a crisis arrives.
