# The Spiritual Meaning of Secularism: Show the Fruit of Your Dharma

On this occasion of Independence, a question arises. This land is Hindustan, the land of Sanatan Dharma. Yet we say we are a secular country.

Is this a contradiction? Or is there a deeper spiritual meaning behind it? Let us understand it through the Guru's eyes.

## What Does It Mean to Be Secular?

In common understanding, you are free to practise any faith you wish. It is not only about dharma.

One says, I do not believe in God. Another says, I believe in God. Someone believes in a particular deity. In this country, all of this is your right.

But the Guru says this issue must be understood, because it is connected to spirituality. No king is giving a speech here; the Guru is speaking.

## One Truth, Many Paths

The spiritual idea behind secularism lies in a single line of the Veda:

> **ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti**
> "Truth is one; the wise speak of it in many ways."
> (Rig Veda 1.164.46)

Truth is one, the truth of the Supreme Lord. The wise explain it in different ways. That is fine, as long as they are all speaking of that one truth.

One who teaches wrongly is no scholar at all. But if all religions and paths convey the same truth, such as a message of love, then all is well.

## The Real Question: How Do I Live My Own Dharma?

The Guru's message is clear. The point is not whether the country is secular or belongs to one religion. The point is **how well I follow my own culture and faith.**

The other person will follow his religion in his own way. Abusing him or hating him brings no benefit.

Acharya Ji says that if we can show the fruit of our faith, no one can break us.

**Phalashruti** means the fruit of a practice, its visible result. Do your sadhana. Show through your life what cannot otherwise be seen.

*Is my faith visible in my conduct, or only in my words?*

## Atithi Devo Bhava, but with Discernment

India was never against any religion. The very meaning of Sanatan Dharma is that we are open to all. We say:

> **atithidevo bhava**
> "Treat the guest as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2)

Regard the guest as a god. But the Guru gives a plain example.

The guest walked straight into your bedroom. From tomorrow he began deciding what is cooked in the kitchen. Then he took over your wealth. And finally he said, from now on you live outside in a refugee area.

So whose fault is it? Yours. The Guru says this is what we have usually done.

Welcoming does not mean keeping anyone, anywhere, in any manner. The rules for those who come from another religion or country are not new; they are very old. That is why the Guru also wishes to share a little of Chanakya's (Kautilya's) Arthashastra and statecraft.

## Not Hatred, but Firmness

Acharya Ji explains that no religion has ever grown great by hating others or by pointing out their faults.

The essential point is how firm I can make my own dharma. That is the whole essence of Antar Yog.

## First, Show the Fruit of the Yamas

Patanjali described the eight limbs of yoga (Ashtanga Yoga):

> **yama-niyama-āsana-prāṇāyāma-pratyāhāra-dhāraṇā-dhyāna-samādhayo 'ṣṭāv aṅgāni**
> "Restraint, observance, posture, breath control, withdrawal of the senses, concentration, meditation and absorption are the eight limbs."
> (Patanjali Yoga Sutra 2.29)

Yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana and samadhi. This is the path to liberation. But the Guru says, take only the first limb, yama.

> **ahiṃsā-satya-asteya-brahmacarya-aparigrahā yamāḥ**
> "Non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, continence and non-possessiveness are the yamas."
> (Patanjali Yoga Sutra 2.30)

- **Satya** (Truthfulness)
- **Ahimsa** (Non-violence)
- **Asteya** (Non-stealing)
- **Brahmacharya** (Continence)
- **Aparigraha** (Non-possessiveness)

Just show the fruit of these five. Then say, I am a Hindu. Then say, I am a yogi. Then say, we believe in the Sutras of Patanjali.

*If someone looked at my life today, would they see even one of these five yamas in it?*

## Key Takeaways

- Truth is one; the wise speak of it in many ways (ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti).
- Whether or not the country is secular, the real question is how well I live my own dharma.
- Abusing or hating others brings no benefit; no religion has grown great that way.
- Atithi devo bhava, but with discernment; welcoming does not mean handing over your home.
- Show the fruit of your faith, and no one can break you.
- Before calling yourself a Hindu or a yogi, bring the five yamas into your life.
