# Why Does Lakshmi Come to Some People and Not Others?

## How Big Is Your Goal?

What is your aim in life? How big is your goal? Do you want to run the world, run a household, or something else?

We often think of Lakshmi as nothing more than an idol in the prayer room. She is not an idol. She is **lakshya** (goal): the single point of focus that makes you say, "This is what I must do, this is what I must achieve."

Acharya Ji says that Lakshmi comes to us when we gain the strength to pursue that goal.

So why does Lakshmi come to some people and not others? She comes to those who gain the strength to pursue a goal. She is restless and does not stay when she comes alone. Her red sari shows what she comes with: unconditional love for those you serve, full-strength action, and sacrifice for society.

## Everyone Has a Goal of Their Own

Some people want to run a household. Others want to run a state or a nation. And some want to do something for the whole world.

The British set out to conquer the world and, to a degree, succeeded. Alexander also wanted to conquer the whole world. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj wanted to free everyone and establish **swarajya** (self-rule).

Each of them had a goal, and no undertaking reaches completion without Lakshmi.

## Why Doesn't Lakshmi Stay?

Lakshmi never comes alone. When she does arrive alone, she does not stay for long, because she is **chanchal** (restless, fickle). Tradition compares Lakshmi on her own to an unmarried woman: nothing binds her to any one place.

Who, then, does she come with? The answer lies hidden in her red garment.

## The First Meaning of the Red Sari: Love

Lakshmi is not a woman standing somewhere. Her red sari is the colour of blood, the colour of love, the colour of the heart. Mother Lakshmi is a symbol of **love**, and she lives in the heart.

Wealth comes when you can hold **unconditional love** within you.

Start by asking how much you love your customers. If you love only the money and not the people you serve, wealth will not come.

Love does not mean sitting with your arm around someone's neck. Love is a feeling, a state of the heart.

*In your work, is your love for the people you serve, or only for the money?*

## The Second Meaning of the Red Sari: Rajoguna and the Spirit of Offering

Red is also the colour of blood, and your blood should be up. Like fire, you should work with all your strength.

Lakshmi also stands for **rajoguna** (the quality of activity and drive). Tradition calls this **yajna bhava** (the spirit of sacrificial offering). We describe the fire of a yajna, the sacred fire ritual, as yellow, yet it is understood to carry a red glow.

This is why the section of the **Durga Saptashati** (the seven hundred verses in praise of the Divine Mother) devoted to Mother Mahalakshmi opens with these words:

> **yajurvedaḥ svarūpam**
> "The Yajurveda is its very form."
> (Durga Saptashati, Madhyama Charitra, Viniyoga)

The Yajurveda stands for the spirit of offering. A yajna means **tyaga** (sacrifice): the willingness to give something up as an offering.

The more you sacrifice for society, the more society gives back to you. If you want everything without giving anything, it will not last. Even if your destiny hands you something, you will not keep it.

## Lakshmi and Alakshmi

Mother Lakshmi follows a few simple rules, and the scriptures describe them. The Durga Saptashati says:

> **yā śrīḥ svayaṃ sukṛtināṃ bhavaneṣv alakṣmīḥ**
> **pāpātmanāṃ kṛtadhiyāṃ hṛdayeṣu buddhiḥ**
> "You, O Devi, are prosperity itself in the homes of the virtuous and misfortune in the homes of the wicked; you are understanding in the hearts of the wise."
> (Durga Saptashati 4.5)

Here the Goddess enters the homes of the virtuous as Lakshmi and the homes of wrongdoers as **Alakshmi** (misfortune, the opposite of Lakshmi).

Does a lack of money mean you are a sinner? Not necessarily. You may be passing through a temporary phase, reaping the fruit of some past action. Or you may have become a **vairagi** (a renunciate, free of worldly desire) like Sai Baba.

*Test yourself honestly: have I truly renounced the world, or do I still have karma left to work through?*

## Lord Vishnu Sustains the World: Whom Do You Sustain?

Lakshmi is married to **Lord Vishnu**, the Sustainer, who looks after the entire universe.

So ask yourself: whom are you looking after? If you decide you will provide for two people at home, Lakshmi says, "That is all you mean to provide for? Then that is all the wealth I will give you." Those who want to work for the world receive the wealth the world's work requires.

Acharya Ji reminds us that the root of the name Lakshmi is lakshya, so the real question is where your focus lies.

## The Owner and the Employee

The owner of an office grows wealthy because the owner's goal is bigger. The employee earns less.

If employees made the company's goal their own, they too would gain a great deal. But if an employee only thinks, "I'll take my salary from here and run my home," then that employee's goal is a household of four. The wealth that comes will be enough for four people, and whether it lasts is another matter.

## The Sweeper and the Cleaning Company

One person sweeps floors. Another starts an entire cleaning company. The labourer stays a labourer because their thinking stays small.

This is not about good or bad people. The richest person may be the biggest thief, and the poorest may be **sattvic** (pure and virtuous). The point here is about Lakshmi alone: to whom does wealth come?

## Think Big, Provide Big

Wealth comes to those who have many to provide for. Sometimes the timing is wrong, and destiny brings a difficult **dasha** (a planetary period in Vedic astrology). Yet those who think big are bound to rise from pauper to king.

*Today, how many people does your goal provide for?*

## Key Takeaways

- Lakshmi is not just an idol but **lakshya**, a goal; she comes when you gain the strength to pursue it.
- Lakshmi is restless; when she comes alone, she does not stay.
- Her red garment stands for love: love the people you serve unconditionally, not just the money.
- Red also stands for rajoguna and the spirit of offering: act with full strength and sacrifice for society.
- The Goddess comes to the virtuous as Lakshmi and to wrongdoers as Alakshmi; a lack of money may also be the fruit of past action or a sign of renunciation.
- Lakshmi is the consort of Lord Vishnu, the Sustainer: the more you provide for, the more wealth you receive.
- Those who think big rise from pauper to king, even after hard times brought by destiny.
