# Chapter: Why Does Lakshmi Come Only to Some People?

## Lakshmi Is Not an Idol, She Is a Goal

What is your aim? How big is your goal? To run the world, to run a household, or to run something else?

We keep Lakshmi merely as an idol. But she is not an idol. She is *lakshya*, a goal. A goal means one aim, one point of focus: I have to do something, I have to attain something.

Someone feels, I have to run my household. Someone feels, I have to run a kingdom. Someone feels, I have to run a nation. And someone feels, I have to do something for the world. The British felt they had to conquer the whole world, and to some extent they did conquer it. Alexander felt he had to conquer the whole world. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj felt he had to free all these people and establish his own *swarajya*, self-rule.

Every person has a goal of their own. When the power to achieve that goal comes, Lakshmi comes to you. Without Lakshmi, none of your work gets done.

## Why Lakshmi Does Not Stay Alone

Lakshmi never comes alone. When she comes alone, she does not stay for long, because Lakshmi is restless. It is said that Lakshmi alone is like one unmarried; she has no bond.

## The Secret of the Red Garment: Love

Now, Lakshmi is not a woman. So where does she stand? Is she just a goddess in a red sari? The red sari means the colour of blood, the colour of love, the colour of the heart. Mother Lakshmi is the symbol of love and dwells in the heart.

So the first thing: there must be selfless love within you. When you can love without conditions, wealth comes to you. Lakshmi says, "Only then will I come. First tell me, how much do you love your customer? If you love only the money and not them, it will not work."

You will ask, what does love mean, sitting with an arm around someone's neck? That is not what is meant. Love is a feeling.

## The Second Meaning of Red: Rajoguna and the Spirit of Yajna

Red is also the colour of blood. The blood should boil. One should work like fire, with full power. Because Lakshmi is also the symbol of *rajas* shakti. *Rajoguna* means activity, dynamism. This is what is called the spirit of *yajna*.

We call the colour of the sacrificial fire yellow, but it is considered to have a reddish tinge. That is why, when we read the Durga Saptashati, at the beginning of Mother Mahalakshmi's episode it is written:

> **yajurvedaḥ svarūpam**
> "Its form is the Yajurveda."
> (Durga Saptashati, Middle Episode, Viniyoga)

Yajurveda means the spirit of yajna. Yajna means sacrifice, the tendency to offer something. The more you sacrifice for society, the more society gives you. If you give nothing and want everything to come to you, it does not come for long. Even if it comes, something comes from *prarabdha*, past karma, but it does not stay.

## Lakshmi and Alakshmi

Mother Lakshmi has some straightforward rules, because they are described in the scriptures. The Durga Saptashati says that the Goddess herself comes as Lakshmi to the virtuous and as Alakshmi to sinners:

> **yā śrīḥ svayaṃ sukṛtināṃ bhavaneṣvalakṣmīḥ pāpātmanāṃ kṛtadhiyāṃ hṛdayeṣu buddhiḥ**
> "She who is Lakshmi herself in the homes of the virtuous, Alakshmi in the homes of the sinful, and wisdom in the hearts of the wise."
> (Durga Saptashati 4.5)

That is, the Goddess who dwells in the homes of the virtuous as Lakshmi and in the homes of the sinful as Alakshmi.

So you will say, am I a sinner because I have no money? Perhaps it is a temporary phase. Some karma is being worked out, there must be some reason. Or the other possibility is that you have become like Sai Baba, and then one is a renunciate. So examine yourself: have I become a renunciate, or is some karma of mine still remaining?

## Vishnu Is the Sustainer: Whom Are You Sustaining?

Lakshmi is married to Vishnu. Who is Vishnu? The sustainer. He sustains the whole world, and Lakshmi is his wife.

So tell me, whom are you sustaining? If you thought, I will sustain the two people of my house, Lakshmi says, "You are only that much of a sustainer, aren't you? You only have to care for two people, so I will give that much wealth." If someone says, I have to do work for the world, then the wealth the world needs will come.

So first think, what is your goal? The very word Lakshmi has its origin in *lakshya*, goal. What is your focus? How big is your goal?

## The Owner and the Employee

When you go to the office, the owner there is wealthy, because his goal is big. His employee earns less. If the employee's goal is tied to the company, he too will receive a great deal.

But if his thinking is, let me take money from here and run my household, then his goal is not the company; his goal is his home, a small one, of four people. So he will have only as much wealth as four people need. Whether it stays or not is a later matter; first, only that much will come. Wealth comes when you hold big thinking.

## The Sweeper and the Cleaning Company

Someone may do a small job, a sweeper perhaps. And someone else opens an entire cleaning company. The cleaning worker remains a worker, because he holds small-scale thinking, and so only that much wealth comes.

I am not talking here about good or bad people. It may be that the richest man is the biggest thief, and the poorest is *sattvic*, pure. That is not the subject here. Here we are talking about Lakshmi.

## One with Big Thinking Rises from Pauper to King

To whom does wealth come? Wealth comes to the one who has to sustain much. You have to accept your own thinking.

Sometimes it happens that the time has not yet come, that due to prarabdha your circumstances are bad. But one whose thinking is big will surely rise from pauper to king.
