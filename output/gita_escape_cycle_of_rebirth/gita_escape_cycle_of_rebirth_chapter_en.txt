# Chapter: The Secret of Freedom from Old Age and Death

## Will That Day Come for Me Too?

I have a question for you. You must have seen many such WhatsApp messages: this actor has passed away, that actor has passed away, a bomb blast has happened somewhere. You heard it all, forwarded it, discussed it, and every night you watch the news. But the question is: have you ever thought that one day that day will come for me too? And what preparation have I made for it?

To those who say, "What now? We have grown old, we are as good as dead already," Shri Krishna says that you have gone astray somewhere.

## Have You Understood Your Duty?

What do you think, are you fulfilling all your duties properly? How many of you feel that you have understood the whole of spirituality, that you have understood all your duties, what you must do in life, and that you have even understood "who am I"? How many such people are here?

So listen carefully, once, to what Shri Krishna says on this. This is the twenty-ninth verse of the seventh chapter of the Gita:

> **jarā-maraṇa-mokṣāya mām āśritya yatanti ye**
> **te brahma tad viduḥ kṛtsnam adhyātmaṁ karma cākhilam**
> "Those who take refuge in Me and strive for liberation from old age and death know that Brahman, the whole of the spiritual Self, and all action."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.29)

Those who take refuge in Me and make an effort, *yatna*, to be free from *jarā*, old age, and from death, they know that Brahman, the whole of spirituality and the whole of action.

## The Plain Meaning of This Verse

This is a profound aphorism, but did you at least understand its plain, literal meaning? Shri Krishna has said something. When was the whole of action known? When was the whole of spirituality known? Who knew the whole of Brahman, that Supreme Being, and when? When you make at least a little effort. For what? To be free from old age and death. First understand the sentence; I will explain its deeper symbolic meaning right now.

## The Meaning of Surrender

At least this much is clear, that there is something here: "those who take refuge in Me", that is, going to the Guru, surrendering, and understanding from him. To give up the ego is to take refuge. To bow before someone is itself to take refuge. So only when the ego goes will there be surrender.

But what is one to learn from the Guru there? The path to freedom from old age and death. Whoever understood this understood action, understood spirituality, understood Brahman.

I am speaking of the Bhagavad Gita itself. Now do not say that the Bhagavad Gita might be wrong. Yes, I will explain its deeper meaning in a little while. But how many of you now believe that if Shri Krishna said it, he must have said it rightly? Good. So what does it mean? I will tell you.

## The Four Sights of Gautama Buddha

Everyone knows the story of Gautama Buddha. In his childhood an astrologer told his father that this child would become either a very great Guru or a very great king. His father must have been a man of ordinary understanding; he wished his son to become a great king. So for a full sixteen years, from childhood, he kept him in *māyā*. He never let him go outside the splendour of the royal palace.

Then one day, somehow, he went out with his charioteer. As soon as he was on the road, the first thing he saw was an old man. He had never seen such a thing, because his father wanted nothing that could awaken dispassion. Seeing the old man, he asked, "Who is this?" The charioteer said, "This is an old man." "What is that?" "After a certain age this happens. The hair turns white, a person walks with a stick, wrinkles appear on the body." He asked, "Will this happen to me too?" "Yes, it will happen to you too." Then he said, "Then I do not want this."

Going further, he saw a sick man. "Who is this?" "This is a sick man." "What does that mean?" "The functioning of his body is not working well." "Can this happen to me too?" "Yes, it certainly can." "No, then I do not want this."

After that he saw a funeral procession. "What is this?" "This is a dead man. He has gone, he has departed." "Will this happen to me too?" "Yes, certainly, one day you too will be in this state." "Then I do not want this." And in that very moment he resolved that he must become a saint.

## Why Do We Not Wake Up Even While Watching the News?

Now my question is the same again. You have seen so many such WhatsApp messages: this actor died, that actor died, a hundred people were killed in such a big bomb blast. You heard all this, forwarded it, even discussed why some actor took his own life, or whether he was hanged, or whether it was murder. Every night you watch the news. But while forwarding all this, did you ever truly think that my day too will come? And what precaution have I taken for it?

## The True Meaning of Freedom from Old Age

Shri Krishna speaks of the one who understands that his day too will come. But *jarā* does not mean that your hair will not turn white or that wrinkles will not come. Striving to be free from old age does not mean going to a cosmetic doctor.

It means this: there are those who say, "What now? We have grown old, we are as good as dead, one of our feet is in the grave." People say things like this, don't they? To them Shri Krishna says that you have gone astray somewhere.

> "The body may grow old, but if your mind has grown old, then once again birth; not one, but many births."

Your work can never be finished. The body may grow old, but if the mind has grown old, Shri Krishna says you will have to be born again, not once but many times. Because you never understood action, you never understood spirituality, and you assumed, "Now it is all over for me." Physical work may come to an end, but the problem begins when the mind starts saying, "Not this any more."

> "Even at the age of ninety-two you should be full of energy."

## Yoga Sadhana and Youthfulness

When a person reaches this state of yoga sadhana, a process of reversing old age begins within him. Even at that age the yogi looks very youthful.
