## 1. Summary

A short talk built on Bhagavad Gita 7.29 (जरामरणमोक्षाय मामाश्रित्य यतन्ति ये): only those who take refuge and strive for freedom from old age and death truly know Brahman, the Self and all action. Acharya Ji reads "taking refuge" as surrender to the Guru (the ego bowing), uses the Buddha's sights of old age, sickness and death to show how rarely we ask "will this happen to me?", and then makes the central point: freedom from jara is not about erasing wrinkles; the real bondage is an old mind ("it's all over for me now"), which leads to birth after birth, while yoga sadhana keeps a seeker energetic at any age. The main intent is informational with a reflective, life-stage edge (ageing, purpose after retirement, fear of death). The opportunity: the video's own framing ("How to Escape the Cycle of Rebirth", thumbnail "Why Are We Born Again?") is a high-interest English query dominated by generic or non-Hindu explainers, and the Hindi verse phrase "जरा-मरण से मुक्ति" has few deep answers. This post offers a verse-anchored, original angle (old mind, not old body). Rebirth itself gets only a sentence in the blog, so titles tie it to the Gita and the old mind rather than promising a full treatise on reincarnation (flag 1).

**English (56 words):** Bhagavad Gita 7.29 says that those who take refuge and strive for freedom from old age and death come to know Brahman, the Self and all action. According to Acharya Upendra Ji, escaping the cycle of rebirth does not mean erasing wrinkles; it means never letting the mind grow old, because an old mind leads to birth after birth.

## 6. FAQ

1. **गीता 7.29 का अर्थ क्या है? / What does Bhagavad Gita 7.29 mean?**
   जो श्रीकृष्ण की शरण लेकर जरा और मरण से छूटने का यत्न करते हैं, वे ब्रह्म, संपूर्ण अध्यात्म और संपूर्ण कर्म को जानते हैं (गीता प्रेस)। / Those who take refuge in Shri Krishna and strive for freedom from old age and death come to know Brahman, the whole of the Self and all action (Gita Press).
2. **"मेरी शरण" का अर्थ क्या है? / What does "taking refuge in Me" mean here?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार इसका अर्थ है गुरु के पास जाकर, अहंकार छोड़कर झुकना और उनसे समझना। / In Acharya Ji's reading, it means going to the Guru, letting the ego bow, and learning from them. (His interpretation; the verse's "Me" is Shri Krishna, flag 2.)
3. **क्या जरा से मुक्ति का अर्थ बुढ़ापा न आना है? / Does freedom from old age mean the body will not age?**
   नहीं। बाल सफ़ेद होंगे, झुर्रियाँ आएँगी; असली बुढ़ापा वह मन है जो कहता है "अब मेरा सब खत्म"। / No. Hair will turn white and skin will wrinkle; real old age is the mind that says "it's all over for me now".
4. **हम बार-बार जन्म क्यों लेते हैं? / Why are we born again?**
   आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि शरीर बूढ़ा हो सकता है, पर अगर मन बूढ़ा हो गया तो फिर से, अनेक बार जन्म लेना होगा। / Acharya Ji teaches that the body may grow old, but if the mind grows old, one must be born again, many times over.
5. **बुद्ध ने बुढ़ापा, रोग और मृत्यु देखकर क्या सीखा? / What did the Buddha learn from seeing old age, sickness and death?**
   उन्होंने पूछा "क्या मेरा भी ऐसा होगा?" और संन्यास का निश्चय किया; हम रोज़ ऐसी ख़बरें देखकर भी यह प्रश्न नहीं पूछते। / He asked "will this happen to me too?" and resolved to renounce; we see such news daily and never ask it.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Rebirth is a short point in the blog, but the primary English keyword.** The video title and thumbnail promise "How to Escape the Cycle of Rebirth" / "Why Are We Born Again?", while the blog states the link in one sentence ("if the mind grows old, you will have to be born again"). The titles, meta and FAQ 4 tie rebirth to Gita 7.29 and the old mind and attribute it to Acharya Ji. Don't write snippets promising a full account of reincarnation, karma and the afterlife; if the talk says more, the editor can expand that paragraph.
2. **"Me" in Gita 7.29 read as the Guru (review issue 1, Minor, not yet applied).** The Key Takeaways state the Guru reading as what the verse says. FAQ 1 keeps the verse's own sense (Shri Krishna) and FAQ 2 attributes the Guru reading to Acharya Ji. Apply the review's correction to the takeaway.
3. **Buddha's "three sights" and "sixteen years" (review issues 2, 3 and 8, Minor).** Traditional accounts have four sights (the fourth, an ascetic, prompts the renunciation), and the "sixteen years" does not match the usual chronology. Don't use "Buddha's three sights" as a keyword or in titles; the low-priority keyword is "Buddha old age sickness death". The Hindi "संत" vs English "monk" mismatch can be fixed with "संन्यासी"; FAQ 5 already uses संन्यास.
4. **Paraphrase wording: "परमात्मा / God" and "spirituality" for ब्रह्म and अध्यात्म (review issues 4 and 5, Minor).** The direct answer and FAQ 1 use "Brahman" and "the Self", matching the verse and the Gita Press translation. Apply the review's wording to the paragraph after the verse.
5. **"Yoga sadhana reverses ageing" and "young at ninety-two".** This is Acharya Ji's teaching, with no scriptural citation, and it borders on a health promise. Keep it out of titles, meta, tags and social snippets ("reverse ageing with yoga" would attract the wrong intent and over-promise). The inline caption is deliberately "Energy at every age". If quoted, attribute it ("Acharya Ji says...").
6. **"Shri Krishna says they have lost their way" (review issue 6, Minor, not yet applied).** No verse says this; it is an application of 7.29. Don't quote it as a Gita statement in snippets or quote cards. Apply the review's rewording.
7. **Death news, suicide and bomb blasts in the opening.** Fitting as a wake-up call in the text, but keep suicide and violence out of titles, meta, Open Graph and social cards; for readers in distress, lead with the hopeful message (the mind need not grow old).
8. **IAST dandas (review issue 7, Minor, optional).** No effect on SEO texts.

- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses (in the `_seo` texts, which are what gets published; the original texts did not use it word for word, see section 2)
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged (the focus keywords are the searched wording; "why are we born again" is a secondary keyword)
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description

- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred) (in the direct answer after the intro of the `_seo` texts)

- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### Images

### Internal linking
