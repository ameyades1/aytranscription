# The Real Bondage Is an Old Mind, Not an Old Body

Every day we hear that someone has died. Have you ever paused to consider that one day the news will be about you?

Drawing on a profound verse of the Bhagavad Gita and the well-known story of Gautama Buddha, this discourse asks a simple question: what does it truly mean to be free of **jara** (old age) and death?

## Will My Day Come Too?

The WhatsApp messages keep arriving. This actor has passed away, that actor is gone, a bomb blast somewhere has killed a hundred people. We read them, forward them, and debate why someone took their own life, or whether it was really murder.

Every night we watch the news as well. Yet while we pass all of this along, one question rarely crosses our minds. Acharya Ji asks whether we have ever considered that our own day will come too, and what we have done to prepare for it.

*The next time you read about someone's death, will it make you think about your own life, or will it pass as just another headline?*

## Do We Really Understand Our Duty?

Many of us believe we are fulfilling our duty well. We feel we know what to do with our lives. We even believe we have grasped **adhyatma** (spirituality), the question of "Who am I?"

For people like this, Shri Krishna offers a test in the seventh chapter of the Gita:

> **jarāmaraṇamokṣāya mām āśritya yatanti ye**
> **te brahma tad viduḥ kṛtsnam adhyātmaṃ karma cākhilam**
> "Those who take refuge in Me and strive for freedom from old age and death come to know Brahman, the whole of the spiritual Self, and all action."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.29)

In other words, you come to understand God, the whole of spirituality and the whole of action once you make at least a small effort to break free of old age and death.

## Surrender: When the Ego Bows

"Taking refuge in Me" means going to the Guru, offering **sharanagati** (surrender), and learning from them.

To take refuge is to bow before someone. You can bow only when the ego lets go. Once the ego is gone, true surrender becomes possible.

And what do you learn from the Guru? The way to break free of old age and death. Those who grasp this have grasped action, spirituality and **Brahman** (the Absolute, the ultimate reality).

## Gautama Buddha's Three Questions

When Siddhartha was a child, an astrologer told his father that the boy would become either a great spiritual teacher or a great king. His father wanted him to be king. He kept the boy for sixteen years in the splendour of the palace, wrapped in **maya** (the world's enchanting illusion), so that nothing that might awaken **vairagya** (dispassion) would ever come before his eyes.

One day the prince went out with his charioteer. First he saw an **old man** with white hair, a stick in his hand and a wrinkled body. "Will I become like this too?" "Yes." "Then I do not want this."

Next he saw a **sick man** whose body was failing him. "Could this happen to me as well?" "Yes, certainly." "Then I do not want this."

Finally he saw a **funeral procession**. "Will this happen to me too?" "Yes, one day." "Then I do not want this."

In that very moment he resolved to become a monk.

The Buddha saw three sights once and woke up. We see such news every day, yet we never ask that question: "Will this happen to me too?"

## Freedom From Old Age Is Not About Erasing Wrinkles

One misconception needs clearing up here. Freedom from old age does not mean your hair will never turn white or your skin will never wrinkle. It does not mean a visit to a cosmetic doctor.

Real old age sets in when someone says, "What's left now? I'm old, I'm as good as dead, I have one foot in the grave." To such people Shri Krishna says that they have lost their way. Acharya Ji explains that the body may grow old, but if the mind grows old, you will have to be born again, and not once but many times.

Your physical work may come to an end, but your purpose in life does not. When the mind declares, "It's all over for me now," it shows that you have not understood action or spirituality at all.

*Is there a voice inside you saying, "I'm too old for this now"? Is that voice coming from your body, or from your mind?*

## Yogic Practice: Where Ageing Begins to Reverse

Acharya Ji says that even at ninety-two we should be full of energy. When you reach this stage of **yoga sadhana** (yogic practice), a process begins within you that reverses ageing. Even at that age, a yogi looks remarkably young.

## Key Takeaways

- News of death arrives every day; the real question is whether we have considered that our day will come too, and how we have prepared for it.
- Gita 7.29: those who take refuge in the Guru and strive for freedom from old age and death fully know Brahman, spirituality and action.
- Surrender means letting go of the ego and bowing.
- The Buddha woke up after seeing old age, sickness and death once; we see them every day and stay asleep.
- Freedom from old age means keeping the mind from growing old, not erasing wrinkles.
- An old mind leads to birth after birth; through yogic practice, a seeker stays energetic and young at every age.
