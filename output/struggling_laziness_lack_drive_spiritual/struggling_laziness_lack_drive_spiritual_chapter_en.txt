# Chapter: From Laziness to Beyond the Gunas

## The Question: Why Does Laziness Not Go Away?

A seeker asked, "Our laziness is not decreasing. What should we do?"

If laziness does not decrease, you must bring in a greater **sense of duty**. Laziness is the evidence of tamoguna. And there is no direct leap out of tamoguna; before leaving tamoguna behind, one has to come into rajoguna.

If a direct leap is to happen, a guru is needed. A guru who is present right in front of you and intervenes in your life. Such a guru, like Narada Muni, can turn Valya into Valmiki.

## The Discipline of the Gurukul

So come to the gurukul. If you come to the gurukul, I will take out all your laziness. From morning to evening I will keep you engaged in such work that laziness will have no room left. I will put a whip behind you, and along with that whip I will also place two watchers, whose only job will be to keep an eye on you. Then tamas will leave on its own.

This happens when discipline is brought in from outside. And when the same discipline comes from inner motivation, laziness also leaves.

## Why Do We Behave Properly?

Understand this: why do we behave properly? Because outside there are the scriptures, there is a king, there are strict rules. Are there or are there not? Then we have to quietly follow them.

The electricity department says that if the bill is not paid on time, the power will be cut. So there is some penalty, is there not? Then no matter how lazy you are, you still have to go and pay the bill.

When demonetisation was announced, did anyone stay lazy? No. You realised that all your money would become waste paper, that there would be a loss, so everyone quietly stood in line.

> "Self-realisation is a far-off matter. When the sense of duty awakens, tamas leaves and you come into rajas."

## Not Preya, but Shreya

But even in rajas, one must not act out of fear. Then the same action will come from sattvaguna. The feeling then will be: I do not want what is merely **preya** (pleasant), I want what is **shreya** (truly beneficial).

In the gurukul the disciple says, "I will do only the work I like." Then the guru says, "No, do the work that is best for you. And then make that best thing your beloved."

The question is: like what you do, or do what you like? If we do only what we like, someone may like drinking alcohol, someone may like laziness. It should not be so.

> "The best action should be dear to me. My best action itself should be my beloved action."

This is the journey toward sattvaguna.

## Beyond Sattva: Gunatita

After sattvaguna comes the state of **gunatita**, beyond the gunas. In that state all three gunas come under my control. Now if I want to rest, I can rest; and if I want to work, I can work.

Then a feeling arises as if I have retired. Yet even in retirement I am fully active. All of this has come under my command.

## Only Two Ways Out of Tamas

So, was anything I said wrong? If my laziness, my negligence, that is, any tamoguna does not end, then I am first brought into rajoguna: some compulsion, some rule, some force. Someone will make you realise that this is a waste of time, a waste of money.

There is a lot within tamas. Ego is also tamas. So someone who breaks the ego has to be sitting in front of you; only then will the ego break.

If there is a troublemaker, a drinker, then some minister, some guru or some activist will have the liquor den shut down, and then it will close. Otherwise the person himself will come to realisation, and then it will close. One of the two has to happen, only then will it close, is it not so?

When do you stop at a signal? When there is a rule there and a policeman standing. Otherwise, in tamoguna, a person drives any way he likes and, in ignorance, crashes into anything.

## The Sense of Duty and the Change of Samskaras

So laziness and negligence come under tamoguna. The remedy is the sense of duty: when the feeling comes, "I simply have to do this."

And the guru changes the samskaras. As soon as the samskara changes, you will feel the urge to act on your own. Either you will want to finish the work quickly and rest, or you will renounce everything and settle into stillness.
