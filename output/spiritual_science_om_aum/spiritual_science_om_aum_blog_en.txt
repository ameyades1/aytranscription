# The Whole Universe Hidden in A U M

We have all heard Om, and chanted it. But have you ever wondered why our Sanatan Gurus chose this very sound? They could have said Som, Bom, Tom or Chom. So why Om?

Acharya Upendra Ji answers this question not with a mystery, but with our own mouth.

## The Play Between Two Ends

Acharya Ji recalls something he said earlier: it is between one end and the other that dharma flows, that all the momentum of life flows.

The same rule applies to syllables. Every syllable is born from our mouth. So let us look at the mouth itself.

## Only Three States of the Mouth

Our mouth can be in only three states:

1. **Fully open**
2. **Fully closed**
3. **Half open**, where the lips come close, as when saying "O"

Now try it. Keep your mouth fully closed and try to say something. The only sound that comes out will be **"M"**. Nothing beyond that. As Acharya Ji says, close your mouth and see: you only say "M".

Experts in sound therapy and our shastra, which is itself a science, both say this. Speak with the mouth open and the sound **"A"** comes. And in the middle state only **"U"** can be said, neither with an open mouth nor with a closed one.

*The next time you say Om, notice: does your mouth pass through all three of these states?*

## A + U + M = Om

So we have three sounds: A, U and M. The first two join to make "O", and then comes "M".

But Acharya Ji makes it clear that this is not just about pronunciation. It is symbolic; it is not about what you pronounce.

The point is that **all the words of the world fall within these three states.** That is why the true meaning of A, U, M is: **that Brahman** (the Absolute, the supreme truth).

## Is Even an Abuse Brahman?

Here Acharya Ji gives a startling example. If I gave you two abuses, I gave them with that same mouth, didn't I?

What I am fighting about is another question. But that word too is a form of the same Brahman, and it too will remain within A, U, M.

*The words you speak every day, sweet or bitter, have you ever seen them as sounds of Brahman?*

## This Is the Vision of the Rishis

Acharya Ji humbly says that he is adding nothing new of his own. This is the thinking of our rishis and munis. He says it may be new to us, something we have not heard before, but we should do our own research.

The rishis say that all sounds, whether we can draw a meaning from them or not, were Brahman. All of it was truly **Omkar** (the primordial sound of Om).

## Key Takeaways

- The choice of Om is not arbitrary; it is based on the three states of the mouth.
- The open mouth gives "A", the half-open mouth "U", the closed mouth "M".
- A + U join to form "O", and "M" completes Om.
- Om is symbolic: all the words of the world are contained in it.
- Every word, even an abuse, is a form of the same Brahman.
- This is the vision of the rishis; test it through your own research.
