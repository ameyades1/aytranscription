# The Whole Universe Hidden in A, U and M

We have all heard Om, and we have all chanted it. But have you ever wondered why the Gurus of our **Sanatan** (eternal) tradition chose this particular sound? They could just as easily have said Som, Bom, Tom or Chom. Why Om?

Acharya Upendra Ji answers this question without appealing to mystery. He points instead to something we all carry: our own mouth.

## Everything Happens Between Two Ends

Acharya Ji recalls a point he made earlier: **dharma** (the right way of living) unfolds between one end and the other, and so does all the momentum of life.

The same rule holds for letters. Every sound we speak is born in the mouth, so let us begin by looking there.

## The Mouth Has Just Three Positions

Your mouth can take only three positions:

1. **Fully open**
2. **Fully closed**
3. **Half open**, with the lips drawn close together, as when you say "O"

Now try a small experiment. Keep your mouth completely shut and try to say something. The only sound that comes out is **"M"**, and nothing beyond it. As Acharya Ji puts it, close your mouth and see for yourself: "M" is all you can say.

Experts in sound therapy agree, and so do our scriptures, which are a science in their own right. Speak with the mouth open and the vowel **"A"** emerges. In the middle position you can say only **"U"**, a sound that neither the open nor the closed mouth can make.

*The next time you chant Om, will you notice whether your mouth passes through all three of these positions?*

## A + U + M = Om

That gives us three sounds: A, U and M. The first two blend into "O", and then comes "M".

Acharya Ji makes it clear, however, that this is not just a matter of pronunciation. Om is symbolic, and the point lies beyond how you happen to pronounce it.

The point is that **every word in the world falls within these three positions.** That is why A, U and M truly stand for **Brahman** (the Absolute, the Supreme Reality).

## Is Even an Insult Brahman?

Here Acharya Ji offers a startling example. If he were to hurl a couple of insults at you, they would come from that very same mouth, wouldn't they?

What the quarrel is about is a separate question. Even those harsh words are a form of the same Brahman, and they too stay within the bounds of A, U and M.

*Have you ever heard the words you speak each day, sweet or bitter, as the sound of Brahman?*

## This Is the Vision of the Rishis

Acharya Ji says with humility that he is adding nothing new of his own. This is how our **rishis** (the ancient seers and sages) understood sound. The idea may be new to us, he admits, something we have never heard before, but we should investigate it for ourselves.

The rishis taught that every sound, whether or not we can draw a meaning from it, was Brahman. All of it was, in truth, **Omkar** (the primal resonance of Om).

## Key Takeaways

- The choice of Om is not arbitrary; it rests on the three positions of the mouth.
- An open mouth gives "A", a half-open mouth gives "U", and a closed mouth gives "M".
- A and U blend into "O", and "M" completes Om.
- Om is symbolic: every word in the world is contained within it.
- Every word, even an insult, is a form of the same Brahman.
- This is the vision of the rishis; investigate it and test it for yourself.
