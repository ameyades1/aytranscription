# What Changed Within You This Year

Every year Shravan comes, festivals come, bells ring, and then the wedding season begins. But have you ever wondered why most festivals come at this particular time?

This short discourse answers that question, and along with it raises another question, one that every seeker should ask themselves every year.

## Chaturmas: A Four-Month Halt

**Chaturmas** means the four months of the rainy season (the four monsoon months), which begin around Shravan. Most of our festivals come in these very months.

To understand why, we first need to know who designed them. The answer: the rishis and munis.

## When the Rishis Walked

At that time there were no cars, no helicopters, no permanent houses. The rishis did not reach homes through some online medium as we do today. They had to walk from village to village themselves.

They asked for alms saying "Om bhavati bhikṣāṃ dehi." Wherever they found a place for the night, they stayed, or they slept outside the village and moved on the next morning. In forest areas they stayed in someone's house for safety.

But in the rains all this was very difficult. Few clothes, mud all around, unpaved roads, and the problem of food and water.

## Four Months, One Village, Complete Guidance

So in the rainy season the rishis would stay in one village. In the house of the village headman, at some respected person's home, or in a temple. There were so many sadhus and saints then that every village had some saint.

Now that the guru was going to stay there for four months, all the **upakrama** (religious observances, rituals and observances) were completed at that time under their guidance.

Acharya Ji says that this is why most festivals come during this time.

## Why Does the Wedding Season Come Right After?

Right after the festivals, the wedding season begins. Why? Because the practice of **brahmacharya** (the life stage of restraint and learning) is now complete; what had to be learned has been learned. Now the person is eligible for the **grihastha ashram** (household stage of life).

Otherwise, what difference does it make whether you marry in January, April or August? We look at the auspicious time, but do we also look at the preparation before it?

*Have you done the inner preparation for the next stage you are moving towards?*

## One Question Every Year

The spiritual meaning of festivals is not that you celebrate them one year and the work is done. They come every year to remind us: **has this happened for me yet or not?**

Acharya Ji explains that a person may get married, grow old and come close to death, and still their ting-ting of the bell has not stopped.

If the whole of life passes only in outer worship, when will **sadhana** and **meditation** happen?

*From the last festival to this one, what has truly changed within you?*

## A Form Imagined for the Good of Seekers

The discourse began with this very line:

> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the welfare of seekers, a form is imagined for Brahman."
> (Popular reading of Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

That is, a form for the formless **Brahman** (the ultimate reality) was conceived for the welfare of seekers. Festivals were made in the same way. The rishis cast sadhana into symbols for the common people.

The rishis could not wait until every child grew up. So they left a method with the parents: explain this much to the child, teach them this, then send them to us, and we will take it further.

## Who Can Start a Festival?

Acharya Ji explains that you and I cannot start a festival; only some authority can start it.

Anyone can start a college festival. But a religious festival can be started only by one who holds the spiritual authority for it.

## Why the Guru Is Above God

The guru's own work is already complete. Yet he carries the disciple's burden. That is why in every religion the guru's place is said to be above God.

But here **viveka** (discernment) is needed on both sides. The guru should understand what kind of disciple to make, and the disciple should understand how far to remain dependent. Whoever understands this has gained wisdom.

## Key Takeaways

- Most festivals come in Chaturmas because at that time the rishis and munis stayed in one village for four months.
- All religious observances were carried out during this period under their guidance.
- The wedding season comes after the festivals, because the grihastha ashram has meaning only after the preparation of brahmacharya.
- Festivals are an opportunity for self-examination every year: how far has my sadhana come?
- Only one with authority can start a religious festival.
- Both guru and disciple need discernment: the disciple should know how far to remain dependent.
