# What Changed Within You This Year?

Every year Shravan arrives, the festivals follow, temple bells ring, and then the wedding season begins. Have you ever wondered why most of our festivals fall in this part of the year?

This short discourse answers that question. It also raises another, one that every seeker should ask themselves each year.

## Chaturmas: Four Months of Pause

**Chaturmas** (the four monsoon months) begins around Shravan, the monsoon month of the Hindu calendar. Most of our festivals fall within these months.

To see why, you first need to know who created these festivals. The answer is the rishis and munis, the sages of old.

## When the Sages Travelled on Foot

In those days there were no cars, no helicopters and no solid houses. The sages could not reach people's homes through some online medium, as teachers do today. They had to walk from village to village themselves.

They asked for alms with the words "oṁ bhavati bhikṣāṁ dehi" (Om, kindly give alms). Wherever they found shelter for the night, they stayed; otherwise they slept outside the village and moved on the next morning. In forested areas, they stopped at someone's house for safety.

The rains made all of this very hard. The sages had few clothes, mud lay everywhere, the paths were unpaved, and finding food and water became a struggle.

## Four Months, One Village, Complete Guidance

So during the rainy season, a sage would settle in a single village. They stayed at the home of the village headman, with some respected family, or in a temple. There were so many holy men in those days that every village had at least one.

With the Guru staying in one place for four months, the village carried out all its **upakramas** (religious rituals and observances) under their guidance during that time.

That, Acharya Ji says, is why most festivals fall in this season.

## Why Does the Wedding Season Follow?

The wedding season begins right after the festivals. Why? Because by then the discipline of **brahmacharya** (the stage of life devoted to self-restraint and learning) is complete, and the young person has learned what they needed to learn. Now they are ready for **grihastha ashrama** (the householder stage of life).

Otherwise, what difference would it make whether you married in January, April or August? We check the auspicious time, the muhurta, carefully. Do we also check the preparation that should come before it?

*Have you prepared yourself inwardly for the next stage of life you are stepping into?*

## A Question for Every Year

The spiritual meaning of a festival is not that you celebrate it once and you are done. Festivals return every year to remind you to ask: **Has this happened in me yet?**

Acharya Ji points to a familiar picture. Someone says they are married, they have grown old, they are close to death, and still the ting-ting of their prayer bell has not stopped.

If your whole life passes in outward worship alone, when will **sadhana** (spiritual practice) and meditation ever begin?

*Between the last festival and this one, what has truly changed within you?*

## Form Imagined for the Seeker's Sake

The discourse opened with this verse:

> **sādhakānāṁ hitārthāya**
> **brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the good of seekers, a form is imagined for the formless Brahman, the Supreme Reality."
> (A popular reading of the Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

Festivals came about in the same way. The sages shaped spiritual practice into symbols that ordinary people could follow.

A sage could not wait in one place for every child to grow up. So they left the method with the parents: explain this much to the child, teach them this, then send them to us, and we will take it from there.

## Who Can Begin a Festival?

Acharya Ji explains that you and I cannot start a festival. Only someone with the proper authority can establish one.

Anyone can launch a college celebration. A religious festival, however, can be founded only by someone who holds the spiritual authority to do so.

## Why the Guru Stands Above God

The Guru's own work is already complete. Even so, they take on the burden of the disciple. That is why every tradition places the Guru above God.

Yet both sides need **viveka** (discernment) here. The Guru must understand how to shape the disciple, and the disciple must understand how far to depend on the Guru. Whoever grasps this has found true wisdom.

## Key Takeaways

- Most festivals fall during Chaturmas because the sages once stayed in a single village for those four months.
- Under their guidance, villages carried out all their religious observances in this season.
- The wedding season follows the festivals because the householder stage has meaning only after the preparation of brahmacharya.
- Each year, festivals offer a chance to look within and ask: how far has my sadhana come?
- Only someone with proper authority can begin a religious festival.
- Both Guru and disciple need discernment: the disciple must know how far to depend on the Guru.
