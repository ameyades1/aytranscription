## 1. Summary

The talk asks why most Hindu festivals fall in Chaturmas, the four monsoon months, and why the wedding season follows them. Acharya Ji's answer is practical and historical: the rishis travelled on foot, the rains made travel impossible, so each sage stayed in one village for four months and the village carried out its observances under the sage's guidance; weddings follow because the preparation of brahmacharya is then complete. He then turns the question inward: festivals return every year to ask "has this happened in me yet?", and a life spent only in outward worship never reaches sadhana and dhyana. Short sections add that only someone with spiritual authority can begin a religious festival, and that the Guru is placed above God because they carry the disciple's burden, with discernment needed on both sides. The main intent is **informational** ("chaturmas ka mahatva", "why festivals in Chaturmas"), with a reflective, emotional layer ("what changed within me this year") that suits year-end and festival-season sharing. Most ranking pages give the Vishnu-yoganidra story, dates and vrat rules; this post's distinct angle is the wandering-sage explanation and the yearly self-examination. Searches for Chaturmas peak in late June to July (Devshayani Ekadashi), so publish or refresh by late May to mid June; the reflective angle can be pushed again around Diwali and Prabodhini Ekadashi, when Chaturmas ends and the wedding season opens. The H1 is a good reflective headline but carries no search term, so the main gain is a `<title>` that leads with "चातुर्मास का महत्व" / "Significance of Chaturmas", which also matches the video thumbnail.

## 6. FAQ

For the WordPress page only. Answers come from the talk, and Acharya Ji's own readings are attributed to him.

**Hindi**
1. **चातुर्मास क्या है?** वर्षा ऋतु के चार महीने, जिनमें हमारे अधिकतर त्योहार आते हैं। परंपरा के अनुसार यह आषाढ़ शुक्ल एकादशी (देवशयनी) से कार्तिक शुक्ल एकादशी (प्रबोधिनी) तक चलता है।
2. **अधिकतर त्योहार चातुर्मास में ही क्यों आते हैं?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार, बरसात में पैदल यात्रा कठिन थी, इसलिए ऋषि चार महीने एक ही गांव में ठहरते थे और उनके मार्गदर्शन में सारे धार्मिक उपक्रम इसी समय कराए जाते थे।
3. **त्योहारों के बाद ही विवाह का मौसम क्यों आता है?** आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि तब तक ब्रह्मचर्य की तैयारी पूरी हो जाती है और व्यक्ति गृहस्थ आश्रम के योग्य होता है।
4. **त्योहारों का आध्यात्मिक अर्थ क्या है?** त्योहार एक बार मनाकर पूरे नहीं होते। वे हर वर्ष यह पूछने आते हैं: मेरी साधना कहां तक पहुंची, पिछले त्योहार से इस त्योहार तक मेरे भीतर क्या बदला?
5. **क्या कोई भी नया धार्मिक त्योहार शुरू कर सकता है?** आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि नहीं; धार्मिक त्योहार केवल वही आरंभ कर सकता है जिसके पास उसका आध्यात्मिक अधिकार हो।

**English**
1. **What is Chaturmas?** The four months of the monsoon, in which most Hindu festivals fall. Traditionally it runs from Devshayani Ekadashi in Ashadha to Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartik.
2. **Why do most festivals fall in Chaturmas?** According to Acharya Ji, travel on foot was very hard in the rains, so a sage would stay in one village for four months, and the village carried out its observances under the sage's guidance.
3. **Why does the wedding season come after the festivals?** Acharya Ji explains that by then the preparation of brahmacharya is complete, and the young person is ready for the householder stage.
4. **What is the spiritual meaning of festivals?** A festival is not done once it is celebrated. It returns every year to ask: how far has my sadhana come, and what has changed within me since the last one?
5. **Can anyone start a religious festival?** Acharya Ji says no: only someone with the spiritual authority to do so can begin one.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Verse source (Sanskrit review issue 1, Minor, not yet applied).** "साधकानां हितार्थाय ब्रह्मणो रूपकल्पना" is a popular reading of the second half of Ramapurvatapaniya Upanishad 1.7, which reads "उपासकानां कार्यार्थं ब्रह्मणो रूपकल्पना". The blog correctly says "popular reading". Don't quote the line in titles, meta or social cards as "the Upanishad says"; if it is used, give the review's fuller reference.
2. **"Every religion places the Guru above God" (review issue 2, Minor, not yet applied).** True of much of the Indian tradition (Guru Gita, Kabir), not of every religion. Reword as Acharya Ji's teaching about "our tradition", as the review suggests. Don't target "why is the Guru above God" with a universal claim in any title or snippet; the FAQ leaves this point out.
3. **When Chaturmas begins (review issue 3, Minor, optional).** The blog says "around Shravan"; traditionally it runs from Devshayani Ekadashi (Ashadha Shukla 11) to Prabodhini Ekadashi (Kartik Shukla 11). FAQ 1 uses the traditional dates, which are not in the talk; publish that sentence only if the editor adds them to the blog. Seasonal promotion should follow these dates (late June/July), not Shravan.
4. **उपक्रम / upakrama (review issue 4, Minor, not yet applied).** The gloss fits the context but not the word, and the English transliteration can be confused with upākarma. The FAQ and snippet use "observances" instead of the term.
5. **"bhavati" gloss (review issue 5, Minor, not yet applied).** English should read "(Om, O revered lady, please give alms)". Not used in any SEO text.
6. **Mixed Hindi/English glosses in the Hindi text (review issue 6, Minor, not yet applied).** No SEO effect beyond readability; apply the review's Hindi glosses.
7. **The festival and wedding timing is Acharya Ji's own reading.** The common traditional explanation is Vishnu's yoganidra from Devshayani to Prabodhini Ekadashi, which the blog does not discuss (though the video thumbnail shows Vishnu on Shesha). Titles, meta and snippets present the sages' monsoon stay as Acharya Ji's explanation ("Acharya Ji explains", "according to Acharya Ji"), never as "the real reason" or "the truth behind Chaturmas", and they don't claim the post explains why weddings are not held during Chaturmas by muhurta rules. If the editor wants the Hinglish query "chaturmas me shadi kyu nahi hoti", it needs a sentence on the traditional view, sourced, added to the blog first.
8. **Video title and thumbnail differ.** The YouTube title ("What Has Changed Within You This Year?") is reflective, while the thumbnail says "Significance of Chaturmas" and "Ganesh Series Ep 16". The post delivers both, so the recommended titles are faithful; but don't add Ganesha to the title or meta, since the post says nothing about Ganesha. "Ganesh Series" stays a tag only.
