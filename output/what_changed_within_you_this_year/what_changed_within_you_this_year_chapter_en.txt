# Chapter: The Secret of Festivals and What Changed Within You This Year

## Why Do Festivals Come in This Season?

Someone has asked a very important question: why do most festivals come in this season? It is good that you asked this question. It is relevant, so I will answer it first.

You know something about Chaturmas. Which four months make up Chaturmas? It all happens after Shravan, doesn't it, and all these festivals come during these very months. So first understand the principle behind it, because it was the rishis and munis who designed it.

## The Rishis Who Walked

At that time, unlike today, there were no cars, no helicopters, no permanent houses. The rishis did not simply reach every village and every home the way the internet does today. They had to walk from place to place themselves.

Wherever they went, they asked for alms saying "Om bhavati bhikṣāṃ dehi." In whichever house they were given a place for the night, they spent the night there, or they slept outside the village and set out for the next village the following day. That was their way of travelling. If the area was like a forest, they stayed in someone's house for safety.

## The Rainy Season and the Four-Month Halt

At that time it was very difficult to travel in the rainy season. When the rains came, they faced hardship. They did not have many clothes, there was mud all around, there were no proper roads, and there was the problem of food and water.

So they would decide in which village they would come and stay. In those days there were so many gurus and sadhus that every village had some saint living in it. They stayed in the house of the village headman or some respected person, or they were asked to stay in a temple.

Since they were now going to stay there for four months, all these observances were completed at that time under the guidance of the gurus. That is why most festivals come during this period.

## Why the Wedding Season?

And once all this is complete, immediately afterwards it is said that the wedding season has now begun. Why the wedding season? Because the practice of brahmacharya is now complete, what was to be attained has been attained. Your period of brahmacharya is over; now you are eligible for the grihastha ashram.

Otherwise it has no meaning. Whether you marry in January, April or August, what difference does it make? You look at the auspicious time, but have you made that much preparation before it?

## The Yearly Self-Examination

The spiritual meaning of this is that it will not happen by celebrating the festivals for one year. Every year it is meant to remind me: has this happened for me yet or not?

Otherwise I got married, I grew old, I came close to death, and still my ringing of the bell has not stopped. So when will my sadhana happen, when will my meditation happen, when will everything happen for me?

## Why Did the Rishis Design Festivals?

So is it now absolutely clear? The rishis and munis designed the festivals. I began with this very line:

> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the welfare of seekers, a form is imagined for Brahman."
> (Popular reading of Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

That is, for the welfare of seekers, a form was conceived for the formless Brahman. In the same way, for the welfare of the common people, the rishis designed something through which they could explain sadhana up to that level. They explained it, gave this method for it, and cast it into a symbolic form.

As a child grew from birth, the rishis could not stay there every time. So they would give the parents some methods: until then, explain this much to the child, teach them this, then send them to us, and we will take it further. That was the idea behind it.

## Who Can Start a Festival?

You and I cannot start a festival. Only some authority can start it. College festivals and that kind of nonsense you can start; I am not talking about the festivals of today's times. Religious festivals can be started only by one who holds the authority for it.

## The Place of the Guru and the Disciple's Discernment

The guru is there, isn't he; his own work is already complete. So why would he take all this on? Yet he still does. That is why in every religion the place of the guru has always been said to be above even God.

So that much understanding should come to you as well. The guru too should have the discernment to know what kind of disciple to make. And the child too should understand how far to remain dependent. Whoever understands this has gained wisdom.
