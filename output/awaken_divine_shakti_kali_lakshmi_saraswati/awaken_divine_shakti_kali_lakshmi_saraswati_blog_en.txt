# Kali, Lakshmi, Saraswati: Awaken the Three Powers Within

## A Journey That Began With Questions

As a child, the Ganapati Atharvashirsha was recited at home. One boy kept asking: what is this knowledge of Ganesha? Why does Ganesha have an elephant's trunk? We ring bells and offer flowers; what does it actually do?

The answer was always, "That's how it is, do the puja." Acharya Upendraji says that these incomplete answers were where his spiritual search began.

He says that in the name of "if you have faith, just do it," a great deal of **blind faith** also creeps into our lives. Worship is not wrong, but the understanding behind it is essential.

*Have questions ever arisen in your mind that no one answered?*

## The Forms of the Gods: A Language of Symbols

As he searched, one thing became clear. The scriptures themselves say:

> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā |**
> "The forms of Brahman are conceived for the benefit of the seekers."
> (Ramapurvatapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

That is, these forms of the formless Brahman were created for the seeker's welfare. We cannot see the formless, so it was explained through form.

Ganapati's big belly did not come from eating modaks. It teaches that **I should know how to digest my joys and sorrows.** Ganapati is the symbol of **discriminating intelligence (viveka-buddhi)**, and his vehicle, the mouse, stands for our desires and cravings, which eat up our time.

Only discriminating intelligence can steer these desires in the right direction. When a symbol is understood, its purpose is fulfilled.

## How Long Should We Worship Images?

A verse of the Gita gives the answer:

> **bahūnāṃ janmanāmante jñānavānmāṃ prapadyate |**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvamiti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ ||**
> "At the end of many births, the one who has knowledge takes refuge in Me, realizing that Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

The one who begins to see God in everything is a great soul, and such a person is extremely rare. Seeing God in everything means even in an enemy, even in a mother-in-law or daughter-in-law standing with a rolling pin, even in a thief who broke into your house.

Until that state comes, **image worship is necessary.** Acharyaji calls it a "practice match." The real service is to be done for living images, that is, for people. True charity of food and clothing is to be given to the people; the stone was only a medium.

> **ananyāścintayanto māṃ ye janāḥ paryupāsate |**
> **teṣāṃ nityābhiyuktānāṃ yogakṣemaṃ vahāmyaham ||**
> "Those who worship Me, thinking of no other, ever steadfast, I carry what they lack and preserve what they have."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

*Paryupasate* means sitting near the Lord, and that Lord is present in the form of people. A good entrepreneur serving people by giving a good product: that is his worship. The Lord says that during your lifetime, I will carry your welfare.

## A Thief Came to the House: What Is the Lesson?

A police inspector asked, "If Vasudeva is in everyone, should we let the accused go?" Acharyaji's answer was no. He must be handed over to the police, but at the same time you should see what lesson God came to teach through this event.

Perhaps I too stole once: stole time. I took a salary for eight hours and spent two of them chatting. Or perhaps the Devi is telling me that I know how to earn but not how to keep my wealth safe.

Acharya Ji says there is certainly some lesson, and the day this begins to be understood, 'Vasudevah sarvam iti' also begins to be understood.

*In the unpleasant events of your life, what lesson was hidden for you?*

## "Refute Kali"

Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped Kali, yet kept saying, "I am not having the experience." His guru Totapuri answered, "When you refute Kali, you will attain Kali."

This does not mean breaking the image; it means **seeing beyond the image.** Form and the formless should both be seen together. What expresses itself as good or bad through two living bodies no longer expresses itself through either body after death. That formless reality which was expressing itself is what is worth knowing. That is spirituality.

In the Durga Saptashati, too, the Devi says, "These are not separate from me; I alone am the One."

## Three Goddesses, Three Gunas

In the Durga Saptashati three goddesses appear: **Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati.** They are forms of the three gunas described in the fourteenth chapter of the Gita.

In Sanskrit, one meaning of *guna* is **a cord.** These are three cords that bind us. Even sattva binds; a golden chain is still a chain. If you become attached even to knowledge, Acharyaji says with a smile, your next birth will be in a library!

- **Mahasaraswati: sattva, white.** Knowledge, discernment, peace.
- **Mahalakshmi: rajas, red.** Action, hustle, "working with blood and sweat."
- **Mahakali: tamas, black.** Rest, destruction, protection.

Life does not run on any one guna. You need sattva for knowledge, rajas for action, and tamas for rest or destruction. The only question is **which guna should predominate at which time.**

That is why a single goddess alone is considered incomplete.

Only tamas means lust, laziness and violence. Only rajas means selfishness and a hunger for power. And only sattva means action stops altogether: "let whatever happens, happen."

*Which guna dominates your life right now?*

## Four Varnas from Three Gunas

The Lord says:

> **cāturvarṇyaṃ mayā sṛṣṭaṃ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ |**
> "The four varnas were created by Me according to the division of qualities and actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.13)

Notice that He said not *jati* (caste) but **varna**. The word "caste" came from the foreign word "casta" and was imposed on us. Our society was based on gunas, and **varnas can change.**

Acharyaji explains it with colours:

- **Brahmin, white:** the one who used intelligence to design a system like the traffic signal, and follows it himself.
- **Kshatriya, pink (white + red):** the one who, impressed by that system, enforces it, and in return wants a little honour and wealth.
- **Vaishya, brown (red + black):** the one who liked the action, wealth and power, but never took in the Brahmin's thinking.
- **Shudra, black:** the servant tendency that goes off for tea the moment the owner leaves. This tendency can arise in any country, in anyone.

This is neither good nor bad. Whether a temple or a building, **every institution needs all four.** If everyone became architects, houses would stay on paper; if everyone became masons, there would be neither plan nor materials. Then we would be cavemen again.

## Shri Ram Asked Hanuman: What Is Our Relationship?

This story answers the whole dispute. Hanumanji answered on three levels.

1. **Duality (at the level of the body):** "You are my master and I am your servant. I know my limits."
2. **Qualified non-duality (at the level of mind and intellect):** "If you are the whole, I am your part. If you are the ocean, I am a drop, but it is drops like us that make up your ocean. If you are fire, I am a spark."
3. **Non-duality (at the level of the Self):** "You and I are exactly the same; we are the one Self."

Acharyaji brings this very awakening to people of all four varnas. If you are in the role of a servant, serve well. But a servant need not remain a servant all his life. **You too can become the master;** just, when you do, be a good provider.

## Read the Manusmriti Through the Gunas

There is great controversy over some statements in the Manusmriti, such as pouring boiling oil into the ears of a Shudra who hears the Vedas. Acharyaji asks: would Manu write something so perverse?

He explains that this is about the **Shudra tendency**, not any community. That tendency can arise even in a Brahmin's mind. The meaning is: do not give Vedic knowledge to one with such a tendency, for he will turn it into black magic, for killing, enchanting and controlling others. And a king with such a tendency will only harm the country.

## The Vibration of "We Are Backward"

In the Agastya Nadi reading for Acharyaji, it is said that uniting the four varnas is his work. His message:

- If society keeps sending out the vibration "we are backward," the country cannot move forward.
- If the king gives you concessions, take them; that is your destiny. But if you come to the guru to learn *Aham Brahmasmi*, the feeling of Brahman will awaken within; then how can you be backward?
- Today this is a political problem. Two hundred seats are built for two thousand students, and before elections we are made to fight in the name of caste.

The guru has no objection to reservations. The objection is when someone takes the facility and still does not study, leaves potholes in the road, or engages in corruption. Because for whoever dies on that road, the guru has to swallow the poison.

## The Conflict of the Gunas: Why Clashes Happen

The fourteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita and Patanjali's Yoga Sutras explain that the three gunas are always in opposition to one another. This is called **gunavritti virodha**, the conflict of the gunas. You cannot be sleeping and running at the same time.

At home, if you work too much they say "rest," and if you rest they say "do some work." The same happens in society. Pride of knowledge comes quickly, and the one below always suffers.

The saints gave the solution: **"Go beyond the gunas, my friend."** Rise above the gunas, and you rise above this conflict too.

## The Three Gunas in Religions Too

According to Acharyaji, **Sanatan** means eternal: the laws of nature that have existed since the beginning. All religions come within it.

The Hindu, Jain, Sikh and Buddhist traditions are considered **sattva-predominant**: non-violence, forgiveness, compassion. But because of this very forgiveness, we keep tolerating crimes. Citing the Bhavishya Purana, he describes some societies as rajas-predominant and some as tamas-predominant.

His conclusion is clear: being merely sattvic Hindus will not work, and we will have to increase our rajasic and tamasic powers too.

Shri Krishna released his discus after Shishupala's hundred offences. So should we wait for a hundred offences? A mother too should sometimes know how to scold:

> **lālayet pañca varṣāṇi daśa varṣāṇi tāḍayet |**
> **prāpte tu ṣoḍaśe varṣe putraṃ mitravadācaret ||**
> "Pamper a son for five years, discipline him for ten; when he reaches sixteen, treat him as a friend."
> (Chanakya Niti 3.18)

## The Devi's Firmness in Our Daughters

Many gurus demand harsh punishment for those who deceive our daughters. Acharyaji agrees with this demand, but adds one question: **were our daughters first taught brahmacharya, self-restraint?**

Sita stayed firm in her virtue even after being abducted, which is why Ravana could not touch her. In the Durga Saptashati, when Shumbha and Nishumbha send the Devi a marriage proposal, she replies that she will marry only the one who defeats her in battle.

Love cannot be prohibited; it is a karmic bond. But a daughter should be able to recognize whether the other person is an asura or not, and there are asuras in every religion.

## World Guru: Not by Weapons, but by Scripture

The prophecy is that India will become the **world's guru**, not the world's conqueror. A guru must first be the ideal, and the guru is called *Mauli*, mother. Has our heart become like a mother's towards others?

Acharya Ji says we are going to win not by weapons, but by scripture.

This does not mean weapons will never be raised. Some people change by words alone, like Valya becoming Valmiki. The one who will not listen, like Duryodhana, has to be shown the sword. Krishna too first went as a messenger of peace and asked for five villages, and then said, "You have to fight; I am only the guide."

## Phalashruti: "Here and Now"

Someone prays in a church, someone in a mosque, someone in a temple. Acharyaji asks: where do you get the **phalashruti**, the result? Whether Shiva sadhana makes an illness go away or not: that is the test.

In Antar Yog, practices have been designed whose result comes **"here and now"**: Shiva sadhana for illness, Kali worship for troubles caused by black magic, Shri Vidya for wealth, and Saraswati Diksha for children.

## Reaching God Through Science

"God made man. Man made science. Who made science?" God himself. The problem is that religion and science were taught separately in schools. In the gurukul, knowledge, science and philosophy were all together.

> **jñānaṃ te'haṃ savijñānamidaṃ vakṣyāmyaśeṣataḥ |**
> **yajjñātvā neha bhūyo'nyajjñātavyamavaśiṣyate ||**
> "I shall teach you this knowledge in full, together with science, knowing which nothing else remains to be known here."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.2)

In mathematics we find X from the known numbers. In the same way the sages searched for the invisible God through His visible effects, and named that X **Om**. Call Him by any name; the answer will be the same.

First the fruit of sadhana should show: in a blood test, in psychology, in the energy field. Results build belief, belief builds faith, and "*shraddhavan labhate jnanam*," the one with faith attains knowledge. Without results, that same faith turns into blind faith.

Ramakrishna Paramahamsa's salt doll, which went to measure the ocean and dissolved in it, is spirituality in the language of chemistry.

## The Ninety-Nine Percent

Travelling across India, Acharyaji saw four kinds of people in society. True seekers are only one percent. The other ninety-nine percent are atheists, rationalists and the superstitious.

The rationalists are not wrong; they are intellectuals, and the intellect is higher than the mind. The problem is that mere emotional devotion cannot answer their questions. Swami Chinmayananda said a time would come when some guru would have to answer in the language of science.

That is why Acharyaji opens up the symbolic meaning of the scriptures. "Shiva said to Parvati" means my Self conversed with my mind and intellect. And **Mahishasura is the ego within me,** which keeps changing its form.

## Why Do We Break a Coconut?

- **The brown outer shell:** the hard ego made of rajas and tamas.
- **Pulling out the fibres:** renouncing desires. Then the three eyes inside appear.
- **Breaking it with force:** the ego does not break on its own; it has to be broken.
- **The white inside:** awakened sattva.
- **The sweet water:** the knowledge of truth, the Ganga of wisdom.

Breaking a coconut before every auspicious work means, "I set out having given up my ego." But people become proud of that very thing: "We broke two hundred coconuts!"

## Mahakali: A Weapon, and an Eye

Kali's garment is not a sari like the other goddesses' but like a skirt; it is modern, a symbol of action. Kali is not an outside woman; **she is my own nature.**

Each head in her **garland of heads** is a vice that has been slain. Miserliness died, and generosity awoke.

Acharya Ji says there should be a rosary in one hand and a spear in the other.

Kali's weapon also has an eye. She knows whom to strike, how much to strike, and where to love. Arjuna knew how to use weapons, but could not understand on whom. So **first learn to use the weapon on your own vices.**

And every discourse is for you, not for your uncle or your wife.

*Which one vice within you would you like to add to this garland?*

## The Body First, Then the Intellect

Mahakali is the power of self-protection and body awareness, which is why the first episode of the Durga Saptashati is hers. The Chandogya Upanishad says:

> **annamayaṃ hi somya manaḥ |**
> "For the mind, my dear, is made of food."
> (Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4)

Uddalaka had Shvetaketu fast for fifteen days, and he could not recite even one mantra properly. Vivekananda said, "First play football, then you will understand Vedanta." If a strong man comes and points a gun, every creation of the intellect can be snatched away in a moment.

Kali power is needed at every level:

- **For an institution:** copyright, trademark, patents and security.
- **For the nation:** the army and the police. Because they stay awake on the border, we can sleep.
- **For every woman:** self-defence such as karate, and along with it brahmacharya.

Acharya Ji warns that if the nation does not survive, dharma will not survive, and when dharma does not survive, culture will not survive either.

## See God in All, Act According to Merit

The Gita (5.18) says that the wise see with an equal eye a learned Brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste. But Acharyaji reminds us: **"See God everywhere" is said, not "treat everyone as God."**

God is in the lion too, but go and bow to it and it will also say its prayer before the meal, and eat you! You know that Brahman is in it, but it does not know that you are Brahman.

> **ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃstathaiva bhajāmyaham |**
> "As people approach Me, so do I receive them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

God is not as small as "tit for tat." Like a mother, He only uplifts, but He adopts a method suited to what we are. Some learn by being explained to, and some, like Duryodhana, by being beaten.

## Woman: Not Equal, but Infinitely More Powerful

Acharyaji says he is the only guru in India who openly says: **even a woman who wears powder and lipstick can attain liberation,** but by learning Vedanta. A businesswoman is a form of Mahalakshmi; a woman in the army or the police is a form of Kali. Why tell them just to sit smeared with ash?

Vivekananda and Tilak wanted women to step out only after understanding Vedanta. That did not happen, and the Devi's two forms were used the wrong way round:

- At home, before the husband: **fierce**
- Outside, where injustice should have been resisted: **gentle**

It should have been the opposite: **extremely gentle at home, and extremely fierce before wrongdoing outside.**

**Ardhanarishvara** means that the woman's love comes into the man and the man's firmness and discipline come into the woman, and both move towards oneness. When a woman, as a mother, holds her child's hand, she is countless times more powerful than him. If at that very time she teaches her son brahmacharya, she will never later need to say, "Be home before seven."

*If you are a woman, in what form does your power show most? And if you are a man, which quality have you learned from which woman?*

## Key Takeaways

- The forms of the gods are symbols; worship is complete only when the symbol is understood.
- Image worship lasts until we learn to see God in living people and serve them.
- Mahasaraswati, Mahalakshmi and Mahakali are sattva, rajas and tamas; all three must be balanced, and a single goddess alone is incomplete.
- Varnas come from gunas, not birth; every institution needs all four, and anyone can move up or down.
- Hanumanji's three answers, servant, part and the one Self, resolve social division.
- Drop the vibration of "we are backward" and learn the feeling of Brahman from the guru.
- True sadhana should show its result "here and now"; science builds belief, and belief builds faith.
- Mahakali teaches: first the body and protection, then the intellect; first the weapon on your own vices, then outside.
- See God in all, but act according to merit.
- Woman is not equal to man but infinitely more powerful: gentle at home, fierce before injustice.
