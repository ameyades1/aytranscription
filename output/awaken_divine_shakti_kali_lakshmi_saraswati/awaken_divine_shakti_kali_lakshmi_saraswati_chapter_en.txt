# Chapter: The Threefold Shakti: Awakening Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati

## The Timeless State: A Prelude

Our discourse on the Durga Saptashati runs for a hundred hours; it is really a twenty-one-day retreat. Sessions of five hours each are held every day, so it comes to about a hundred hours. And today I have been asked to say what I have to say in half an hour or an hour!

The seekers who come to my retreats know that our record is seventeen hours of speaking without a break. A retreat that begins at six in the morning sometimes ends only at eleven at night. There are no speeches in it, no bhajans, nothing else; only knowledge, only awakening. On Mahashivratri, the discourse that begins at six in the evening ends at eleven the next morning, and not just for one day; sometimes it continues for fifteen or twenty days at a stretch.

Today I was not permitted to speak that long. This is what is called the **timeless state**, where the clock and time no longer bind you.

## The Questions of Childhood

The journey across India that was just mentioned had a purpose behind it. I began teaching spirituality at a very young age, and that too came from the atmosphere at home. Both my mother and my father gave me a spiritual environment. Our family has had a spiritual background for many generations.

Just as Maharashtrian families recite the Ganapati Atharvashirsha every year and perform it a thousand times over, so it was in our home. In that recitation these words were spoken again and again:

> **saiṣā gaṇeśavidyā | gaṇaka ṛṣiḥ | nicṛdgāyatrī chandaḥ | gaṇapatirdevatā | oṃ gaṃ gaṇapataye namaḥ ||**
> "This is the knowledge of Ganesha. Ganaka is the seer, Nichrid Gayatri the metre, Ganapati the deity. Om Gam, salutations to Ganapati."
> (Ganapati Atharvashirsha)

So I would ask: what is this knowledge of Ganesha? Why has Ganesha been given a trunk like an elephant? No one could give a straight answer. They would just say, "That is how it is, do the puja." I would ask why other religions do not have gods like these, why only ours? We ring bells, we offer flowers; what does it actually do?

Such questions must arise in many children's minds at some point. But how many parents can really answer them? Most do not know themselves. They say, "If you have faith, do it." And in the name of this faith, all kinds of blind faith creep in as well. I am not saying all worship is blind faith. No, somewhere at home there was the belief that there is a power.

Then gradually the worship of other deities came along. As a child my mother taught me to worship Lord Shankar. Then came the worship of the Devi, and we began observing Navaratri too. So I began asking: what is the difference between these two? My mother explained the relationships: this is Shankar, this is his wife Parvati, that is Durga, and their son is Ganapati. I asked, "Then why are there these quarrels? How can there be so much disagreement within one family?" My mother said, "There is no quarrel between them." But I saw that there were Shaivas, Shaktas, Vaishnavas and Ganapatyas, all separate sects. I must have been about ten or twelve years old then.

From childhood the Puranas were read at home. They would begin, "Shiva said to Parvati," and then "said this to Ganapati," "Kartikeya said." I began reading the Shiva Purana and the Devi Purana written in Hindi, and the question came: where exactly is Shiva? All these questions arose in my mind from childhood.

## The Secret of the Symbols

What you see today, how we reached here, and why other gurus say, "Acharyaji, some great work will be done through you," let me tell you the reason.

As I studied and searched, one thing finally became clear: these Puranas are meant to be understood by those up to about fifteen years of age. These forms of the gods are imagined. The scriptures say:

> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā |**
> "The forms of Brahman are conceived for the benefit of the seekers."
> (Ramapurvatapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

All these forms, all these symbolic creations, exist because we cannot see the formless. So it has been presented in a form so that we can understand. And when these symbols are understood, the purpose behind them is fulfilled.

For example, why does Ganapati have a big belly, a huge body? He did not grow fat just by eating modaks. It tells me that I should know how to digest my joys and sorrows. Until we understand this symbol, the matter remains incomplete. Having one tusk is a symbol too; the Devi's eighteen arms are a symbol too.

In reality, if someone grows even one extra finger on a hand or foot, we go to a doctor for surgery. Then how can the Devi have eighteen arms? How would clothes be stitched for her? Ganesha has a face like an elephant, but have you ever seen such a human being? And how can he ride a mouse? If someone that heavy sat on it, why would the mouse not be crushed?

Then the answers began to make sense. Ganapati is the symbol of **discriminating intelligence (viveka-buddhi)**. And the mouse is the desire, the cravings, within all of us. They eat up our time; they are also time itself. Discriminating intelligence is the power that can steer these desires in the right direction. That is why it is said that these forms were created only for the welfare of the seeker.

## How Long Should We Worship Forms?

Now let us turn to the Bhagavad Gita. The nineteenth verse of the seventh chapter tells us how long this worship of forms should continue:

> **bahūnāṃ janmanāmante jñānavānmāṃ prapadyate |**
> **vāsudevaḥ sarvamiti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ ||**
> "At the end of many births, the one who has knowledge takes refuge in Me, realizing that Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.19)

That is, at the end of many births, in the last birth, the wise person takes refuge in Me. "The last birth" means the birth of his liberation. It has to be the birth of a sage or an incarnation; otherwise it cannot be the last birth.

*Prapadyate* means he worships. The wise also worship; it is not that they do not. Ramakrishna Paramahamsa was a realized soul, and he worshipped Mother Durga until his last moment. Here in Maharashtra, Saint Tukaram and the other saints kept chanting "Vitthal, Vitthal" to the very end. Devotees of Shiva, worshippers of the Devi, all have done it. The question is: until when, and how?

*Vasudevah sarvam iti*: God alone is everywhere, Vasudeva is everything. If you are a Shaiva, you should see "Shiva is everything"; if you worship the Devi, you should see "the Devi is everywhere." When you see this, the seeker is no longer a seeker; at that very moment he becomes a *mahatma*, a great soul. But such a mahatma is *sudurlabha*, extremely rare. You do not come across such a person on the streets. One who sees God in all becomes like a living, realized guru.

But seeing God in everyone is not easy. It means you should see God even in your enemy. In a quarrel between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, each should see God in the other, even if one of them is standing there with a rolling pin in her hand! In a fight between husband and wife, Shiva should be seen in the husband and Parvati in the wife. If your house is burgled, you should see God even in that thief.

This is described as the final step of the spiritual journey. Until this state is attained, the worship of images is necessary.

## The Feeling Behind Worship and the Power of Tirtha

We perform the fivefold worship (panchopachara), with sandal paste, flowers, incense, lamp and food offering; in the sixteenfold worship (shodashopachara) there is also the ritual bath and more. These are all methods of worship. Now the question arises: does that image, made of metal, clay or marble, or in the form of a photo, need any of this? Does it need clothes, a bath, an abhisheka? In reality it does not.

Yet we do it, because our sages taught us. Why? Because it is a medium for expressing our devotion, a way of expressing our love in the form of service. The offerings we make, whether fivefold, tenfold, sixteenfold or sixty-fourfold, we dedicate to a photo, an image or a yantra.

The important point is this: I have accepted that photo, image or yantra as the Supreme Lord. Another religion may not accept it, but I was told from childhood, "Consider this Bappa," so for me it is God. Once I have accepted it as God, the water of the abhisheka I performed, that *tirtha*, has come out after touching God. When I take that tirtha into my body, my feeling should be that before any medicine, this will heal me.

The final line of the sixteenfold worship is exactly this: the ultimate proof of whether my worship was right is that I hold that tirtha within me.

> **akālamṛtyuharaṇaṃ sarvavyādhivināśanam |**
> "It removes untimely death and destroys all disease."
> (Tirtha-prashana mantra)

This verse says that before any outside medicine, drinking this tirtha will remove disease, ailment, and dangers like untimely death. Now notice: what has this water touched? The Supreme Lord. Who accepted Him as the Supreme Lord? I did. Who performed the abhisheka? I did, or the priest performing it. Now if I feel, "What benefit will I get from drinking this tirtha?", it means it never reached the Lord, and then the entire worship becomes futile. This is given at the end of our own ritual of worship.

That is, if the faith did not awaken in you that this tirtha is truly divine, the worship remains a mere action. I am not saying do only this and abandon science. No, science is necessary too, but this feeling is needed along with it. This is what the nineteenth verse of the seventh chapter of the Gita teaches.

## God Even in the Thief: Seeing the Lesson

From the twentieth verse onward, the Gita takes up this question: if the Supreme is everywhere, why do we worship images? If the thief is also the same Lord, should we worship the thief too? This is a very deep question.

A police inspector asked me, "You teach unconditional love, so should we let an accused person go, because he too is a form of Vasudeva?" I said, no. It is not that you let him go; there is a method to it.

Take the example of a mother and her son. It is said:

> **lālayet pañca varṣāṇi daśa varṣāṇi tāḍayet |**
> **prāpte tu ṣoḍaśe varṣe putraṃ mitravadācaret ||**
> "Pamper a son for five years, discipline him for ten; when he reaches sixteen, treat him as a friend."
> (Chanakya Niti 3.18)

For five years give the child only care and love. Up to ten years, give education and discipline, and if needed, loving correction. And after sixteen, explain things to him as a friend.

Now suppose a thief came to your house. Then you should see that God has come into my life in the form of this person to teach me a lesson, or to play some divine play. If you are a realized guru, you will say, "This is the Lord's play." If not, he has come to teach you a lesson. First I must understand what that lesson is. This does not mean he should not be taken to the police station.

The first lesson should be: if he was a thief, then I too must have stolen at some time. Someone will say, "I have never stolen, I do not remember any, I am completely pure from birth." Fine, but the Lord says, the Devi says, that you have stolen; otherwise the thief could not have come. You must have done it in a past life. He says, "I remember my past life too, and I did not steal even once then." All right, still you must have stolen time. At your job you took a salary for eight hours and spent two hours chatting. You spent extra time in the name of tea breaks. You took the pay for that wasted time for free. You have stolen something, which is why that thief came to your house today to teach you this lesson; otherwise he would not have come.

Then he thinks back: "I did not do even that, I used to work longer hours. Then why was I robbed?" No matter. Then the Devi must be telling you, to make your Shri Vidya bear fruit, that you know how to earn but not how to keep. This is Kali Yuga; you must have forgotten, you must leave the keys lying on top. A bank manager does not own the money, yet he has to look after others' money. If he starts keeping it carelessly, it will not do.

There is certainly some lesson. The day this begins to be understood, *Vasudevah sarvam iti* begins to be understood.

## Serving the Living Image

Until then, worship of the image is necessary, and offering it fruit and flowers is necessary, although the image did not need any of it. In verses twenty to twenty-three of the seventh chapter, Shri Krishna explains that you should have learned to do all this for the living image. Image worship is to be done until you are able to do it for the living image. It is a practice match.

Where were you supposed to give true charity of food and clothing? To the people. You were supposed to give it to the living goddess and the living God. They need that charity, that food; the stone does not. It was only a medium.

> **patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyaṃ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati |**
> **tadahaṃ bhaktyupahṛtamaśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ ||**
> "Whoever offers Me with devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water, that offering of love from a pure heart I accept."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.26)

The Lord says, offer a leaf, a flower, a fruit, *toya* meaning water, anything at all. Offer even a brinjal, anything; if there is devotion in you, offer whatever is fitting and sattvic. And further He says that I come in a form and eat it.

It is not that you must worship the physical image every day. **Work itself is worship.** As they say, "My work is my worship." Until you reach the living image, you need the image.

> **ananyāścintayanto māṃ ye janāḥ paryupāsate |**
> **teṣāṃ nityābhiyuktānāṃ yogakṣemaṃ vahāmyaham ||**
> "Those who worship Me, thinking of no other, ever steadfast, I carry what they lack and preserve what they have."
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.22)

In *paryupasate*, *pari* means intensely, *upa* means near, and *asana* means sitting. I must sit near the Lord, who is present in the form of people, through the service of people. If you do this, then *nitya*, always, permanently, the Lord will carry your welfare.

An insurance company puts "Yogakshemam vahamyaham" in its emblem, meaning we will take care of you after you die. But Krishna says that while you are alive, during your lifetime, I will take care of your welfare. Do not worry about how you will get your salary; it will come on its own, you serve the people. If you are an entrepreneur, be a good entrepreneur and give a good product. That will be your worship, those will be your offerings. Take it forward; Lakshmi will come to you on her own. What has to stay will stay, what has to come will come.

This does not mean give up worship. But the form of worship should gradually move in another direction: first toward all living people, and then beyond them as well, toward the formless. Through those in whom the formless shines, we have to go to the formless.

## "Refute Kali": Seeing Beyond the Image

Ramakrishna Paramahamsa worshipped the Divine Mother. His guru was Totapuri, a worshipper of the formless. Ramakrishna kept saying again and again in his Bengali, "Kali! I am not having the experience. I worship you so much, I worship your image, I adore you in the form of Kali, so why am I not having the experience?"

His guru gave only this answer:

> "When you refute Kali, you will attain Kali."

That is, do not stop at the image; look beyond it. You should see both the form and the formless together. If the guru is seen only as a body, it is of no use, because this body will perish one day. Do you see both the guru and the guru-principle speaking through him at the same time?

We say one person is good and another is bad. Everyone thinks, "I am the good one, the other is bad." All right, suppose I am good and the other is bad. There are two bodies, and good or bad is expressed through them. But after some time, when both die, does good action come from my dead body, or bad from the other's? No. Neither good nor bad comes from either dead body. So what was being expressed through both while they lived was that formless reality. That is what needs to be known. That is spirituality, the study of the Self.

So "refute Kali" does not mean breaking the image, but learning to see beyond it.

That is what is called *Purushottama*. In the Gita the Lord says that this city is made of Purusha and Prakriti. This body with nine gates is the city. Know the one who sleeps in it or is sometimes awake. And the highest among them is the Purushottama, the Supreme Person. That is why Shri Ram is called Maryada Purushottama. So whether it is a goddess or a god, Shiva or Shankar, we have to go beyond them too.

Read the Devi Purana; it clearly says that the One alone is everywhere. The Durga Saptashati also describes many goddesses, Aindri, Maheshwari and others. But during the battle with Shumbha and Nishumbha, the Devi herself proclaims, "These are not separate; it is I alone. I am the One, do not think of me as separate." This is the awakening that began in Antar Yog.

## Three Goddesses, Three Gunas

In the Durga Saptashati three goddesses appear: **Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati.** You cannot work with only one of them. If you take only Goddess Kalika and leave the others, there will be imbalance. That is why it is necessary to understand the fourteenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, which describes the three gunas called sattva, rajas and tamas.

> **sattvaṃ rajastama iti guṇāḥ prakṛtisambhavāḥ |**
> **nibadhnanti mahābāho dehe dehinamavyayam ||**
> "Sattva, rajas and tamas, the gunas born of nature, bind the imperishable soul to the body, O mighty-armed one."
> (Bhagavad Gita 14.5)

Now what does the word *guna* mean? Most people take guna to mean a characteristic, a property or quality. But look in a Sanskrit dictionary and it is clear that one meaning of guna is **a cord, a thread.** These are three cords that bind us. "Quality" is a secondary meaning; the main meaning is "a binding cord." There are three cords that can bind me; this is maya.

In the Gita the Lord says that even sattva binds. Whether it is a golden chain or an iron chain, if it holds you bound, neither is of any use. Knowledge, happiness, dispassion, awareness, discernment all come from sattva. But if I become attached to that knowledge, that I must read and keep reading, then my next birth will be in a library! Then bondage has come. I am searching for happiness, striving for happiness; that too is a problem. If I keep chanting only "Guru, Guru, Guru," I will have to be born again to serve the guru; there will be no liberation.

That means neither sattva alone, nor rajas alone, nor tamas alone is suitable. To act, you need rajas. To gain knowledge, you need sattva. And to rest, or to destroy or bring something to an end, you need tamas too.

But do we do only one of these things all day? What you are taking in right now is knowledge, so you are in a state of sattva. If you now fall asleep, tamas has come, but at the wrong time. You should have slept at night and be awake now, right? And the act of being awake and sitting here is also an action, and that has rajasic energy in it. That is, all three gunas are needed together; one only has to understand which guna should predominate where.

## The Colours of the Goddesses' Garments

The goddess of sattva is **Mahasaraswati.** The colour of sattva is white, so Saraswati's garment is white.

Rajas is when you say, "I work with blood and sweat," running around, doing this, doing that, "my blood is boiling." Pressure rises on the heart, on the blood pressure, on the breath; all this is rajasic energy. Its colour is said to be red. That is why **Mahalakshmi's** garment is always red. In the same way the colour of tamas is black, so **Mahakali** wears black.

But in practice, have you ever seen a woman who owns clothes of only one colour? There may be a uniform for the office sometimes, but otherwise it is not possible. If it were, how easy it would have been for Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh, buying just one sari! In the shop women say, "Show me another design in this colour." The poor shopkeeper shows another design, and they say, "Show me another colour in this design." This cycle does not end easily. So whoever imagined these forms changed only the colour of the border of their saris. In truth these three colours are the colours of the three gunas.

## Four Varnas from Three Gunas

From these three gunas, the four varnas are formed: Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra.

> **cāturvarṇyaṃ mayā sṛṣṭaṃ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ |**
> "The four varnas were created by Me according to the division of qualities and actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.13)

The Lord says, "I created the four varnas by the division of guna and karma." He did not use the word *jati* (caste); He used the word *varna*. We had only lineage, gotra and varna. Jati is a matter for animals like dogs, cats, oxen, horses and cows; there are different breeds, like the Jafarabadi buffalo. There, jati applies. But the word "caste" in that sense is foreign. From "casta" came the word "caste," and it was imposed on us because we were under subjugation for a long time. The idea that a potter's son must only be a potter and a king's son must only be a king was not in our tradition. Our society was based on gunas, and from those gunas the four varnas arose.

**Brahmin: white.** Those in whom Saraswati predominated, the intellectual, the reasoning, the seekers of knowledge, those who practised God-realization, became the Brahmin class.

Take the example of a traffic signal. When nothing was known, someone researched and found that people see red most quickly, while most things around appear green. So red was kept for stop, green for go, and orange as a warning to stop. Someone must have discovered this through learning, right? Then the zebra crossing, where vehicles must stop. Because of the intelligence of whoever designed this, accidents will not happen. Consider him the Brahmin of that field. I am saying of that field, the one who applied his intellect.

But the useful Brahmin is the one who understands the rule of the signal and stops his own car too. The Brahmin who only gives discourses, who is a guru but does not follow it himself, is useless. If he has complete sattva, the white colour, his car will certainly stop.

**Kshatriya: pink.** But sattva does not have the power to compel others, "Hey, stop!" If I stop and nobody else does, there will still be a traffic jam. Then what use is the signal? But the system was good, the idea was good; everyone's welfare lay in stopping. Yet people's tendency is not to stop, because not every person is sattvic.

Then someone is impressed by the intelligence of the first person. He says, "Look! He worked eighteen hours a day to create this system. I too will follow it and make others follow it. But in return I want a little honour and wealth, a little Lakshmi, a little rajas. I will mix my white and red, sattva and rajas, and become a pink Kshatriya." He says, "I am fully impressed by the guru or the Brahmin, and now I will use the power of action, the power of authority; in return, give me wealth." Because some desires remain in him. This is not improper. *Dharmasya mulam arthah*: wealth is needed to uphold dharma. He says, "I will collect road tax and other taxes. This is not a Brahmin's work; it is a Kshatriya's work." This is how the system runs. That is why a king should be a consecrated king, because he must obey the guru and follow sattva.

**Vaishya: brown.** A third person was constantly watching this king. He liked the rajas in it very much: eighteen hours of action, and from it wealth and power. But he never took the Brahmin's thinking into his head, and he added a little tamas too, for self-interest. So that class became the Vaishya, the pure trader. Black and red together make maroon, brown. His attention will be mostly on wealth; if public welfare happens, it is secondary. One who has learned Shri Vidya, who comes to the temple to worship Lakshmi and adds sattva to it, is a different person. Understand why the pure, born Vaishya comes third in order.

**Shudra: black.** The fourth is the serving class, considered purely tamasic. That is, the moment the owner leaves, the foreman leaves, he goes outside to smoke a bidi. Whether in America, Mexico, England or India, the worker's tendency will be the same. See what tendency comes on its own when we ourselves take a government job. If we take up the work of a servant, tamas will increase on its own: "Boss, this fan is not working, the AC is not working, that job can't be done." We will immediately find a reason. "The computer shut down, let's rest, I have to go for tea." This is neither good nor bad. Work has to be got out of them.

These four varnas arise because of the three gunas. If this is understood, mutual love begins, because the importance of all three gunas is understood.

## Every Institution Needs All Four

Tell me, which institution does not need all four to run? There is a temple, so a priest is needed. But if there are only priests, will the temple run? Who will manage it? A Kshatriya is needed. Someone to bring materials and flowers, manage the money, find sponsors: a Vaishya is needed. And in the end, if everything is done but there is no one to clean? All four are needed, aren't they? If everyone said, "I only want to be a priest," what would happen?

To build a building, there will be an architect. Consider him the Brahmin of that building, because the plan and the intelligence are his. The engineer is the Kshatriya, because he has to go into the field. The supplier who provides cement and everything else is the Vaishya. And at the end you need the mason who quickly lays the cement. If everyone said, "I'll be the architect," all houses would remain on paper. If all became engineers, there would be no plan. If all said, "I'll supply the bricks," they would not even know what the bricks are for. And if all wanted to be only masons, there would be no work, no materials, no management and no plan. Then we could only be cavemen. So all four varnas are necessary.

## The Prophecy of Sage Agastya

The division that arose in India is something Antar Yog is working on a great deal; we are constantly spreading this awakening. If even one story from the Ramayana is understood, this dispute will end.

In what Sage Agastya revealed through Nadi astrology, my leaf contains this description: "You will leave this life only after solving the problem of this country's varna system. The work of uniting the four varnas is yours. Through this very body the quarrels and disputes of Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra will end." Sage Agastya said this two thousand years ago.

My retreats that spread this awakening are held in many places. Until we solve this, how will we become the world's guru? What ideal will we show other countries? That burn their scriptures, do this to them, give reservations only to these people? How will equality come from that? You have seen those three goddesses and their importance; from them come the three gunas, and from those three gunas the four varnas arose.

Can every person become a priest? Can every person become an architect? And if everyone did, would that be right? Not everyone will have those qualities, their birth will not be for that work, and not everyone will think that way. Why doesn't everyone feel, "I want to be Prime Minister," "I want to sit here as a guru," "I want to be a doctor"? All this is each person's destiny (prarabdha) and karmic bonds (runanubandha) from past lives; only then do I come to that place.

Whether you run an institution or a nation, all four varnas are needed, and within them the three gunas should be present in the right measure. But all this should happen under the guidance of the guru. The king should act under the guru's guidance, trade under the king's guidance, and service should run smoothly under the guidance of these three. It was not said "be subordinate"; it was said **"live with love."**

## Shri Ram and Hanuman: A Relationship on Three Levels

So listen to this story of Shri Ram and Hanuman, and much will become clear. People call Hanuman a devotee of Ram, a servant. In the devotion of a servant, Hanuman would say, "Yes, I am Shri Ram's servant."

Once Shri Ram asked Hanuman just one question: "What is the relationship between you and me? What do you truly feel? Why do you serve me? Tell me only this: what is our relationship?"

Hanuman's thoughts are clear. In one stroke he tells Shri Ram:

**The first level: duality.** "If you are asking from the awareness of the body, then your body and my body are separate. You asked about my relationship with you, so you must have asked from the sense of duality. Then you are my master and I am your servant. This is certain. In this birth I will not come to sit on your throne. I know my limits. I was born for this work; whatever karmic bonds there are, there are. I don't go looking at horoscopes. You are the one I worship."

**The second level: qualified non-duality.** Then he immediately says, "If you asked from the sense of qualified non-duality or duality-in-non-duality, from the level of mind and intellect, then you are the whole and I am your part." Had he spoken in English, he would have said, "You may be the whole, sir, but I am also a part." Do not think of me as incomplete.

He says, "You may be the ocean, H₂O and NaCl, water and salt. But I am a drop, so I too am H₂O and NaCl. The only difference is that ships cannot sail in my single drop, fish cannot live in it. But only when drops like me come together does your ocean form. Ships sail over you, but because of drops like us, one by one."

"If you are fire, I am its spark. If the sparks separate and leave one by one, your fire does not remain. Fire can burn anything, cook anything, but whatever it does, it does because of sparks like us. If you are king, it is because soldiers and servants like us exist that you sit on this throne as king."

Sattva opposes rajas and tamas, rajas opposes sattva and tamas, and tamas opposes rajas and sattva. When we run around, that is rajas. When we sit peacefully in meditation, that is sattva. How twelve arms? What does the elephant's trunk mean? If this worship is done, why and how will it bear fruit? And when we cannot show them the result of mantra practice, that is when the problem arises. Not being able to show the result means that the power has not yet awakened within the practitioner. That is, there is some flaw in our method; we have not yet reached the level of the Self, we have not followed a real guru at all. Only devotion is going on, and actions are limited to the level of the body.

Swami Chinmayananda used to say that a time would come when rationalists would greatly increase. At that time mere emotional devotees would not be able to satisfy them; then some guru would have to awaken and answer them in the language of science.

## The Symbolic Meaning of the Scriptures

When I remembered this, I began presenting all my discourses and scriptures, such as the Durga Saptashati, from a modern perspective, revealing their hidden meaning, so that today's people can understand.

For example, "Shiva said to Parvati" really means that my own Self conversed with my own mind and intellect. "Ganapati, do this" means that the discriminating intelligence within us should awaken. Chanda and Munda fought: who fought? Shumbha and Nishumbha came: who came? The Devi, as Mahishasuramardini, slays Mahishasura; **Mahishasura means the ego within me.** He keeps changing seven or eight forms, just as the ego keeps changing its forms. These are all symbolic stories. They do not happen somewhere outside; they happen within. In the same way I began decoding the symbolism of the Ganesha Atharvashirsha and other scriptures.

## The Secret of the Coconut

Then even rationalists began coming to us. They would ask: why must a coconut be offered? What is in it?

I told them that the brown outer covering of the coconut is a symbol of rajas and tamas. Red and black combine to make brown; that is, from rajas and tamas is born the ego with its hard shell. It is also full of desires, which have to be pulled out. When we pull out its tuft, its fibres, that is the renunciation of desires. Then the three eyes within become visible: your third eye will awaken.

The coconut has to be broken with force. This means the ego is not destroyed easily; it has to be broken. Only then does the white inside appear, meaning sattva awakens. And the sweet water that comes out of it is the knowledge of truth, the Ganga of wisdom.

That is why at the start of every programme we first offer a coconut. It is a symbol of giving up the ego. A factory starts, a coconut is broken; before starting a journey, the message is the same: "I set out having given up my ego." But people become proud of that very thing: "We broke two hundred coconuts!" Explain to them with logic, or at least by showing them the science. All this awakening goes on in Antar Yog.

## The Form of Mahakali

There is a lot more to say, but time is limited. Finally, this is a Kali temple, so I will speak in that context. Although all three forms of the goddess are popular, I will say just a few sentences about Kali and stop.

We have come here to worship Kali. Goddess Kali should awaken in all of us; that is what I say. What does Mahakali mean? She also has the power of destruction. She bears weapons, and she is of a very modern form. If you have looked at her dress, the other goddesses wear saris, but Kali's garment is like a skirt; it is modern. If you haven't seen it, go home and look at a picture of Kali.

What does that garment symbolize? Hands, that is, action. It is also for covering shame. Kali is not some outside woman; she is me, my own nature, which can be put to shame in society because of my actions.

She wears a **garland of heads**. The garland of heads does not mean only the destruction of outer enemies; it is the removal of the vices within me. If I was miserly and that miserliness was killed, I will become generous; one vice within me is gone. So each head in the garland symbolizes a vice; when it is slain, the garland is formed.

First our attention should be on killing our vices, and when needed, we should also know how to destroy the enemy outside. But that right comes only when you have become sattvic. Kali has a weapon, but that weapon also has an eye. She knows whom to kill and how much, where to love and where not. This discernment is shown by that eye. The weapon itself is not at fault; the problem arises when it is misused. The gurus say:

> "There should be a rosary in one hand and a spear in the other."

Every one of our gods also carries a weapon, don't they? When needed, we do need the power of Mahakali; there is no objection to that. The question is where, on whom, how and when that spear is to be used.

Did Arjuna know how to use weapons? He did. The problem was that he could not understand whom to use them on. The very first chapter of the Gita is Arjuna's Despondency. He did not have the strength to raise weapons against his own kin, and so the problem arose.

It is not that only other religions have wicked people; there are faults within us too. So first learn to use the weapon on your own vices. That is where the radiance of Mahakali appears. When we say that the power of the Lord wants to flow through me into the world, it is first necessary that there be purification within me. If there is no inner purification, talk of changing others is futile.

We go to hear a discourse and there is a delusion: "Oh! This applies to our uncle; this teaching is for my wife; Guruji said nothing for me." But the truth is that **every discourse is only for you.** In it you have to understand what you must do and what change you must bring into your own life.

## The Body First: Why Kali Worship Comes First

The power of Mahakali symbolizes self-protection, awareness of the body. So first of all our body should be strong. What do we see first, the Self or the body? When asked, "Who are you?", the first answer is: I am so-and-so, I am dark or fair, tall or short. We see the body first, and that is what seems important. The worship of Mahakali teaches this first truth.

The British said, "The pen is mightier than the sword." But open our Upanishads and you will see that the body comes first, the intellect after it. If given the choice, the body should first be strong and powerful. That is why Kali worship is taught first, because intellect alone does not work.

Someone became intelligent, became a scientist, devised formulas, but a strong man came, pointed a gun, and seized your poem or your work; that knowledge becomes useless in a moment. Intellect alone is not enough; strength is needed with it. "A sound mind dwells only in a sound body." Swami Vivekananda said the same abroad: "First play football, then you will understand Vedanta."

The same teaching is in the Chandogya Upanishad:

> **annamayaṃ hi somya manaḥ |**
> "For the mind, my dear, is made of food."
> (Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4)

The sage Aruni Uddalaka told his son and disciple Shvetaketu, "Fast for fifteen days, then tell me what you experience." After fifteen days of fasting, his body grew weak; he could not recite even one mantra properly, and he became dizzy. Then the guru says, "First you need strength, physical capacity and the power of protection." So Kali worship is the first worship. The first episode of the Durga Saptashati is also Kali's.

## Protecting the Institution and the Nation

When an institution or a company starts, the aspiration for its expansion is naturally born, the feeling that it will become big. "If you think, think big": that is the teaching of Shri Vidya. Fine, one should think big, but one must also think about what will result. When an organization expands, opponents, enemies and obstacles from the conflict of the gunas are bound to appear.

So first secure your copyright, trademark and patents. That too is a form of Kali power. This understanding should come: we have to protect our creation, our work. Then the institution grows further and guards have to be hired for its security.

In ancient times, when a yajna was to be performed, the sages called upon Shri Ram and Lakshman to protect it with bow and arrow, because demons came to disrupt the yajna. The same rule applies today. To do the work of non-violence, you need a protective force with you, that is, Kshatriyas. Today this work is done by the police, the army and our security guards, and that is Kali power. There is nothing wrong in it.

Today Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan are near us. How much of our worship do you think happens there? Hardly any. Why? It once did. Fifty years ago Bangladesh was ours; seventy-five years ago Pakistan was ours; a hundred years ago Afghanistan too was part of Bharatvarsha. Then we had our sacred seats of power there, worship was performed, our culture was present. Why is it not there today? Because that land is no longer ours. We could not protect that region; our borders changed.

> "If the nation does not survive, dharma will not survive. And when dharma does not survive, culture will not survive either."

When we first lost a part of our border, when part of Kashmir was lost, should it not have hurt our hearts even sitting here? Then Aksai Chin was lost; should we not have grieved then too? It was one piece at a time that the Mughals too were able to come deep inside.

Once the importance of the army is understood, in Antar Yog we always keep praying for the army and the police. Because they stay awake on the border at night, I can sleep peacefully here. This is the importance of Kali. Whether it is my company or this temple, security is necessary everywhere. Theft happens in temples too; in Kali Yuga people go after even the Devi's ornaments. So protection is extremely important. This is the message of Kali worship.

## Every Woman Is a Form of Mahakali

That was at the level of the institution. But every individual must also understand this. Woman too is a form of Mahakali. So to protect her virtue, to protect herself, there must first be practice of self-defence. This does not mean she should wield a sword, but practice of karate and the like is necessary. A mother should teach this to her daughter from childhood. Games like fugdi alone are not enough; this skill is essential along with them. This is the very form of Mahakali's worship.

That is why I always insist that schools and colleges should first of all have self-defence training, and along with it the observance of brahmacharya. Both are extremely important. After that, when the time for household life comes, desire as sanctioned by the scriptures.

## See God Everywhere, but Act According to Merit

When Hanumanji went to Lanka as a messenger, Ravana did not welcome him properly. Then Hanumanji slowly raised his own tail and made his own seat. Ravana had his tail set on fire, but with that very fire Hanumanji burned all of Lanka and returned victorious. This attitude comes from Mahakali. The scriptures do not say to tolerate everything.

The scriptures have said, "See God everywhere," but not "treat everyone as God."

> **vidyāvinayasampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini |**
> **śuni caiva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ samadarśinaḥ ||**
> "The wise see with an equal eye a learned and humble Brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste."
> (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

Shri Krishna says, whether it is a learned and humble Brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog or an outcaste, see the Lord in all. He said "see God in all," not "behave towards all as God." Remember this; I am telling you where Kali is.

A lion is standing in front of you. Is God in it or not? He is. Go and bow to it saying "O Lord," and see what happens! The lion will bow too, but it will say "Vadani kaval gheta naam ghya Shriharinche," the prayer before a meal; its intention is to eat you. Because you have come to know that Brahman is in it, but it has not come to know that you are Brahman. Sometimes the other does not have this realization.

That is why it is said: **treat on merit.** As the person is, so the conduct; quality for quality.

> **ye yathā māṃ prapadyante tāṃstathaiva bhajāmyaham |**
> "As people approach Me, so do I receive them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

The Lord says, "In whatever way people worship Me, in that way I respond to them." That is, as you behave towards Me, so will I behave with you. So has God taught "tit for tat" here, like small children: you hit me, I'll hit you? Would our God be so small? He is the Supreme Lord. He says, I will uplift you like a mother; I will not give up that quality of mine, but I will adopt a method suited to what you are.

You are Duryodhana, aren't you? You don't understand even when explained to, so learn by being beaten. In the end you do learn. Even Duryodhana realized at the end that he was wrong: "Everything is gone." But when did Duryodhana and Dhritarashtra understand? Finally, only by being beaten. Some understand by being beaten, some by being explained to, some by being given money, some only through some experience. This discernment should come within us. That is why today I spoke on the subject of Kali.

## The Modern Woman and Spirituality

Finally, I want to say one thing. Why are today's women, or people with modern thinking, not so eager to come to a guru or an ashram? Because we have made its form such that it is necessary to wear white clothes, to apply ash or sandalwood paste. They have not yet risen above those gunas.

I am saying this especially for women. A woman's nature is like nature itself, colourful; it is a basic inclination, however much you explain. In a shop, men's work is done in five minutes: "Give me this shirt, this design, this checked one," and it's done. But it can never be like that with women, because their nature is such that they look at everything in detail, and if they don't, it is as if they have done something wrong. The very concept of adornment has been given for women. So saying "give it all up now" is not immediately possible.

The Durga Saptashati describes that whether a little girl, a young woman, a mature woman or an old mother, all women are forms of the Devi.

> **vidyāḥ samastāstava devi bhedāḥ striyaḥ samastāḥ sakalā jagatsu |**
> "O Devi, all forms of knowledge are aspects of You, and so are all women throughout the world."
> (Durga Saptashati 11.6)

Among them there will be a woman in business, a great entrepreneur; is she not a form of Mahalakshmi? Should she then just sit with ash smeared on her? We just spoke of Kali, of soldiers. A woman in the army or the police: is she not a form of Kali? Should she too just sit with sandalwood and ash? She also goes into sports; she is active in every field.

Then Antar Yog began giving them this awakening, began honouring women, to show that they are just as capable. I am the only guru in India who openly says in discourses that **even a woman who wears powder and lipstick can attain liberation.** If you don't believe it, take out a picture of Lakshmiji and look. But will one attain liberation just by wearing lipstick and powder? No; by learning Vedanta, as Gurumaa just said.

## Freedom Without Vedanta: Gentle and Fierce Reversed

What did Vivekananda say? Women had long been in bondage; people wanted to bring them out, to give them freedom. And freedom did come. But both Vivekananda and Lokmanya Tilak were against women stepping out of the home without understanding Vedanta, because it could lead to their downfall. At that time neither of them prevailed, because that work was to be made possible through Antar Yog. What happened then? Women began earning money, but along with it infidelity increased, divorces increased, ego came.

The Durga Saptashati says that the Devi's form is both extremely gentle and extremely fierce.

> **atisaumyātiraudrāyai natāstasyai namo namaḥ |**
> "Salutations again and again to Her who is extremely gentle and extremely fierce."
> (Durga Saptashati 5.13)

Not understanding Vedanta, the opposite happened: at home, before their husbands, they became fierce, and outside, where wrong was being done, where they should have resisted, they became gentle: "Oh, that happens, it goes on." But what they truly should have done was this: **be extremely gentle within the home, and outside, wherever anyone's step is seen going in the wrong direction, take on an extremely fierce form.** That was understanding the true meaning of Vedanta. That is why Antar Yog today explains this to every woman.

## Ardhanarishvara: The True Meaning of Equality

As Gurumaa just said and as I keep saying, until we reach the form of **Ardhanarishvara**, neither will equality come nor will India become the world's guru. Other religions too have a male-dominated culture. Our scriptures say that the state of Ardhanarishvara is needed.

Ardhanarishvara means that the purpose of marriage is not merely the togetherness of husband and wife, man and woman; it is much deeper. Gradually, as the qualities of the woman are absorbed into the man and the qualities of the man into the woman, both have to move towards oneness. From the woman some loving feeling should come, and from the man the qualities of firmness, discipline and steadiness; this exchange is essential. Only by doing this does one move from vanaprastha towards sannyasa.

## Woman Is Not Equal to Man; She Is Infinitely More Powerful

There was talk earlier of women's equality. Today women are saying, we want to be equal to men. I say, no.

> "Woman was never equal to man; she was a thousand times more powerful."

Who gave birth to that man? Woman herself. When she was a mother, she carried the little child in her arms and held his hand to take him to school. At that time her power was not a thousand times but countless times greater than the child's. But at that very time she did not teach him brahmacharya, did not punish him for his wrong steps; that is where the mistake happened. Now she says, "You must be home before seven," because that same man has now gone astray. But he too was some mother's son, wasn't he?

The day women understand when and how they are a thousand times more powerful, everything will become clear. There is no need to describe the power of a devoted wife; it is said that a devoted wife has more spiritual power than the austerity a seeker performs by going to the Himalayas. Within her is austerity, power, spiritual strength. This should be spread quickly.

## Conclusion: Spreading Knowledge

This is a temple of the Devi. My request is that a great deal of devotion and action is already happening here; now programmes of knowledge should certainly be organized too. It will be of great benefit. If today we have a hundred or a hundred and twenty-five people, tomorrow there will be a thousand here, then ten thousand, then a hundred thousand, and then it will reach the whole country. How this practice of the Durga Saptashati, this composition of seed mantras, can reach at least three crore people, not just in one city but across all of India: that is the aim.

So today, in the end, I will ask only this: did you receive some new understanding, some experience, some change?
