# Chapter: What Is Your Spiritual Age?

## A Hard Truth

Today, let this hard truth settle firmly in your mind. Your age may even be ninety-five, yet if, as soon as Ganapati arrives, the first thought in your heart is "Come, let us see the decorations, let us go to that temple in the lane," then your **spiritual age** is still only zero to six years.

Tell me honestly, how old are you? I am speaking of the body's age. Whether your physical age is thirty, forty, sixty, eighty or ninety-five, if you still feel that going to the temple means only seeing what the idol looks like, "Come, there are decorations, the lights are up, oh, that bell is so big, they decorate with huge flowers over there, the lighting of that pandal is always so beautiful," then I am showing you your reality. On the spiritual journey you are still only zero to six years old. After that, there has been no spiritual progress at all.

## What Do You Go to See in the Temple?

I am speaking of those who go only to see this much. Saints also go to temples. But if your aim has remained limited to this, then you are still six years old, perhaps four, and perhaps even two. Whether you are six, three or two depends on what you look at when you go there.

Yes, if you are older and are taking a child, then you are going for **karma yoga**. That is fine. The child is six years old, you are only fulfilling your duty for the child, and you yourself are a level ahead, so there is no problem in that.

So what was the first point? If this is your habit of seeing, you are six years old.

## Stories, Puranas and Serials: Six to Twelve Years

Now suppose you no longer enjoy these decorations and festivities. But your mind still says, "Come, let us read the Puranas. A Bhagavat Saptah is on, let us listen to the Bhagavat Purana. A Ram Katha is on, let us hear the story of Ram. This is on at the satsang. An animated film about God is showing. A Krishna serial is on, so let us watch the serial of Krishna Leela." Then your spiritual age is six to twelve years.

I am not saying, do not watch it. But also check where you have reached. Spiritually, you are now twelve years old. When your body falls away, the journey beyond this will begin in the next birth. I am not saying that listening to stories is wrong. But even at the age of eighty-eight, do you still need these picture-book stories?

Yes, if you are telling these stories to a child of six to ten, you are doing karma yoga. I have no complaint about that. My complaint is when you yourself are stuck there: Ganapati Bappa means the one with the trunk, who eats one hundred and twenty-one modaks at once. Within that, "Come, let us hear a new story, let us open the Purana again, that Maharaj's Purana katha is on television," then know for certain, directly or indirectly, spiritually you are twelve years old, not more than twelve. Even within that, depending on how long you sleep during the katha, you are nine, eight, six or five.

## I Am Waking You Up

Am I hurting your ego? I am waking you up, awakening you.

> "Listen to the Guru's words once with your heart, and that arrow will pass right through."

Then you will be reborn. First, all of that within you will die, and you will take a new birth.

Did I say that any of these things is wrong? This is a **sequence**. If it happens at that age, it is better, but your journey will continue. So I am not saying "stop." Either increase your speed, or in the next birth the journey will continue from that very point. This much I tell you, and I will not say a single thing that is untrue.

## Darshan from Outside and Attendance with God: Twelve to Fourteen Years

Now the next level. Wherever you come and go, do you feel the wish to bow at a temple at least once? If you do, you are twelve to fourteen years old.

Coming and going, people throw money from outside while driving past. You must have especially noticed it: the scooter stops on the road, there is a Ganesh temple outside, the dakshina from right there and the namaskar from right there, so that God does not say you did not mark your attendance. And there is also a "pistol namaskar"; what it means, I do not know. Have you seen it or not? I have seen it many times. After that, people keep lightly slapping their own cheeks. Having done all this, they are free to commit new sins.

This is the stage of a twelve to fourteen year old child. At that age, this is fine. But if you are forty-five and still doing this, then spiritually you are still twelve. There has been no progress after that; your spiritual journey stopped there. It suited that age, not now. I am not saying stop it either; I am saying there is much more to do beyond it.

And people have become clever too. Earlier they would tell someone, "Here, take this money, put it in the donation box inside," and he would put it in his own pocket. So now, at the temple on the main road, the donation box has been placed outside, so that whatever is to be given goes straight into it. And while they drop the money, the scooter stands right beside them. Darshan from outside, a quick glimpse of the face through the glass, in a hurry.

So this is the spiritual age of twelve to fourteen: looking inside through the glass and staying outside. And the people there are so devoted that the money goes in from outside, the pistol namaskar from outside, and God's attendance is marked: "God, I am going to the office now, remember me." This is the stage of early childhood in spiritual life.

After this, if your small problems get solved and your desires are also simple and small, you are still twelve to fourteen years old.

## Sulking and Crying Before God: Fifteen to Eighteen Years

Now tell me, do you sit and cry before God? "O God, what have you given me?" Out of worry, the drama of crying for hours and hours in the temple: "O God, I am keeping you in water. It is my vow; until my wish is fulfilled, I will not take you out. O God, what have you ever given me?"

> "Laughing, playing, crying: if you do this, understand that you are 15 to 17 years old."

Seventeen or eighteen. You still have not understood anything. That small idol was not going to do anything; its very deep meaning had to be understood, and that has not been understood at all so far. But it is all right; something more has been understood, some faith has certainly come. You have reached the age of fifteen to eighteen.

## The Ego of Atheism: Twenty-Five Years

After this come those who consider themselves even cleverer. In the name of science, it has settled in their minds that all this is false: "Ganapati? Is there anyone like an elephant? Nothing like that exists. I wore a ring, nothing happened. I wore this, nothing happened. It is all a bluff."

If this is what you have understood, then living among atheists with a negative intellect, ego has come into you. This is the age of up to twenty-five. It suits only up to that age, not beyond.

## A Life of Foolishness

After that, whatever your age, you may be caught in negativity, corruption, wrongdoing, cunning, alcohol, cigarettes, lust chasing after women, harming society, violence, or if nothing else, crying over your own troubles and slipping into depression; any of these can happen. Hurting yourself, hiding yourself away in isolation, "I do not want to talk to the world at all," "What can I do, my son is just like this, I will just sit quietly."

"In any case, there is no God, nothing really happens," and yet, "Come, go to this astrologer, go to that astrologer." Doing all this, a person truly becomes **mad**. I do not mean the madness of a mental hospital; I mean foolishness. If, saying "life is just like this," you have formed some new theory of your own, then you belong to that category of the twenty-five or twenty-six year old ignorant, foolish intellectual. Foolish and intellectual, both together.

## The One Who Truly Learns Vedanta

And if you have come with a sincere heart to learn Vedanta, to know what that Ganapati truly is, then either you are directly that eight-year-old who has come to the gurukul, or you have come at any age after being beaten by life.

> "But when you go from here after learning, you will go having understood perfect Vedanta; this I can guarantee."

## What Was the Festival, and What Are We Doing?

For the ten days in between, we only played loud music and looked at decorations, and what did we do on the tenth day? "Now there is no need for this idol." "Oh, your Ganapati was just the same. It is twenty-two feet tall, so it holds just that much clay."

Is the only use of that symbol to pass ten days? "Your Ganapati is big, ours is small, this decoration is nice." What was the meaning of the festival, and what are we doing? First, understand at least this much.
