# What Is Your Real Age? Not of the Body, but of the Soul

The calendar says you are thirty, fifty or eighty. But there is another age that no birth certificate records: your **spiritual age**.

In this discourse, Guruji holds up a direct, somewhat stinging, but very necessary mirror. It is not an insult, he says; it is a **reality check**.

## Ganapati Arrived, and Where Did Your Mind Go?

Guruji says that even if you are ninety-five, if the moment Ganapati arrives your heart immediately thinks, "Come, let us see the decorations, let us go to that temple in the lane," then your spiritual age is still **zero to six years**.

How big the bell is, how many flowers are arranged, how beautiful the pandal lights are: if this is the whole purpose of going to the temple, the spiritual journey has stopped right there.

Saints also go to temples. The difference lies in **what you go to see** there. That decides whether you are six, four or two.

*The last time you went to a temple, were your eyes searching for the meaning behind the idol, or only for the decorations?*

## Going with a Child: Karma Yoga

Guruji also keeps a balance here. If you are older and are taking a six-year-old child to the temple or a katha, you are doing **karma yoga** (selfless duty). You are fulfilling your duty for the child, and you yourself are a level ahead. There is no problem in that.

The problem is when **you yourself** are stuck there.

## Stories, Puranas and Serials: Six to Twelve Years

The next level belongs to those who no longer enjoy the decorations, but whose mind says: "A Bhagavat Saptah is on, let us listen. A Ram Katha is on. A Krishna Leela serial is showing, let us watch."

Guruji says the spiritual age of such a person is **six to twelve years**. And depending on how much they sleep during the katha, nine, eight or even five!

He makes clear that listening to stories is not wrong. But if even at eighty-eight our understanding is stuck at Ganapati Bappa being the one with the trunk who eats one hundred and twenty-one modaks at once, the question must arise. And if we do not move forward in this birth, the journey will continue from here in the next.

Acharya Ji says that he is not hurting our ego; he is awakening us.

## The Guru's Arrow and a New Birth

Guruji says that if you listen to the Guru's words once with your heart, that arrow passes right through. All the old within you dies, and there is a **rebirth**, in this very life.

This is not condemnation. It is a **sequence**. Every stage is good at its own age. Guruji is not saying "stop." He says: **increase your speed**.

## Darshan from Outside, Attendance with God: Twelve to Fourteen Years

If you see a temple while coming and going and feel the wish to bow, you are **twelve to fourteen years** old.

Guruji describes a very familiar scene. The scooter stops on the road, dakshina from outside, namaskar from outside, so that God does not say attendance was not marked. A quick glimpse of the face through the glass, and then: "God, I am going to the office, remember me." Some lightly slap their own cheeks, and then they are free to commit new sins!

The temple people have become clever too. Earlier, people would send money through someone, and he would keep it in his own pocket. Now the donation box has been placed right on the main road.

This suits a twelve-year-old child. But if the same thing is going on at forty-five, spiritual progress has halted there. Even if small problems get solved and desires are small, you are still at this stage.

*Is your prayer a relationship with God, or just attendance?*

## Sulking with God: Fifteen to Eighteen Years

Then comes the stage of complaint. "O God, what have you ever given me?" Crying for hours in the temple, keeping God's idol in water: "Until my work is done, I will not take you out."

Acharya Ji says that if laughing, playing and crying is what we do, we should understand that we are 15 to 17 years old.

Guruji says that some faith has come here, but understanding still has not. That small idol was not going to do anything; its **deep meaning** had to be understood.

## "It Is All False": The Ego of Twenty-Five

After this come those who say in the name of science: "Is there a God like an elephant? I wore a ring, nothing happened. It is all a bluff."

According to Guruji, this **ego**, picked up from living among **atheists** with a negative intellect, is the age of twenty-five. It suits up to that age, not beyond.

## The Foolish Intellectual

And after this, at any age, a person may fall into corruption, cunning, alcohol, cigarettes, lust, harming society, or else sink into depression, loneliness and crying over their own troubles.

"There is no God," and yet the running from this astrologer to that one. Guruji calls this **foolishness**, not madness. The person who builds the theory that "life is just like this" is, in his words, **foolish and intellectual** at the same time.

## The True Seeker: A Student of Vedanta

So which is the right direction? The one who comes with a sincere heart to know **what that Ganapati truly is** is either an eight-year-old who has come to the gurukul, or a seeker who has come at any age after being beaten by life.

Acharya Ji guarantees that whoever learns here will leave having understood perfect Vedanta.

## What Was the Festival, and What Are We Doing?

Ten days of loud music, decorations, "your Ganapati is big, ours is small," and on the tenth day: "Now there is no need for the idol." If the Ganapati is twenty-two feet, it is just that much clay.

Is the only use of the symbol to pass ten days? Guruji asks: **what was the festival, and what are we doing?** First, understand at least this much.

*When the festival comes this time, will you look beyond the decorations for its meaning?*

## Key Takeaways

- The body's age and **spiritual age** are different; the second does not grow on its own.
- Going to the temple only to see decorations: **0 to 6 years**.
- Understanding limited to stories, Puranas and serials: **6 to 12 years**.
- Namaskar from outside, attendance-style devotion, small desires: **12 to 14 years**.
- Sulking and crying before God: **15 to 18 years**.
- The atheist ego of "it is all false": **25 years**.
- Doing all this for a child is **karma yoga**, not wrong.
- This is a sequence; Guruji says not "stop" but **"increase your speed,"** or the next birth will continue from there.
- The true seeker looks for the meaning behind the idol, and that is where **Vedanta** begins.
