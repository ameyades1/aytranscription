# How Old Are You Really? Not Your Body, Your Soul

The calendar says you are thirty, fifty or eighty. Yet you have another age, one that no birth certificate records: your **spiritual age**.

In this discourse, Acharya Ji holds up a mirror that is simple, a little uncomfortable and deeply necessary. This is no insult, he says. It is a **reality check**.

Spiritual age is measured by understanding, not by the body. Someone who visits temples only to see the decorations is zero to six years old spiritually, even at ninety-five. Understanding that stops at stories, prayer as mere attendance and sulking at God are the next stages; the true seeker looks for the meaning behind the idol.

## When Ganapati Arrives, Where Does Your Mind Go?

You may be ninety-five years old. But if the moment **Ganapati** (Lord Ganesha) arrives for his festival, your first thought is "Let's go and see the decorations, let's visit the temple in that lane", then your spiritual age is still **zero to six years**.

How big is the bell? How lavishly are the flowers arranged? How beautifully is the pavilion lit? If seeing these things is the whole reason you visit a temple, your spiritual journey has come to a standstill right there.

Saints go to temples too. The difference lies in **what you go there to see**. That alone decides whether you are six, four or two.

*The last time you visited a temple, were your eyes searching for the meaning behind the idol, or only admiring the decorations?*

## Taking a Child Along: Karma Yoga

Acharya Ji keeps a balance here as well. If you are an adult taking a six-year-old to the temple or to a religious recitation, you are practising **Karma Yoga** (selfless performance of duty). You are fulfilling your duty towards the child while standing a level ahead yourself. There is nothing wrong with that.

The problem arises when **you yourself** are stuck at that stage.

## Stories, Puranas and TV Serials: Six to Twelve Years

The next level belongs to those who no longer care for decorations but whose minds say, "A **Bhagavat Saptah** (a seven-day recitation of the Bhagavata Purana) is on, let's go and listen. There's a **Ram Katha** (a narration of the life of Shri Rama). A serial about **Krishna Leela** (the divine play of Shri Krishna) is starting, let's watch it."

The spiritual age of such a person, Acharya Ji says, is **six to twelve years**. Depending on how much they doze off during the recitation, it could even be nine, eight or five!

He makes it clear that listening to these sacred stories is not wrong. But if at eighty-eight our understanding still stops at "Ganapati Bappa is the one with the trunk who eats a hundred and twenty-one **modaks** (sweet dumplings) at a sitting", we should start asking questions. And if we do not move forward in this life, the journey will resume from this very point in the next.

Acharya Ji says he is not trying to wound our ego; he is trying to wake us up.

## The Guru's Arrow and a New Birth

Once you truly take the Guru's words to heart, that arrow passes clean through you. Everything old inside you dies, and you experience a **rebirth** within this very life.

This is no condemnation. It is a **sequence**, and every stage suits its own age. Acharya Ji is not saying "Stop." He is saying: **move faster**.

## A Quick Bow from the Roadside: Twelve to Fourteen Years

If you pass a temple and feel the urge to bow, you are **twelve to fourteen years** old.

Acharya Ji describes a very familiar scene. A scooter pulls up by the road. The rider drops a **dakshina** (an offering of money) from outside and bows from outside, just so God cannot say they skipped attendance. A quick glimpse of the deity's face through the glass, and then: "Lord, I'm off to the office, keep me in mind." Some people give their own cheeks a light slap of repentance, and then they feel free to commit a fresh sin!

Temples have grown wise to this. People used to send money through someone else, who would quietly pocket it. Now the donation box sits right on the main road.

Such behaviour is charming in a twelve-year-old. But if it is still going on at forty-five, your spiritual growth has stalled there. Even if your small problems get solved and your wishes stay modest, you remain at this stage.

*Is your prayer a relationship with God, or just a way of marking attendance?*

## Sulking at God: Are You Fifteen to Eighteen?

Next comes the stage of complaint. "O Lord, what have You ever given me?" People weep for hours in the temple, or put the idol in water and declare, "I won't take You out until my work gets done."

If laughing, playing and crying like this is all we do, Acharya Ji says, we should understand that we are fifteen to seventeen years old.

At this stage some faith has arrived, he explains, but understanding has not. That small idol was never going to do anything for you; what you needed was to grasp its **deeper meaning**.

## "It's All a Lie": The Ego of Twenty-Five

After this come the people who speak in the name of science: "Is there really a god who looks like an elephant? I wore the gemstone ring and nothing happened. It's all a sham."

According to Acharya Ji, this **ego**, picked up from living among negative-minded **atheists**, belongs to the age of twenty-five at most. It suits someone of that age, and no one older.

## The Foolish Intellectual

Beyond this, at any age, a person can drift into corruption, cunning, alcohol, cigarettes and lust, or into harming society. Or they can sink into depression, loneliness and endless self-pity.

They insist that God does not exist, and yet they run from one astrologer to the next. Acharya Ji calls this **foolishness**, not madness. The person who builds a whole philosophy out of "That's just how life is" is, in his words, a **fool and an intellectual** at the same time.

## The True Seeker: A Student of Vedanta

What, then, is the right direction? Those who come with a sincere heart to learn **what Ganapati truly is** are either eight-year-olds entering a **gurukul** (a traditional school where students live with the Guru), or seekers of any age who arrive after life has dealt them its blows.

Acharya Ji guarantees that anyone who learns here will leave with a sound understanding of **Vedanta** (the philosophy of the Upanishads).

## What Was the Festival For, and What Are We Doing?

For ten days there is loud music, decoration and rivalry: "Your Ganapati is bigger, ours is smaller." Then on the tenth day: "We don't need the idol any more." A twenty-two-foot Ganapati is, in the end, just that much clay.

Is the only use of this sacred symbol to help ten days pass? Acharya Ji asks: **what was the festival meant for, and what are we doing with it?** Understand that much first.

*When the festival comes around this year, will you look past the decorations and search for its meaning?*

## Key Takeaways

- Your physical age and your **spiritual age** are different; the second does not grow by itself.
- Visiting temples only to see the decorations: **ages 0 to 6**.
- Understanding that stops at stories, Puranas and TV serials: **ages 6 to 12**.
- Bowing from the roadside, devotion as attendance, small wishes: **ages 12 to 14**.
- Sulking and weeping at God: **ages 15 to 18**.
- The atheist's ego of "It's all a lie": **age 25**.
- Doing all this for a child's sake is **Karma Yoga**, not a fault.
- This is a sequence; Acharya Ji does not say "Stop", he says **"Move faster"**, or the next life will pick up from the same point.
- True seekers look for the meaning behind the idol, and that is where **Vedanta** begins.
