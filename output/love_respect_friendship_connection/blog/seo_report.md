## 1. Summary

A very short discourse (the video is under two minutes; about 350 words per language) in which Acharya Upendra Ji widens the idea of love beyond marriage: love is one, but it takes a different name with each relationship, sneh (affection) toward those younger, prem or mitrata (friendship) toward equals, and aadar and samman (respect) toward elders. A fourth form is love before the Guru or someone worthy of reverence, and the Guru holds the highest place. To learn love, go first to the Guru, who lives in advaita (seeing no one as separate); after that, how to treat a child, a peer and an elder becomes clear by itself, and it becomes possible to love everyone by seeing each person as God. The main intent is informational ("प्रेम के प्रकार", "types of love", "is love only in marriage"), with an emotional layer from readers who think of love only in romantic terms. Generic "types of love" queries in English are dominated by articles on the Greek words for love; the realistic opportunity is the Hindi query "प्रेम के प्रकार" and the English phrase "love, respect and friendship" (also the video's title), where an Indian, Guru-centred answer stands out. The post works best as a short supporting post in the Love & Relationships cluster, linking to the deeper talks on love and on the Guru.

1. **क्या प्रेम केवल शादी में होता है? / Is love found only in marriage?**

2. **स्नेह, मित्रता और आदर में क्या अंतर है? / What is the difference between affection, friendship and respect?**
   ये एक ही प्रेम के नाम हैं: छोटों के प्रति स्नेह, बराबर वालों के प्रति मित्रता, बड़ों के प्रति आदर और सम्मान। / They are names of one love: affection for the younger, friendship for equals, and respect and honour for elders.
3. **प्रेम का चौथा रूप क्या है? / What is the fourth form of love?**
   गुरु या किसी सम्माननीय व्यक्ति के पास जाकर प्रेम व्यक्त करना; इनमें सबसे ऊँचा स्थान गुरु का है। / Love expressed before the Guru or someone worthy of reverence; of these, the Guru holds the highest place.
4. **प्रेम सीखने गुरु के पास ही क्यों जाएँ? / Why learn love from the Guru first?**
   क्योंकि गुरु स्वयं सबके साथ अद्वैत में जीते हैं; उनसे प्रेम समझ लेने पर बाकी रिश्तों में व्यवहार अपने आप समझ आने लगता है। / Because the Guru lives in oneness (advaita) with everyone; once love is understood from the Guru, how to treat everyone else begins to make sense on its own.
5. **क्या सबको भगवान मानकर प्रेम करना संभव है? / Is it possible to love everyone as God?**
   आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि हाँ, यह संभव है, और गुरु से प्रेम समझना इसका मार्ग है। / Acharya Ji says yes, it is possible, and understanding love through the Guru is the way to it.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Presumption vs statement about the Guru** (from the Sanskrit review, issues 1 and 4). The Hindi says the Guru "would be practising" advaita (करता होगा, non-honorific), the English says the Guru "is the one who practises it" and, in the takeaways, "lives non-duality every day". Recommendation: check the transcript and make both languages say the same thing, with the honorific form in Hindi (करते होंगे / करते हैं). The titles, meta, direct answer and FAQ use the neutral, supported wording "the Guru lives in oneness with everyone" (both languages state this in the next paragraph) and never "every day". No in-text link anchor uses the sentence in question, so the edit will not break a link.
2. **Gloss of आदर और सम्मान** (from the Sanskrit review, issue 2). Hindi glosses only "(respect)"; English has "respect and honour". Optional fix in the Hindi body; the SEO texts use "आदर और सम्मान" / "respect and honour" as in the takeaways.
3. **Romanisation of the five terms** (from the Sanskrit review, issue 3): sneha / prem / mitrata / aadar / samman mix conventions. Pick one system. The English SEO texts use the English words (affection, friendship, respect), plus "sneha" only in the direct answer, copied from the blog; update it if the editor changes the spelling.
4. **Advaita is used in its devotional sense.** The talk means the bhava of seeing no one as separate, not Advaita Vedanta doctrine. The tags therefore do not use "अद्वैत वेदांत" / "Advaita Vedanta", and the schema's `about` says "अद्वैत भाव" / "Advaita (non-duality)". Do not add Upanishad or Shankara references to titles or snippets.
5. **Tag "सर्वत्र भगवत् बुद्धि" / "Sarvatra Bhagavat Buddhi".** The blog does not use this term; it says "सबको भगवान मानकर प्रेम करना" / "seeing each person as God", which is the same idea as the term used in see_god_even_enemy. The tag groups these posts; it is not presented as a quotation from this talk. Drop it if the editor prefers tags only from the text.
6. **"Types of love" queries.** English searchers often expect the Greek eros/philia/agape list. The post does not discuss that and should not be framed as a comparison; the titles stay with the talk's own scheme (affection, friendship, respect, love for the Guru).
