# Chapter: The Difference Between Death and Liberation

## The Questions of Pitru Paksha

During the days of Pitru Paksha, some questions often arise in our minds, yet we do not know their right answers. Who are the *pitars*, the departed ancestors? What is the true meaning of *shraddha* and *tarpan*? What knowledge lies behind these traditions? And above all, who can truly give liberation to our ancestors? Can another person bring liberation to our ancestors? And if we ourselves do not become *pitars*, can we be liberated after death?

This is a very beautiful question, and among ordinary people there is also a great misconception about it. People assume that once a person has died, he has been liberated. Yes, it may be that he has been freed for a while from the problems he had here. But that is only a temporary release; much of his journey still remains. There is a great difference between death and liberation. Death and moksha are two different things, and you must understand the meaning of both words properly.

## Death: The Body Is Gone, but the Living Remains

Death means that only the body has gone, but the living remains. Many desires and wishes were still left. To fulfil them, a person will come back taking a new body. Whether he takes a human body, an animal's, a bird's, or anyone's at all, he must return in some form. This is what is called death. According to Hindu belief, such a being will certainly be born here again.

## Moksha: The Dissolving of Attachment

And the very meaning of the word moksha is **the dissolving of moha, attachment**. This worldly web of attachment and illusion in which I get stuck, all these desires, cravings and attachments, are dissolved in moksha. And moksha happens while one is alive. In it my body remains, but the "living" comes to an end.

This does not mean that the person has become lifeless or without breath. Nor does it mean that he no longer has enthusiasm. It is simply that his small worldly desires and cravings are no more. He has become **jivan mukta**, liberated while living, like a sannyasi. You should attain this state. This is what is called moksha.

## Only One Who Is Free Can Give Freedom

Now, when we speak of liberating our ancestors, we are speaking of the time after they have left this world. The right to do this belongs only to one who has already become liberated while living. Only such a person can bring moksha to another.

Understand it through an example. If someone is wealthy, only a wealthy person can give wealth to another. If someone is strong, only a strong person can use his strength to support someone or help in some other way, and give it away. In just the same way, if you want to bring liberation to someone, you yourself must have that state of being liberated while living.

## The Testimony of the Vishnu Purana

Even if you have a thousand brahmins, look at the fifteenth chapter of the third part of the Vishnu Purana. Read its fifty-sixth verse, the last verse of that chapter, and the three or four verses before it. There it is clearly said that even if you feed a thousand brahmins and have worship performed by them, it is still difficult for the ancestors to be liberated, whereas a single yogi can liberate them at once.

> (Vishnu Purana, Part 3, Chapter 15, Verse 56 and the verses before it)

This does not mean that brahmins cannot give liberation. It means that he must be an ascetic, one who has reached that state. Understand this carefully:

> "Only a person liberated while living can give liberation to another, and liberation is given while living."

The guru himself is alive, so while he lives he can bring this about.

## Do Not Wait for Your Son's Shraddha

Never wait in the hope that your son will someday come and perform your shraddha, and then you will be liberated. Never live in this worry.

There have been great rishis and sages. There is a gita called the "Putra Gita". In it there is a young brahmin boy, and his father is a learned brahmin. The father says to him, "Come now, study. Later on you will have to perform our shraddha and the rest; you will also have to liberate us. This is your responsibility."

Then the little boy says, "Father, so many rishis and sages never even marry. Some do marry, but they have no sons or daughters." Like Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Mother Sarada Devi; they had no children. So are they not liberated? Do they not attain moksha?

## Atma-Shraddha: Shraddha for Oneself

Many rishis and sages perform their own shraddha while still alive. The Garuda Purana also describes this, and it is called **atma-shraddha**, shraddha performed for oneself. One who has done this is liberated already. And only through such a person will your ancestors truly attain moksha.

## Pind Daan and the Test of the Crow

The test of this is also very simple. These days people are seated anywhere at all, the *pind daan* is performed, and the whole ritual is over in fifteen or twenty minutes. The crows, too, touch the *pind*, the offering of rice, and leave.

But I want to caution you a little about some of today's priests. They play a small trick. Listen carefully. Crows will not touch the *pind* so easily, and the priests know it too; then who would pay the fee? So they pour curd on top of the *pind*. Crows love curd, but even then the crow picks the curd off the top with its beak and does not touch the *pind* at all. Pay attention to this; it is happening everywhere these days. You should see whether the crow is touching the whole *pind* or not.

## From Nara to Narayana

This is the gap between liberation and death. I trust that you have now understood clearly that your resolve should be to attain moksha while living:

> "From nara to Narayana, from man to the Divine."
