# Death Is Not Liberation: The True Meaning of Pitru Paksha

When **Pitru Paksha** (the fortnight each year devoted to honouring one's ancestors) arrives, many questions rise in the mind. Who are the **pitras** (departed ancestors)? What do **shraddha** (remembering one's ancestors with reverence and offerings) and **tarpan** (the offering of water to the departed) really mean?

Beneath them lies the deepest question of all: who can truly set our ancestors free? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji sheds light on these questions with great simplicity.

Can shraddha liberate ancestors? According to Acharya Ji, death and moksha are not the same. Death takes only the body; unfulfilled desires bring the soul back to another birth. Moksha, as he explains it, is the dissolving of attachment, and it happens while living. So only one who is already free while living can liberate the ancestors; completing the ritual alone is not enough.

## Does Dying Set You Free?

Many of us assume that whoever has passed away has been set free. Acharya Ji calls this a common misunderstanding.

Yes, after death a person escapes the problems of this world for a while. That escape is temporary, though, and much of their journey is still unfinished. Death and **moksha** (liberation), Acharya Ji says, are two different things.

## Death: The Body Goes, the Living Remains

Death means that only the body has gone, while the "living" is left undone. Unfulfilled wishes and longings still remain.

For their sake the soul returns in a new body, whether human, animal, bird or any other. According to Hindu belief, such a soul is bound to be born here again.

*If you left your body today, which desires would pull you back here?*

## Moksha: The Dissolving of Attachment

The word **moksha** itself carries its meaning: **moha-kshaya**, the dissolving of attachment. Worldly attachment and illusion, our cravings, desires and clinging, all the things we get stuck in, dissolve in moksha.

This happens while you are still alive. The body remains, but the "living" comes to an end.

It does not mean that the person becomes lifeless or loses all enthusiasm. The small worldly cravings are simply gone. Such a person becomes **jivan mukta** (liberated while living), much like a renunciate. This, Acharya Ji says, is the state we must reach.

## Who Can Liberate Our Ancestors?

When we speak of liberating our ancestors, we mean what happens after they have gone. Only someone who is already free while living has the power to do this.

Acharya Ji's example is very direct. Only a wealthy person can give someone wealth. Only a powerful person can use that power to help another. In the same way, only someone who lives in that state of freedom can grant liberation, and it is granted while they are alive.

## A Thousand Brahmins and a Single Yogi

Acharya Ji cites the Vishnu Purana as proof. Read the fifty-sixth verse of the fifteenth chapter in its third part, which is the chapter's last verse, along with the three or four verses before it.

> (Vishnu Purana, Part 3, Chapter 15, Verse 56)

The passage states plainly that even if you feed a thousand brahmins and have them perform worship, your ancestors will still struggle to find release, whereas a single yogi can free them at once.

This does not mean brahmins cannot grant liberation. It means that whoever performs the rite must be an ascetic, established in that state.

## Why Wait for Your Son to Perform Shraddha?

Acharya Ji says you should never sit waiting for the day your son comes to perform your shraddha, thinking that only then will you be free.

In the **Putra Gita** (a dialogue between a father and son on what truly frees a person), a brahmin father tells his young son, "Study hard. When you grow up, it will be your responsibility to perform our shraddha and set us free."

The boy replies, "Father, so many sages and seers never marry, and some who do have no children." Think of Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Sarada Devi. Does that mean they never attain moksha?

## Atma-Shraddha: Performing Your Own Rites While Living

Many sages perform their own shraddha while they are still alive. The Garuda Purana describes this practice and calls it **atma-shraddha** (shraddha performed for oneself).

Whoever has done this is already free. And it is through such a person that your ancestors truly find liberation.

*Have you handed your own freedom to someone else and stopped worrying about it?*

## Pind Daan and the Test of the Crow

These days **pind daan** (offering balls of rice, called pinds, to the ancestors) is finished in fifteen or twenty minutes, and the crows, it seems, touch the pind and fly off. Acharya Ji, however, sounds a note of caution.

Crows do not touch a pind easily, and some priests know it. So they pour curd over it. The crow pecks the curd off the top with its beak but does not touch the pind at all.

Watch carefully, then, to see whether the crow is touching the whole pind or not.

## From Nara to Narayana

This is the distance between death and liberation. Acharya Ji's message is clear: resolve to attain moksha while you are alive, and rise from **nara** (a human being) to **Narayana** (the Divine).

## Key Takeaways

- Death takes only the body; unfulfilled desires force the soul to be born again.
- Moksha means the dissolving of attachment, and you attain it while living.
- A person liberated while living is not lifeless; their worldly cravings are simply gone.
- Only someone liberated while living can grant liberation to others, ancestors included.
- According to the Vishnu Purana, a single yogi can do at once what even feeding a thousand brahmins struggles to achieve.
- Do not wait for your son to perform shraddha; sages become free while living through atma-shraddha.
- Stay alert during pind daan and check whether the crow truly touches the whole pind.
