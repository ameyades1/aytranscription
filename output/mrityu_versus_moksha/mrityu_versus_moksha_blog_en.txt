# Death Is Not Liberation: The True Meaning of Pitru Paksha

When Pitru Paksha comes, many questions arise in the mind. Who are the *pitars*, our departed ancestors? What is the true meaning of *shraddha* (remembrance of the ancestors performed with reverence) and *tarpan* (the offering of water)?

And the deepest question: who can truly give liberation to our ancestors? Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji shed light on these questions with great simplicity.

## Is Dying the Same as Being Liberated?

Many of us assume that one who has passed away has been liberated. Acharya Ji says this is a common misconception.

Yes, after death a person is freed for a while from the problems of this world. But that release is temporary. Much of the journey still remains. As Acharya Ji says, death and moksha are two different things.

## Death: The Body Is Gone, but the Living Remains

Death means that only the body has gone, but the "living" remains. Unfinished desires and wishes are still there.

For their sake the being returns with a new body: human, animal, bird, anything at all. According to Hindu belief, such a being must be born here again.

*If your body were to fall away today, which desires would pull you back here?*

## Moksha: The Dissolving of Attachment

The very meaning of **moksha** is **the dissolving of moha, attachment**. The worldly web of attachment and illusion, the desires, cravings and attachments in which we get stuck, are dissolved in moksha.

And this happens while one is alive. The body remains, but the "living" comes to an end.

This does not mean the person becomes lifeless or loses enthusiasm. It is simply that the small worldly desires are gone. He has become **jivan mukta** (liberated while living), like a sannyasi. Acharya Ji says this is the state we must attain.

## Who Can Give Liberation?

When we talk about liberating our ancestors, we are talking about the time after they have gone. The right to do this belongs only to one who has himself become free while living.

Acharya Ji's example is very simple. Only the wealthy can give wealth to someone. Only the strong can help someone with their strength. In the same way, only one who has that state of being liberated while living can give liberation. And that liberation is given while living.

## A Thousand Brahmins and One Yogi

Acharya Ji cites the Vishnu Purana. Read the fifty-sixth verse of the fifteenth chapter of the third part, the last verse of that chapter, and the three or four verses before it.

> (Vishnu Purana, Part 3, Chapter 15, Verse 56)

There it is clearly said that even if you feed a thousand brahmins and have them perform worship, it is still difficult for the ancestors to be liberated, whereas a single yogi can liberate them at once.

This does not mean that brahmins cannot give liberation. It means the one who performs it must be an ascetic, established in that state.

## Why Wait for Your Son's Shraddha?

Acharya Ji says: never wait in the hope that your son will someday come and perform your shraddha, and then you will be liberated.

In the "Putra Gita", a brahmin father says to his young son, "Study. Later on it will be your responsibility to perform our shraddha and to liberate us."

The boy replies, "Father, so many rishis and sages never marry, and some have no children." Like Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and Sarada Devi. So do they not attain moksha?

## Atma-Shraddha: One's Own Shraddha While Living

Many rishis and sages perform their own shraddha while still alive. The Garuda Purana describes this, and it is called **atma-shraddha**.

One who has done this is already liberated. And it is through such a person that your ancestors attain true moksha.

*Are you resting easy, having left your liberation in someone else's hands?*

## Pind Daan and the Test of the Crow

These days the *pind daan* is finished in fifteen or twenty minutes, and the crows touch the *pind*, the rice offering, and leave. But Acharya Ji gives a warning.

Crows do not touch the *pind* easily, and some priests know this. So they pour curd on the *pind*. The crow picks the curd off the top with its beak but does not touch the *pind* at all.

So watch carefully whether the crow touches the whole *pind* or not.

## From Nara to Narayana

This is the gap between death and liberation. Acharya Ji's message is clear: resolve to attain moksha while living, to rise from nara to Narayana, from man to the Divine.

## Key Takeaways

- Death is only the departure of the body; unfinished desires compel the being to be born again.
- Moksha means the dissolving of attachment, and it is attained while living.
- A person liberated while living is not lifeless; only his worldly desires are gone.
- Only one who is liberated while living can give liberation to another, ancestors included.
- According to the Vishnu Purana, what is difficult even by feeding a thousand brahmins, a single yogi can do at once.
- Do not wait for your son's shraddha; sages become free while living through atma-shraddha.
- Stay alert during pind daan and see whether the crow truly touches the whole pind.
