# What Is a Guru, and Why We Must Understand It Before Becoming a Vishwaguru

## What Is a Guru: a Person or the Doorway to Truth?

What is a Guru? Is the Guru just a person, or the doorway to the ultimate truth itself?

The scriptures say that without the Guru there is no knowledge, and without knowledge there is no **mukti** (liberation). Yet today, understanding a true Guru has become harder than ever.

*What does the Guru mean in your life: a person, or a path to the truth?*

## The Guru Gita: The Secret of the Guru Principle

The **Guru Gita** appears in the Skanda Purana, one of the ancient Puranic scriptures, as a dialogue between Lord Shiva and Devi Parvati. It reveals the true nature of the Guru, the Guru's glory, and the complete mystery of the bond between Guru and disciple.

It explains why the Guru is essential, how the Guru's grace works, and how taking true refuge in the Guru ends the ignorance that clouds our lives. It is not merely a collection of verses but a scripture that gives life its direction.

**Jivanmukta** (liberated while still living) Sadguru **Brahmarshi** (a sage who has realised the Absolute) Acharya Upendra Ji has presented the meaning of every verse in simple language, with a moving commentary and examples drawn from real life, so that anyone can read and understand it with ease.

## "May the Whole World Be Well"

The Honourable Nitin Gadkari Ji, India's Union Minister of Road Transport and Highways, released the book. He reminded everyone of the core spirit of our culture: we do not pray for our own welfare alone; we pray for the welfare of the whole world.

He said that Acharya Ji guides everyone to awaken this spirit, and to study and reflect on our own ways of worship, especially from a scientific point of view.

## Before Becoming a Vishwaguru

Everywhere today people talk about India becoming a **Vishwaguru** (teacher to the world). Acharya Upendra Ji raises a basic question within this conversation: if India is to be a Vishwaguru, everyone should first understand what a Guru is.

## In Whom Should We Take Refuge Today?

Shri Krishna said to Arjuna:

> **sarvadharmān parityajya māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja**
> **ahaṃ tvā sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Set aside all duties and take refuge in Me alone. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

Acharya Ji points out that Arjuna at least had Shri Krishna standing before him. For us, the question remains: to whom should we surrender as a **sharanagata** (one who has surrendered)? Who is a Guru, how many kinds of Guru are there, and what are we meant to do once we take refuge?

*When you hear the word "refuge", what questions rise in your mind?*

Acharya Ji explains that no text available today answers these questions, and where a text does exist, no commentary on it is available. That is why he wrote a detailed commentary on the Guru Gita of the Skanda Purana, and he has put everything into this publication.

## Key Takeaways

- Without the Guru there is no knowledge, and without knowledge there is no liberation.
- The Guru Gita of the Skanda Purana, a dialogue between Lord Shiva and Devi Parvati, reveals the nature of the Guru and the mystery of the Guru-disciple bond.
- Our culture prays for the welfare of the whole world, not for "my welfare" alone.
- Before India can become a Vishwaguru, we must understand what a Guru is.
- After "māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja" (take refuge in Me alone), the question is in whom we should take refuge today; Acharya Upendra Ji's commentary on the Guru Gita answers it.
