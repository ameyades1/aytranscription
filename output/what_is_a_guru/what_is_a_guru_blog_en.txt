# What Is a Guru and Why We Must Understand It Before Becoming the World's Guru

## The Question That Comes First

What is a Guru? Is the Guru only a person, or the very door to the Supreme Truth?

The scriptures say that without the Guru there is no knowledge, and without knowledge there is no liberation. Yet today, understanding a true Guru has become the hardest thing of all.

*What does Guru mean in your life: a person, or a path to the truth?*

## The Guru Gita: The Secret of the Guru Principle

The **Guru Gita**, which appears in the Skanda Purana as a dialogue between Shiva and Parvati, reveals the true nature of the Guru, the Guru's glory, and the complete secret of the Guru-disciple relationship.

It explains why the Guru is necessary, how the Guru's **kripa** (grace) works, and how taking true **sharan** (refuge) in the Guru ends the ignorance of life. It is not merely a collection of verses but a scripture that gives direction to life.

Jivanmukta Sadguru Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji has presented the meaning of every verse, with a moving commentary and real examples from life, in simple language, so that even an ordinary person can read and understand it with ease.

## "May the World Be Well"

This scripture was released by Honourable Shri Nitin Gadkari Ji, Minister of Road Transport and Highways, Government of India. He reminded us of the core feeling of our culture:

> "We do not say, may I be well; we say, may the world be well."

He said that Acharya Ji gives everyone guidance for awakening this feeling, and for studying and reflecting on our modes of worship, especially from a scientific point of view.

## Before Becoming the World's Guru

Everywhere today there is talk that India should become the Vishwaguru, the Guru of the world. Into this discussion Acharya Upendra Ji places a basic question:

> "If we are to become the Guru of the world, then everyone should first understand what a Guru is."

## Whose Refuge Should We Take Today?

Shri Krishna said to Arjuna:

> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> **ahaṁ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> "Abandoning all duties, take refuge in Me alone. I shall free you from all sins; do not grieve."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

Leave aside all dharmas and come to My refuge alone. I will free you from all sins, do not worry.

Acharya Ji says that for Arjuna, at least, Shri Krishna was there in person. But for us the question is: to whom should we become **sharanagat** (one who has surrendered)? Who is a Guru, how many kinds of Gurus are there, and what does one actually do after taking refuge?

*When you hear the word "refuge", what questions arise in your mind?*

Acharya Ji explains that no scripture is available today for these questions, and where one is, its commentary is not. That is why he wrote a detailed commentary on the Guru Gita of the Skanda Purana, and in this publication he has given everything.

## Key Takeaways

- Without the Guru there is no knowledge, and without knowledge there is no liberation.
- The Guru Gita of the Skanda Purana, as a dialogue between Shiva and Parvati, reveals the nature of the Guru and the secret of the Guru-disciple relationship.
- Our culture does not say "may I be well" but "may the world be well".
- Before becoming the world's Guru, we must understand what a Guru is.
- After "take refuge in Me alone", the question arises of whose refuge we should take today; Acharya Upendra Ji's commentary on the Guru Gita answers exactly this.
