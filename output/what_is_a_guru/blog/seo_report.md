what_is_a_guru: SEO report

## 1. Summary

A short talk (4:07 video) given at the release of Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji's commentary on the **Shri Guru Gita** (Skanda Purana, a dialogue between Shiva and Parvati), released by Union Minister Nitin Gadkari. It opens with the question "गुरु क्या है? / What is a Guru: a person, or the doorway to the ultimate truth?", says that without the Guru there is no knowledge and without knowledge no liberation, quotes Gadkari Ji on "विश्व का कल्याण हो", and makes its central point: if India is to be a Vishwaguru, everyone must first understand what a Guru is. It ends on Gita 18.66: Arjuna had Krishna before him, but in whom do we take refuge today? The Guru Gita commentary is offered as the answer. The main intent is informational ("guru kya hai", "what is a guru", "गुरु गीता"), with a navigational layer (Acharya Ji's Guru Gita commentary, the book launch). "What is a guru" is a broad, competitive query (dictionaries, Wikipedia, large yoga and ISKCON sites), so the realistic opportunity is the Hindi query "गुरु क्या है" and the Guru Gita cluster ("Guru Gita Skanda Purana", "Guru Gita commentary"), where pages are mostly verse lists without explanation. The blog is short and announces the commentary more than it answers the question in depth; it works best as an entry page that links readers into the deeper Guru & Disciple posts.

## 6. FAQ

Answers come only from the blog. Kept in this report; no FAQPage schema is added (the FAQ is not on the page).

1. **गुरु क्या है? / What is a Guru?**
   The talk asks whether the Guru is just a person or the doorway to the ultimate truth itself, and says that without the Guru there is no knowledge, and without knowledge no liberation.
2. **गुरु गीता कहाँ से है? / Where does the Guru Gita come from?**
   The blog presents it as part of the Skanda Purana, in the form of a dialogue between Lord Shiva and Devi Parvati. It explains why the Guru is essential, how the Guru's grace works and how true refuge in the Guru ends ignorance.
3. **Why should we understand the Guru before India becomes a Vishwaguru?**
   Acharya Ji's point is simple: if India is to be a teacher to the world, everyone should first understand what a Guru is.
4. **In whom should we take refuge today?**
   Krishna told Arjuna "मामेकं शरणं व्रज" (Gita 18.66), and Arjuna had Krishna before him. For us the question remains whom to take refuge in, who a Guru is and what to do once we take refuge; Acharya Ji wrote his commentary on the Guru Gita because no available text or commentary answered these questions.
5. **Who released Acharya Ji's Guru Gita commentary?**
   Union Minister of Road Transport and Highways Nitin Gadkari Ji, who recalled that our culture prays "विश्व का कल्याण हो" (may the whole world be well).

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Gita 18.66 Hindi meaning "तू चिंता मत कर"** (Sanskrit review, minor): Gita Press renders "मा शुचः" as "तू शोक मत कर". Recommendation: fix in the blog text; no SEO text quotes this line.
2. **"धर्म" as "duties" in English** (review, minor): Hindi keeps "धर्मों"; English could read "Abandoning all dharmas (duties) ...". Editorial choice; not used in any SEO text.
3. **IAST word break "sarvadharmān parityajya"** (review, minor): not wrong; change only if the house style mirrors the Devanagari joins.
4. **Source of the Guru Gita stated without qualification** (review, minor): the blog says the Guru Gita is in the Skanda Purana; tradition (the text's own colophon, Uttara Khanda) supports this, but it is not in every printed edition. The title tag and meta say "स्कंद पुराण की गुरु गीता" and the secondary keywords include "Skanda Purana", following the blog. Recommendation: acceptable as is; if the editor adopts the review's "traditionally held to be from the Uttara Khanda" wording, the meta stays accurate. Do not claim a specific chapter or verse count.
5. **Term glosses differ between Hindi and English** (review, minor): formatting only.
6. **"गुरु के बिना ज्ञान नहीं, और ज्ञान के बिना मुक्ति नहीं" is attributed to "the scriptures" without a verse** (review, could not verify): the FAQ and direct answer repeat it as "the scriptures say", exactly as the blog does, and add no reference. Do not attach a specific verse in schema, FAQ or snippets unless one is verified.
7. **Thumbnail slogan "The Most Important Book for Kaliyug"**: promotional; not used in titles, metas or FAQ, to avoid a superlative the blog does not make.
8. **Title promise**: "What is a Guru?" sets the expectation of a full answer, while the talk mainly poses the question and points to the commentary. The recommended titles pair the question with "the Guru Gita" so the page delivers what the title promises (the Guru Gita's view of the Guru, as introduced in the talk). No title claims to list kinds of Guru, which the talk does not do.
