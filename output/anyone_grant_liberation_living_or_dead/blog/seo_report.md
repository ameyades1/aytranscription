## 1. Summary

The talk asks a question many families carry, especially around Pitru Paksha: can a priest, a ritual or anyone at all grant moksha to a departed parent, or to oneself? Acharya Ji's answer has three parts. Pitars are souls that got stuck on the journey from nara to Narayana. Death means the body goes while the mind keeps "living"; moksha means the body stays while the "living" ends (jivanmukti), so liberation does not come automatically with death. And only a Guru who is himself free can free others, living or dead. The main intent is **informational** ("who can", "difference", "meaning"), with an emotional layer for readers grieving a parent or worried about rites at Gaya or Trimbakeshwar. The opportunity is the narrow question "who can grant moksha / liberation": most results are generic definitions of moksha or ritual guides for pind daan, and few answer who can actually liberate. Two other talks on this site cover the death-versus-moksha definitions closely (see section 7, cannibalization), so this post should own the "who can grant liberation" query (focus keywords "मोक्ष दिला सकता है" / "grant liberation", taken from the body's own wording) and leave the broader "मृत्यु और मोक्ष" queries to them. Searches rise around Pitru Paksha (usually September); refresh the post 4 to 6 weeks before.

## 6. FAQ

For the WordPress page only. Answers come from the talk, and Acharya Ji's own readings are attributed to him.

**Hindi**
1. **मोक्ष कौन दिला सकता है?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार केवल वह गुरु जो स्वयं जीवन्मुक्त है, मोक्ष या कैवल्य की अवस्था में है, वही किसी को मरने के बाद भी और जीते-जी भी मुक्ति दिला सकता है। जो खुद बंधन में है, वह दूसरे को बंधन से नहीं छुड़ा सकता।
2. **मृत्यु और मोक्ष में क्या अंतर है?** मृत्यु में शरीर छूट जाता है पर मन अपनी वासनाओं और संस्कारों के साथ जीवित रहता है। मोक्ष में शरीर रहता है पर इच्छा, वासना और "जीना" समाप्त हो जाता है।
3. **क्या मरते ही अपने-आप मोक्ष हो जाता है?** आचार्य जी कहते हैं, नहीं। जो सोचता है कि मरकर छूट जाएगा, वह उल्टे संस्कारों के कारण पच्चीस जन्मों में भी नहीं छूटता। मोक्ष शरीर छूटने से नहीं, मन के छूटने से होता है।
4. **मोक्ष शब्द का क्या अर्थ है?** आचार्य जी मोक्ष को "मोह का क्षय" कहकर समझाते हैं। मोक्ष धर्म, अर्थ और काम के साथ चार पुरुषार्थों में से एक है।
5. **क्या गया या त्र्यंबकेश्वर में पिंडदान से पितरों का मोक्ष हो जाता है?** आचार्य जी के अनुसार केवल कुछ श्लोक पढ़ देने और कौवे के पिंड छू लेने से मोक्ष इतनी आसानी से नहीं होता। श्राद्ध और पिंडदान पितरों की तृप्ति के लिए शास्त्रसम्मत कर्म हैं, पर मुक्ति के लिए स्वयं मुक्त गुरु चाहिए।

**English**
1. **Who can grant liberation (moksha)?** According to Acharya Ji, only a Guru who is a jivanmukta, established in moksha or kaivalya, can grant liberation to anyone, whether after death or during life. A person who is still bound cannot release anyone else from bondage.
2. **What is the difference between death and moksha?** In death the body falls away, but the mind lives on with its cravings and samskaras. In moksha the body remains, but desire, craving and the "living" come to an end.
3. **Does liberation come automatically after death?** Acharya Ji says no. Whoever thinks "once I die, I will be free" will not be free even in twenty-five lifetimes, because their samskaras point the wrong way. Liberation comes when the mind is released, not when the body falls.
4. **What does the word moksha mean?** Acharya Ji explains moksha as moha-kshaya, the dissolving of attachment. It is one of the four purusharthas, with dharma, artha and kama.
5. **Does pind daan at Gaya or Trimbakeshwar liberate the ancestors?** According to Acharya Ji, freedom does not come as easily as a priest reciting a few verses and a crow pecking at the pinda. Shraddha and pinda offerings are rites for the ancestors' satisfaction, but liberation needs a Guru who is himself free.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"Liberation after death is wrong" stated as scripture (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The blog says the scriptures' definitions rule out liberation after death, but scripture describes liberation at the moment of leaving the body (Gita 8.5, 8.13; videhamukti). Acharya Ji's point is that liberation does not come *automatically* with death. All SEO texts above use that wording ("मरते ही अपने-आप मोक्ष नहीं होता" / "does not come automatically when we die"). Apply the review's correction to the body and the Key Takeaways before publishing. Don't use titles like "There Is No Moksha After Death" or "Scriptures Say Moksha Never Comes After Death".
2. **Pitars described as ghosts (review issue 2, Major, not yet applied).** The blog treats all pitṛs as wandering ghosts stuck between Bhuh and Bhuvah and implies this comes from the Gita, Garuda Purana and Katha Upanishad. In scripture the wandering soul is the preta; a pitṛ has reached Pitṛloka (Gita 9.25), and the "between Bhuh and Bhuvah" placement has no verified source. This can upset readers who perform shraddha. Attribute it to Acharya Ji as the review suggests. Keep "ghost", "भूत" and "pitru dosh" out of titles, meta descriptions and social snippets; the keyword "पितरों की मुक्ति" is used without fear language.
3. **"Moksha = moha + kshaya" (review issue 3, Minor, not yet applied).** This is a teaching breakdown (nirukti), not grammar; mokṣa comes from the root muc / mokṣ, "to release". FAQ 4 attributes it ("आचार्य जी ... समझाते हैं" / "Acharya Ji explains"). Don't target "मोक्ष शब्द की व्युत्पत्ति" / "moksha etymology" with this reading.
4. **"Seven levels of worlds" (review issue 4, Minor).** The vyahritis name the seven upper lokas of fourteen. Not used in any SEO text; apply the review's "seven upper worlds" wording in the body.
5. **Vyahriti source and IAST (review issues 5 and 6, Minor).** Add the Mahanarayana Upanishad / Taittiriya Aranyaka 10 source if wanted, and write "oṃ" for "oṁ". No SEO text quotes the mantra.
6. **Dismissing shraddha (review issue 7, Minor, not yet applied).** The Gaya / Trimbakeshwar passage can read as rejecting shraddha and pinda-dana, which the Garuda Purana prescribes for the ancestors' satisfaction. This post targets grieving families ("liberation of ancestors"), so add the review's bridge sentence. FAQ 5 uses it only if the editor adds it to the blog. Don't write snippets like "Pind daan is useless" or "Priests cannot help your ancestors".
7. **"Only a Guru can free you" and lineage claims.** The post's central claim is Acharya Ji's teaching; present it as such ("according to Acharya Ji"). Don't imply in titles, meta or ads that the Foundation or Acharya Ji offers liberation of ancestors as a service, and don't pair this post with paid-service calls to action (Naadi Jyotish, puja bookings) on the page; that would read as selling moksha, which is the very thing the talk criticises (worry about dakshina).
