# Moksha After Death or Liberation While Living

Only one who is himself free can grant moksha to another. It sounds simple, but a great truth is hidden within it.

Many people believe their moksha will happen on its own after death. Today let us understand this illusion, and see the real difference between **death** and **moksha**.

## Pitars: Those Stuck on the Journey

To become a pitar (ancestral spirit) means to be stuck. Our journey is from **nara to Narayana**, from man to the Divine, and pitars are those who stopped somewhere in between.

The scriptures describe seven levels of worlds:

> **oṁ bhūḥ oṁ bhuvaḥ oṁ svaḥ oṁ mahaḥ oṁ janaḥ oṁ tapaḥ oṁ satyam**
> (The seven vyāhṛtis)

Bhūḥ is this earth, then comes bhuvaḥ, then the heavenly world, and progressing like this one reaches **Satya Loka**, the final step.

Pitars get stuck between bhūḥ and bhuvaḥ itself. They have made no progress; only the body has been left behind. They wander here as ghosts and want to return, but they are not yet even fit to take on a body. And they stay right around you.

How this happens, why the desire to free them arises in you, what trouble they cause and how to recognise them, all this will be learned from scriptures like the Gita, the Garuda Purana and the Katha Upanishad. For now this much is clear: **the pitars have not been liberated, and that is why there is a problem.**

## The Meaning of Moksha: The Decay of Moha

Now let us leave the pitars and talk about ourselves. This birth has been given to us precisely to become free.

**Dharma, artha, kama and moksha** are the four **purusharthas** (the four aims of life). **Moksha** means **the decay of moha** (attachment): moha + ksha.

## "I Want Moksha", But Before That?

People talk big: I want moksha. Ask them what they want before that, and a whole list comes out: I want to get married, I want to do an MBA, I want to be rid of this disease.

> "Having come to the Guru, you tell lies. As long as this remains, your moksha is not going to happen anyway."

*Is there a long list of desires standing behind your own wish for "moksha" too?*

## "When I Die, I Will Be Free": The Greatest Illusion

Some people say, once I die, I will be free. One who says this will never be freed, not even in twenty-five births, because his very **samskara** is reversed.

## What Is Death?

We have a body and a mind. At death the body stays here, and what goes out is your **mind** itself, along with its desires and samskaras.

The principle of the Atman is everywhere. The combination of mind and Atman is called the **jivatma**.

> "The body has gone, but the living remains."

This is **death**: the body has been left, but the mind is still alive.

## What Is Moksha?

A **jivanmukta** (one free while living) still has the body, but the living has ended. Desire, craving, no problem remains. This very state is called **moksha** or **mukti**.

That is why **the idea of "moksha after death" is itself wrong.** If the scriptures give these two definitions of death and moksha, moksha cannot be an event that happens after death.

*Is it only your body that is living, or is your mind still entangled in "living"?*

## Can Anyone Free Our Ancestors?

We say that I will liberate those of ours who have passed away. But is this within the power of an ordinary person? No.

Going to Trimbakeshwar or Gaya, where some local pandit recites four shlokas and a crow pecks at the pinda, it does not happen that easily. If it did, there would be no problems left in our country.

## Only One Who Is Free Can Free Others

Only the Guru who is truly a jivanmukta, in the state of **moksha** or **kaivalya** (complete liberation), can grant liberation to anyone, both after death and while living.

> "Only one who is himself free can grant moksha to another. One who is himself in bondage, how will he free another from bondage?"

Only one who has seen that world can show you that world. One who himself fears death, who worries about his own job, who worries about your dakshina, what will he free you or your ancestors from?

## Key Takeaways

- Pitars are souls stuck between the bhūḥ and bhuvaḥ worlds on the journey from nara to Narayana.
- Moksha means the decay of moha.
- Death: the body has gone, but the living (mind, desires, samskaras) remains.
- Moksha: the body remains, but the living has ended. This is jivanmukti.
- The idea of "moksha after death" is wrong; one with reversed samskaras is not freed even in twenty-five births.
- Liberating ancestors is no simple ritual; only a Guru who is himself free can grant liberation.
