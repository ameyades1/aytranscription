# Chapter: In Life or After Death, Who Can Grant Liberation?

## A Fundamental Question

Only one who is himself free can grant liberation to another, isn't that so? Many people believe that they will attain moksha after death. So today, understand well this important difference between death and moksha.

## Pitars: Souls Stuck on the Journey

To become a pitar (ancestral spirit) means to be stuck. Our journey was from nara to Narayana, from man to the Divine, and somewhere along it we got stuck. There are seven levels of worlds:

> **oṁ bhūḥ oṁ bhuvaḥ oṁ svaḥ oṁ mahaḥ oṁ janaḥ oṁ tapaḥ oṁ satyam**
> (The seven vyāhṛtis, the expanded form of the Gayatri mantra)

Bhūḥ means this earth. Then comes bhuvaḥ, then the heavenly world, and progressing in this way one reaches Satya Loka, the ultimate and final step. The pitars who get stuck are stuck between bhūḥ and bhuvaḥ itself. They have not progressed at all. Only the body has been left behind. Even now they wander here in the form of ghosts. They want to come back here once more, but their state is not yet even fit to take on a body. This is something strange, and they wander right around you.

What happens and how it happens, we will learn. We will also study scriptures such as the Gita, the Garuda Purana and the Katha Upanishad. Why the desire to free them arises in you, we will learn that too. Since they keep wandering around you, what trouble they cause you will be explained, along with how to recognise that they are around you.

This simply means that they have not been liberated. That much is clear, isn't it? The pitars have not been liberated, and that is why there is some problem.

## The True Meaning of Moksha

Now leave the pitars aside; let us talk about you. We have been given this birth precisely to become free, haven't we? Dharma, artha, kama and moksha are the four purusharthas. Moksha means the destruction of moha, of attachment. Moha and ksha: the decay of moha.

## "I Want Moksha", But Before That?

People talk very big: I want moksha, I want moksha. Ask someone what they want, and they say straight away, I want moksha. I said, all right, fine, and what do you want before that? Then the whole world's things come out: I want to get married, I want to do an MBA, I want to be rid of this disease.

So say plainly that you want that. Having come to the Guru, you tell lies. As long as all this is within you, your moksha is not going to happen anyway.

## "When I Die, I Will Be Free": The Illusion

People think they will attain moksha after death: once I die, I will be free. There are people who say this. One who says this will never be freed. He will not be freed even in twenty-five births, because his very samskara is reversed.

## What Is Death?

So understand today this important difference between death and moksha. We have a body, and we also have a mind. After death the body remains here and something else goes out. That is nothing but your mind. Desires, mind and samskaras all go with it. The principle of the Atman is everywhere. This combination of mind and Atman is what we call the jivatma.

So my body has been left behind, but the living remains, meaning the mind is still alive. What is this called? Death.

> "The body has gone, but the living remains."

This is death. Did you understand this?

## What Is Moksha?

And what would moksha mean? A jivanmukta, one liberated while living, is one whose body remains, but whose living has ended. Desire, craving, no problem remains. The living has ended, yet the body remains. This very state is called moksha, or mukti.

> "The body remains, but the living has ended. This state is called moksha."

Therefore the very idea of "moksha after death" is wrong. Understand this much first. Have you truly absorbed this sentence? If our scriptures give these two definitions of death and moksha, then is the idea of moksha after death wrong or not?

## Can Anyone Free Our Ancestors?

And yet we say that I will liberate those of ours who have passed away. First tell me, is this within the power of an ordinary person? No, it is not within the power of an ordinary person. Going to Trimbakeshwar or Gaya or somewhere else, where some local pandit recites four shlokas and has a crow peck at the pinda, it will not happen that easily. That much you understand now, don't you? If it were that easy, there would be no problems at all in our country.

## Only One Who Is Free Can Free Others

Only the Guru who is truly a jivanmukta, established in the state of moksha or kaivalya, can grant liberation to anyone, both after death and while living.

> "Only one who is himself free can grant moksha to another."

How will one who is himself in bondage free another from bondage? Do you agree with this sentence? Only one who has seen that world can show you that world, isn't that so?

One who is himself stuck here, who himself fears death, what will he free you from? What will he save you from? What will he do for your ancestors? He himself is worried about when he will get a job. You will bring such a Bhatt ji or such a Guruji and say, perform the puja, but he is worried about your dakshina. What will he liberate you from? Or your ancestors?
