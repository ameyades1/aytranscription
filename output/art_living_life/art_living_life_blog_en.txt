# Beyond the Art of Living: The Art of Leaving the World

## What in Life Comes with a Guarantee?

Think for a moment. What in life is fully **eternal** and certain?

Getting a job, no guarantee. Getting married, no guarantee. If it happens, that it will last, no guarantee. If it lasts, that it will bring happiness, no guarantee either.

Wealth will come, no guarantee. If it comes, how long it will stay, no one knows. So what is certain? If you have taken birth, **death**. That is sure to happen.

## However Long the Life, It Is Not Infinite

Someone leaves at one year of age, someone even while being born. There are records of people living 180 years. Yogis in the Himalayas are said to live 400 and 800 years.

Of Changdev Maharaj, we read of an age of 1400 years in the time of Dnyaneshwar. The scriptures also describe the **chiranjeevi** (great beings held to be immortal).

But however long life is, it **cannot be infinite**. For an ordinary person the rule is simple: where there is birth, there is death.

## Do You Know When That Day Is?

What is certain must be prepared for. But can anyone say whether that day is today, tomorrow, or a hundred years away?

We keep doing things for others, for ancestors and forefathers, for neighbours. But your own turn may come before theirs.

You may slip and fall, an accident may happen, a heart attack may strike while sitting at home, an earthquake may come. You were fine until yesterday; today anything can happen. Acharya Ji asks: when its date is not even known, and one must be ready for it, then how should life be lived?

*If you had to leave the stage today, would you be ready?*

## The Art of Living and the Art of Leaving

Many people can teach the Art of Living: how to live here, what one's duty is.

But Acharya ji speaks of another art: **the art of leaving the world** (Art of Leaving). Life is a play, and one day this play is bound to end. So learning how to leave the stage is a very important knowledge.

Where will this knowledge be found? With a film star? With a businessman, a politician or a school teacher? No.

For this, it is essential to go to a **jivanmukta Sadguru** (one who has become free of all bonds while still living). Here there is no option; it is a necessity.

## One Can Give Only What One Has

If you want wealth, you must go to a wealthy person. Only one who has plenty can give.

Only one filled with love can give love. Only one who is forgiving will forgive.

In just the same way, only one who is himself **free**, liberated while living, **videhamukta** (risen above the limits of the body), can grant you liberation. The rest can only talk.

If you are to have real experience, the Guru must know how to give **shaktipat** (the transmission of spiritual energy from Guru to disciple), for you and for your forefathers too.

*Has the one you are learning from experienced it himself?*

## Theory Versus Experience

That is why the true authority is only a living Sadguru, one in the state of liberation while living. Everything else is merely bookish knowledge.

Acharya ji gives an example. Suppose someone knows all the theory of mechanical engineering but has never held a spanner. He knows the theory of computers but has never pressed a single button.

Or someone teaches the art of war, knows a great deal of theory, but has never held a sword. Is there any benefit in learning from him?

One must learn from the one who has attained it, who has seen that other world. There lies the benefit of charity, of knowledge, of meditation. Acharya Ji says that otherwise, however much one does, it is a waste of time, a waste of money, a waste of life.

## Key Takeaways

- Only death is certain in life; nothing else comes with a guarantee.
- However long life is, it is not infinite, and no one knows the date of death.
- Along with the art of living, it is essential to learn the art of leaving the world.
- This knowledge can come only from a liberated Sadguru who is beyond the body.
- One can give only what one has; only the free can grant freedom.
- Theoretical knowledge without experience brings no real benefit.
