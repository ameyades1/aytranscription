# Chapter: The Art of Living and the Art of Leaving the World

## The Only Certain Truth in Life

If there is one thing in life that is eternal, that is certain, do you know what it is? One thing is sure to happen. That you will get a job, there is no guarantee. That you will marry, no guarantee. If you do, that it will last, no guarantee. If it lasts, that you will find happiness, there is no guarantee either. That wealth will come, no guarantee. If it comes, how long it will stay, that too is uncertain.

So only one thing is certain. If you have taken birth, what is it? **Death.** This much is understood. This is sure to happen.

## However Long the Life, It Is Not Infinite

Someone departs at one year of age. Some are born dead; someone leaves even while being born. There are records of people living 180 years. And among those who live in the Himalayas, there are some who remain for 400 and 800 years. Of Changdev Maharaj, we read of an age of 1400 years in the time of Dnyaneshwar.

Whatever it may be, life **cannot be infinite.** The scriptures also describe some immortal great beings (chiranjeevi). But for ordinary people the rule is this: if there is birth, there is death.

## Preparing for What Has No Known Date

So what is eternal, what is certain, must be prepared for. For can even one of you say that tomorrow is the day of my death, or today, or right now? Or that it is one year, ten years or a hundred years away?

While doing things for others, for ancestors, for the departed forefathers, for the neighbour, your own turn may come before theirs. Isn't it so? Sometimes you may slip and fall, sometimes an accident may happen, something may happen while sitting at home, a heart attack may strike. You were fine until yesterday; today anything can happen. An earthquake can come.

> "When its date is not even known, and one must be ready for it, then how should life be lived?"

This question is very, very important.

## The Art of Living and the Art of Leaving

Many people can teach the art of living: how to live here, what one's duty here is. But there is also an art of leaving this world. How is this world to be left? One day or another, all this is to end. **The play is bound to end.** So has the preparation for it been made? You will have to learn this. It is a very important knowledge.

Did you not understand? If one day the play must end, then one has to leave the stage. So how does one leave the stage? Will you learn this by going to a film star? Will you get it by going to a businessman? Is it going to be found with a politician? Will you get it from a school teacher?

For this knowledge, it is absolutely essential to go to a **liberated Sadguru (jivanmukta)**. There is no option in this; it is a necessity.

## One Can Give Only What One Has

What is the reason? If I want wealth, I will have to go to a wealthy person. Only one who has plenty will give. **One can give only what one has.** Isn't that right?

If someone is a lover, love will fill him, and only then can he give love. A forgiving person will forgive you. In the same way, one who is free, who is already liberated, that is, liberated while living (jivanmukta), liberated beyond the body (videhamukta), only he can grant you liberation. The rest can only talk.

So if you are to have a real experience, that Guru must know how to give **shaktipat**, for your forefathers as well as for you. Such knowledge must be imparted that you understand what you have to do, how to live in this world.

## Who Is the True Authority?

So who is the worthy one for this? Who is the qualified authority? A living Sadguru, a supreme Guru in the state of liberation while living. One who is himself beyond the body, who has risen above the body, and is liberated while living, only such a Guru truly has the right to teach this. Everything else is merely bookish knowledge.

## Theory Alone Achieves Nothing

Suppose I know only the theory of mechanical engineering, of electrical currents, of everything, but have never actually held a spanner. Or I know the theory of computers but have never pressed a single button. Am I really teaching you anything? If I am teaching the art of war, I know a great deal of theory, but have never held a sword in my hand, is there any benefit in it?

So one must learn this from the one who has attained it, who has seen that other world. That is where the benefit lies. Giving in charity there brings benefit, taking knowledge from there brings benefit, meditating on him brings benefit. The benefit will be there alone.

> "Otherwise, however much you do, it is a waste of time, a waste of money, a waste of life."
