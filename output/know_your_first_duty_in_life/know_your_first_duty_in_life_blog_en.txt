# Do You Remember Your First Duty

In life we do so much. Studies, work, family, dreams. But have you ever wondered what, before all of this, your **first action** was?

In this short but deep discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji reminds us of something we all once said to ourselves, and then forgot.

## What Is the Very First Action?

Acharya Ji says that kindling the **fire of knowledge** within oneself is the very first action.

If I am to do anything in life, the first thought should be that there is a Guru in my life. Not just any Guru, but a **realized Guru** (a realized master), one who has known the truth himself.

> "Let there be a Guru in my life, a realized Guru, who takes me from nara to Narayana."

**From nara to Narayana** means the journey from an ordinary human being to divinity.

## A Promise Made in the Womb

This is not a new idea. Acharya Ji explains that this is the very promise each of us made to ourselves in the **womb**.

According to the Garuda Purana, the soul does not feel good in the mother's womb. It remembers things like the Vaitarani river. It understands where it is going, and that it has committed sins.

Then it resolves: on coming out, I will again meet a Guru, attain the **Vishnu tattva** (the divine principle), and correct the mistakes of my past birth.

## Twelve Days, and Then Forgetting

Here a wonderful point emerges. For twelve days after birth, the child remembers this resolve.

After the twelfth day, in **Vaishnavi Maya** (the veil of divine illusion), it forgets again what its first duty was. That is why the naming ceremony is prescribed after the twelfth day.

> "Past birth still remembers, but cannot express."

When a small child cries now and then, we think it wants milk. But Acharya Ji says it cries because *do not leave me again, I have not found it yet, in twelve days I am going to forget again.*

Acharya Ji asks whether we have read all this in the Garuda Purana and the Katha Upanishad. It has also been taught in detail in the Pitru Mukti Shivir.

*Have you ever paused to think about what you came to do in this birth?*

## The Guru Imposes Nothing, Only Reminds

This is the most beautiful point of the discourse. Your first action does not need to be imposed on you by anyone. By reading the scriptures, you should remember it yourself.

Guruji or Acharya Ji only reminds you what *you yourself* had said your first action would be.

## Four Steps to Becoming the Doer

That resolve, which we made to ourselves, had a clear order:

1. **Search for the Guru:** finding your worthy Guru in this birth.
2. **Cutting old karma:** cutting the wrong karmas of past births and doing **prayaschitta** (atonement) for the sins committed.
3. **Merit and tangible work:** only then doing new, good work. **Sakar karya**, work that the world can see, visible and concrete.
4. **Moksha:** removing all attachment through meditation and merging. Acharya Ji gives the meaning of **moksha** as the dissolution of attachment (moha).

## Are We Living Asleep?

Acharya Ji says this is the duty of every one, and this is the **true doer**.

Whatever else we do, leaving this aside, we are still the doer in it, but we are doing it in sleep.

> "You are not risen up, you are not woken up. Till now you are in a sleeping state."

*Whatever you are doing today, are you doing it awake, or asleep?*

## Key Takeaways

- The very first action is to kindle the fire of knowledge within and to find a realized Guru.
- In the womb every soul makes this promise to itself, as described in the Garuda Purana.
- This memory remains for twelve days after birth and then fades in Vaishnavi Maya. That is why naming is done after the twelfth day.
- The Guru imposes nothing, only reminds you of your own resolve.
- The order of becoming the doer: search for the Guru, atonement, merit and tangible work, and moksha through meditation.
- Every action done without this is an action done in sleep.
