# Do You Remember Your First Duty?

Life keeps us busy with studies, work, family and dreams. But have you ever asked yourself what your **first action** was meant to be, before any of these?

In this short but profound discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji brings back something each of us once told ourselves, and then forgot.

## What Comes Before Everything Else?

Acharya Ji says that the very first action is to kindle the **fire of knowledge** within yourself.

If I want to do anything with my life, my first thought should be to have a Guru in it. Not just any Guru, but a **realized Guru**, someone who has known the truth directly.

Our deepest wish, Acharya Ji explains, should be for such a Guru to come into our lives and lead us from nara to Narayana.

**Nara** (a human being) to **Narayana** (the Divine) describes the journey from an ordinary person to divinity.

## A Promise Made in the Womb

This is not a new idea. Acharya Ji explains that each of us made this very promise to ourselves while still in the **womb**.

According to the **Garuda Purana** (an ancient scripture on death, the afterlife and rebirth), the soul is unhappy in the mother's womb. It remembers things like the Vaitarani, the dreaded river that souls must cross after death. It understands where it is heading, and it knows it has sinned.

So it makes a resolve: once I am born, I will find a Guru again, attain the **Vishnu tattva** (the divine principle), and set right the mistakes of my past life.

## Twelve Days of Memory, Then Forgetting

Here something remarkable comes to light. For twelve days after birth, the child still remembers this resolve.

After the twelfth day, **Vaishnavi maya** (the veil of divine illusion) closes over it, and the child forgets once more what its first duty was. This is why tradition holds the **naamkaran** (the naming ceremony) only after the twelfth day.

Acharya Ji explains that the child still remembers its previous life, but has no way to express it.

When a newborn cries every now and then, we assume it wants milk. But Acharya Ji says the baby is really crying out: *Don't leave me again. I haven't found what I came for yet. In twelve days I am going to forget.*

Acharya Ji asks whether you have read this in the Garuda Purana and the **Katha Upanishad** (the Upanishad in which the young Nachiketa questions Yama, the lord of death). It is also taught in detail at the Pitru Mukti **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching), which is devoted to the liberation of one's ancestors.

*Have you ever stopped to ask what you came into this life to do?*

## The Guru Does Not Impose, Only Reminds

This is the most beautiful point of the discourse. No one needs to impose your first action on you. Reading the scriptures should bring it back to you on its own.

The Guru, or Acharya Ji, only reminds you of what *you yourself* promised your first action would be.

## Four Steps to Becoming a True Doer

The resolve we made to ourselves followed a clear sequence:

1. **Finding the Guru:** Seek out a worthy Guru in this life.
2. **Cutting Away Old Karma:** Undo the wrong deeds of past lives and make **prayaschitta** (atonement) for the sins you committed.
3. **Merit and Tangible Work:** Only then take up new, good work, the kind of **sakar karya** (tangible work) the world can see, concrete and real.
4. **Liberation:** Through meditation, let go of every attachment and dissolve. Acharya Ji explains **moksha** (liberation) as the wearing away of **moha** (attachment).

## Are We Living in Our Sleep?

Acharya Ji says this is everyone's duty, and this is what it means to be a **true doer**.

Whatever else we do, leaving this aside, we are still doers, but we act while asleep.

We have not yet risen, Acharya Ji says. We have not woken up. We are still in a state of sleep.

*Are you doing today's work awake, or in your sleep?*

## Key Takeaways

- The very first action is to kindle the fire of knowledge within and find a realized Guru.
- Every soul makes this promise to itself in the womb, as the Garuda Purana describes.
- The memory lasts for twelve days after birth, then fades under Vaishnavi maya, which is why the naming ceremony comes after the twelfth day.
- The Guru imposes nothing and only reminds you of your own resolve.
- The path to becoming a true doer runs through finding the Guru, atonement, merit and tangible work, and liberation through meditation.
- Every action taken without this is an action taken in sleep.
