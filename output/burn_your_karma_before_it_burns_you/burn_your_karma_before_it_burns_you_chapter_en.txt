# Chapter: Burn Your Karma Before It Burns You

## Accumulated Karma: Enduring It Is Not the Only Way

The first obstacle on the path of sadhana is previously accumulated karma (sanchit karma). Everyone's accumulated karma is different, and it can appear as an obstacle. But who said that accumulated karma must first turn into prarabdha, be endured, and only then be done with? Burn your accumulated karma.

So look into it: look at your birth chart, ask the Guru, attend a shivir, and burn those karmas in advance.

If anger comes today, a heart attack is bound to come tomorrow. If you do not have unconditional love, your blood pressure is bound to rise. The end of rajoguna is sorrow; life is bound to be shortened. So find a remedy for it today, right? We should have at least this much sense: what will anger do to us?

> "If you are going to be angry anyway, then be angry as an act, so that your blood pressure does not rise."

## Dasha and Mahadasha: The Art of Understanding the Planets

Burning accumulated karma is fine, but which dasha is running right now? Dasha and mahadasha are not in my hands, but some remedy can certainly be applied before a dasha arrives.

For this, one must learn how a planet works. The planet does nothing sitting up there in the sky. The representatives of that planet's energy are right here, in my relationships. So shouldn't I learn how to behave with them so that they are pleased?

## Good Times, Bad Times: A New Way of Seeing

The real question is not good time or bad time. The question is what is expected of me in good times, and what is expected of me in bad times.

We had read the Gita of Maharshi Manki; in it there is no such thing as good time or bad time. He dwells in every particle, He dwells in every moment. If the Supreme Lord alone dwells in every moment, how can any moment be bad or good?

> "From the angle of Maya there is bad and good; from the angle of liberation, both are good."

That is why bad times are not called bad times. They are the time for me to take up vairagya (detachment) and knowledge. And when the time is good, it is the time for devotion, for action, for giving and for service. A mahadasha can be one of my obstacles, and it can be resolved with discernment (vivek-buddhi).

## Artha: Not Only Money

The next obstacle is artha. Artha does not mean only money. Property, respect, society, woman, time and health: all of these can become obstacles for me.

Not receiving respect, not having people with you, not having love at home: the remedy for all of these too must be found through discernment.

## Speech: The Dialogue Outside and Within

Then comes your speech, your communication. It is through speech that we connect with the world, and because of it tension comes back again: "Oh, he did not listen to me, I will go and give him a piece of my mind."

So we must see how you communicate, and how you communicate with yourself too, within. There are many kinds of speech:

- **Para vani:** how I listen to the Guru's words.
- **Pashyanti:** how the "I" within me hears that voice.
- **Madhyama:** how I talk in murmurs with everyone around me; how I make small talk, pure gossip.
- **Vaikhari:** how loudly I shout, what I do.

## Listening: Hearing Can Also Become an Obstacle

Then, how do I listen? Listening itself can be my problem. There can be a wrong understanding of the right scripture; this happens even when the Guru's words are heard in a twisted way.

When people, husband and wife, quarrel, they draw only the opposite meaning. The poor woman said something just now in anger, started shouting, "Your family is like this, it is poor so it is like this, you are that, you are this." But how did such a huge meaning come out of it?

So that listening can be my obstacle. Of shravana, manana and darshana (hearing, reflection, seeing): shravana being too little or wrong.

## Ego: It Must Be Broken Like a Coconut

Then ego can be my obstacle. I had said this before, right? Do you remember everything? I am revising quickly.

How does ego go? By understanding that someone is surely greater than me. But while ego will go this way, thinking "someone is greater than me" can also bring jealousy. That must not come either; only the ego is to be lost.

Ego will go only when it is broken forcefully, the way a coconut is broken. By reducing desire a little, by seeing the third eye, that is the Ajna chakra, by listening to the Guru, it then has to be broken forcefully.

> "Listening to the Guru is itself surrender, and surrender is the opposite of ego."

Only then will ego go; this must be done.

## Faith and Trust: The Example of the Doctor and the Medicine

Then the absence of shraddha and trust will be an obstacle. Everything else is there, but the doctor said take the medicine, and there is no trust in the doctor himself. Second, there is no trust in the medicine.

One person trusts the medicine but not the doctor: "He must have given poison, the medicine would be good." Another trusts the doctor but not the medicine: "Oh, the man is good, but he messes things up." This is how it becomes.

So there should be no doubt; then the obstacles will go. Having no doubt does not mean following blindly. Having no doubt means first clearing all your doubts, then starting the work. But do not go into overthinking. If you cannot understand a decision, just ask the Guru, and make that a habit.

Before this, ego was spoken of. When ego is removed, doubt goes through faith, because the scripture says:

> **saṁśayātmā vinaśyati**
> "The doubting soul perishes."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.40)

That is, a person filled with doubt is destroyed.

## Pramada, Alasya and Dirghasutrata

All this is there, I have no doubt, but can there not be pramada, alasya, tamoguna within me? And dirghasutrata too.

- **Pramada:** waste of time. Watching everything here and there, watching TV, watching this, watching that; not understanding one's duty.
- **Alasya:** laziness.
- **Dirghasutrata:** "We will see later", that is, procrastination.

Obstacles come from doing exactly this. Apart from these, you know the other obstacles; the scriptures have certainly described at least these many obstacles.
