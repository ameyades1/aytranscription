**English**
1. **Must you suffer through your accumulated karma?** Acharya Ji asks who said sanchit karma must first become prarabdha and be lived through. With the Guru's guidance, a Shibir and sadhana, it can be burnt before it ripens.
2. **What is the remedy for planets and dashas?** A planet does nothing while sitting in the sky; the people who carry its energy are in your relationships. Treat them in a way that pleases them.
3. **What should you do in hard times?** Hard times are for vairagya and gaining knowledge; good times are for devotion, action, charity and service. Viveka can see you through even a difficult mahadasha.
4. **How does the ego go?** By easing desires a little, seeing the Ajna chakra and listening to the Guru. Listening to the Guru is itself sharanagati, the opposite of ego.
5. **What are pramada, alasya and dirghasutrata?** Forms of tamas: heedless waste of time and blindness to duty, sluggishness, and "let's see later" procrastination.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Four levels of speech (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major).** The blog gives para, pashyanti, madhyama and vaikhari the speaker's practical meanings as if they were the scriptural definitions. Do not target "para pashyanti madhyama vaikhari meaning" as a primary keyword, and do not rename the H2 to "What are the four levels of speech?" until the blog separates the classical sense from Acharya Ji's application, as the review proposes. Recommendation: apply the review's wording, then the long-tail keyword is safe.
2. **Manki Gita claim (review issue 2, Major).** "No good or bad time" and "God in every particle and every moment" are attributed to the Manki Gita but could not be verified there. Do not use the Manki Gita in titles, meta, FAQ or snippets; the FAQ above avoids it. Recommendation: present it as Acharya Ji's teaching drawing on the Manki Gita, as the review suggests.
3. **"महर्षि मंकी" (review issue 3, Minor).** Spelling should be मंकि/मङ्कि, and the "Maharshi" title is doubtful. Editor's decision.
4. **Gita 4.40 quote (review issues 4 and 5, Minor).** "संशयात्मा विनश्यति" is only part of the verse; mark it "(४.४०, अंश)" / "(4.40, part)", and the English IAST should be "saṃśayātmā". If it is used in an FAQ or quote card, cite it as part of 4.40.
5. **Shravana, manana, darshana (review issue 6, Minor).** The standard triad is shravana, manana, nididhyasana; the English calls the speaker's list "the three steps". No SEO use is made of this; fix per the review.
6. **स्त्री / "a spouse" under artha (review issue 7, Minor).** The Hindi and English differ, and including a spouse under artha is the speaker's extension. Keep it out of meta and snippets; editor to align the two texts.
7. **Health claims.** "If you are angry today, a heart attack is bound to follow tomorrow" and "your blood pressure is bound to rise" are spiritual teaching, not medical fact. Do not quote them in titles, meta, social cards or the direct-answer paragraph as health promises; the snippet above speaks only of rajas leading to suffering. Recommendation: leave the body as is (it is Acharya Ji's teaching) but never use it as a health hook.
8. **Astrology and fear.** The talk mentions dasha, mahadasha and looking at one's birth chart, but its teaching is that the planets' remedy lies in how we treat people. Avoid fear-based queries and titles ("bad mahadasha remedies", "dangerous dasha") and do not present the post as a paid-remedy page. The recommended titles keep the focus on karma and sadhana.
9. **"Burning" karma.** The idea that accumulated karma can be burnt before it ripens is in line with Gita 4.37 (per the Sanskrit review), but the blog does not cite that verse. Titles and meta present it as Acharya Ji's teaching, not as a scriptural quote; do not add "Gita says" to any title or snippet unless the editor adds the 4.37 citation to the blog.

## 12. SEO checklist

### Keywords
- [x] Hindi and English each have a focus keyword
- [x] The focus keyword isn't used by another post (no cannibalization)
- [x] 2–4 secondary keywords per language
- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best

### Category and tags
- [x] One category, from the approved 14
- [x] 3–6 tags per language
- [x] No tag repeats a category name
- [x] Existing tags reused; no near-duplicates
- [x] Each tag can be shared by at least 3 posts
- [x] No broad labels that only restate a category
- [x] Series tags named exactly as the series
- [x] `festivals` only when the post is about the festival itself; Pitru topics in `pitru`

### Structured data (in this report, not on the page)
- [x] One JSON-LD block per language: BlogPosting, Person, Organization, VideoObject (if there is a video), BreadcrumbList
- [x] Hindi and English versions linked as translations

### Content trust
- [x] Titles, metas and snippets faithful to the talk
- [x] No unsupported scriptural claims
- [x] Health promises, fear-selling and attacks on other traditions flagged
