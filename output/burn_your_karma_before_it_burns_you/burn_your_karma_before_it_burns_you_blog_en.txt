# Burn Your Karma Before It Burns You

Everyone who walks the path of **sadhana** (spiritual practice) gets stuck at some point. Sometimes old karma stands in the way, sometimes the timing is wrong, and sometimes the trouble is our own mind. In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji quickly runs through the obstacles that hold us back and offers a remedy for each one.

## Must You Suffer Through Your Accumulated Karma?

**Sanchit karma** (accumulated karma) is the stored-up account of past lives and past deeds. It is different for everyone, and it can become an obstacle.

But Acharya Ji asks: who said this karma must first turn into **prarabdha** (the karma that has already begun bearing fruit in this life) and be lived through before you can be free of it? Burn it instead. Look at your birth chart, ask the Guru, attend a **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching), and burn it before it ever ripens.

If you are angry today, a heart attack is bound to follow tomorrow. If your love comes with conditions, your blood pressure is bound to rise. **Rajas** (the quality of restless passion and craving) always ends in suffering. That is why the remedy has to begin today.

Acharya Ji says that if you must get angry, let it be acted anger, the kind that does not raise your blood pressure.

*What is your anger taking from you today that you will have to pay for tomorrow?*

## The Planets Live in Your Relationships, Not in the Sky

Burning old karma is good, but which **dasha** (a planetary period in Vedic astrology) are you in right now? Neither the dasha nor the **mahadasha** (a major planetary period) is in your hands, but you can take remedial steps before it arrives.

To do that, you need to understand how a planet works. It does nothing while sitting up there. The people who carry its energy are right here, in your relationships. Learn to treat them in a way that pleases them.

## Hard Times Are Not Really Bad

The real question is not whether a time is good or bad. It is what life expects of me when times are good, and what it expects of me when they are hard.

In Maharshi Manki's Gita, there is no such thing as a good time or a bad time. God dwells in every particle and in every moment. How, then, can any moment be bad?

Acharya Ji explains that from the point of view of **maya** (the illusion of the world), times are good or bad. From the point of view of liberation, both are good.

A hard time is the time for **vairagya** (detachment) and for gaining knowledge. A good time is the time for devotion, action, charity and service. A mahadasha can be an obstacle, but **viveka** (discernment) can see you through it.

*Do you treat your hard times as a punishment or as a chance to learn?*

## Artha Means More Than Money

**Artha** (wealth and worldly means) covers far more than money. Property, respect, community, a spouse, time and health can all become obstacles.

Not being respected, having no one stand by you, finding no love at home: you have to meet each of these with discernment too.

## How You Speak, to Others and to Yourself

Speech is how we connect with the world, and it is also where much of our stress comes from: "He didn't listen to me, so I'll go and give him a piece of my mind."

Yet we speak not only to others but also to ourselves. The scriptures describe four levels of speech:

- **Para:** how I hear the words of the Guru.
- **Pashyanti:** how the "I" within me hears that voice.
- **Madhyama:** what I say in a low voice to the people around me, the petty talk and gossip.
- **Vaikhari:** how much I shout out loud.

## Do You Really Listen?

Listening can be a problem as well. You can misread a true scripture, and you can hear even the Guru's words in a twisted way.

Look at a quarrel between husband and wife. In a moment of anger, the poor woman says, "Your whole family is like this, and you're no different." Her husband reads a huge meaning into it. Of the three steps of **shravana, manana, darshana** (listening, reflection and direct realisation), weak or faulty listening is an obstacle too.

## Crack Your Ego Like a Coconut

Ego fades when you realise that someone is greater than you. But be careful, because that same thought can breed envy. You must not invite envy in; the only thing to lose is the ego.

The ego goes only when you crack it hard, the way you break a coconut: by easing your desires a little, by seeing the third eye, the **Ajna chakra** (the energy centre between the eyebrows), and by listening to the Guru. Listening to the Guru is itself **sharanagati** (surrender), and surrender is the opposite of ego.

*Is your humility genuine, or is there a comparison hiding behind it?*

## Trusting the Doctor but Not the Medicine?

A lack of faith and trust is another obstacle. The doctor gives you medicine, yet you don't trust the doctor. Or you trust the medicine but not the doctor: "He has probably given me poison." Or you trust the doctor but not the medicine: "He's a good man, but he makes mistakes."

Being free of doubt does not mean following blindly. It means clearing all your doubts first and then starting the work. Don't slide into overthinking, though. When you can't see what to decide, ask the Guru, and make that a habit.

When the ego leaves, faith clears away doubt, because:

> **saṁśayātmā vinaśyati**
> "The one consumed by doubt is destroyed."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.40)

## Heedlessness, Laziness and "Let's See Later"

Everything may be fine and your doubts may be gone, yet **tamas** (the quality of inertia and dullness) can still live within you:

- **Pramada** (heedlessness): wasting time here and there, watching TV and one thing after another, and failing to see your duty.
- **Alasya** (laziness): sluggishness.
- **Dirghasutrata** (procrastination): "Let's see later," putting things off again and again.

Keep living this way and obstacles will come. The scriptures describe the rest of them as well.

*Which task are you leaving for "later" today?*

## Key Takeaways

- Don't wait to suffer through accumulated karma; burn it early through the Guru's guidance and sadhana.
- The energy of the planets lives in your relationships; keeping those relationships happy is the remedy.
- Hard times are for detachment and knowledge; good times are for devotion, charity and service.
- Artha includes property, respect, community, a spouse, time and health; meet any lack of them with discernment.
- Watch all four levels of your speech, para, pashyanti, madhyama and vaikhari, and learn to listen well.
- Surrender breaks the ego; don't let envy take its place.
- Clear your doubts first, then move forward without overthinking.
- Guard against heedlessness, laziness and procrastination.
