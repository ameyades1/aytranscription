# The Truth About Brahmacharya That No One Tells You

Does marriage make you spiritually "lesser"? Does wearing saffron robes make you celibate? In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji challenges these beliefs head-on and, with the scriptures as his evidence, explains what **brahmacharya** (moving in Brahman, the Absolute; true self-restraint) really means.

## Is It Wrong to Marry?

Many people, renunciates and householders alike, hear that someone is getting married and immediately start muttering "Shiva Shiva, Rama Rama", as though marriage were a sin. That reaction rests on a misunderstanding.

Look at the facts. Our **Saptarishis** (the seven great sages of Vedic tradition) were married. Every one of our avatars was married, with a rare exception such as Hanuman. Acharya Ji estimates that eighty to ninety percent of saints and sages have been married, and ten or twenty percent, perhaps fewer, have stayed single. Neither choice, he says, makes a person worthy or unworthy.

## Every Renunciate Was Someone's Son

Acharya Ji puts a simple question to us. Where did the people you regard as celibate come from? Did they fall from the sky? The act of their parents that brought them into the world is itself **kama purushartha** (the pursuit of desire, one of life's rightful goals).

Shri Krishna himself names four **purusharthas** (the four aims of human life: dharma, artha, kama and moksha, that is, righteousness, wealth, desire and liberation), and kama is one of them. Never call it "wrong".

## The Real Test of Brahmacharya

You do not need to peer into someone's bedroom to know whether they practise brahmacharya. You recognise it by its **phalashruti** (the fruits that spiritual practice bears).

*brahmaṇi carati iti brahmacaryam* (one who moves in Brahman lives in brahmacharya).

Suppose someone has never married but cannot control their anger. That anger reveals that brahmacharya is missing, because desire and anger travel together. When a person cannot forgive and has no heart, lust is still at work in their mind.

Acharya Ji reminds us that tradition describes eight forms of **maithuna** (sexual indulgence), from thinking and talking about it to the act itself, so this is far more than a question of marriage. True brahmacharya shows itself in:
- **medha shakti** (a sharp intellect and strong power of retention)
- the **tej** (radiance) of the face
- a youthfulness that shines in the face

*Do I measure my self-control only by outward rules, or also by my anger, my capacity to forgive and the state of my mind?*

## The State of the Gopis

Consider how the scriptures describe the **gopis** (the cowherd women of Vrindavan devoted to Shri Krishna). Even fully unclothed before Shukadeva and Shri Krishna, they feel no shame. Why?

Once you reach the state of **atma-tattva** (your true nature, the essence of the Self), neither man nor woman remains, and fear disappears as well. That is the state of brahmacharya.

Can you reach it merely by staying unmarried, or by putting on saffron?

## Either Scripture-Sanctioned Desire or Complete Celibacy

Acharya Ji issues an open challenge. Gurus who brand others as "wrong", whether people in films or anyone else, should first read the scriptures. Desire within the bounds the scriptures set is acceptable, he explains, but frivolous indulgence is not.

The same holds for husband and wife. There are only two paths:
- **Scripture-sanctioned desire**: as the Gita and the sixth chapter of the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad teach it
- **Complete brahmacharya**: like Hanuman, like Swami Vivekananda

Nothing in between counts. In Acharya Ji's view, the real trouble lies with the person stuck halfway.

## What the Scriptures Say

The Mahakalika Vidya teaches that two people become equal to Lord Shiva as **Smarahara** (the destroyer of Kamadeva, the god of desire). One spends the whole day remembering and worshipping Shiva. The other spends the whole night in complete union yet keeps the name of Durga, of God or of the Guru on their lips.

For proof, Acharya Ji asks you to read the Mahakali Stava, the Kali Vidya and the Bhairava Tantra. In the Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna himself declares:

> **dharmāviruddho bhūteṣu |**
> **kāmo 'smi bharatarṣabha ||**
> "O best of the Bharatas, in all beings I am the desire that does not go against dharma."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.11)

> **prajanaś cāsmi kandarpaḥ ||**
> "Among those who create, I am Kandarpa, the god of love who brings forth new life."
> (Bhagavad Gita 10.28)

For this reason, both the householder who lives by the scriptures and the one who is fully celibate are called **brahmacharis** (those who live in brahmacharya).

## Women Can Tell

One of the great tests of brahmacharya is how safe women feel in a person's presence, whether that person is a yogi or a film actor. The reverse is just as true: when a man looks at a woman, he should not see her as a mere dancing girl or as a woman of easy virtue.

God has given women a deeper **antarjnana** (intuition). In the Gita, Shri Krishna says:

> **kīrtiḥ śrīr vāk ca nārīṇāṁ |**
> **smṛtir medhā dhṛtiḥ kṣamā ||**
> "Among women, I am fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness."
> (Bhagavad Gita 10.34)

Acharya Ji does add that in matters of romance, when women step down from the intellectual level to the emotional one, they can misjudge. The rest of the time, their perception runs deep.

## A Twenty-Minute Bus Ride

Acharya Ji often uses this example. Imagine every window seat on a bus taken by a young man. Even for a twenty-minute ride, a young woman will go and sit beside the one she feels safest with. If someone follows her, she recognises his ill intent at once.

Young men rarely have this sense; only a yogi can read someone's intentions that well. The young woman may not be able to tell whether a man's restraint comes from scripture-sanctioned desire or from the **sanyam** (abstinence) of brahmacharya. The scriptures, however, treat both as equal, and Acharya Ji says we have no right to set them apart.

## Saffron Robes or the Householder's Life?

Even the rishis and munis had wives and children. When a householder Guru truly lives by **yama** (the ethical disciplines of self-restraint), the one in saffron must bow before him too.

Renunciates today teach the Gita of Shri Krishna. Everyone reads the story of Shri Rama and longs for **Ram Rajya** (the ideal reign of Shri Rama). Will they now say that Rama was lesser because he was a householder who wept and cried out "Sita, Sita"?

Acharya Ji is blunt: those who cannot grasp this should speak less. They are still students.

## Shankaracharya and Mandana Mishra

Adi Shankaracharya once held a **shastrartha** (a formal scriptural debate) with the scholar Mandana Mishra. When Mandana Mishra lost, his wife questioned Shankaracharya directly on the subject of desire.

Shankaracharya realised he could not answer without experience. Through **parakaya pravesha vidya** (the yogic art of entering another body), he entered the body of a king who had died and gained that experience. The story itself shows that this subject calls for understanding, not neglect.

## The Fruit of Brahmacharya: Courage

Those with a clean heart and **sattva guna** (the quality of purity and clarity) grasp this simple truth. The Puranas, the Upanishads and the Gita all say the same.

True brahmacharya awakens **Bhairava shakti** (the fierce, protective power of Lord Shiva's Bhairava form) in a person. They become courageous, so much so that even the mightiest enemy bows down of his own accord.

*Do I carry the radiance, the courage and the protective presence for others that true self-restraint brings?*

## Key Takeaways

- Marriage does not make you spiritually unworthy; most sages, avatars and saints were married.
- Kama is one of the four purusharthas; scripture-sanctioned desire is not wrong.
- You judge brahmacharya by its phalashruti, not by the bedroom: intellect, radiance, youthfulness, mastery over anger and forgiveness.
- There are only two paths: scripture-sanctioned desire or complete brahmacharya; the trouble lies with the one stuck in between.
- How safe women feel around a person is a strong sign of brahmacharya.
- True brahmacharya, lived through genuine yama, brings Bhairava shakti and courage.
