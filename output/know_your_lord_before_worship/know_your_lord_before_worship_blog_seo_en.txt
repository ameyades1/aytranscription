# Know Before You Worship: Do You Really Know Your God?

Most of us have worshipped since we were children. We fold our hands, bow, and sing the **aarti** (a devotional song offered with a lamp), and we inherited every bit of it.

But have we ever stopped to ask who the One we worship really is? In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji puts this plain and profound question before us.

Who is a jnani bhakta? A devotee who does not merely believe in God but knows Him thoroughly. Acharya Ji explains that accepting a deity without understanding is like falling for a photograph, and in the Bhagavad Gita (7.17) Shri Krishna says such a devotee is exceedingly dear to Him.

## Who Is Ganesha? Who Is the Devi?

Acharya Ji asks: if you want to worship God, who is He? Who is Lord Ganesha, who is Lord Shiva, who is Lord Vishnu? Who are Lakshmi, Saraswati, Maa Durga and all the other gods and goddesses?

Have you ever tried to get to know them? And if you never even tried, haven't we just **believed** in them without knowing them?

## "Say Bappa, Bappa"

When we were small, someone pointed and said, "Call him **Bappa**" (an affectionate name for Lord Ganesha). So we agreed that yes, this is Bappa. The elders bowed, and we bowed along with them.

We had no idea why. We never learned how Bappa came to have this form, and we never wondered why he appears as an elephant rather than, say, a horse.

Acharya Ji asks whether that is good enough.

*How much do you really know about the deity you bow to every day?*

## Why Did We End Child Marriage?

Acharya Ji offers a simple example. Child marriage was once common. Families fixed the match in childhood, and the children found out who their wife or husband was only after they grew up.

Why did the custom end? Because people saw that it was wrong. **A relationship formed in ignorance is not right.** You should enter a bond only once you understand it, knowing whom you are joining.

## One Photograph, and "I'm in Love"

Now imagine someone simply hands you a photograph. Perhaps you spot a girl's picture in a newspaper, or find one lying on the ground. And at once you declare, "Wow, I've fallen in love with her. From today she is my girlfriend!"

Does love really work that way? You have no idea who she is, where she lives, what her name is or who her parents are. Someone told you a name, and you decided, "She's the one."

"It's love, I'm in love." But in love with whom? You don't even know this person. You liked how she looked, and you called it love.

## Believing Versus Knowing

Acharya Ji says this matters just as much in **spirituality** as it does in worldly relationships.

If we do not know our God at all, he says, belief alone will not take us far.

"I have accepted Ganapati as my God." That kind of belief is no different from the photograph. It is like a man saying, "I have accepted this girl as my wife." But has she accepted you as her husband?

**A relationship needs acceptance from both sides.** Our bond with God cannot rest on a one-sided belief either.

*Does your devotion resemble falling for a photograph, or does it rest on truly knowing Him?*

## Who Is a Jnani Bhakta? Dearest to God

Shri Krishna says this himself in the Bhagavad Gita, and Lord Ganesha says it himself in the Ganesha Gita. Throughout his own Purana and in other scriptures too, Ganesha declares that the **jnani bhakta** (the devotee endowed with knowledge) is supremely dear to him.

> **priyo hi jñānino 'tyartham**
> **ahaṁ sa ca mama priyaḥ**
> "I am exceedingly dear to the one who knows Me, and that one is exceedingly dear to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.17)

Such a devotee is someone who knows God thoroughly, inside and out.

Acharya Ji reminds us that we were asked to know Him, not merely to believe in Him.

## Key Takeaways

- Before you worship, learn who it is you are worshipping.
- Accepting a deity since childhood without understanding is not true devotion.
- A relationship formed in ignorance, like child marriage, is not right, and a bond with God formed without knowing Him is incomplete.
- Faith without knowledge is as hollow as "love" for a face in a photograph.
- A relationship needs acceptance from both sides, including your relationship with God.
- The Gita and the Ganesha Gita agree that the jnani bhakta is supremely dear to God, so seek to know, not merely to believe.
