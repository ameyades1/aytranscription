# Chapter: Know Your Lord Before You Worship

## Have You Ever Tried to Know Your God?

If you want to worship God, first ask yourself who He is. Who is Ganesha? Who is the Devi? Who is Shiva? Who is Vishnu? Who is Lakshmi? Who is Saraswati? Durga and all the other gods and goddesses: have you ever tried to know them? And if you never even tried to know them, haven't you simply believed in them?

From childhood we were taught to believe. Someone said, "Call him Bappa, Bappa." So we too said, yes, he is Bappa. The elders bowed to Bappa, so we bowed too. Why we did it, we do not know. How this form of Bappa came about, we do not know. Why we should call him Bappa, we do not know either. Why he has the form of an elephant, and not of a horse or some other form, is a question we never asked.

> "So will this do?"

## A Lesson from the Custom of Child Marriage

Take the example of marriage. In earlier times, child marriages used to take place. The match was settled in childhood, and only after growing up did a person understand who their wife or husband was. So why was this custom stopped? Because it did not seem right.

**Relationships joined in a state of ignorance are not right.** A bond that is to be formed should be formed after understanding, with knowledge.

## Love from a Single Photograph

Now imagine that someone simply handed you a photograph. Or you suddenly came across a photo of some girl in a newspaper, or lying somewhere. Can you immediately say, "Wow, I have fallen in love with her! From today she is my girlfriend, I am her boyfriend, her name is so-and-so"? Does this ever happen?

You have only seen a photograph, you have seen nothing real, and you want to marry her on that alone. Who she is, what she is, where she is, you do not know. What her name is, you do not know. Someone told you her name is so-and-so, and you accepted it: "Yes, she is the one." If you sit there saying "she is the one, she is the one, she is the one," will that work?

"I am in love, I have fallen in love." But with whom? You do not even know that person. Where she lives, you do not know. Who her parents are, you do not know. You just liked her and fell in love.

## In Spirituality Too, Knowing Is Just as Essential

As important as this is in worldly life, spirituality gives it the same importance. **If you do not even know your God, mere believing achieves nothing.**

People say, "I have accepted Ganapati, he is my God." This acceptance has become just like that photograph. It is as if someone said, "I have accepted this girl as my wife." But has she accepted you as her husband? Is it making a little sense? **The other side must also accept the relationship.**

## The Wise Devotee Is Dear to God

This is what Shri Krishna himself says in the Bhagavad Gita, and what Ganesha himself says in the Ganesha Gita. Throughout his Purana and in all the other scriptures, Ganesha says just this: the wise devotee, the devotee of knowledge, is extremely dear to him. In the Shrimad Bhagavad Gita, the Lord says:

> **priyo hi jñānino 'tyartham ahaṁ sa ca mama priyaḥ**
> "For I am exceedingly dear to the wise one, and he is dear to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.17)

A wise devotee means the devotee who knows God very well.

> "So we were told to know, not told to merely believe."
