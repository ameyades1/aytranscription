know_your_lord_before_worship: SEO report

## 1. Summary

In this short talk (3.5 minutes) Acharya Ji asks a plain question: if we worship Ganesha, Shiva, Vishnu, Lakshmi, Saraswati or Durga, do we know who they are, or have we only accepted them because elders told us "say Bappa"? He uses two comparisons: child marriage, which ended because a bond formed in ignorance is not right, and "falling in love" with a stranger's photograph. A relationship has to be accepted from both sides. He closes with Bhagavad Gita 7.17 (and says the Ganesha Gita teaches the same): the jnani bhakta, the devotee who knows, is exceedingly dear to God. The main intent is informational and reflective. The site already has three posts on "know vs believe" (`know_your_god_transformative_path` owns "भगवान को जानना या मानना" / "knowing God vs believing in God", and `gita_warning_worship_without_understanding` owns "worship without understanding"), so this post takes the narrower term it answers best: "ज्ञानी भक्त" / "jnani bhakta" (searched as "ज्ञानी भक्त कौन है" / "who is a jnani bhakta"), backed by Gita 7.17. Few pages explain the jnani bhakta in plain language, and this talk ties it to a concrete, relatable point: know Him before you bow.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi:** ज्ञानी भक्त वह है जो भगवान को केवल मानता नहीं, उन्हें तत्त्व से जानता है। भगवद्गीता (७.१७) में श्रीकृष्ण कहते हैं कि ज्ञानी को वे अत्यंत प्रिय हैं और ज्ञानी उन्हें अत्यंत प्रिय है। आचार्य उपेंद्र जी के अनुसार, बचपन से बिना समझे किसी देवता को मान लेना वैसा ही है जैसे केवल एक तस्वीर देखकर प्रेम हो जाना।

**English:** A jnani bhakta is a devotee who does not merely believe in God but knows Him in truth. In the Bhagavad Gita (7.17), Shri Krishna says He is exceedingly dear to the one who knows Him, and that devotee is exceedingly dear to Him. Acharya Upendra Ji compares belief without knowing to falling in love with a photograph.

## 6. FAQ

**Hindi**
1. **ज्ञानी भक्त कौन है?** वह भक्त जो भगवान को भली-भांति जानता है। गीता ७.१७ में श्रीकृष्ण कहते हैं कि ज्ञानी उन्हें अत्यंत प्रिय है और वे ज्ञानी को अत्यंत प्रिय हैं।
2. **क्या भगवान को केवल मानना काफ़ी नहीं है?** आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि अगर हम अपने भगवान को जानते ही नहीं, तो सिर्फ मानने से काम नहीं चलता। हमें जानने को कहा गया था, केवल मानने को नहीं।
3. **आचार्य जी बाल विवाह का उदाहरण क्यों देते हैं?** क्योंकि अज्ञात अवस्था में जोड़े गए रिश्ते उचित नहीं होते। भगवान से संबंध भी समझकर, जानकर जोड़ना है।
4. **"रिश्ता दोनों तरफ से" का क्या अर्थ है?** आचार्य जी की उपमा में, किसी की तस्वीर को "पत्नी मान लेना" एकतरफा है। भगवान के साथ संबंध भी केवल एकतरफा मान्यता नहीं हो सकता।

**English**
1. **Who is a jnani bhakta?** A devotee who knows God thoroughly. In Bhagavad Gita 7.17, Shri Krishna says such a devotee is exceedingly dear to Him, and He is exceedingly dear to that devotee.
2. **Isn't believing in God enough?** Acharya Ji says that if we do not know our God at all, belief alone will not take us far. We were asked to know Him, not merely to believe in Him.
3. **Why does Acharya Ji mention child marriage?** Because a relationship formed in ignorance is not right. A bond with God, too, should be formed with understanding.
4. **What does "acceptance from both sides" mean?** In Acharya Ji's analogy, declaring a stranger in a photograph your wife is one-sided. Our bond with God cannot rest on one-sided belief either.

(The FAQ cites only the Bhagavad Gita for the jnani bhakta; the Ganesha Gita parallel is left to the body text. See section 10, flag 3.)

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Gita 7.17 is quoted as a half-verse without saying so (Minor, from the Sanskrit review).** Both versions cite "७.१७" / "7.17" for the second half only. Recommendation: the editor should add "उत्तरार्ध" / "second half" or quote the full verse. The full verse is worth it here: its first half ("तेषां ज्ञानी नित्ययुक्त एकभक्तिर्विशिष्यते") is what supports the H2 "सबसे प्रिय" / "Dearest to God" and this post's primary keyword. The titles, meta, snippet and FAQ say "अत्यंत प्रिय" / "exceedingly (supremely) dear", which the second half supports on its own.
2. **Verse meaning drops "हि" and "तत्त्व से" (Minor, from the review).** The review suggests "क्योंकि मुझे तत्त्व से जानने वाले ज्ञानी को मैं अत्यंत प्रिय हूं…" / "For I am exceedingly dear to the wise one who knows Me in truth…". The direct-answer snippet already uses "तत्त्व से जानता है" / "knows Him in truth". Recommendation: the editor applies the review's wording in the body.
3. **Ganesha Gita parallel not verified (from the review).** The blog says Ganesha says the same in the Ganesha Gita and "throughout his own Purana", but no verse is cited and the review could not confirm one. Recommendation: titles, meta, FAQ and schema cite only Bhagavad Gita 7.17 (as here). No "Ganesha Gita" tag is used (no other post shares it). Do not quote a Ganesha Gita verse number until one is checked (Ganesha Purana, Krida Khanda).
4. **IAST "ahaṁ" vs "ahaṃ" (Minor, from the review).** Keep one form across the site.
5. **"Relationship from both sides" applied to God.** This is Acharya Ji's analogy, not a scriptural statement about God "accepting" a devotee. Recommendation: present it as his comparison (FAQ 4 does); do not turn it into a title or meta claim such as "Does God accept you?".

Recommendation: keep both out of titles, meta and social cards, where they could read as clickbait or be misread out of context; the child-marriage heading suggestion in section 4 is optional for the same reason.
