# Recognition Before Worship, Do You Know Your God

Most of us have been worshipping since childhood. Folding our hands, bowing, singing the aarti: all of this came to us as inheritance.

But have we ever paused to ask who the One we worship really is? In this short discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji places exactly this simple yet deep question before us.

## Who Is Ganesha? Who Is the Devi?

Acharya Ji asks: if you want to worship God, who is He? Who is Ganesha, who is Shiva, who is Vishnu? Who are Lakshmi, Saraswati, Durga, and all the other gods and goddesses?

Have you ever tried to know them? And if you never even tried, haven't we simply **believed** in them?

## "Say Bappa, Bappa"

In childhood someone said, "Call him Bappa." So we said, yes, he is Bappa. The elders bowed, so we bowed too.

Why we did it, we do not know. How this form of Bappa came about, we do not know. Why he has the form of an elephant and not of a horse, we never asked.

Acharya Ji asks whether this will really do.

*How much do you truly know about the deity before whom you bow your head every day?*

## Why Did Child Marriage End?

Acharya Ji gives a simple example. Earlier, child marriages took place. The match was settled in childhood, and only on growing up did one learn who the wife or husband was.

Why was this custom stopped? Because it did not seem right. **Relationships joined in a state of ignorance are not right.** A bond that is to be formed should be formed after understanding, with knowledge.

## One Photograph, and "I Am in Love"

Now imagine someone just handed you a photo. Or you found a picture of some girl in a newspaper, or lying somewhere. And you immediately say, "Wow, I have fallen in love with her, from today she is my girlfriend!"

Does this happen? Who she is, where she lives, what her name is, who her parents are, you know nothing. Someone told you a name and you accepted it: "She is the one."

"I am in love, I have fallen in love." But with whom? You do not even know that person. You just liked her, and you fell in love.

## Believing Versus Knowing

Acharya Ji says that as important as this is in worldly relationships, it is just as important in **adhyatma** (spirituality).

He says that if we do not even know our God, mere believing does not work.

"I have accepted Ganapati, he is my God." This acceptance is just like that photo. It is as if someone said, "I have accepted this girl as my wife." But has she accepted you as her husband?

**A relationship must be accepted from both sides.** Our relationship with God, too, cannot be a one-sided assumption.

*Is your devotion like love for a photograph, or does it rest on true recognition?*

## The Wise Devotee: Dearest to God

Shri Krishna himself says this in the Bhagavad Gita, and Ganesha himself in the Ganesha Gita. Throughout his Purana and the other scriptures, Ganesha says that the **jnani bhakta** (the wise devotee) is extremely dear to him.

> **priyo hi jñānino 'tyartham ahaṁ sa ca mama priyaḥ**
> "For I am exceedingly dear to the wise one, and he is dear to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 7.17)

A wise devotee is the devotee who knows God very well (one who knows me very well).

Acharya Ji explains that we were told to know, not merely to believe.

## Key Takeaways

- Before worship, it is essential to know who it is that we worship.
- Merely believing since childhood, without understanding, is not true devotion.
- Just as relationships joined in ignorance (child marriage) are not right, connecting with God without knowing Him is incomplete.
- Like "love" from a mere photograph, faith without knowledge is hollow.
- A relationship must be accepted from both sides, with God too.
- Both the Gita and the Ganesha Gita say the wise devotee is extremely dear to God. We are meant to know, not merely believe.
