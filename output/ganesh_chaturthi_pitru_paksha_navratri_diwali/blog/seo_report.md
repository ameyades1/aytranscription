The talk (Ganesh Series, episode 10) explains why India's autumn festivals follow a fixed order: Ganapati awakens discernment (viveka), Pitru Paksha frees the ancestors and repays pitru rina, Navratri brings mastery of the three gunas, Naraka Chaturdashi removes the last inner darkness, Lakshmi Puja and Padwa follow, and Raksha Bandhan, which comes first, is read as protection through brahmacharya. Its thesis is that India is a moksha pradesh and every festival is a pause for the journey from Nara to Narayana, not a vacation. The main intent is informational ("why are Hindu festivals in this order", "spiritual meaning of Diwali / Naraka Chaturdashi"), with strong seasonal peaks from August (Raksha Bandhan, Ganesh Chaturthi) to November (Diwali). The opportunity is a single post that connects the whole festival season: most ranking pages explain one festival at a time, so a "sequence" angle is distinctive, and the page can serve as a hub linking the separate Ganesh, Pitru Paksha, Navratri and Lakshmi Puja posts. ## 6. FAQ

For later use; answers come only from the talk.

1. **गणेश चतुर्थी के बाद ही पितृपक्ष क्यों आता है? / Why does Pitru Paksha come after Ganesh Chaturthi?**
   गणपति विवेक बुद्धि का जागरण हैं। जो स्वयं वासना में अटका है, वह तमस में अटके पितरों को मुक्त नहीं कर सकता; जिसके गणपति के दस दिन भीतर पूरे हुए हैं, वही पितरों को मुक्त करने का अधिकारी है।
   Ganapati stands for awakened discernment. Someone still caught in desire cannot free ancestors trapped in tamas; only those who have truly completed the ten days of Ganapati within are fit to set their ancestors free.
2. **नरक चतुर्दशी का आध्यात्मिक अर्थ क्या है? / What is the spiritual meaning of Naraka Chaturdashi?**
   साधक बनने के बाद भी भीतर नकारात्मकता एक बार फिर लौटती है; आचार्य जी इसे नरकासुर का प्रतीक मानते हैं। उसके जाने के बाद लक्ष्मी पूजन होता है और साधक ॐ तक पहुँच सकता है।
   Even after one becomes a seeker, negativity returns once more; Acharya Ji sees it symbolised in Narakasura. Once it departs, Lakshmi Puja follows and the seeker can reach Om.
3. **दिवाली के पटाखों का असली अर्थ क्या है? / What do Diwali firecrackers really mean?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार पटाखा भीतर के आनंद का प्रतीक है; जैसे मन में लड्डू फूटते हैं, वैसे ही पटाखे भीतर फूटने थे, बाहर नहीं।
   For Acharya Ji, a firecracker symbolises inner bliss: like joy bursting in the heart, the crackers were meant to burst within, not outside.
4. **रक्षाबंधन का आध्यात्मिक अर्थ क्या है? / What is the spiritual meaning of Raksha Bandhan?**
   साधक को सभी स्त्रियों में माता और बहन दिखें, और वह स्वयं ब्रह्मचर्य का पालन करके अपनी बहन की रक्षा करे। यह सांसारिक नहीं, आध्यात्मिक रक्षा है।
   The seeker learns to see a mother and a sister in every woman and protects his sister by practising brahmacharya himself. It is spiritual protection, not worldly protection.
5. **भारत में इतने त्योहार क्यों हैं? / Why does India have so many festivals?**
   भारत मोक्ष प्रदेश है। हर त्योहार अवकाश नहीं, नर से नारायण की यात्रा के लिए लिया गया विराम और एक जाँच है।
   India is a land of liberation. Each festival is not a vacation but a pause, and a test, for the journey from Nara to Narayana.

## 10. Authenticity flags

From the Sanskrit review (`tools/review/ganesh_chaturthi_pitru_paksha_navratri_diwali.md`, verdict "Needs correction"):

1. **Rahu called Narakasura (Major).** The Naraka Chaturdashi section reads as if scripture names Rahu "Narakasura"; they are different beings (Bhagavata 8.9 and 10.59). Recommendation: apply the review's wording, which presents the link as Acharya Ji's symbolic reading. Until then, do not use "Rahu" or "Narakasura" claims in titles, meta or FAQ (FAQ 2 already says "Acharya Ji sees it symbolised in Narakasura").
2. **Apta leaves placed on Padwa (Major).** The exchange of apta leaves as "gold" is a Vijayadashami (Dasara) custom. Recommendation: apply the review's correction; do not target "Padwa apta leaves" queries until fixed.
3. **"Scriptures describe crackers that burst within" (Major).** No scripture is named; this is Acharya Ji's metaphor. Recommendation: attribute it to Acharya Ji as the review suggests. The secondary keyword "spiritual meaning of Diwali firecrackers" and FAQ 3 are already worded as his teaching, not as a scriptural claim.
4. **Shri Vidya gloss too broad (Minor, English).** Use the review's gloss (Lalita Tripurasundari, Shri Chakra).
5. **Durga Saptashati gloss (Minor, English, optional).** "700 mantras of the Devi Mahatmya, Markandeya Purana".
6. **"तुरीया" (Minor, Hindi).** Should be "तुरीय" as a noun.
7. **"सण" (Minor, Hindi).** Marathi word; use "त्योहार".
8. **"गुरुदेव" vs "आचार्य जी" (Minor, Hindi).** Use "आचार्य जी" throughout, matching the English and the schema's author entity (consistent entity naming also helps AI search).
9. **"Shri Krishna's childhood" (Minor, English).** The Hindi says "कृष्ण लीला"; use "leelas".

SEO-specific flags:

10. **Wealth promise.** The text says that after Pitru Paksha "power, wealth and wisdom come to you together". Do not lift this into the title, meta description or snippets as a promise; it is a stage of sadhana in the talk, not a result of observing a festival.
11. **Raksha Bandhan passage.** The fear wording ("someone will deceive her, exploit her") should not be used in snippets, social cards or meta; it would read as fear-selling out of context. The FAQ answer keeps only the positive teaching.
12. **Spiritual age remarks.** "Spiritual age zero to six" is a wake-up call in the talk; do not use it as a shaming hook in titles or social posts.

The keyword "त्योहारों का आध्यात्मिक क्रम" / "spiritual order of Hindu festivals" is the talk's own thesis, so no wording was bent to fit a query.
