# The Hidden Spiritual Sequence of Festivals from Ganapati to Diwali

According to Acharya Ji, the spiritual order of Hindu festivals, from Ganesh Chaturthi to Diwali, maps the seeker's inner journey. Ganapati awakens discernment, Pitru Paksha frees the ancestors, Navratri brings mastery of the three gunas, Naraka Chaturdashi removes the last negativity, and Padwa makes the seeker worthy of sharing the golden aura. Each festival is a pause from Nara towards Narayana, not a vacation.

## Are Holidays Only for Travel and Fun?

India celebrates more festivals than any other land, and its people enjoy more holidays in a year than almost anyone else. We assume these days exist for fun and leisure.

Acharya Ji says plainly that they are not a vacation. The real question is this: who is a true holiday actually for?

Think about it. Why does Ganapati come first? Why does **Pitru Paksha** (the fortnight for honouring one's ancestors), also called Mahalaya, follow it? And why do Navratri and then Diwali come next, with Naraka Chaturdashi, Lakshmi Puja and Padwa falling in exactly that order?

*Have you ever asked why your festivals arrive in the order they do?*

## Why Does Ganapati Come First? Awakening Discernment

Ganapati stands for **viveka** (discernment), the inner understanding that tells right from wrong. The journey ahead begins only once this faculty awakens.

If we are still caught in desire and have not reached **turiya** (the fourth state of consciousness, the state of the pure witness), how can we free ancestors who were themselves trapped in **tamas** (the quality of darkness and inertia)? Only those who have truly completed the ten days of Ganapati within themselves are fit to set their ancestors free.

## Why Does Navratri Follow Pitru Paksha?

Once you are free of **pitru rina** (the debt owed to one's ancestors), you can take full control of the three **gunas** (the qualities of nature: purity, activity and inertia) and become their master. Power, wealth and wisdom then come to you together.

Through the practice of the **Durga Saptashati** (seven hundred verses in praise of Maa Durga), such a seeker grows into a great practitioner of **Shri Vidya** (the sacred tradition of worshipping the Divine Mother).

## Naraka Chaturdashi: The Last Darkness

Even after you become a seeker, negativity rises within you one more time. In the **samudra manthan** (the churning of the cosmic ocean by gods and demons), Rahu slips in at the very end to steal the nectar of immortality. Here that same final intruder is called Narakasura, the demon whose defeat Naraka Chaturdashi celebrates.

This darkness too departs. Then comes Lakshmi Puja, then Mahalakshmi Puja, and after that the seeker can reach **Om**, the primordial sound.

## The Firecrackers Were Meant to Burst Within

A firecracker symbolises the inner state of bliss.

Acharya Ji puts it this way: just as we say joy bursts in the heart like sweets, the firecrackers too were meant to burst in the mind, not outside.

The scriptures never speak of chemical firecrackers set off outdoors. They describe crackers that burst within. We have set off plenty of them outside, yet nothing has happened inside.

*This Diwali, what lit up within you?*

## Padwa: Becoming Worthy of Sharing the Golden Aura

After this, the seeker sees the whole world as one within, and everything begins to look auspicious. Then comes Padwa, when people give one another "gold" in the form of leaves from the **apta** tree.

This gold is nothing other than the **golden aura**, and the seeker now becomes worthy of sharing it. The full secret of Vijayadashami, Diwali and the **sade teen muhurat** (the three and a half most auspicious days of the year) will be explained in detail at the **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching).

## Raksha Bandhan: Brahmacharya Is the Real Protection

Raksha Bandhan means that the seeker begins to see a mother and a sister in every woman, not only in his own sister.

He realises that if he does not practise **brahmacharya** (mastery of the senses), the same danger will one day reach his own sister. Someone will deceive her, exploit her, and ruin her character and her life.

Acharya Ji explains that protecting your sister does not mean picking up a gun. You protect her by practising brahmacharya yourself.

This is spiritual protection, not worldly protection. That is why Raksha Bandhan comes before Ganapati: you must first see this sacred form in every woman. Bhaubeej, the brother-sister festival of Diwali, carries the same spirit. The sages did not set this sequence down at random.

## A Land of Enjoyment Versus a Land of Liberation

Abroad, people barely get five or six days off, and they think we Indians do not work. But theirs is a **bhoga pradesh** (a land devoted to enjoyment), while our India is a **moksha pradesh** (a land devoted to liberation).

Every one of our festivals is a pause taken for the journey from **Nara to Narayana** (from the human being to the Divine). Each holiday is a test: have I moved from Nara to Narayana? Then comes a little worldly work, and then the next festival, the next season for that same journey.

Acharya Ji says this is no vacation. It is an important pause from worldly illusion, given so that you can travel from Nara to Narayana.

He urges us not to imitate the West blindly. If you wish, become a **Vishwaguru** (a teacher to the whole world) and show them too what a real holiday is for.

## How Old Are You Spiritually?

The sages were not foolish to leave us so many festivals. If we have not grasped their purpose, our spiritual age is still somewhere between zero and six. If watching a television serial about Shri Krishna's childhood only makes you laugh, play and cry, consider yourself fifteen to seventeen years old.

Acharya Ji says this to wake us up. He also assures us that those who learn here will leave with a complete understanding of Vedanta.

*When the next festival arrives, will you treat it as a holiday or as a step from Nara towards Narayana?*

## Key Takeaways

- Ganapati awakens discernment, the doorway to the journey ahead.
- In Pitru Paksha, only those who have risen above desire and tamas can free their ancestors.
- Navratri marks mastery of the three gunas and the practice of Shri Vidya.
- Naraka Chaturdashi destroys the last inner negativity; firecrackers symbolise inner bliss.
- The gold of Padwa is the golden aura, which the seeker becomes worthy of sharing.
- Raksha Bandhan at its core is spiritual protection through brahmacharya.
- India is a land of liberation; every festival is a pause for the journey from Nara to Narayana, not a vacation.
