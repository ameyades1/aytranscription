# Chapter: The Order of the Festivals, the Journey from Nar to Narayan

## Why Does Ganpati Come First?

India is a land where the most festivals are celebrated and the most holidays are given in a year. But this is not a vacation for sightseeing. So the question arises: whom is the real holiday actually for?

Have you ever wondered why Ganpati comes first? Why does Pitru Paksha, that is Mahalaya, come only after it? Why does Navratri come only after that? And why does Diwali come after that? Within it, as Narak Chaturdashi arrives, why is there then Lakshmi Puja? Why do festivals like Padwa come in between? Did anyone ask this?

## Ganpati: The Awakening of Discerning Wisdom

Ganpati means discerning wisdom (*vivek buddhi*). Only when your discerning wisdom awakens, only after that state arrives, does the way ahead open.

Now consider: if you yourself are stuck in desire and have not reached the turiya state, how will you free the ancestors who were themselves stuck in tamas? The one whose ten days of Ganpati have truly been completed, meaning that state has really arrived within them, only that person is eligible to release their ancestors.

## Pitru Paksha and Navratri: Freedom from Ancestral Debt and Mastery over the Three Gunas

The one whose ancestors have been freed, that is, who has been released from ancestral debt (*pitru rin*), can now gain full control over the three gunas and become their master. Such a person will attain power, wealth and wisdom together. Doing the sadhana of the Durga Saptashati, they will then become a great Shri Vidya sadhak.

## Narak Chaturdashi: The Destruction of the Last Negativity

Even after becoming such a sadhak, negativity returns within once more. You know that in the churning of the ocean, Rahu comes at the very end to steal the nectar. This is what is called Narakasura. That too will go. Then comes Lakshmi Puja, Mahalakshmi Puja, and then you can reach Om.

## The Firecrackers Were Meant to Burst Within, Not Outside

Then firecrackers are burst. A firecracker is a symbol of the state of inner joy. The outer firecrackers were never spoken of.

> "We say laddoos are bursting in the mind, don't we? In the same way, the firecrackers were to be burst in the mind, not outside."

Nowhere do the scriptures say to set off chemical firecrackers outside. The scriptures describe firecrackers bursting within. We set them off outside, but did nothing within.

## Padwa: The Right to Share the Golden Aura

After that, the whole world is seen as one within oneself. Then everything begins to appear auspicious. Then comes Padwa. You know that at that time people give one another gold; we call it the apta leaf. This is nothing but the golden aura.

Now you have become worthy of sharing it. By then you should have reached the state of the golden aura. Vijayadashami will come in Navratri, and later Diwali too; all this concerns the three and a half auspicious muhurtas, which will be explained in detail at the camp (*shibir*). But the essence is that the sadhak now becomes entitled to share the golden aura.

## Raksha Bandhan: Brahmacharya Is the True Protection

Raksha Bandhan means that the sadhak now begins to see a sister and a mother in all women, not only in their own sister. When they protect, they understand that if I do not practise brahmacharya, then one day this same danger will come upon my sister too. Someone will trap her, rob her, ruin her, spoil her character, and her life too will fall.

> "Protecting your sister does not mean taking up a gun. Only by practising brahmacharya yourself can you protect your sister."

In the same way the mother and the wife are protected; without it, that protection does not happen at all. This is not worldly protection being spoken of, but spiritual protection. Its meaning is very deep.

Bhaubeej in Diwali is a festival of the same spirit. Raksha Bandhan has already come before Ganpati. So you should have asked: why did Raksha Bandhan come first and Ganpati afterwards? Because first comes the practice of brahmacharya: first I must see this form in all women. The rishis and munis gave this order; they did not give it just to pass the time.

## The Land of Enjoyment and the Land of Liberation

India has the most festivals and holidays in the year. We think holidays are for fun. Abroad there are hardly five or six days of holiday, and they think that you do not work and they do. Theirs is a birth of enjoyment (*bhog yoni*), theirs is a land of enjoyment (*bhog pradesh*). Our Bharat is a land of liberation (*moksha pradesh*).

So none of our festivals is meant just for taking a holiday. It is for taking a break for the journey from nar to Narayan, from the human to the Divine. Let them work; this is their time of decline. Someday they will come to Bharat and learn, and then they will understand.

Here, in every holiday, the thing to see is: did you reach from nar to Narayan or not? You did. Come, now let us do a little worldly work again. That work done, another *san* has come, another festival has come, the time for nar to Narayan has come. Festivals used to be for this reality check.

## This Is Not a Vacation but a Break from Maya

Please do not blindly imitate the West; do not follow that nonsense. If you wish, slowly become the world's teacher (*vishwaguru*) and teach them too what the real holiday is for. It is not a holiday for fun, as in "Come, the Ganpati holidays are here, let us go travelling."

> "This is not a vacation. It is an important break from worldly maya, given for going from nar towards Narayan."

The rishis and munis were not mad to keep so many *san*, so many festivals. If you do not understand this, your spiritual age is still only zero to six years; you have made no spiritual progress at all. I am not hurting your ego, am I? If watching a Krishna Leela serial you laugh, play and cry, and that is all, then understand that you are fifteen to seventeen years old.

I am awakening you, making you aware. But when you leave here having learnt, you will leave having understood complete Vedanta; this I can guarantee. Today this hard truth must be set in the mind.
