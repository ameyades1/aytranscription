# Chapter: Om in the Final Breath and the Secret of Liberation

## What Should We Do at the Time of Death?

What we must do at the time of death is clearly explained in the eighth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita. The Lord says:

> **yad akṣaraṁ veda-vido vadanti viśanti yad yatayo vīta-rāgāḥ**
> **yad icchanto brahmacaryaṁ caranti tat te padaṁ saṅgraheṇa pravakṣye**
> "That which the knowers of the Vedas call imperishable, which ascetics free from attachment enter, and desiring which they practise celibacy, that supreme state I shall declare to you in brief."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.11)

The supreme state of Sat-Chit-Ananda that the scholars who know the Vedas call imperishable, that is, the state of Omkar; which renunciate sages, free from attachment and ever striving, enter; and desiring which the brahmacharis observe celibacy: that supreme state I shall describe to you in brief.

In the Gita the Lord has stated it briefly, but I am teaching it to you in detail.

## Closing the Gates of the Senses

Now look at the twelfth and thirteenth verses that follow. They tell us what to do at the time of death:

> **sarva-dvārāṇi saṁyamya mano hṛdi nirudhya ca**
> **mūrdhny ādhāyātmanaḥ prāṇam āsthito yoga-dhāraṇām**
> "Restraining all the gates of the body, confining the mind in the heart, placing the life breath in the head, established in yogic concentration."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.12)

Restraining the gates of all the senses means stopping the work of all your senses. If there is eating and drinking, then do not eat now; give up all that desire. There is the nose, so nothing to smell now. There are the ears, so nothing to hear now. There are the eyes, so nothing to see now. Having entered that state, the mind is to be fixed in the heart.

Fixing the mind in the heart means doing sadhana selflessly. Sadhana is not to be done for some reason, "I want this, I want a benefit." Fixing the mind in the heart means being established in **unconditional love for the Lord**.

## Establishing Prana at the Third Eye

Then, through that conquered mind, the prana is to be placed in the head. In the head means taking it to the third eye. This is union with the Guru principle. Remembering the Guru, gathering the prana, the entire life force energy, at the third eye, one is to be established in yogic concentration upon the Supreme.

## Om: Brahman in the Form of One Syllable

> **om ity ekākṣaraṁ brahma vyāharan mām anusmaran**
> **yaḥ prayāti tyajan dehaṁ sa yāti paramāṁ gatim**
> "Uttering Om, the one-syllabled Brahman, and remembering Me, one who departs leaving the body attains the supreme goal."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.13)

The person who, uttering "Om", this Brahman in the form of one syllable... Here the one who does it is called "Purusha". Purusha means that you yourself are the Supreme Lord.

Om is called "Brahman in the form of one syllable", you can see it, can't you? This is called **Akshara Brahman**. Has everyone understood? In the same way, which is the second Brahman mentioned? The one of sound, **Shabda Brahman**. And then the third Brahman.

## Without Understanding the Meaning, Chanting Brings No Benefit

Uttering Om, this Brahman in the form of one syllable, **and contemplating Me, the Nirguna Brahman, who am its meaning**. Who will look at the meaning? It is said that the meaning must be understood.

This Nirguna Brahman is the origin. The Brahman in the form of a syllable is only its signifier, its name. To reach there, first understand its meaning.

Now the question is: what are we told to contemplate? The Brahman of the syllable, or the Nirguna Brahman? It is the Nirguna Brahman that is to be contemplated. But if I do not know the Nirguna at all, do not understand it at all, then merely uttering the syllable Om brings me no benefit. Is this much clear? I am showing you this by reading from the Bhagavad Gita.

The person who leaves the body contemplating that Nirguna Brahman attains the supreme goal. So who will be able to do this? Only the one who has stayed devoted to the pursuit of Akshara Brahman.

## "Antakāle Ca": The Great Meaning of a Small Word

Now let us go to the fifth verse of the eighth chapter:

> **anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram**
> **yaḥ prayāti sa mad-bhāvaṁ yāti nāsty atra saṁśayaḥ**
> "And whoever, at the time of death too, leaves the body remembering Me alone, attains My being; of this there is no doubt."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.5)

Is "ca" written there or not? Read the "ca". **Antakāle ca** means "and at the time of the end too". People always forget this word "ca". People think that I only have to say Om at the end. But saying Om only at the end does not bring liberation.

Adi Shankaracharya has given a very deep exposition of this word "ca". His analysis, his commentary on this single word "ca" perhaps runs to several pages. **Antakāle ca mām eva** means: liberation is declared for the one who remembers Me through the whole of life, and in the final moment too.

"The person who at the time of the end **too**..." What is the meaning of this "too"? Understand the importance of this word "too". "Even at the last moment" means he must have been doing the same at earlier times as well. If someone kept remembering his girlfriend all his life and said Om at the end, he will not attain liberation. Is this much clear?

The person who leaves the body remembering Me alone at the time of the end too attains My very form; of this there is no doubt.

## The Problem: We Do Not Know When the End Will Come

But do you know what the problem is? I do not know when my end is. I told you only yesterday: for birth we get a notice nine months in advance, but at death we do not get even nine seconds of notice.

So I cannot calculate the last moment and say, "Now let me say Om." That is why I must do my practice **treating every moment as the last moment**.

## As the Feeling, So the Destination

Look at the next verse:

> **yaṁ yaṁ vāpi smaran bhāvaṁ tyajaty ante kalevaram**
> **taṁ tam evaiti kaunteya sadā tad-bhāva-bhāvitaḥ**
> "Whatever state one remembers when leaving the body at the end, that alone one attains, O son of Kunti, being always absorbed in that state."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.6)

O Arjuna, son of Kunti, whatever feeling a person remembers while leaving the body at the time of the end, that alone he attains, because he has always been absorbed in that feeling.

You do not know when the last moment will come, right? That much is certain. So whether Omkar comes to you in the last moment will depend on whether you have practised Om all your life. Otherwise, at the last moment you will have the same desire, you will think the same thing, toward which your destination is set.

Oh, someone wants to eat gulab jamun, gulab jamun, didn't get a samosa, and kept up this feeling even after reaching the hospital's intensive care unit; then in the next birth he may be born as a beggar outside a sweet shop. Or he may come as a glutton in some rich house where there is plenty of food.

Oh, that lust! If even at the last moment one saw a dog engaged in that act, then in the next birth one may get a birth just like that, or something else; you may even become an animal, remember. And if at the end one remembered "Guru, Guru", then one will be born near the Guru once again.

## At the End We Remember What Was the Essence of Life

Do you know what is remembered at the last moment? The very thing that was the essence of the whole life, because one has always been absorbed in that feeling.

Do you understand? If the whole life was "son, son, son", "money, money, money", worry upon worry, then at the last moment you will not remember Om at all. You may have done Guru, satsang, everything, yet whatever was the **predominant thought**, that thought will come on that day before death.

## Remember Me at All Times, and Fight Too

Look at the next verse. So what advice does the Lord give?

> **tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca**
> **mayy arpita-mano-buddhir mām evaiṣyasy asaṁśayam**
> "Therefore at all times remember Me and fight. With mind and intellect offered to Me, you will surely come to Me alone."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.7)

Therefore, O Arjuna, remember Me constantly at all times, and fight too. What does "at all times" mean? **Sarveṣu kāleṣu**, at every moment. And fight too; that is fine.

That is, do the work that has to be done. If you have to fight against white hair, then even while applying dye, do it saying Om, and understanding what Om is: "Within me, all this is temporary." Every battle is temporary. If I have an illness, my battle is with my body.

In this way, with mind and intellect offered to Me, you will without doubt attain Me alone.

## Practice Does Not Happen in the Final Moment

The Lord says further:

> **abhyāsa-yoga-yuktena cetasā nānya-gāminā**
> **paramaṁ puruṣaṁ divyaṁ yāti pārthānucintayan**
> "With a mind disciplined by the yoga of practice, not wandering elsewhere, constantly contemplating, one reaches the supreme divine Person, O Partha."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.8)

O Partha, this is the law: a person who, joined in the yoga of the practice of meditation on the Supreme, with a mind that does not wander elsewhere, constantly contemplates, attains the supremely radiant divine Person, that is, the Supreme Lord.

Can practice happen at the last moment? Practice never happens at the last moment.

## As the Mind at the End, So the Destination

Have you understood? A little, or a lot? Has some new awakening happened, or have you understood nothing new?

**"Ant mati so gati"**, as the mind at the end, so the destination: this is correct. But which state of mind will be there at the end, that is what I am telling you. The direction you have built over your whole life will become your state of mind at the end, and the onward journey will follow accordingly.

So this is not about saying Om at the last moment. First understand Om in life, and chant it with understanding, in accordance with Akshara Brahman and Shabda Brahman; only then does it bring benefit. You will have to take initiation from someone who has truly heard and understood that Om, and gradually understand its meaning from them as well.
