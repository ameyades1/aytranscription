## 1. Summary

A close reading of Bhagavad Gita chapter 8 (8.5-8.8, 8.11-8.13) on what to do at the moment of death. Acharya Ji lays out the method of 8.12-13 as three steps (close the gates of the senses, steady the mind in the heart, raise the prana to the head while remembering), explains Om as Akshara Brahman whose meaning is Nirguna Brahman, and then turns the popular belief around: the little word "च" in "अन्तकाले च" ("also at the end") shows that remembrance must run through the whole life, because death gives no warning and the last thought is the one that ruled the life (8.6, "अंत मति सो गति"). The main intent is informational and practical ("मृत्यु के समय ॐ", "what to do at the time of death", "Gita 8.13 meaning"), with a strong emotional layer: people caring for a dying parent, or afraid of their own death, search for what to say or chant at the end. The opportunity is that most pages answer with a list of mantras to recite in the last moments; this post answers honestly that the end cannot be prepared for at the end, and grounds every step in a cited verse. It sits in the `moksha` cluster next to `biggest_spiritual_misconception_about_death` and `anyone_grant_liberation_living_or_dead`; its own angle (the moment of death and Om, Gita chapter 8) does not overlap their focus keywords.

## 5. Direct answer paragraph

**Hindi (52 words):** क्या मृत्यु के समय ॐ बोलने से मोक्ष मिल जाता है? आचार्य उपेंद्र जी गीता (८.५) के "अन्तकाले च" के "च" पर ध्यान दिलाते हैं: स्मरण अंतकाल में "भी" होना चाहिए, अर्थात पूरे जीवन चलता रहे। मृत्यु की कोई सूचना नहीं मिलती और अंत में वही याद आता है जो जीवन का प्रधान विचार था। इसलिए हर क्षण को अंतिम क्षण समझकर साधना करनी है।

**English (57 words):** Does chanting Om at the moment of death bring liberation? Acharya Upendra Ji points to the small word "ca" in Bhagavad Gita 8.5, "also at the hour of death": remembrance must already fill the whole life. Death gives no warning, and at the end the mind returns to the thought that ruled your life. So practise as though every moment were your last.

## 6. FAQ

1. **क्या मरते समय भगवान का नाम लेने या ॐ बोलने से मोक्ष मिल जाता है? / Does saying God's name or Om at death bring moksha?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार केवल अंत में ॐ बोलने से मोक्ष नहीं होता। गीता ८.५ का "च" बताता है कि स्मरण पहले से चल रहा होना चाहिए। / According to Acharya Ji, saying Om only at the end does not bring liberation. The "ca" in Gita 8.5 shows that the remembrance must already have been going on.
2. **गीता के अनुसार मृत्यु के समय क्या करना चाहिए? / What does the Gita say to do at the time of death?**
   गीता ८.१२-१३: इंद्रियों के द्वार रोककर, मन को हृदय में स्थिर करके, प्राण को मस्तक में स्थापित करके, ॐ का उच्चारण और भगवान का स्मरण करते हुए शरीर छोड़ना। / Gita 8.12-13: restrain the gates of the senses, hold the mind in the heart, place the prana in the head, and leave the body uttering Om and remembering the Lord.
3. **"अंत मति सो गति" का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "anta mati so gati" mean?**
   जैसी मति अंत में होगी, वैसी गति होगी (गीता ८.६)। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि अंत में मति कैसी होगी, यह पूरा जीवन तय करता है। / As the mind at the end, so the journey beyond (compare Gita 8.6). Acharya Ji adds that the whole life decides what the final state of mind will be.
4. **ॐ के उच्चारण के साथ किसका चिंतन करना है? / What should one contemplate while uttering Om?**
   ॐ अक्षर ब्रह्म है, उसका नाम है; गीता प्रेस का अनुवाद "मेरा स्मरण" को ॐ के अर्थस्वरूप निर्गुण ब्रह्म के रूप में समझाता है। / Om is Akshara Brahman, its name; the Gita Press translation explains "remembering Me" as Nirguna Brahman, the meaning of Om.
5. **क्या स्मरण के लिए कर्म छोड़ना पड़ेगा? / Must one give up action to remember God?**
   नहीं। गीता ८.७: "हर समय मेरा स्मरण कर और युद्ध भी कर।" / No. Gita 8.7: "Remember Me at all times, and fight."

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"Purusha" is not a word of Gita 8.13 (review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The English glosses it as "the Supreme Person", which makes the departing seeker the Paramapurusha in the verse itself. Apply the review's wording, and don't use "you are the Supreme Lord" as a snippet or social quote unless it is attributed to Acharya Ji's Advaita reading.
2. **Unverified claim about Adi Shankaracharya (review issue 2, Major, not yet applied).** The blog says Shankara analyses the "च" in depth; his Bhashya on 8.5 does not appear to. Soften it as the review suggests. Don't use "Shankaracharya reveals" or similar in titles, meta, FAQ or video descriptions; FAQ 1 and the direct answer attribute the "च" reading to Acharya Ji.
3. **Nirguna Brahman presented as the verse's words (review issue 3, Minor).** The verse says "remembering Me"; Nirguna Brahman is the Gita Press gloss. FAQ 4 already uses the bridging wording; apply it to the blog too.
4. **Third eye and Guru presented as what the Gita prescribes (review issues 4 and 5, Minor).** मूर्ध्नि means the head; the third eye, remembrance of the Guru and "selfless love" are Acharya Ji's readings. FAQ 2 gives the verse's method plainly. Don't write "Gita says focus on the third eye at death" in snippets or YouTube descriptions.
5. **No promise of moksha by a technique.** The search intent ("chant this at death for moksha") pulls toward a guarantee; the talk says the opposite. Titles are phrased as questions, and nothing should promise liberation from chanting Om, at the end or otherwise.
6. **Rebirth examples (animal birth from lust, beggar outside a sweet shop).** These are Acharya Ji's applications of 8.6, attributed to him in the blog. Keep them out of titles, meta, tags and thumbnails; they invite fear-based clicks and would distress readers caring for a dying relative.
7. **Sensitive audience.** Readers searching "मृत्यु के समय क्या करना चाहिए" are often sitting with a dying parent. The page does not judge anyone's final moments; keep any added text (FAQ, social) free of blame such as "if they did not say Om they will not be freed".
8. **Small text fixes (review issues 6-9, Minor, not yet applied).** Missing vocatives in the Hindi of 8.6 and 8.8, the English gloss inside the Hindi translation of 8.8, "संग्रहेण", and IAST "ṃ". None affects the SEO texts.
