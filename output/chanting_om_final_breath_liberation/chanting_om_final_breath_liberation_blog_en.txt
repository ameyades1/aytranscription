# Om in the Final Breath: Is Liberation Possible at the Moment of Death?

Many people believe that whatever you do in life, just take the Lord's name at the last moment and liberation will follow. But is that really so?

In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji reads from the eighth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita to show what is actually to be done at the time of death, and why that preparation takes a lifetime, not a final moment.

## What Should We Do at the Time of Death?

In the Gita, the Lord speaks of the supreme state that the knowers of the Vedas call imperishable:

> **yad akṣaraṁ veda-vido vadanti viśanti yad yatayo vīta-rāgāḥ**
> **yad icchanto brahmacaryaṁ caranti tat te padaṁ saṅgraheṇa pravakṣye**
> "That which the knowers of the Vedas call imperishable, which ascetics free from attachment enter, and desiring which they practise celibacy, that supreme state I shall declare to you in brief."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.11)

This supreme state is the **state of Omkar**. Renunciates free from attachment enter it, and to attain it brahmacharis observe celibacy.

The Gita states this in brief. Acharya Ji teaches it in detail.

## First Step: Closing the Gates of the Senses

> **sarva-dvārāṇi saṁyamya mano hṛdi nirudhya ca**
> **mūrdhny ādhāyātmanaḥ prāṇam āsthito yoga-dhāraṇām**
> "Restraining all the gates of the body, confining the mind in the heart, placing the life breath in the head, established in yogic concentration."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.12)

**Sarva-dvārāṇi saṁyamya** means stopping the work of all the senses. If there is a desire to eat, do not eat now. The nose is to smell nothing now, the ears to hear nothing, the eyes to see nothing.

## Second Step: Fixing the Mind in the Heart

Fixing the mind in the heart means **selflessness**. Sadhana is not to be done for the reason "I want this, I want a benefit."

It is the state of **unconditional love** for the Lord.

*Is your sadhana tied to a wish for some benefit, or is it only love?*

## Third Step: Taking Prana to the Third Eye

Then, through that conquered mind, **prana** (life force energy) is to be placed in the head, that is, at the third eye.

This is union with the **Guru principle**. Remembering the Guru, gathering the entire life force at the third eye, and being established in yogic concentration upon the Supreme.

## Om: Akshara Brahman, and the Meaning Behind It

> **om ity ekākṣaraṁ brahma vyāharan mām anusmaran**
> **yaḥ prayāti tyajan dehaṁ sa yāti paramāṁ gatim**
> "Uttering Om, the one-syllabled Brahman, and remembering Me, one who departs leaving the body attains the supreme goal."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.13)

Here the one who does this is called "Purusha". Acharya Ji says Purusha means that you yourself are the Supreme Lord.

Om is called **Akshara Brahman**, Brahman in the form of one syllable. In the same way, as sound it is **Shabda Brahman**.

But the verse does not speak only of uttering it. It says: while uttering it, contemplate the **Nirguna Brahman, which is its meaning**.

Nirguna Brahman is the origin. The Brahman in the form of a syllable is only its signifier, its name.

> "But I do not know the Nirguna at all, do not understand it at all, so there is no benefit in my saying this syllable Om."

## The Small "Ca" That Everyone Forgets

> **anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram**
> **yaḥ prayāti sa mad-bhāvaṁ yāti nāsty atra saṁśayaḥ**
> "And whoever, at the time of death too, leaves the body remembering Me alone, attains My being; of this there is no doubt."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.5)

This verse contains a small word: **"ca"**, meaning "and". **Antakāle ca** means "at the time of the end **too**".

People think that Om is to be said only at the end. But saying Om only at the end does not bring liberation. Adi Shankaracharya gave a deep analysis of this single "ca" in his commentary.

"Too" means the same was going on in earlier times as well. Acharya Ji says plainly:

> "One who remembers his girlfriend all along and says Om at the last will not get liberation."

## The Problem: No Notice Comes Before the Final Moment

Birth gives notice nine months in advance. But death does not give notice even nine seconds before.

So it is not possible to calculate the last moment and say Om then. There is only one way: **practise treating every moment as the last moment.**

## As the Feeling, So the Destination

> **yaṁ yaṁ vāpi smaran bhāvaṁ tyajaty ante kalevaram**
> **taṁ tam evaiti kaunteya sadā tad-bhāva-bhāvitaḥ**
> "Whatever state one remembers when leaving the body at the end, that alone one attains, O son of Kunti, being always absorbed in that state."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.6)

Whatever feeling a person remembers at the end, that is what he attains, because he has always lived in that feeling.

Acharya Ji's examples are direct. One who kept thinking of gulab jamun and samosas even in the intensive care unit may be born in the next life as a beggar outside a sweet shop, or come as a glutton in some rich house.

One whose mind held lust until the end may even be born as an animal. And one who said "Guru, Guru" at the end will be born near the Guru once again.

## At the End We Remember What Was the Essence of Life

At the last moment one remembers what was the essence of the whole life. If all of life was "son, son", "money, money" and worry, Om will not come to mind at the end at all.

Even if one did Guru, satsang and everything else, whatever was the **predominant thought** is what will come before death.

*If today were your last day, which thought would come to your mind first?*

## Remembrance at All Times, and the Fight Too

> **tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca**
> **mayy arpita-mano-buddhir mām evaiṣyasy asaṁśayam**
> "Therefore at all times remember Me and fight. With mind and intellect offered to Me, you will surely come to Me alone."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.7)

The Lord does not ask us to give up action. **Sarveṣu kāleṣu**, remember Me at all times, **and fight too**.

Acharya Ji connects this to everyday life. If your fight is with white hair, say Om even while applying dye, and say it understanding that all this is temporary. If there is illness, it is a temporary battle with the body.

## Practice Does Not Happen in the Final Moment

> **abhyāsa-yoga-yuktena cetasā nānya-gāminā**
> **paramaṁ puruṣaṁ divyaṁ yāti pārthānucintayan**
> "With a mind disciplined by the yoga of practice, not wandering elsewhere, constantly contemplating, one reaches the supreme divine Person, O Partha."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.8)

Only one joined in **abhyasa yoga** (the yoga of practice), contemplating constantly with a mind that does not wander elsewhere, attains the supreme divine Person.

Practice does not happen at the last moment. It is the work of a whole lifetime.

## As the Mind at the End, So the Destination

> "The direction you have given your whole life is what will be your state of mind at the end."

"Ant mati so gati", as the mind at the end, so the destination, is true. But what the mind will be at the end is decided by the whole of life.

So first understand Om in life, and chant it with understanding, in accordance with Akshara Brahman and Shabda Brahman. You will have to take **initiation** from one who has truly heard and understood that Om, and gradually understand its meaning from them.

## Key Takeaways

- The method for the time of death is in the Gita (8.12-13): restrain the senses, fix the mind in the heart with selfless love, and take the prana to the third eye while remembering the Guru.
- Om is Akshara Brahman, but its meaning is Nirguna Brahman. Without understanding the meaning, mere utterance brings no benefit.
- The "ca" in "antakāle ca" shows that remembrance must span the whole life, not only the end.
- Death gives no notice, so practise treating every moment as the last moment.
- At the end one remembers what was life's predominant thought, and the next birth follows from it.
- Action is not to be abandoned: remember at all times while fighting your own battle.
- To understand the true meaning of Om, initiation from a Guru who has truly known Om is necessary.
