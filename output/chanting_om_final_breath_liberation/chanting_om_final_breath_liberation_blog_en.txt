# Om with Your Last Breath: Can You Find Liberation at the Moment of Death?

Many people believe they can live however they like, as long as they say the Lord's name in their final moment, and **liberation** (moksha) will follow. But does it really work that way?

In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji reads from the eighth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita to show what you are actually meant to do at the time of death, and why the preparation for it takes a whole lifetime rather than a final moment.

## What Should You Do as Death Approaches?

In the Gita, the Lord speaks of the supreme state that those who know the Vedas call imperishable:

> **yad akṣaraṁ veda-vido vadanti viśanti yad yatayo vīta-rāgāḥ**
> **yad icchanto brahmacaryaṁ caranti tat te padaṁ saṅgraheṇa pravakṣye**
> "That which the knowers of the Vedas call imperishable, which ascetics free from passion enter, and in longing for which seekers practise celibacy: that supreme state I will describe to you briefly."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.11)

This supreme state is the **state of Omkara** (the sacred syllable Om in its fullness). Renunciates free of attachment enter it, and celibate seekers keep their vow of **brahmacharya** (celibacy and self-restraint) in order to reach it.

The Gita describes it in brief. Acharya Ji teaches it in detail.

## Step One: Close the Gates of the Senses

> **sarva-dvārāṇi saṁyamya mano hṛdi nirudhya ca**
> **mūrdhny ādhāyātmanaḥ prāṇam āsthito yoga-dhāraṇām**
> "Closing all the gates of the senses, holding the mind steady in the heart, and placing the life force in the head, one becomes established in yogic concentration."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.12)

**Sarva-dvārāṇi saṁyamya** means bringing all the senses to rest. If you feel like eating, you do not eat for now. The nose smells nothing, the ears hear nothing, and the eyes see nothing.

## Step Two: Steady the Mind in the Heart

Here, holding the mind in the heart is read as **selflessness**. You do not practise your **sadhana** (spiritual discipline) because "I want this" or "I want to gain something".

It is a state of **unconditional love** for God.

*Is your practice tied to a hope of gain, or does it come from love alone?*

## Step Three: Raise the Prana to the Third Eye

With the mind now mastered, you then place your **prana** (life force energy) in the head, at the third eye.

This is union with the **Guru Tattva** (the principle of the Guru). Remembering the Guru, you gather your entire vital energy at the third eye and settle into yogic concentration on the Divine.

## Om: The Imperishable Brahman and the Meaning Behind It

> **om ity ekākṣaraṁ brahma vyāharan mām anusmaran**
> **yaḥ prayāti tyajan dehaṁ sa yāti paramāṁ gatim**
> "Whoever leaves the body uttering Om, the one syllable that is Brahman, and remembering Me, attains the supreme goal."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.13)

The one who departs in this way is called the **Purusha** (the Supreme Person). As Acharya Ji puts it, Purusha means you yourself: you are the Supreme Lord.

The Gita calls Om **Akshara Brahman**, Brahman in the form of a single syllable. In the same way, as sound it is **Shabda Brahman** (Brahman as sacred sound).

Yet the verse speaks of more than pronouncing a syllable. As you say it, you are to contemplate **Nirguna Brahman** (the formless Absolute, beyond all qualities), which is its true meaning.

Nirguna Brahman is the source. The syllable is only what points to it, its name.

This raises a doubt: if you neither know nor understand the formless Absolute, does saying Om bring you any benefit at all? It does not.

## The Little Word "Ca" That Everyone Overlooks

> **anta-kāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram**
> **yaḥ prayāti sa mad-bhāvaṁ yāti nāsty atra saṁśayaḥ**
> "And whoever, even at the hour of death, leaves the body remembering Me alone attains My nature; of this there is no doubt."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.5)

This verse contains a tiny word: **"ca"**, meaning "and". **Anta-kāle ca** means "**also** at the hour of death".

People assume they need to say Om only at the very end. But saying Om at the end alone does not bring liberation. In his commentary, Adi Shankaracharya analyses this single "ca" in depth.

"Also" means the same remembrance was already going on long before. Acharya Ji says it plainly: someone who spends a lifetime thinking about a girlfriend and then says Om at the end will not be set free.

## The Problem: No One Warns You When the End Is Near

You get nine months' notice before a birth. Before death, you do not get even nine seconds.

You cannot time your Om to the final moment, then. There is only one way: **practise as though every moment were your last.**

## As Your Inner State, So Your Destiny

> **yaṁ yaṁ vāpi smaran bhāvaṁ tyajaty ante kalevaram**
> **taṁ tam evaiti kaunteya sadā tad-bhāva-bhāvitaḥ**
> "O son of Kunti, whatever state of being a person remembers when leaving the body at the end, that very state they attain, for they have always been absorbed in it."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.6)

Acharya Ji's examples are blunt. Someone who lies in intensive care still dreaming of gulab jamun and samosas could be reborn as a beggar outside a sweet shop, or as a glutton in some wealthy household.

A person whose mind clings to lust until the end may even be born as an animal. And one who calls out "Guru, Guru" at the end will be born near the Guru once again.

## At the End, You Remember What Your Life Was About

In the final moment, what comes to mind is the essence of your whole life. If you spent your years thinking "my son, my son" or "money, money", always worrying, Om will not even occur to you at the end.

You may have followed the Guru and attended every **satsang** (a gathering for spiritual teaching), yet whatever thought ruled your life is the one that will rise before death.

*If today were your last day, what would be the first thought in your mind?*

## Remember Me Always, and Fight

> **tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca**
> **mayy arpita-mano-buddhir mām evaiṣyasy asaṁśayam**
> "Therefore remember Me at all times, and fight. With your mind and intellect offered to Me, you will surely come to Me."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.7)

The Lord does not ask you to give up action. **Sarveṣu kāleṣu**: remember Me at all times, **and fight as well**.

Acharya Ji ties this to everyday life. If you are battling grey hair, say Om even while you apply the dye, knowing that all of it is temporary. If you are ill, see the illness as a passing battle with the body.

## Practice Does Not Happen in the Final Moment

> **abhyāsa-yoga-yuktena cetasā nānya-gāminā**
> **paramaṁ puruṣaṁ divyaṁ yāti pārthānucintayan**
> "O Partha, with a mind trained in **abhyasa yoga** (the yoga of practice) that does not stray elsewhere, whoever constantly meditates on the supreme divine Purusha reaches Him."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.8)

You cannot practise in the final moment. Practice is the work of a lifetime.

## As the Mind at the End, So the Journey Beyond

Acharya Ji explains that the direction you have given your whole life will become your state of mind at the end.

The saying "anta mati so gati" (as the mind at the end, so the journey beyond) is true. But your whole life decides what that final state of mind will be.

So first come to understand Om during your life, and chant it with an understanding of it as Akshara Brahman and Shabda Brahman. You will need to take **diksha** (spiritual initiation) from someone who has truly heard and understood Om, and gradually learn its meaning from them.

## Key Takeaways

- The Gita (8.12-13) gives the method for the time of death: restrain the senses, steady the mind in the heart with selfless love, and raise the prana to the third eye while remembering the Guru.
- Om is Akshara Brahman, but its meaning is Nirguna Brahman. Chanting it without understanding that meaning brings no benefit.
- The "ca" in "anta-kāle ca" shows that remembrance must fill your whole life, not just its end.
- Death sends no warning, so practise as though every moment were your last.
- At the end you remember the thought that ruled your life, and your next birth follows from it.
- Do not give up action: keep remembering God at all times while you fight your own battles.
- To understand the true meaning of Om, you need diksha from a Guru who has truly known it.
