# A New Birth Every Moment in the Presence of the Guru

## A New Person in Six Months

Imagine spending eight to twelve hours a day in the Guru's **aura**. Karmas are continuously being exchanged there, and you do not even notice it.

A sadhika says from her own experience that if you stay in the Guru's presence like this for six months, at the end of six months you will be a completely new person. As if you had been reborn. You will not even remember what your earlier life was like.

For this transformation, the Guru gives one means: **seva** (selfless service).

## Why Was a Seva Rota Created?

In the early shivirs, the session began at eight, and the gurukul had to be cleaned before that. In a group of fifteen or twenty, the same four or five people came early; the rest arrived comfortably at eight. The thinking was: at first there is only cleaning, what does that have to do with us?

Guruji saw that the same people were doing the same seva again and again, and also bringing the food. So a **seva timetable** was created. Every task, from cleaning onward, had to be done by every person at least once or twice during the shivir.

Just as the Guru teaches the sadhak, sometimes the sadhaks' behaviour also prompts the Guru to make new rules.

## "Surely We Can Keep a Cook for This"

At the shivir there was only one meal between eight in the morning and eight at night. It was never ordered from outside. Only **sattvic food**, a few chosen items, and best of all, fruit.

The speaker shares very honestly that in her first shivir she was full of **tamoguna** (inertia) and **rajoguna** (restlessness). Getting up in the morning, cooking for twenty people and leaving home by seven may be easy for many, "but I was not one of them." The mind said: so much education, surely we can keep a cook for this.

For the first ten or fifteen days there was enthusiasm. Then "a slightly manipulative intellect" started up: how will we do this for six months?

## When the Whole Group Arrived With "Options"

The plan was to catch Guruji in a good mood and talk to him. The options were ready:

- Keep a sattvic cook right here, and she will earn an income too
- Or everyone brings their own tiffin

The whole group agreed at once. After all, they were being spared cooking at home and bringing food! And everyone said, "You are there to do the talking."

Guruji listened calmly to it all. He is a very good listener and never stops you midway. Remembering that scene today, the speaker says: this is where I went wrong. And she shares it so that we do not repeat the same mistake, because those who learn from the mistakes of others are good people.

## How Big Is the Definition of Family?

After hearing everything, Guruji said:

> "So many years have gone by in 'my own, my own', in 'I and mine'. What did you get from it? Expand the size of your family for once."

At home, the child needs a tiffin at seven, so we get up and cook a new dish every day. Then Guruji asked:

> "Are these fifteen people not family now? Can you not cook for them with love?"

That was the "very big lesson". The small task of seva that the Guru shares among everyone: if someone truly learns from it, their whole life can change. Because behind that task there is a very great hand.

*Where does the boundary of your "family" end? Is there someone you could do something for with love, but have kept putting off by saying "not mine"?*

## Words That Become Sanskara

After that, cooking in the morning began with a **feeling of oneness**, and the whole approach changed. Why is the definition of family so limited? Otherwise what is the use of learning divinity here? Suddenly the dots began to connect.

What the Guru says does not remain merely something heard. It goes into our **sanskaras** (deep impressions), permanently. Next time, if even the thought of that mistake arises, that sanskara rises before you and Guruji's words ring in your mind like a bell.

## From Tamas to Rajas, From Rajas to Sattva

This transformation was a ladder. At that time it was a move from tamoguna to rajoguna, because getting up in the morning to cook needs a little rajoguna too! In the Gita, Lord Krishna describes exactly this sequence:

> **rajas tamaś cābhibhūya sattvaṁ bhavati bhārata**
> "Sattva grows by overpowering rajas and tamas."
> (Bhagavad Gita 14.10)

That is, sattvaguna grows by overpowering rajoguna and tamoguna. First we overpower tamas and come into rajas, then by overpowering rajas, sattva grows.

Every day a new test, the realisation of some new mistake. "Guruji literally washed us." And then this deep insight:

> "When you feel your whole life is wrong, that is the very day your right life begins."

## "Do Not Run After Your Own Remedy"

On one yatra, everyone went to do their own **upay** (remedies). Everyone competed: this should be my remedy. They listened to the recording four times over and went with paper and pen, their remedies written out.

But Guruji's lesson was just one: **do not run after your own remedy.**

There is only one thing to do: follow the **Guru's paduka** (the Guru's sandals). The Guru Gita says this too. Worshipping the paduka means walking the path the Guru walks.

And what is the Guru's path? He never does "I and mine". His own individual priority is always last.

## The Shani Temple, and the Guru's Silent Sacrifice

On the second-to-last evening of the yatra, everyone was at the Shani temple. Almost everyone had received a Shani remedy. When everyone's work was complete, the words came from Guruji's mouth: "Everyone's remedies are all done, complete." Everyone was happy; the yatra was a success.

But in that very moment Guruji took everyone's collective **halahal** (poison, meaning the burden of collective karma) upon himself.

That night the session went on until midnight. The next morning Guruji did not come out. In the evening they learned that he had had a fever. But when he met them in the evening, there was not a trace of fever on his face. Through yoga and tapasya he had digested all that halahal in a single day.

No complaint, no feeling of "Do you deserve this? Why should I take your halahal?" The same happiness, the same smile, the same energy. Nobody even knew.

> "We must not see a human intellect in the Guru."

We will never understand what the Guru feels within, for the disciple, and when the disciple does wrong. It is beyond our imagination. So whatever the Guru says, we just do that much.

*Have you ever weighed some word of your Guru or guide with your human intellect, without knowing what lay behind it?*

## Seven Hundred Kilos, and Still "I Am Not Happy"

On the yatra, charity was distributed, seven hundred kilos. Everyone was very happy. But Guruji was not at all pleased. He reminded them that this land of Shri Vidya, where every child knew Shri Vidya, was the golden bird, and today even after giving this much it feels as if we are not doing even 0.01 percent for society.

> "What will happen to her by taking one bag from you? How will she get next month's ration?"

Seva means not our own satisfaction, but the other person's real need.

## The True Meaning of Paduka Worship

The path the Guru has walked, we simply have to walk it. Then our welfare is certain. Trying to live that way little by little, as we are able, is the true worship of the paduka. Otherwise merely worshipping the sandals achieves nothing.

## Key Takeaways

- In the Guru's presence karmas are continuously exchanged; in six months a person experiences something like a new birth.
- **Seva** is the means of transformation, and even the smallest task of seva has a great purpose behind it.
- The real lesson is to drop "I and mine" and widen the definition of family.
- The Guru's words settle within permanently as **sanskaras**.
- Sadhana moves step by step: from tamas to rajas, from rajas to sattva (Gita 14.10).
- The day you feel your whole life is wrong is the day your right life begins.
- Do not run after your own remedy; follow the Guru's paduka.
- The Guru silently takes the disciples' halahal upon himself; do not see a human intellect in him.
- True paduka worship is walking the Guru's path, not merely worshipping the sandals.
