# Chapter: A New Birth Every Moment in the Presence of the Guru

## Time Spent in the Guru's Aura

We had a shivir called Yog Kram, and I hope all of you can experience a shivir like that, where you can spend eight to twelve hours physically in the Guru's aura. In the Guru's aura, karmas are continuously being exchanged, continuously, and you do not even notice it.

If you stay like this in the Guru's presence for six months, at the end of six months you will be a completely new individual. It will feel as if you have been reborn. You will not even remember what your earlier life was like.

And for this, the Guru has us do one remedy: **seva** (selfless service).

## How the Seva Timetable Came About

Guruji's rules have also evolved over time. Just as the Guru teaches the sadhak, sometimes the sadhaks also teach the Guru.

Earlier, some people used to come before time to the shivir. Suppose the shivir was to begin at eight and the gurukul had to be cleaned before that; then one had to reach at least twenty to twenty-five minutes early. Only if people arrived then could the cleaning happen. At that time a batch had fifteen or twenty students. Of them, the same four, five or six would come early, and the rest would arrive comfortably at eight. They knew that at first there was only cleaning to be done, so what did it have to do with them? Sadhaks are of every kind.

Then Guruji created the timetable system. He saw that the same people were doing the same seva again and again, and the same people were also carrying the responsibility of bringing food. The seva was not being shared equally. Some were doing a great deal of seva, others very little. So he said: divide it equally. Everyone must do every task, from cleaning onward, at least once or twice during the shivir. That is where the timetable came from.

## My First Shivir and the Tamoguna Within

Let me tell you my own experience. When the first shivir started, there were about fifteen people. We had to reach the gurukul by eight. I lived close by, yet I had to leave home at quarter past seven. Some people had to leave at six or even earlier. Having reached at eight, we had to stay there until about eight at night.

Between eight in the morning and eight at night we ate only once. And that food was not ordered from outside even a single time. Bringing only sattvic food was compulsory. Only these items were allowed, nothing more. The best was to bring fruits, just a fruit meal.

Until there was a system of turns, it kept happening that the responsibility fell on only a few people. That was my first shivir, and I was so full of tamoguna, so full of rajas! My mother-in-law and my mother both know that getting up in the morning and working in the kitchen was never my strength. Going into the kitchen in the morning, cooking for twenty people and leaving home by seven may be a very easy task for many women, but I was not one of them. I used to think: so much education, this and that, surely we can keep a cook for this.

I think it has been three and a half years since my journey started and I met Guruji. This is from that time. For the first ten or fifteen days there was enthusiasm, and I got up early and cooked. Then the intellect started working. A slightly manipulative intellect started up: I cannot do this for six months, this is not work I like. Why do this for six months!

## The "Options" Put Before Guruji

Look at how sadhaks begin. I am telling you myself what mistakes I made and what I learned from them.

So it began in my mind: catch Guruji in a good mood and talk to him about this. Why not keep a good sattvic cook right here? Lots of thoughts, lots of options, a full discussion on what could be done. Then the leadership quality also woke up, so everyone was asked. Everyone is always ready for an option like that! Nobody objected; we were going to be spared cooking at home and bringing food, so the whole batch was ready. Everyone said, "Yes, fine, and you are there to talk, you talk to Guruji."

Many options came up. Why not everyone bring their own tiffin? There is no difficulty in bringing your own tiffin: bring your own two rotis, fill in a vegetable, whatever you want. That too was put forward as an option.

Guruji was calm. I still remember that scene. He was quietly listening to all my chatter. Even today, when I remember it, I feel: this is where I went wrong. I am telling you this so that you do not walk the same path and make the mistakes I made. I hope you are better sadhaks and will not make such mistakes. Those who learn from the mistakes of others are good people.

I explained everything: how this was not possible, people come from so far, so either we can each bring our own tiffin, or we can give the work to some good sattvic cook here, and she will earn an income too. Look at the kind of thoughts! She will get work, her household will be helped as well.

## "Are These Fifteen People Not Your Family?"

Guruji heard it all. Guruji listens to everyone; he is a very good listener. It is not that he stops you midway. After listening to all my chatter, he said:

> "So many years have gone by in 'my own, my own', in 'I and mine'. What did you get from it? Expand the size of your family for once."

At home we do it for however big the family is, don't we? The child needs a tiffin at seven, so you get up for that; you cook a new dish every day. Guruji said:

> "Why can't you raise the family size to fifteen? Are these fifteen people not family now? Can you not cook for them with love?"

That was a very big lesson for me, a very, very big lesson.

The small task of seva that the Guru shares out among everyone: if someone truly learns from it, their whole life can be transformed. Because behind the task you have been given there is a very great hand.

## When the Dots Began to Connect

After that, in getting up and cooking every morning, and with that **feeling of oneness**, my whole approach changed. Yes, why is the definition of family so limited? Then what is the use of learning divinity here? Suddenly the dots began to connect. This is very wrong; this approach has to change.

And from that very moment Guruji's words became fixed within. What he said, he placed into our **sanskaras** (deep impressions). It is not that he merely spoke and we heard it in one ear and let it out the other. When he speaks, it becomes our sanskara permanently. Next time, if even the thought of making such a mistake arises, or such a situation comes before you, that sanskara will rise before you, and Guruji's words will ring in your mind like a bell.

## From Tamoguna to Rajoguna, From Rajoguna to Sattvaguna

This was my own transformation out of tamoguna. At least at that time, things reached from tamoguna to rajoguna. To get up in the morning and cook, you need a little rajoguna too, don't you!

In the Gita, Lord Krishna says that first tamas has to be suppressed; by suppressing it we come into rajoguna, and then by suppressing rajoguna, sattvaguna grows.

> **rajas tamaś cābhibhūya sattvaṁ bhavati bhārata**
> **rajaḥ sattvaṁ tamaś caiva tamaḥ sattvaṁ rajas tathā**
> "O Bharata, sattva grows by overpowering rajas and tamas; rajas grows by overpowering sattva and tamas; and tamas grows by overpowering sattva and rajas."
> (Bhagavad Gita 14.10)

In this way, step by step, every day, every day, Guruji literally washed us. Every day there was a new test, the realisation of some new mistake. Every day we realised that we were wrong in many places, that our whole life was wrong. Believe me, the day you feel your whole life is wrong is the day your right life begins.

## "Do Not Run After Your Own Remedy"

You too will now go on a yatra and do remedies. Let me tell you just one sentence of Guruji's: **do not run after your own remedy.**

We learned this lesson the hard way. We all went to do our own remedies. Each of us competed at our own level: this should be my remedy. We listened to the recording four times over: this is the remedy, this is the remedy. Even there we went with paper and pen, having written out our remedies carefully.

## Following the Guru's Paduka

There is only one thing to do: **follow the Guru's paduka** (sandals). This is written in the Guru Gita as well. What does following the Guru's paduka mean? What does worshipping it mean? It means walking the path the Guru walks.

The Guru never does "my own, my own", never does "mine". "I should get this, this is my remedy, this is my thing, mine should grow": never. His own individual priority is always the last priority.

He took so many people along. It was not that we had great devotion within us or were great sadhaks, the way Shankaracharya would take his special disciples along. We were nothing like that. We never deserved to be travelling with him. Yet in his own aura he had us do all our remedies properly.

## That Evening at the Shani Temple

On the second-to-last evening we were all at the Shani temple. And believe me, almost everyone had received a Shani remedy. Those who have done the Navagraha sadhana know how big that is. Guruji stood in the Shani temple; that was our last destination.

After everyone had done what they had to do there, these words came from Guruji's mouth:

> "Everyone's remedies are all done, complete."

Everyone was happy, everyone felt elevated. Yes, the yatra was successful.

## The Guru Who Digests the Halahal

The moment Guruji said this, in that very instant, all of our collective **halahal** (poison), our collective karma, he took entirely upon himself.

Even so, our session went on that night, a midnight session. The next morning Guruji did not come out. We did not even know. We were told that Guruji would not come today, that we all had to do this, this was the plan, and we were to follow it. In the evening we learned that Guruji had had a fever. And in the evening, when Guruji met us, there was not a trace of the fever on his face. The fever was gone.

Through yoga and tapasya he digested all that halahal that day. Having digested our entire halahal in such a short time, by evening he was absolutely normal. Not the slightest trace of the feeling, "Do you people deserve this? Why should I take your halahal?" Nothing at all. As happy as ever, as smiling, as energetic as always. No one would even realise that anything like this had happened.

## Do Not See a Human Intellect in the Guru

This is a very big thing. Sometimes you will feel: oh, he said this, he said that. But we must not see a human intellect in the Guru. Nothing there happens at the human level.

We will never be able to understand what the Guru's state is, what the Guru feels within, what he feels about the disciple, and what he feels when the disciple does wrong. It is beyond our imagination. So whatever the Guru says, we only have to do that much.

## Seven Hundred Kilos of Charity and Guruji's Displeasure

On that yatra we were giving charity, and we were very happy: oh, we distributed seven hundred kilos, we did this, we did that. But Guruji was not at all happy. He said:

> "My country still needs this much to be given today. My country of Shri Vidya, where every child knew it, where Shri Vidya was in every home, my Bharat that was the golden bird! Today, even after giving this much, it feels as if we are not doing even 0.01 percent for society."

There too he gave a lesson:

> "You are very happy, so many bags are filled, this and that. I am not happy. What will happen to her by taking one bag from you? How will she get next month's ration?"

## The True Meaning of Paduka Worship

There is so much to learn. We only have to follow the Guru's paduka. The path he has walked, we simply have to walk that path. Then everything happens by itself; our welfare is certain. We must not leave that path.

We will try to live that way, little by little, as we are able, and that alone is truly our worship of the Guru's paduka. Otherwise, merely taking the sandals and worshipping them will achieve nothing.

Thank you.
