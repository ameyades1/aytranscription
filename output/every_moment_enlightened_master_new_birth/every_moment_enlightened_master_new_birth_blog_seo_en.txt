# In the Guru's Presence, Every Moment Is a New Birth

## A New Person in Six Months

Imagine spending eight to twelve hours a day within the Guru's **aura**. All that time, an exchange of karma is quietly taking place between you, and you never even notice it.

One **sadhika** (a woman on the spiritual path) speaks from her own experience. Spend six months in the presence of such a Guru, she says, and by the end you will be an entirely new person. It feels like being born again. You will hardly remember what your earlier life was like.

For this transformation, the Guru gives you one tool: **seva** (selfless service).

The importance of Guru seva is that small tasks, such as cleaning or cooking for fellow seekers, break the habit of "me and mine" and widen your idea of family. The Guru's words settle in as lasting samskaras, the seeker moves step by step from tamas to rajas and on towards sattva, and life feels like a new birth.

## How Did the Service Rota Begin?

In the early days, the **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching) began at eight in the morning, and the **Gurukul** (the ashram where the Guru teaches) had to be cleaned before then. In a group of fifteen or twenty, the same four or five people arrived early, while the rest strolled in at eight. Their thinking went like this: it's only cleaning, so why should we bother?

The Guru noticed that the same people were doing the same service again and again, and bringing the food as well. That is when a **service timetable** came into being. Every task, from cleaning onwards, now had to be done by every person at least once or twice during the Shibir.

The Guru teaches the seeker, but sometimes it works the other way too: the way seekers behave prompts the Guru to set new rules.

## "Surely We Could Hire a Cook for This"

At the Shibir, from eight in the morning to eight at night, there was just one meal. It was never ordered from outside. It was always **sattvic food** (pure, simple, wholesome food), a few carefully chosen dishes, and the finest fruit.

The speaker admits with great honesty that at her first Shibir she was full of **tamoguna** (inertia) and **rajoguna** (restlessness). Getting up in the morning, cooking for twenty people and leaving home by seven might come easily to many, "but I was not one of them." Her mind kept saying: we have so much education, surely we could hire a cook for this.

For the first ten or fifteen days, enthusiasm carried her along. Then "a slightly clever streak of thinking" crept in: how will we keep this up for six months?

## When the Whole Group Arrived with "Options"

The plan was to approach the Guru when he seemed to be in a good mood. The options were ready:

- Hire a local woman to cook sattvic food, which would give her an income too
- Or let everyone bring their own lunch box

The whole group agreed at once. After all, it meant no more cooking at home and carrying the food in! And everyone said, "You're the one to talk to him."

The Guru listened calmly to everything. He is a wonderful listener and never interrupts. Looking back on that scene today, the speaker says: this is where I was wrong. She shares it so that we do not repeat the same mistake, because good people are those who learn from the mistakes of others.

## How Wide Is Your Idea of Family?

After hearing everyone out, the Guru said that they had spent all these years thinking only of "me and mine", and asked what it had brought them. At some point, he said, you have to let your family grow larger.

At home, when your child needs a lunch box by seven, you get up and cook something new every day. The Guru then asked: aren't these fifteen people your family right now? Can't we cook for them with love?

This was "a very big lesson." The Guru shares out small acts of service among everyone, and anyone who truly learns from one of them can see their whole life change. Behind each small task lies a far greater hand.

*Where does your "family" end? Is there someone you could do something for with love, yet keep putting off because they are "not mine"?*

## Words That Become Samskaras

From then on, the morning cooking began in a spirit of **ekatva** (oneness), and the whole outlook changed. Why should the idea of family be so narrow? Otherwise, what is the point of coming here to learn about the Divine? Suddenly, the dots began to connect.

What the Guru says does not stay a passing remark. It sinks into our **samskaras** (deep impressions in the mind) and stays there for good. The next time even the thought of a similar mistake arises, that impression stands up to meet it, and the Guru's words ring in the mind like a bell.

## From Tamas to Rajas, and from Rajas to Sattva

This change was a ladder. At that stage it meant climbing from tamoguna to rajoguna, because getting up early to cook takes a little rajas too! In the Bhagavad Gita, Shri Krishna describes exactly this sequence:

> **rajas tamaś cābhibhūya sattvaṁ bhavati bhārata |**
> "O Bharata, sattva grows by overcoming rajas and tamas."
> (Bhagavad Gita 14.10)

First you overcome tamas and rise into rajas. Then, as you overcome rajas, **sattva** (purity and clarity) grows.

Every day brought a new test and a fresh awareness of some mistake. "The Guru truly gave us a thorough washing." And then comes a deep insight: the day you feel your whole life has been wrong is the very day your right life begins.

## "Don't Chase After Your Own Remedies"

On one pilgrimage, everyone had gone to perform their own **upayas** (spiritual remedies). Each person was eager to make sure their particular remedy got done. People listened to the recordings four times over and arrived with pen and paper, the remedies written down.

Yet the Guru's teaching was always the same: **don't chase after your own remedies.**

There is only one thing to do: follow the **Guru's paduka** (the Guru's sandals). The Guru Gita says the same. To worship the paduka is to walk the path the Guru walks.

And what is the Guru's path? He never thinks in terms of "me and mine". His own personal needs always come last.

## The Shani Temple and the Guru's Silent Sacrifice

On the second-to-last evening of the pilgrimage, everyone was at the Shani temple, dedicated to Saturn. Almost everyone had been given a Saturn remedy. Once all the rituals were done, the Guru said, "Everyone's remedies are complete, all of them." Everyone was delighted, and the pilgrimage had succeeded.

Yet in that very moment, the Guru took the group's collective **halahala** upon himself. Halahala is the poison Lord Shiva drank to save the world; here it means the weight of everyone's karma.

That night the session ran until midnight. The next morning, the Guru did not come out. By evening, people learned that he had been running a fever. Yet when they met him that evening, there was no trace of fever on his face. Through yoga and austerity, he had absorbed all that poison in a single day.

He made no complaint and showed no hint of "Do you deserve this? Why should I take on your poison?" He had the same cheerfulness, the same smile, the same energy. Nobody even noticed.

Acharya Ji explains that we must not look at the Guru through ordinary human reasoning. We will never understand what the Guru feels inside, for the disciple, and when the disciple goes wrong. It lies beyond our imagination. So whatever the Guru says, simply do that.

*Have you ever weighed the words of your Guru or mentor with ordinary human reasoning, without knowing what lay behind them?*

## Seven Hundred Kilos, and Still "I Am Not Happy"

During the pilgrimage, the group gave away seven hundred kilos of provisions in charity. Everyone was overjoyed. The Guru, however, was not pleased at all. He reminded them that this land of Shri Vidya, where every child once knew Shri Vidya, was called the golden bird. Today, he said, even after giving so much, it feels as if we are not doing even 0.01 percent for society. What difference will one bag from us make to a family, he asked, and where will next month's rations come from?

Service is not about your own satisfaction. It is about what the other person truly needs.

## What Does Guru Paduka Worship Really Mean?

Walk the path the Guru has walked, and your well-being is assured. Trying, little by little and as far as you are able, to live the way he lives is true paduka worship. Otherwise, worshipping a pair of sandals alone will achieve nothing.

## Key Takeaways

- In the Guru's presence, karma is constantly exchanged; within six months, it feels like a new birth.
- **Seva** is the tool of transformation, and even the smallest act of service carries a larger purpose.
- The real lesson is to drop "me and mine" and widen your idea of family.
- The Guru's words settle within you as lasting **samskaras**.
- **Sadhana** (spiritual practice) unfolds step by step: from tamas to rajas, and from rajas to sattva (Gita 14.10).
- The day your whole life feels wrong is the day your right life begins.
- Don't chase your own remedies; follow the Guru's paduka.
- The Guru silently takes on the disciples' poison; never judge him by human reasoning.
- True paduka worship means walking the Guru's path, not merely worshipping sandals.
