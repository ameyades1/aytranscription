# The True Meaning of Pitru Rin: Service Before Shraddha

When **Pitru Paksha** (the fortnight each year dedicated to honouring the ancestors) arrives, many questions stir in the mind. Who are the ancestors we honour? What do **shraddha** (rites performed with reverence for the departed) and **tarpan** (offering water to satisfy and nourish the ancestors) truly mean?

These questions came up in a conversation with Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji. One of them went deeper than the rest: if people fail to respect their elders while they are alive, yet perform an elaborate shraddha after they are gone, does it do them any good?

What is pitru rin? In Acharya Ji's teaching, it is the debt we owe our parents for all they gave up for us. We repay it while they are alive, by spending time with them and being their joy, not just their walking stick. Shraddha performed later is not wrong, if it is not for show or out of fear.

## Why Do We Overlook What Is Close to Us?

Acharya Ji calls this the way of **Kali Yuga** (the present dark age). As long as something or someone is with us, we do not see its worth. Once it is gone, we go looking for it.

The same happens with our parents and ancestors. We are so wrapped up in our own **desires** that we fail to give them the respect they deserve while they are still here.

They want to sit with us lovingly and share a few words. We never have the time. There is the job, the travel, the children to look after, the worry about money. Then one day, without warning, they are gone.

What follows is loneliness, and sometimes fear. Some people perform shraddha out of a sense of duty, others because they are afraid.

*Is there someone in your life who only wants a few words with you, and you keep telling them "later"?*

## When Does Someone Become a Pitar?

Here Acharya Ji shares a profound point. The scriptures say we are not meant to enter the state of a **pitar** (an ancestral spirit that remains unfulfilled after death) at all. A person avoids it by leaving the body in happiness, in a state of bliss.

Illness in old age is natural. Elders suffer, though, when the family gives them no comfort and pays them no attention. Acharya Ji says no one should have to depart in such neglect, because that is how a person becomes a pitar.

## It Is Never Too Late to Wake Up

Is there no way forward, then, for someone who realises this too late? Acharya Ji answers with a familiar saying: **the moment you wake up is your morning.**

If your eyes opened only later, there is nothing wrong with performing shraddha afterwards. The scriptures say you can still change. Through charity, good deeds and service, you can still become a **kul-deepak** (a lamp who brings light to the whole family line). Just as the bandit Valya transformed into Maharshi Valmiki, the author of the Ramayana, such a change is possible for you too.

Keep two things in mind, though:

- Do not perform shraddha for **show**, with the feeling of "there, my duty is done."
- Do not perform shraddha out of **fear**.

## Why Wait at All?

The biggest question is this: why let it come to "better late than never" in the first place? We should live an ideal life while our elders are still alive. The scriptures proclaim it as loudly as they can:

> **mātṛdevo bhava |**
> **pitṛdevo bhava |**
> **ācāryadevo bhava ||**
> "Regard your mother as God. Regard your father as God. Regard your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

Even this body is a gift from our parents. They sent us to school, carried us on their shoulders, and cared for us in everything, from our meals to every illness.

## How Do We Repay Pitru Rin? Be Their Joy, Not Just Their Support

In old age, parents hope for one thing: that their children will be the walking stick they lean on. Acharya Ji goes a step further. Children should not stop at being a support; they should also bring their parents joy.

Spend time with them. They have given up so much for us. **This is pitru rin (the debt we owe our parents and ancestors), and to repay it, we must at least give them that much of our time.**

*How much time do you give your parents or elders today, and do you give it as a duty or as a joy?*

## A Home That Never Needs Shraddha

The scriptures say that a family which lives this way will never need shraddha at all, because the values in that home were so good.

Such a family need only do **go-seva** (caring for cows) in their ancestors' name, give to Brahmins, or read a little scripture at home. Even this keeps their ancestors free forever, and those ancestors go on blessing the whole family.

## Sadhana Works Like Science

Acharya Ji offers a beautiful example. Science tells us that when you press a switch, the fan or the light is bound to come on, whether a king presses it or a pauper, a monk or a thief, an old person or a young one.

In the same way, the **phalashruti** (the fruit a practice bears) of the Antar Yog sadhanas comes quickly. This holds for Ganesha Vidya, Shri Vidya, Saraswati Diksha (initiation into the practice of Saraswati), the sadhana for the liberation of ancestors and for healing, and Shiva sadhana alike. Most people receive that fruit right here and now, as direct experience.

## Key Takeaways

- Value those close to you while they are here; once they are gone, only regret remains.
- A person becomes a pitar by leaving the body unhappy and neglected.
- It is never too late: shraddha performed later is not wrong, as long as it is not for show or out of fear.
- Mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava: serving your parents while they live is the true repayment of pitru rin.
- Be more than a support in their old age; be their joy as well.
- A home where elders are happy never needs shraddha; the ancestors stay free and keep blessing the family.
