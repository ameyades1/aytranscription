# Chapter: The True Meaning of Pitru Rin

During the days of Pitru Paksha, many questions arise in our minds to which we often do not know the right answers. Who are the Pitars (ancestors)? What is the real meaning of Shraddha and Tarpan? And what wisdom lies behind these traditions? It was on these questions that a dialogue took place with Brahmarshi Acharya Upendra Ji. One question stood out in particular: today we see that many people do not respect their elders while they are alive, yet after they pass away, they perform Shraddha with great ceremony. In such a situation, is there any benefit in performing Shraddha and the other rites?

## The Glory of Kali Yuga: What Is Near Is Not Valued

This is the glory of Kali Yuga: as long as some thing or some person is with you, it has no importance. When it goes away or is lost, we keep searching for it. Unfortunately, the same thinking applies to our parents and our ancestors. People in this world are so surrounded by their desires that as long as that person is alive, they do not receive as much respect.

When parents, grandparents or other elders are alive and want to come and meet us with love, to share a few words, we have no time. There is the job, there is travel, there is looking after the children, there is the worry about money. Then one day, suddenly, they are gone.

Then loneliness suddenly fills the mind, or sometimes fear arises, or some work does not succeed, or one feels it is one's responsibility: come, Shraddha must be done. Some do it in this spirit, some do it out of fear.

## When Does One Become a Pitar?

But there is another side to this as well, which we must understand carefully. The scriptures say that we are not meant to enter the Pitar state (pitar yoni) at all. That is possible only when you leave the body in a state of happiness, a state of bliss.

That is why no one in the home, whether parents, grandparents or any ancestor, should depart in a state of sorrow. They may be ill, that is natural; but they become sorrowful when the people of the home do not give them happiness and do not pay attention to them. No one should depart in such a neglected state. **It is when they depart in such a state that they become Pitars.**

## When You Wake, It Is Morning

Now suppose this situation has already come about in the life of some son or daughter. Then we say: when you wake, it is morning. If their eyes open after that, should they not express gratitude? They performed the Shraddha later, and there is nothing wrong in that. They did not realise it while the parent was alive, but they realise it now, so now they are performing Shraddha.

Our scriptures still say that such a person can reform. A change will come in their mind. They will do charity, good deeds and service for their parents. Even now they can become the lamp of the family (kul-deepak) and bring honour to its name. An invaluable transformation can happen in them. Just as Valya the bandit became Valmiki, such a transformation is also possible.

So if they begin performing Shraddha even later, there is no problem in it. Yes, but two things are very important:

- Shraddha should not be **for show**, not in the spirit of "that's it, my responsibility is over from today."
- Shraddha should not be done **out of fear**.

## Why Come to "Better Late Than Never" at All?

And the most important point is this: why should we land in a "better late than never" situation at all? We should live an ideal life while they are alive. The scriptures are proclaiming it loudly:

> **mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Treat your mother as God. Treat your father as God. Treat your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shikshavalli 1.11.2)

That is, regard your mother as divine, regard your father as divine, regard your teacher as divine.

Even this body was given to you by your parents. It was they who must have sent you to school. When you were small, it was they who must have carried you on their shoulders. From your food to your illnesses, they took care of everything.

## Be Not Only the Staff of Old Age, but Also Its Joy

So it is your responsibility that, in their final moments, you fulfil the expectation they have: that you become the staff (lathi) of their old age.

> "Do not become only the staff; give them joy as well."

Spend a little time with them, because they have made so many sacrifices for us. **To repay that debt, we must give at least that much time.** This is the true meaning of Pitru Rin, the debt to one's ancestors.

## A Home That Never Needs Shraddha

When we do this, the scriptures say that there will be no need for Shraddha in that home, not even the need for that rite, because the samskaras (values) of that family were so good.

Now, if for the sake of their ancestors they only do service of cows (gau-seva) or give charity to Brahmins, or even if a little reading of the scriptures takes place at home, their ancestors will always be liberated. They will keep blessing them and the entire family forever.

## The Swift Fruit of Sadhana

Just as science says that if you press the button, the fan or the light must come on, whether a poor man presses it or a king, a renunciate or a thief, an old person or a young one, in the same way a law works in the sadhanas of Antar Yog too. Whether it is the sadhana of Ganesh Vidya, Shri Vidya sadhana, Saraswati Diksha, the sadhana of Pitru-mukti or healing, or Shiva sadhana, its phalashruti (fruit) comes swiftly, to most people and in great measure. Here people receive the fruit of this principle right here and now, as direct experience.
