# Chapter: The Fullness of Life Through the Spirit of Renunciation

## Varna by Qualities, Ashrama by Age

Fundamentally, the four varnas have arisen according to the three gunas. Alongside these four varnas there is one more group, belonging to the forbidden side. Whatever a person does becomes action according to their varna. That is one way of seeing it. Now look from another angle and see what else lies within it.

Age also makes a difference. The first phase of life is when education must be received. The son of a Brahmin must learn, the son of a Kshatriya, the prince, must learn, and the son of a merchant must certainly learn. Those who serve also have children, don't they? Whether one is a craftsman, an employee or a labourer, the child has to learn, does it not? Each must learn their own knowledge, knowledge at their own level. They are fully free to go higher or lower, to become a doctor, an engineer or something else. But it is the age of learning. What is the main duty at that age? That age is called **Brahmacharya Ashrama**, and that age too has its own duties.

## Grihastha Ashrama: A Door Open to All

After this, a person may become a householder. A Brahmin may also marry. Why, even the avatars married, so it is certainly possible. Grihastha means that one lives in the home, whether one lives there with the spirit of renunciation is a separate matter. But this second step, from one becoming two, is permitted for the Brahmin too. We have seen it even among those beyond the gunas; even the Saptarishis married. So a king may marry and bring a queen; so may a merchant, a servant, even a thug or a dacoit. So will there not be a distinct duty for a certain period in the Grihastha Ashrama?

## Vanaprastha and Sannyasa: For Everyone

Then comes Vanaprastha. The labourer, too, must retire some day. The merchant will retire from business. The king, too, must step down from the throne some day. And the Brahmin cannot work that much until the moment of death. So Vanaprastha Ashrama, and the fourth, Sannyasa Ashrama, will apply to all four varnas.

The one who is beyond these is already in the spirit of renunciation. The scripture gradually says this: in every situation of your life, begin to live with the spirit of renunciation. Right after Brahmacharya Ashrama, do the work of a householder, but live with the spirit of renunciation. Live in Vanaprastha, lead a retired life, but with the spirit of renunciation. Why? Do you know?

## Twenty-Five Years at a Time

Because it is said: from zero to twenty-five years is Brahmacharya Ashrama, from twenty-five to fifty is Grihastha Ashrama, from fifty to seventy-five is Vanaprastha Ashrama. But do not think that my marriage ends after the age of fifty-one. It was not said that it ends; only its peak is over. Now retirement means **letting go of attachment**.

Twenty-five years were given to practise becoming unattached, to practise winding up. After fifty-one, while winding up, do not keep expanding bigger and bigger; less, less, less. Even if property doubles, that is fine, but let interest in it also become less, less, less. Preparation to hand over to others must be done by sixty. Retiring by sixty-two or sixty-three, after seventy it is said that no new active work is needed. And from seventy-five to a hundred, you are in Sannyasa Ashrama; so it is said.

## Who Can Give a Guarantee?

Now, can anyone guarantee that I will live to eighty-two, that I will not be in a coma then, that I will not have suffered paralysis, that my intellect will be sound, or that further layers of ego will not have built up on me? At the age of seven, eight, twelve or fifteen, can you tell whether you will even cross fifty-four, or that you will not have an accident at twenty-eight? Think and tell me.

And if there is no guarantee of this, how can you say that spirituality is to be done after sixty? Because its preparation is described after Vanaprastha, people say: after sixty, once we are between fifty and seventy-five, we will do it. So on what assurance did you say that my Sannyasa Ashrama, my Vanaprastha, will begin only then?

## Brahmacharya and Sannyasa: Brothers

That is why our scripture says that Brahmacharya Ashrama, that is, student life, is close to Sannyasa; the two are brothers. In between comes a detour. So pick up the spirit of renunciation right after it. Live as a householder, later in Vanaprastha, but there is no need to wait until seventy-five or a hundred for Sannyasa. From twenty-five itself one can come directly into the spirit of renunciation.

> "Do not become a sannyasi, but one can live with the spirit of renunciation."

## How This Knowledge Transforms Life

If this knowledge comes to you, you will on your own become a merchant of **Shri Vidya**. The king, too, will become the kind of king a truly anointed king is. The Brahmin, too, will be of a high level, having studied all the Vedas, having learnt the whole of Vedanta, and later will himself become like a sannyasi guru. A guru is, after all, of that level.

And that poor dacoit, too, can go from Valya to Valmiki, because of this very knowledge, because of the spirit of renunciation. If some Narada comes to him, can he not change directly? So it is changeable; this much is clear.

What happened in the house of Hiranyakashipu: they were people of that forbidden side. But did his son Prahlada not come to this side on his own? Did he not become a devotee of Vishnu? So it is changeable, not rigid and fixed.

> **cāturvarṇyaṁ mayā sṛṣṭaṁ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ**
> "The four varnas were created by Me according to the division of qualities and actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.13)

The Lord says that the four varnas were created by Him according to the division of qualities and actions. When the basis is qualities and actions, varna is not fixed; it keeps changing.

## All Four Varnas in a Single Day

> "It changes every single day."

When I pray in the morning, do sadhana or listen to satsang, I am working to attain my **Brahma bhava**. When I meditate, go on pilgrimage or go to the temple, at that time, to that extent, I am a **Brahmin**.

When I do something for self-protection, for security, the work of protecting, who have I become? A **Kshatriya**.

When I go out for my livelihood, do a job, earn money, think about it, I become a **Vaishya**.

And when I want to rest, or want to do general service, serving one another, or do some other work of my own, I have become a **Shudra**.

After that, when I cause trouble to others, or cause trouble to my own body, when I become a troublemaker, I myself become the one on the forbidden side.

And if I rise above all four varnas, I myself have become a saint, I myself am on the path of liberation, I am free.
