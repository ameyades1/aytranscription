# Why Wait Until Sixty for Spirituality

Many of us think spirituality is something for after retirement. First studies, then a job, a family, and when everything is settled, then God.

Acharya Upendra Ji puts a direct question to this thinking: **who will give you the guarantee?** Let us look at the ancient system of varna and ashrama with fresh eyes.

## Varna by Qualities, Ashrama by Age

Fundamentally, four **varnas** have arisen according to the three **gunas** (sattva, rajas, tamas: the three basic natures of prakriti). Alongside them is one more group, belonging to the forbidden side.

But that is only one view. The other view is **age**. The first phase of life is for learning. Whether a Brahmin's son, a prince, a merchant's son or a labourer's child, everyone must learn knowledge at their own level.

Whether they become a doctor or an engineer, they are fully free. But this is the age of learning. It is called **Brahmacharya Ashrama**, and it has its own duties.

## Grihastha: The Step From One to Two

Next comes **Grihastha Ashrama**. A Brahmin may also marry. Even the avatars married, even the Saptarishis.

A king, a merchant, a servant, even a dacoit, all can become householders. Living at home, but living with the spirit of renunciation, is a separate matter.

## Everyone's Retirement Comes

Then **Vanaprastha**. The labourer will retire, so will the merchant, the king must leave the throne, and even the Brahmin cannot work as much until the end of life. Finally, **Sannyasa Ashrama**. These four ashramas are for all varnas.

The scriptural order is:

1. Zero to 25 years: Brahmacharya
2. 25 to 50 years: Grihastha
3. 50 to 75 years: Vanaprastha
4. 75 to 100 years: Sannyasa

## The Real Meaning of Vanaprastha

This does not mean marriage ends after fifty-one. Acharya Ji makes it clear that only its peak ends. Retirement means **letting go of attachment**.

Twenty-five years were given to practise becoming unattached. After fifty-one, do not keep expanding; less, less, less. Even if property doubles, that is fine, but let your interest in it keep shrinking.

By sixty, be ready to hand over to others. After seventy, no new active work is needed.

## Who Can Give a Guarantee?

Now the real question. Can anyone guarantee that at eighty-two you will not be in a coma, will not have paralysis, that your intellect will be sound, and that new layers of ego will not have built up?

At fifteen, can anyone say they will cross fifty-four, or that there will be no accident at twenty-eight?

*If there is no guarantee of life itself, on what assurance are you postponing your spirituality until after sixty?*

## Brahmacharya and Sannyasa: Brothers

The scripture says that student life and Sannyasa are close to each other; they are brothers. In between comes the detour of householder life. So the spirit of renunciation need not wait until seventy-five; it can be embraced from twenty-five.

> "Do not become a sannyasi, but one can live with the spirit of renunciation."

**Sannyasa vritti** (renunciate attitude) does not mean leaving home, but remaining inwardly unattached while doing every task. Do the householder's work, but with the spirit of renunciation. Live a retired life, but with the spirit of renunciation.

## What This Knowledge Changes

When this knowledge comes, a merchant becomes a merchant of **Shri Vidya**. A king becomes a truly anointed king. A Brahmin, mastering the Vedas and Vedanta, himself becomes like a guru.

And the dacoit? He can go from Valya to **Valmiki**. Narada Ji came, and his life changed directly.

Prahlada, born in the house of Hiranyakashipu, became a devotee of Vishnu on his own. This system is not rigid; it is changeable.

> **cāturvarṇyaṁ mayā sṛṣṭaṁ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ**
> "The four varnas were created by Me according to the division of qualities and actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.13)

The Lord says the four varnas were created by the division of qualities and actions. When the basis is qualities and actions, varna too can change.

## All Four Varnas in a Single Day

> "It changes every single day."

Acharya Ji explains this through our own day:

- In the morning, during prayer, sadhana, satsang, meditation or a temple visit, I am a **Brahmin**, because I am moving towards **Brahma bhava** (the experience of the supreme truth).
- When doing the work of self-protection or security, I am a **Kshatriya**.
- When doing a job for my livelihood, earning money and thinking about it, I am a **Vaishya**.
- When resting or doing general service, I am a **Shudra**.
- When causing trouble to others or to my own body, when becoming a troublemaker, I am on the forbidden side.

> "If I rise above all four varnas, I myself have become a saint, I myself am on the path of liberation."

*How much of your day today did you spend in which varna?*

## Key Takeaways

- Varnas arise from qualities and ashramas from age; all four ashramas are for every varna.
- Vanaprastha means practising letting go of attachment: less, less, less.
- Life has no guarantee, so postponing spirituality until after sixty is not wise.
- Becoming a sannyasi is not necessary, but one can live with the spirit of renunciation from twenty-five.
- Varna is changeable: Valya became Valmiki, Prahlada became a devotee of Vishnu.
- We live all four varnas in a single day; rising above all four is the path to liberation.
