# Why Wait Until Sixty for Spirituality?

Many of us believe spirituality belongs to retirement. First comes education, then a career and a family, and once all of that is settled, there will be time for God.

Acharya Upendra Ji answers this belief with one plain question: **who can guarantee it?** Let us take a fresh look at the ancient system of **varna** (social class) and **ashrama** (stage of life).

In the varna and ashrama system as Acharya Ji explains it, varna arises from the three gunas and ashrama from age: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha and Sannyasa. If varna rests on qualities and action, it can change. Since life itself comes with no guarantee, spirituality need not wait until after sixty; the spirit of renunciation can be embraced from twenty-five.

## Varna by Nature, Ashrama by Age

At its root, the four varnas arise from the three **gunas** (sattva, rajas and tamas, the three basic qualities of nature). Alongside them stands one more group, which falls on the forbidden side of life.

Yet this is only one way of looking at it. The other is **age**. The first stage of life is for learning. Whether a child is born to a **Brahmin** (the priestly and scholarly class), a king, a merchant or a labourer, every child has to learn what suits their own level.

Each child is completely free to become a doctor or an engineer. But this age belongs to learning. It is called **Brahmacharya ashrama** (the student stage), and it carries duties of its own.

## Grihastha: When One Becomes Two

Next comes **Grihastha ashrama** (the householder stage). A Brahmin can marry too. Even the avatars married, and so did the **Saptarishis** (the seven great sages of ancient India).

Kings, merchants, servants, even bandits can all become householders. Living at home while keeping the inner spirit of a renunciate is another matter altogether.

## What Are the Four Ashramas of Life?

Then comes **Vanaprastha** (the stage of withdrawal from worldly life). The labourer will retire, and so will the merchant. The king must one day give up the throne, and even a Brahmin cannot keep working at the same pace until the end of life. Last comes **Sannyasa ashrama** (the stage of renunciation). All four stages apply to every varna.

The scriptures lay out this sequence:

1. Birth to 25: Brahmacharya
2. 25 to 50: Grihastha
3. 50 to 75: Vanaprastha
4. 75 to 100: Sannyasa

## What Vanaprastha Really Means

This does not mean married life ends at fifty-one. Acharya Ji makes it clear that only its peak passes. Withdrawal here means **letting go of attachment**.

You are given twenty-five years to practise detachment. After fifty-one, stop expanding; let things grow less, less and less. Even if your wealth doubles, that is fine, as long as your interest in it keeps fading.

By sixty, be ready to hand things over to others. After seventy, you have no need to take on any new active work.

## Who Can Promise You Your Later Years?

Now comes the real question. Can anyone guarantee that at eighty-two you will not be lying unconscious or paralysed, that your mind will still be clear, and that fresh layers of ego will not have settled over you?

Can anyone tell a fifteen-year-old that they will live past fifty-four, or that no accident will strike them at twenty-eight?

*If life itself comes with no guarantee, what makes you so sure you can put off your spiritual life until after sixty?*

## Brahmacharya and Sannyasa: Two Brothers

The scriptures say that student life and renunciation stand close to each other; they are like brothers. Householder life is simply a turn in the road between them. So you need not postpone the spirit of renunciation until seventy-five. You can embrace it from the age of twenty-five.

Acharya Ji puts it this way: do not become a **sannyasi** (a monk who has renounced the world), but you can still live like one on the inside.

**Sannyasa vritti** (a renunciate attitude) does not mean leaving home. It means staying inwardly unattached while you carry out every task. Do the work of a householder, but with this attitude. Live your retired years, but with this attitude.

## Can Varna Change? How This Knowledge Transforms a Life

Once this knowledge takes root, a merchant becomes a merchant of **Shri Vidya** (the sacred knowledge and worship of the Divine Mother). A king becomes a true king, rightly consecrated to rule. A Brahmin masters the Vedas and Vedanta and becomes almost a Guru in his own right.

And the bandit? Valya, the highway robber, can become **Valmiki** (the sage who composed the Ramayana). The sage Narada crossed his path, and his life turned around completely.

Prahlada was born in the house of the demon king Hiranyakashipu, yet he became a devotee of Lord Vishnu all on his own. This system is not rigid; it leaves room for change.

> **cāturvarṇyaṁ mayā sṛṣṭaṁ guṇakarmavibhāgaśaḥ**
> "The Lord says: I created the four varnas according to the division of qualities and actions."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.13)

If varna rests on qualities and action, then a person's varna can change as well.

## All Four Varnas in a Single Day

Your varna shifts every day. Acharya Ji explains this through our own daily routine:

- In the morning, during prayer, **sadhana** (spiritual practice), **satsang** (a gathering in the company of truth), meditation or a visit to the temple, I am a **Brahmin**, because I am moving towards **Brahma bhava** (the experience of the Supreme Truth).
- When I defend myself or keep others safe, I am a **Kshatriya** (the warrior class).
- When I work for a living, earn money and plan my finances, I am a **Vaishya** (the merchant class).
- When I rest or do ordinary service, I am a **Shudra** (the serving class).
- When I hurt others or my own body, or stir up trouble, I am on the forbidden side.

Acharya Ji says that if we rise above all four varnas, we become saints ourselves and step onto the path of **moksha** (liberation).

*How much of your day today did you spend in each varna?*

## Key Takeaways

- Varna comes from the gunas and ashrama from age; all four stages apply to every varna.
- Vanaprastha means practising the release of attachment: less, less and less.
- Life comes with no guarantee, so do not put off spirituality until after sixty.
- You need not become a sannyasi; you can live with sannyasa vritti from twenty-five.
- Varna can change: Valya became Valmiki, and Prahlada became a devotee of Lord Vishnu.
- We live all four varnas in a single day; rising above them is the path to liberation.
