## 1. Summary

A short talk in which Acharya Ji argues from the body's own design that human beings are not built for meat. The human digestive tract is long and the intestine is coiled and ridged, like the corrugated pipe under a wash basin, made to absorb nourishment and essence (सत्व, रस) from food. A carnivore's tract is short and smooth, like a garden hose, so meat passes straight out. In the human gut, he says, meat lingers, ferments and brings disease, which is why a lion can eat any number of deer and stay healthy while a human does not. The main intent is informational with a lifestyle edge: "are humans meant to eat meat", "human vs carnivore digestive system", "non veg khana sahi hai ya nahi". The opportunity is a clear, memorable analogy (two pipes, the lion) that ranks for comparison queries and feeds the Mindful Eating cluster. The topic is health-adjacent (YMYL), so the medical-sounding claims must stay attributed to Acharya Ji (section 10).

## 6. FAQ

1. **क्या मनुष्य का शरीर मांसाहार के लिए बना है? / Are humans meant to eat meat?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार नहीं: मनुष्य का लंबा, लहरदार पाचन तंत्र भोजन से सत्व और रस सोखने के लिए बना है, जबकि मांसाहारी प्राणियों का छोटा, चिकना तंत्र मांस को तुरंत बाहर निकालने के लिए। / According to Acharya Ji, no: the long, ridged human tract is built to absorb nourishment from food, while a carnivore's short, smooth tract is built to expel meat quickly.
2. **मनुष्य और मांसाहारी प्राणियों के पाचन तंत्र में क्या अंतर है? / How does the human digestive system differ from a carnivore's?**
   मनुष्य का पाचन तंत्र बहुत लंबा है और आँतें घुमावदार हैं; मांसाहारी प्राणियों का तंत्र बहुत छोटा है। / The human tract is very long and coiled; a carnivore's is very short.
3. **दो नलियों का उदाहरण क्या है? / What is the example of the two pipes?**
   वॉश बेसिन के नीचे की लहरदार नली में चिकनाई अटकती है, बगीचे की चिकनी नली से सब तुरंत निकल जाता है। मनुष्य की आँत पहली जैसी है, मांसाहारी प्राणियों की दूसरी जैसी। / Grease gets stuck in the ridged pipe under a wash basin but rushes through a smooth garden hose. The human intestine is like the first, a carnivore's like the second.
4. **सिंह मांस खाकर बीमार क्यों नहीं होता? / Why doesn't a lion fall ill from eating meat?**
   आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि उसकी नली तुरंत निकाल देने के लिए बनी है: जितना चाहिए उतना रह जाता है, बाकी बाहर फेंक दिया जाता है। / Acharya Ji explains that its tract is built to expel quickly: the body keeps what it needs and throws the rest out.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **"Food eaten ten years ago stays in the intestines in layers" (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The blog states this in its own voice, right after citing research and medical science; mainstream medicine does not support it. The transcript confirms Acharya Ji said "दस साल" literally, which settles the review's open question: keep "ten years" but attribute it ("आचार्य जी के अनुसार…" / "According to Acharya Ji…") in the body and both Key Takeaways lists. Until then, keep the claim out of every title, meta, snippet, FAQ and social card, and don't target "does meat stay in your body for years", even though the video title leads with it.
2. **"Research confirms this" (review issue 2, Minor, not yet applied).** It should back only the length of the digestive tract, not the rotting and disease claims. Add the review's attribution at the start of "जब भोजन भीतर ही सड़ने लगे" / "When Food Starts to Rot Within". The snippet (section 5) and FAQ already attribute every claim to Acharya Ji.
3. **YMYL and a contested query.** "Are humans meant to eat meat" is a debated question where top results come from nutrition and medical sources. Rank on the talk's own angle (the two-pipe analogy and the lion) and present it as Acharya Ji's teaching. Don't add nutrition facts, studies, disease names or health promises to compete, and don't frame meat as certain to cause disease in any SEO text.
4. **The lion "never falls ill" from meat.** Teaching analogy; keep it attributed ("Acharya Ji points out…") wherever it is quoted, as the blog already does in section 4.
5. **Video title and thumbnail.** "Remains in the Body for Years – Startling Truths" is close to the clickbait the guidelines avoid. If the YouTube title is ever revised, use a question form such as "What Is the Human Body Built to Eat? | Acharya Upendra Ji | Mindful Eating Series".
6. **सत्व / सत्त्व (review issue 3, Minor).** Optional spelling fix. The links in seo.json quote the current text ("बहुत सत्व, बहुत रस"); if the spelling is changed, update that link phrase and section 11.
7. **Thin content.** About 350 words per language. Don't pad it for SEO; if more depth is wanted, the editor can add the direct-answer paragraph and FAQ, which only restate the talk.
