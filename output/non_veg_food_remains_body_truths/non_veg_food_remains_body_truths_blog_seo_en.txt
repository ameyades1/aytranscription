# What Is Your Body Built to Eat?

## Are Humans Meant to Eat Meat?

We often ask what we should eat and what we should avoid. Part of the answer lies hidden in the design of our own bodies.

According to Acharya Upendra Ji, the human body is not built for meat. Our digestive tract is long and our intestine works like a ridged pipe, made to absorb nourishment and essence from food. A carnivore's tract is short and smooth, so meat passes straight out. In us, he explains, meat lingers, ferments and brings disease.

Look at it for a moment from a medical point of view. Study the small intestine and the whole digestive system, and you will find that the human digestive tract is very long. Meat-eating animals have a strikingly short one. Research confirms this, and medical science knows it well.

Why the difference? Meat has to leave their bodies quickly, because it carries a great deal of waste.

## When Food Starts to Rot Within

A vegetarian meal and a meat meal behave differently. Meat stays in the stomach much longer, and because our intestines are coiled, it begins to rot and ferment inside us during all that time.

Meat is already dead matter, and its decomposition has begun before you ever eat it. When it lingers that long in the body, disease is bound to follow.

That is why food a person ate ten years ago can still sit deposited in the intestines. Its layers build up slowly, and the passage keeps growing narrower.

*Have you ever wondered how many years today's meal might stay inside your body?*

## Two Pipes, Two Designs

Acharya Ji offers a simple example. Look at the pipe under a wash basin. It is usually white and corrugated, ringed with ridges. Now picture the perfectly smooth hose you use to water the garden.

Oil, ghee and grease rush straight through the smooth hose. In the ridged pipe, though, everything gets stuck.

A pipe is smooth, Acharya Ji explains, wherever food needs to pass through in a flash. The digestive tract of a carnivore is built that way, so that nothing catches and everything moves straight out. The human tract is built for absorption. We are meant to draw a great deal of nourishment and essence from our food.

## If the Lion Stays Healthy, Why Don't We?

A lion can eat any number of deer and never fall ill from it. A human being will.

Acharya Ji points out that our pipe is ridged, designed to extract everything it can. Theirs is designed to expel quickly: the body keeps what it needs and throws the rest out. Ours is made to wring every last drop from what passes through, so whatever we put into it also tends to get stuck.

*The next time you choose a meal, will you remember how your body is made?*

## Key Takeaways

- The human digestive tract is long; a carnivore's is very short.
- Meat is already dead matter, and when it stays long in the body it ferments and rots, which brings disease.
- Food eaten long ago builds up in the intestines in layers and narrows the passage.
- The human intestine works like a ridged pipe, built to absorb nourishment and essence; a carnivore's works like a smooth hose, built to expel quickly.
- That is why a lion stays healthy after eating deer, while meat makes human beings ill.
