# The Medicine of Faith: When Shraddha Itself Becomes the Cure

Have you ever wondered why the same medicine cures one person and has no effect on another? In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji speaks about the hidden science of healing that the medical world calls **placebo** and our tradition calls **shraddha**.

## The Two Faces of Healing

Whatever the healing may be, in the language of healing it is called the **placebo effect**. It also has another side, called the **nocebo effect**.

In simple words: placebo is when healing begins through belief, and nocebo is when, through disbelief, even the best medicine becomes ineffective.

## A Village, Some Tablets and an Injection

Acharya Ji shares a story told by a doctor. The doctor went to a village and gave tablets for some small illness. At once there was an uproar across the village: "Some bad doctor has come, he has come to kill us!"

What was the reason? He had not given an injection. In the villagers' understanding, however correct the medicine was, no injection meant a bad doctor.

Then the senior doctor came up with a very simple solution: he gave everyone an injection of **distilled water**. The result? The doctors became good doctors, and the patients got well.

There was no medicine in it, only water. The patient did not know this. His belief was, "If I get an injection, I will be well," and that very belief healed him.

> "Belief heals."

## The Three Pillars of Healing

Acharya Ji says, remember this much. Healing rests on three beliefs:

- **Belief in the doctor**
- **Belief in yourself**
- **Belief in the medicine**

You need not even know the medicine's name, only the feeling, "What I am taking is going to make me well." Without this feeling there is no cure. Most often, it is the patient himself who gives up hope.

*When you take a medicine, is there a feeling in your mind of "I will get well," or of "Let's see, maybe it will work"?*

## Doctors Are Taught This Too

Acharya Ji explains that this science of self-confidence is taught to doctors themselves: develop the placebo effect in your patients. It is part of medical ethics.

An allopathic doctor will of course give allopathic medicine, but the belief must awaken within the patient himself. "I am taking it because you are telling me to." Such a feeling does not have much effect. On the contrary, the poisonous effect comes at once: the effect is negative, not positive.

## Nocebo: The Slow Poison of Disbelief

Nocebo means that even the finest medicine was given, but you made up your mind, "This cannot work." Then that medicine will not work. Gradually its effect diminishes until it is gone.

That is the difference: the same medicine, placebo on one side, nocebo on the other.

## Shraddha: A Complete Science

In our own language this is called **self-confidence**, it is called faith, it is called **shraddha**. According to Acharya Ji, it is a complete science.

People often say, "I take the opinion of four doctors," or "I act after asking four gurus." This simply means there is no faith in the first one.

> "Choose the first one so well that you can surrender to him completely."

**Samarpan** (surrender) does not mean blind trust; it means choosing thoughtfully and then staying with that choice wholeheartedly.

*Is there someone in your life you trust completely, or do you keep looking for a "second opinion" every time?*

## When the Doctor Was "Kaka"

Forty or fifty years ago, not very long ago, every home had a **family doctor**. Nobody used their real name. Not Doctor Sharma, Doctor Verma, Doctor Kulkarni, but "Doctor Kaka has come, Doctor Mama has come, Doctor Chacha has come."

They were like members of the family. They would come home, ask how everyone was, and in the middle of the chat pop two tablets into your mouth, "Here, take this, you are fine." And the patient would actually get well. Because the feeling was: "He has given it, that means I am cured."

## Where Has Faith Gone Today?

Today the patient searches the internet, looks everything up himself and starts doing half of the treatment on his own. There is no complete faith in anyone.

Acharya Ji says this is the problem. That is why the placebo effect, the healing power of faith, keeps decreasing.

> "There is no faith in anyone; that is the problem."

## Key Takeaways

- Behind every healing the **placebo effect** is at work: healing through belief.
- In the **nocebo effect**, disbelief makes even the best medicine ineffective.
- Healing has three pillars: belief in the doctor, in yourself and in the medicine.
- **Shraddha** is a complete science; seeking four opinions shows a lack of faith in the first.
- Choose the first one so well that you can surrender to him completely.
- The era of the family doctor was an era of trust; today's habit of self-treatment has reduced that trust.
