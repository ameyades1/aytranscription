## 1. Summary

This short discourse (video "Who Really Creates Your Life? The Two Causes You Must Know", 8:36) takes the Vedanta pair **upadana karana** (material cause) and **nimitta karana** (efficient cause) and, through the potter-and-pot example, moves body, mind, desire, planning and mood one by one to the material side. In the end the ego's claim "I made it" falls away, and Acharya Ji names Ganapati as the true doer above the ego. The main intent is **informational**: students and seekers looking up "उपादान और निमित्त कारण" / "material and efficient cause", plus an **emotional/life** layer ("who really does my work?", "why can't I work all the time?"). Most of the pages that rank for the Hindi query are dry Nyaya/philosophy-exam notes. A clear, story-led explanation that ends in a practical insight about ego and doership is a real opening, especially in Hindi.

**English:** In Vedanta, every creation needs two causes: the material cause (upadana karana), the stuff a thing is made of, like clay for a pot, and the efficient cause (nimitta karana), the maker, like the potter. Acharya Upendra Ji shows that, looked at closely, the maker's body, mind, desire and mood are material too, and a higher power stands beyond the ego that says "I made it."

## 6. FAQ

1. **उपादान कारण क्या है? / What is the material cause?**
   वह सामग्री जिससे कोई वस्तु बनती है, जैसे घड़े के लिए मिट्टी। / The stuff from which a thing is made, such as clay for a pot.
2. **निमित्त कारण क्या है? / What is the efficient cause?**
   वह चेतन कर्ता जो सामग्री को काम में लाता है, जैसे कुम्हार। / The conscious maker who works with the material, such as the potter.
3. **क्या केवल कुम्हार और मिट्टी से घड़ा बन जाता है? / Are the potter and clay enough to make a pot?**
   नहीं; कुम्हार में घड़ा बनाने की इच्छा भी चाहिए। आचार्य जी कहते हैं, यहीं से वेदांत आरंभ होता है। / No; the potter must also wish to make it. Acharya Ji says this is exactly where Vedanta begins.
4. **"मैंने बनाया" कहना गलत क्यों है? / Why is "I made it" not the whole truth?**
   क्योंकि शरीर, मन, बुद्धि, इच्छा, योजना और मनःस्थिति भी उपादान हैं; अगर सब व्यक्ति के हाथ में होता, तो कुम्हार चौबीसों घंटे घड़े ही बनाता। / Because body, mind, intellect, desire, planning and mood are also material; if everything were in the person's hands, the potter would make pots around the clock.
5. **आचार्य जी के अनुसार असली कर्ता कौन है? / According to Acharya Ji, who is the true doer?**
   आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि अहंकार के ऊपर की उच्चतर स्थितियों में पहला तत्व गणपति है, वही वास्तविक कर्ता बनता है। / Acharya Ji teaches that the first principle above the ego is Lord Ganesha, who becomes the true doer. (Keep this attributed to Acharya Ji; see flag 2.)

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Tools counted as upadana (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major).** Both versions place the wheel, water and tools (hammer, nails) in the material cause and open with "वेदांत कहता है". In the standard Nyaya/Vedanta usage only the clay is upadana; tools such as the wheel and stick are on the nimitta side. Recommendation: apply the review's wording that marks the broader grouping as Acharya Ji's simplification. The meta description, direct answer and FAQ above already use only clay as the example of upadana, so they stay accurate either way.
2. **Ganapati as the true doer (review issue 2, Major).** This is Acharya Ji's teaching from his tradition, not a statement of the Brahma Sutras; in Advaita the Atman is akarta. Do not put "Vedanta says Ganesha is the doer" in any title, meta or snippet. The FAQ answer attributes it to Acharya Ji; apply the review's attribution in the blog body and in the last takeaway as well.
3. **चैतन्य used as an adjective (review issue 3, Minor).** "चैतन्य व्यक्ति" / "chaitanya person" should be चेतन / "chetana", or glossed as "consciousness". Not used in any SEO field.
4. **"The foundational text of Vedanta" (review issue 4, Minor).** The Brahma Sutras are one of the three (prasthanatrayi). Avoid "the foundational text" in any snippet; the English blog gloss should be corrected as the review suggests.
5. **Title vs. content.** The video title "Who Really Creates Your Life?" promises more life guidance than this short piece delivers. The recommended titles stay with the pot example and the two causes, which the article fully delivers. No clickbait used.

- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best
