# Who Makes the Pot: The Secret of Upādāna and Nimitta

Nothing happens by itself. From a simple clay pot to a complex computer program, every creation has causes behind it.

Vedanta says that for any creation to be completed, **two causes** are needed: the **upādāna kāraṇa** and the **nimitta kāraṇa**. At first glance this seems very simple. But within this simple idea hides a deep question of life: *who is really the doer?*

## What Does It Take to Make a Pot?

Suppose you have to make a pot. First you need clay. But will clay alone be enough?

No. You also need water, and that round, spinning wheel. All the things that go into this work together are called the **upādāna kāraṇa** (material cause), the material from which something is made.

Without these things, the pot simply cannot be made.

## And the Potter?

But material alone does not make a pot either. Someone has to turn the wheel; only then does the clay come together. Someone has to mix the water; only then is a paste formed. Someone has to lift off the wet pot and set it down; only then does the sun do the work of drying it.

This living person who does the work, the potter, is the **nimitta kāraṇa** (efficient cause).

The same rule holds in every field. The carpenter has wood, a hammer and nails; the goldsmith has gold, chemicals and a small hammer; the musician has instruments; the one who works on a computer has a computer. On one side the material, on the other side the doer. Acharya Ji says that until both these causes are present, the action will never happen.

## Why Is Such a Simple Idea in the Brahma Sutra?

Acharya ji raises a sharp question. Anyone in the world understands this much. Then why does a great scripture like the **Brahma Sutra** discuss it?

Because a factor is missing here.

All the things are **jaḍa** (inert). They do not come together on their own. A **chaitanya** (conscious) person is needed to gather them. So far so good.

## When the Potter Has No Wish

Now think. The potter is there; clay, water, the wheel, everything is there. But the desire to make a pot never arises in his mind.

Will the pot be made? It will not.

Acharya ji says, **this is where Vedanta begins.**

## The Person Too Is Material

The one we took to be the nimitta, the potter, goldsmith, carpenter or blacksmith, is not the real nimitta either. His **body, mind and intellect** are also upādāna.

A talented person is part of the material too. Acharya Ji explains that something higher must then make him do the work; only then does creation happen.

## Look Beyond "I Made It"

Our **ego** says, "I made it." Acharya ji says, do not stop there. There is some force beyond it; understand that first.

If everything were in the person's hands alone, the potter would keep making pots twenty-four hours a day. The goldsmith would keep working gold without pause. The doctor would keep healing patients round the clock.

But that does not happen. Because desire, planning, all of it also falls on the list of upādāna.

*In whatever you do, how much is "I" and how much is something else?*

## The List Keeps Growing

Vedanta places things on the side of upādāna one by one.

- **The body:** if the potter dies, his body lies there, but no pot is made. So the body too is upādāna.
- **The state of mind:** work happens only when the mood is right. This is a changing element; it too is upādāna.

On the other side, the side of nimitta, very little now remains.

## The Real Doer

Proceeding this way, the seeker comes to understand that he himself is the **Atman**. And among the higher states above the ego, the first principle that comes is **Ganapati**. He becomes the real doer.

Everything else goes into upādāna, and he becomes the nimitta. In Acharya ji's words, we have come "one step a little higher".

## Key Takeaways

- Every action needs two causes: **upādāna** (the material) and **nimitta** (the doer).
- If either one is missing, the action is not completed.
- Material is inert; consciousness is needed to bring it together.
- Without desire, nothing is made even when the doer is present; this is where Vedanta begins.
- A person's body, mind, intellect, desire, planning and state of mind are all upādāna.
- Beyond the ego that says "I made it" there is a higher power, which is the real doer.
