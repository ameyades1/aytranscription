# Chapter: The Two Causes Behind Every Action

## Two Causes Behind Every Creation

Whatever is to be created, whatever task is to be completed, two causes are needed. One is the **upādāna kāraṇa** (material cause) and the other is the **nimitta kāraṇa** (efficient cause). Which two? Upādāna and nimitta.

## The Example of the Pot: The Material Cause

Suppose I have to make a pot. I will need clay. But will clay alone do? No, all the materials will be needed. Clay will be needed, water will be needed, and that round, spinning wheel will be needed too. Whatever goes into this work is all upādāna. That is one cause.

Do you follow? All these materials are what we call the **material cause**. Now tell me, without all these things, without the material cause, can your pot be made? It cannot.

## The Potter: The Efficient Cause

But who is the nimitta of this pot? The potter. Only if he is there will the pot be made. Only then will the wheel turn, only then will the clay gather in one place, only then will the water mix in and form a paste. Only then will he lift the wet pot off and set it somewhere, and only then will the sun do its work of drying out all the water.

So is a living being needed here or not? This is called the **efficient cause**.

## The Same Rule in Every Field

In the same way, in woodwork there is the wood, the hammer, the nails and so on on one side, and the carpenter on the other. Agreed? On one side the gold and all its materials, the chemicals, the small hammer and so on; and on the other side the goldsmith.

Do you see what I want to say? One is the material cause and the other is the efficient cause. Until both these causes are present, the final action will never be completed. I may be the efficient cause, but if the material is not there, the action still will not happen.

Now let us see, according to Vedanta, what is to be called upādāna and what nimitta. Let us go a little further. All these materials that we see, were they the material cause or not? Certainly. The materials are upādāna and the doer is nimitta.

Now let us analyse this further. Whether the material cause belongs to the goldsmith, the carpenter, the blacksmith or the potter's pot; the one who works on a computer will also have a computer, only then will a program be made and a result come. This rule applies in every field. If there is a musician, instruments will be needed for music to come forth, and everything else; and will the person himself be there or not? He is the nimitta.

## Then Why Do the Scriptures Discuss It?

This much not only we, even a madman understands. Anyone in the world will understand this much. If the matter were only this, why would a great scripture like the **Brahma Sutra** discuss it? Because some factor is missing.

The point is that all the things are there, but they were inert (jaḍa). The one who gathers them all and brings them to one place will be a nimitta person. A goldsmith is needed, or a carpenter, or a blacksmith, or a potter. Only then will he bring his inert material cause together. The things will not come on their own; he brings them. Agreed? This living one, the one with consciousness (chaitanya), will bring them. Fine, no problem with that.

## When There Is No Desire

Now the question arises. The potter is there, and the clay, the water, the wheel, everything is there, but there is no desire in his mind. Despite being the efficient cause, if the desire to make a pot never arises in him, will the pot be made? Think about it a little. It will not, will it? That is why **this is where Vedanta begins.**

## The Person Too Is Only Material

The one who seemed to you to be the nimitta, the person, the goldsmith, blacksmith, potter or carpenter, even he cannot be the actual nimitta. **His body, mind and intellect are also part of the material cause.** They are all on this side, and above them there is something which can be the nimitta. Is this much clear?

Among these very materials, a talented person must also come in. And some higher power must make him do it; only then will it be made.

> "Something higher should make him do it; only then will it be made."

## The Force Beyond "I Made It"

That is why it is said: do not stop your understanding at the part of the ego that says "I made it". There is some force beyond that; understand that first.

Otherwise the potter should go on making pots continuously, twenty-four hours a day. If he is a goldsmith, he should go on working gold continuously. Do you follow or not? The doctor should go on healing patients twenty-four hours a day.

Here the subject is his desire. His planning, all of this, Vedanta places this too in the material cause. First let it be clear up to here, then we can go further.

## The List of Material Causes Grows

Many factors will go to this side, the side of upādāna. Very little now remains on that side.

His being there is also essential. The potter is there, but he has died; only a dead body lies there, so what use is it? That means the potter's body too has come into the material cause. The way he used to work before, he no longer does. So that too has gone to this side, into upādāna, hasn't it? His body being there is also an essential element. Are you understanding little by little?

Then his state of mind must also be right. Only when the mood is right will the pot be made. This is a variable component; it too will go into upādāna. Without it, the work does not happen.

## Towards the Real Doer

In this way, step by step, Vedanta leads us on. Then the seeker understands that he himself is the Atman. And in the highest state, among the states above his ego, the first principle that comes is **Ganapati**; he alone will become the real doer to that extent.

All this will go into upādāna, and that will become the nimitta.

> "All this will go into upādāna and this will become the nimitta."

Is this a little clearer now? We have climbed one step higher. Understood?
