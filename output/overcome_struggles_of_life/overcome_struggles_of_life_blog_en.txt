# What Comes Will Also Go: Overcoming Life's Struggles Through the Gita

The doctor said, "There is no remedy for this." And we became afraid. Then we sat with that illness for the rest of our life.

Sometimes a relationship breaks, there is a quarrel with someone, and we carry that quarrel for lifetimes. Sometimes it is a lack of money, poverty, no job, and a whole life passes just like that.

So the real question is: **why does this situation not change?**

## What Is the Gita's First Teaching?

To find the answer, we have to open the Bhagavad Gita, Chapter Two, verse fourteen. This is where Shri Krishna gives Arjuna his first teaching.

He says, the situation you are in has come partly from **prarabdha** (the fruit of past actions); there must have been such karmas, there must have been such **samskaras** (impressions left on the mind).

> **mātrā-sparśās tu kaunteya śītoṣṇa-sukha-duḥkha-dāḥ**
> **āgamāpāyino 'nityās tāṁs titikṣasva bhārata**
> "O son of Kunti, the contacts of the senses with their objects, which give cold and heat, pleasure and pain, come and go and are impermanent; endure them, O Bharata."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.14)

O son of Kunti, the contacts of the senses and their objects that give cold and heat, pleasure and pain, keep coming and going; they are impermanent. O Bharata, endure them.

## Dvandva: The Pairs of Opposites in Life

Cold and heat are **dvandva** (pairs of opposites) that come at the level of the body. Pleasure and pain are pairs of opposites that come at the level of the mind.

These are what we call favourable and unfavourable circumstances. These ups and downs will keep coming in life.

Where do they come from? From the meeting of our senses with outer objects. If there is a tongue, there is food, whether idli-sambar or anything else. If there are ears, there is music, there are words. Sound, touch, form, relationships, the five great elements, all come into contact, and from that contact either pleasure or pain is born.

But Shri Krishna says two things about them: they are **subject to arising and perishing**, and they are **anitya** (impermanent).

## Then How Can Illness Be Permanent?

Here the teacher puts a direct question. How can a doctor say that cancer is permanent? Or that once diabetes comes, it will stay for life? When Shri Krishna himself says that it is impermanent. Acharya Ji says that nothing is permanent, the only constant is change, and change must happen. The same applies to poverty. A situation that has arisen must also perish; not you, but that situation should be destroyed.

*Which situation in your life are you living with as if it were "permanent"?*

But keep one thing in mind: that situation will not go on its own. Something has to be done for it.

## Endurance: The First Step, Not the Destination

Shri Krishna's first teaching is **titiksha**, that is, endurance. But is this the final teaching?

Just look. In the first chapter, only Arjuna speaks. In the second chapter too, up to the tenth verse, only Arjuna speaks. Shri Krishna's words begin from the eleventh verse, and this first point comes in the fourteenth verse itself.

The problem is that a person cannot even endure properly. And Shri Krishna never said, "Keep enduring until your whole life is spent." After endurance, the whole journey up to the eighteenth chapter remains.

## The Gita's Journey Through Eighteen Chapters

After endurance, the Gita lays out an entire process:

- **Chapter three, Karma Yoga:** some new action has to be done. Not only action, but yoga, that is, something has to be joined.
- **Chapter four, Yajna:** the word yajna comes from the root "yaj", which means to sacrifice, to renounce. It is not only an outer ritual.
- **Chapter five:** the talk of bhakti.
- **Chapter six:** how sadhana is to be done, the state of meditation.
- **Chapters seven to twelve:** the process of knowing "how I myself am that Brahman"; the eleventh chapter describes the cosmic form.
- **Chapters thirteen to eighteen:** how it all comes about, and what the three gunas are.

In this way all four yogas, karma, bhakti, jnana and dhyana, are explained.

*If the first teaching were everything, why would Shri Krishna tell Arjuna such a long process?*

## Summary: Key Takeaways

- No hard situation, whether illness, a broken relationship or poverty, is permanent.
- Pleasure and pain are born from the contact of the senses with their objects, and they are impermanent (Gita 2.14).
- A situation that has arisen must perish; not you, but the situation.
- The situation will not change on its own; something has to be done for it.
- Endurance is the Gita's first teaching, not its last.
- After it comes the whole journey of action, yajna, devotion, meditation and the knowledge of Brahman.
