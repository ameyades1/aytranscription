# When to Let Go of the Staff of Karma

## A Game That Explains All of Life

Have you ever watched a pole vault? The athlete runs with a long pole, plants it in the ground and leaps over the bar fixed high above.

But notice: while crossing, he does not take the pole along. He does not hold on to it like a sword. He has to let go of the pole, and only then does he reach the other side.

And yet, without that pole he could never have got there.

## The Pole Is Karma Yoga, the Summit Is Jnana Yoga

This pole is **Karma Yoga** (action performed without selfish motive, the Path of Action). And where you must reach is **Jnana Yoga** (the path of Self-knowledge, the Path of Knowledge), where **jnana nishtha** (steadfastness in knowledge) becomes firm.

Ultimately, that is where one must reach before death. But without action no one gets there.

> "Without it he cannot even reach that end."

That is why leaving action and going straight to the Himalayas, as Arjuna proposes, is simply not possible. Devotion, knowledge and meditation were all spoken of, yet the answer remained: you will have to act. And that the pole must be dropped afterwards was also said later on.

*Do you too sometimes think of leaving everything and running away, without completing the very action that is meant to carry you forward?*

## Will All of Life Be Spent Only in Work?

The opposite mistake is just as big. "I will do only action my whole life" will not work either.

Yes, for the major part of life action will carry on. Just as the athlete runs in from far away and the pole travels with him. Then the pole plants, he rises on its support, and then he lets it go.

The purpose of Karma Yoga is to take you **one step further**. In the end, the whole world has to be dropped.

## Set a Limit for Yourself

No one knows when death will come. Who has seen tomorrow? So set a target for yourself: at least for these many years I will work.

It is not necessary that the target will be achieved:

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> "Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

Your right is only to action, never to the fruit. But some limit there must be. "I will work my whole life" simply cannot happen.

## The Chain Experiment: Feel Old Age Today

Young people have great enthusiasm. For them, Acharya ji has an unusual suggestion.

Buy a small chain from the market, like the ones on prisoners' feet in jail. Tie it between your two feet. Bend halfway down, and tie another chain from your neck to your feet. Now try walking.

An old person's gait is exactly like this. The foot moves only a little forward; one has to walk stooped. Even if you want to, you cannot do anything.

> "Feel that state first, then you will understand: will I be able to act my whole life?"

At that age you will have only two things: **contemplation** or **worry**. And that day will surely come. Has anyone guaranteed that you will not stoop?

*The way you are living today, will you have contemplation at that age, or worry?*

## The Arithmetic of Old Age: Less Income, More Expense

Since we are speaking of Karma Yoga, wealth will also come up. Earning wealth is also one aim of life.

Today you are working actively, so there is money. You earned it and blew it. But in old age the source of income will close, or shrink to a pension.

And expenses will rise. If life passed without Karma Yoga, in anger and rush, the result will be illnesses, and their costs too.

On top of that, if you stayed alone in the zeal of your own work and did not share much love, there will be no one to support you either. A servant will have to be kept, and more money goes into that.

That is why this plan should be made in youth itself: until when will I be able to work?

## Who Is a Karma Yogi?

We all pray to remain happy and healthy until the end of life. But in old age the strength of youth cannot return.

One day I should retire on my own, rather than the government or nature forcing me to.

> "The person who works accordingly is called a Karma Yogi."

His vision is clear: ten years of work, and after that this work will happen. Nadi astrology may say there will be work till the end, but he has his own plan.

## Guru Mauli and the Successor

One who has become a guru, a **Guru Mauli** (a guru like a mother), comes to have many children. The mother cannot simply leave them and go.

That is why it was said that your dream will be fulfilled when you appoint someone equal to yourself. Otherwise, if the guru departs suddenly, the disciples become like orphans.

## Vivekananda's Question, Ramakrishna's Answer

Acharya ji himself has been asking for six years: when is retirement? Then Swami Vivekananda came to mind.

Vivekananda too had told his guru, "Now I have received all the knowledge, now I will go." Sri Ramakrishna replied, "Will you become so selfish? You have attained knowledge; will you not give it to people?"

In that moment he understood and began to teach. At the end he had thirty-one illnesses, yet he worked for the world.

So do such work. But after a limit, you must also stop.

## "It'll Do" Doesn't Do

Went to America for a job? Then you must think about when to return to India, or whether to stay there permanently. "If it goes, it goes," without any plan, does not work.

A Karma Yogi has a **definite end point** in mind. It is not a wish to die; it is a **sankalpa** (resolve), an **iccha shakti** (willpower): I will work up to here, and after that I will live in peace. And for that one must gain the right knowledge and perform the right action.

*What is the end point of your work? Have you ever decided it?*

## Key Takeaways

- Karma Yoga is like the vaulter's pole: without it reaching Jnana Yoga is impossible, yet to arrive you must let it go.
- Renunciation without first acting is not possible, and only acting all your life will not work either.
- Set a limit in years for your work, without anxiety over the fruit.
- Feel old age today: at that age there will be only contemplation or worry.
- Income will fall and expenses will rise, so prepare both wealth and love in youth.
- A guru's duty is to prepare a successor equal to himself.
- The Karma Yogi's end point is a resolve, which needs both knowledge and action.
