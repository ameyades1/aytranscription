# When Should You Let Go of the Pole of Action?

## A Sport That Explains All of Life

Have you ever watched the pole vault? The athlete sprints with a long pole, plants it in the ground and springs over the bar set high above.

But notice this: the vaulter does not carry the pole over the bar. They do not cling to it like a sword. They have to let it go, and only then do they reach the other side.

And yet, without that pole, they could never have risen so high.

## The Pole Is Karma Yoga, the Summit Is Jnana Yoga

The pole is **Karma Yoga** (the path of action performed without desire for its fruits). The place you are trying to reach is **Jnana Yoga** (the path of Self-knowledge), where **jnana-nishtha** (steadfast abiding in knowledge) becomes firm.

That is where you must finally arrive before death comes. But Acharya Ji says no one can reach that end without action.

This is why abandoning your work and heading straight for the Himalayas, as Arjuna wanted to do, is simply not possible. Devotion, knowledge and meditation were all discussed, and still the answer stayed the same: you have to act. The teaching then goes further and says that after acting, you must also let go of the pole.

*Do you ever dream of dropping everything and running away, before finishing the very work that is meant to carry you forward?*

## Will Your Whole Life Go Into Work?

The opposite mistake is just as serious. Saying "I will do nothing but work for the rest of my life" is no answer either.

Yes, action will fill most of your life, just as the athlete runs a long way with the pole in hand. Then the pole is planted, it lifts them upward, and then they release it.

The purpose of Karma Yoga is to carry you **one step further**. In the end, you will have to leave the whole world behind.

## Set a Limit for Yourself

No one knows when death will come. Who has seen tomorrow? So set yourself a goal: "I will work for at least this many years."

There is no guarantee that you will reach this goal, as the Gita reminds us:

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> "Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

Still, you need some limit. Working your entire life away is not possible.

## The Chain Experiment: Feel Old Age Today

The young are full of energy, and Acharya Ji has an unusual suggestion for them.

Buy a short chain from the market, like the ones prisoners wear on their feet, and tie it between your ankles. Bend halfway forward, and fasten another chain from your neck to your feet. Now try to walk.

This is exactly how an old person moves. Each foot moves forward only a little, and you have to walk stooped. However much you want to, you cannot do much. Acharya Ji says you should experience that state first, and then you will understand whether you can really keep working all your life.

At that age you will have only two things left: **reflection** or **worry**. That day is bound to come. Has anyone promised you that your back will never bend?

*The way you live today, will it leave you with reflection or with worry in old age?*

## The Arithmetic of Old Age: Less Income, More Expense

When we talk about Karma Yoga, money comes into the picture too. Earning wealth is also one of life's goals.

Today you are actively working, so you have money. You earn it and you spend it freely. But in old age your income will stop, or shrink to a pension.

Your expenses, meanwhile, will rise. If you spent your life without Karma Yoga, rushing about in anger, illness will follow, and so will its bills.

On top of that, if you were absorbed in your own ambitions and did not share much love, there will be no one to support you. You will have to hire someone to care for you, and that too will cost money.

This is why you need to plan while you are young: how long will I be able to work?

## Who Is a Karma Yogi?

We all pray to stay happy and healthy until the end of life. But in old age the strength of youth cannot return.

One day you should step back of your own accord, before the government or nature forces you to. Acharya Ji explains that a person who works with this in mind is called a Karma Yogi.

Their vision is clear: "I will work for ten years, and after that I will turn to this." **Nadi Jyotish** (an ancient system of Indian astrology read from palm-leaf manuscripts) may predict that they will keep working until the end, but they have a plan of their own.

## The Guru as Mother, and a Successor

Someone who becomes a Guru, a **Guru Mauli** (a Guru who is like a mother), comes to have many children. A mother cannot simply walk away from them.

This is why it is said that your dream is fulfilled only when you appoint someone like yourself to carry on. Otherwise, if the Guru leaves suddenly, the disciples are left like orphans.

## Vivekananda's Question, Ramakrishna's Answer

For six years, Acharya Ji himself has been asking: when will I step back? That question brought Swami Vivekananda to mind.

Vivekananda, too, once told his Guru, "I have received all the knowledge now, so I will go." Shri Ramakrishna replied, "Will you be so selfish? You have attained knowledge, and you will not share it with people?"

He understood in that moment and began to teach. By the end of his life he suffered from thirty-one illnesses, yet he kept working for the world.

Act like that. But beyond a certain point, you also have to stop.

## Drifting Along Does Not Work

Did you go to America for a job? Then you need to decide when you will return to India, or whether you will settle there for good. Drifting along without a plan, telling yourself "it's fine as it is", does not work.

A Karma Yogi holds a **clear end point** in mind. This is not a wish to die. It is a **sankalpa** (a firm resolve), an act of **willpower**: "I will work up to here, and after that I will live in peace." To get there, you need the right knowledge and the right practice.

*What is the end point of your work? Have you ever actually decided it?*

## Key Takeaways

- Karma Yoga is like the vaulter's pole: you cannot reach Jnana Yoga without it, and you must release it to get there.
- You cannot leap straight into renunciation by abandoning action, and a lifetime of nothing but work is not the answer either.
- Set a limit of years for your work, without worrying about the fruits.
- Feel old age today: at that age you will have only reflection or worry.
- Income will fall and expenses will rise, so build up both wealth and love while you are young.
- A Guru has a duty to prepare a successor like themselves.
- A Karma Yogi's end point is a resolve, and reaching it takes both knowledge and practice.
