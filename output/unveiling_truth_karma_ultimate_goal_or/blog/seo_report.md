## 1. Summary

Through the image of a pole vault, Acharya Ji teaches that Karma Yoga is the pole that lifts a seeker toward Jnana Yoga (ज्ञाननिष्ठा), and that the pole must be released at the top: neither escaping action early nor working until death is the answer. A Karma Yogi sets a clear end point for their work, plans for old age (income, health, love), and, if a Guru, prepares a successor. The main intent is informational ("who is a karma yogi", "is karma yoga the ultimate goal", "karma yoga vs jnana yoga"), with an emotional/life-planning layer (fear of old age, when to step back from work). The opportunity: most pages on "karma yogi" give a dictionary definition or a list of Gita verses; this talk answers the less-served question of where Karma Yoga leads and when to let it go, with a memorable image and practical advice. The video title ("Karma Yog: Ultimate Goal or Just the Beginning?") supports a question-style secondary target.

## 6. FAQ

**Hindi**
1. **कर्मयोगी कौन है?** जो कर्म करते हुए यह स्पष्ट रखता है कि एक दिन उसे स्वयं निवृत्त होना है, सरकार या प्रकृति के बाध्य करने से पहले। उसकी अपनी योजना होती है: इतने वर्ष काम, फिर शांति।
2. **क्या कर्मयोग अंतिम लक्ष्य है?** नहीं। आचार्य जी के अनुसार कर्मयोग बाँस-कूद के बाँस की तरह है: उसके बिना ज्ञानयोग तक नहीं पहुँच सकते, पर पहुँचने के लिए उसे छोड़ना भी होता है।
3. **क्या कर्म छोड़कर सीधे वैराग्य ले सकते हैं?** नहीं। कर्म छोड़कर सीधे हिमालय चले जाना संभव नहीं; पहले कर्म करना ही होगा।
4. **कर्म की सीमा क्यों तय करें?** क्योंकि बुढ़ापे में सामर्थ्य लौटता नहीं, आय घटती है और खर्च बढ़ता है। उस उम्र में केवल चिंतन या चिंता बचेगी, इसलिए योजना युवावस्था में ही बनानी चाहिए।

**English**
1. **Who is a Karma Yogi?** Someone who works with the clear understanding that one day they must step back of their own accord, before the government or nature forces them to. They have their own plan: work for so many years, then live in peace.
2. **Is Karma Yoga the ultimate goal?** No. Acharya Ji compares it to a vaulter's pole: you cannot reach Jnana Yoga without it, but you must release it to get there.
3. **Can I renounce action and go straight to renunciation?** No. Abandoning your work and heading for the Himalayas is not possible; you have to act first.
4. **Why set a limit on how long you work?** Because the strength of youth does not return, income falls and expenses rise in old age. At that age only reflection or worry remains, so the plan must be made while you are young.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Arjuna and the Himalayas** (from the Sanskrit review): the body says "जैसा अर्जुन कहता है" / "as Arjuna wanted to do" about going to the Himalayas; in the Gita Arjuna speaks of living on alms (2.5) and refusing to fight (2.9), not the Himalayas. Recommendation: use the review's rewording. Keep this line out of titles, meta and snippets (it is).
2. **Gita 2.47 is only half quoted** (review): mark the reference as "२.४७, पूर्वार्ध" / "2.47, first half". The secondary keyword "Bhagavad Gita 2.47 meaning" is only supporting; do not promise a full commentary on the verse in any title.
3. **Gita bridge only in English** (review): English adds "as the Gita reminds us"; align the Hindi as suggested in the review.
4. **Vivekananda and Ramakrishna** (review): the quoted exchange is a paraphrase, "in that moment" compresses years, and "thirty-one illnesses" is unverified. Recommendation: use the review's rewording; do not use this anecdote in the meta description, FAQ or social snippets until it is checked.
5. **Nadi Jyotish gloss** (review): English says "ancient"; use the neutral gloss suggested. Also note that the Foundation offers Naadi Jyotish as a service: the post should not be used to promote it, and the passage says the Karma Yogi follows their own plan regardless of a prediction.
6. **Hindi H1 "लाठी" vs body "बाँस"** (review): the title tag already uses बाँस; change the H1 to match if the editor agrees.
7. **"Ultimate goal" framing:** the video title asks whether Karma Yoga is the ultimate goal. The blog's answer is no (it leads to Jnana Yoga). Titles and the secondary keyword keep this as a question; never phrase a title as "Karma Yoga Is the Ultimate Goal". The sequential reading (Karma Yoga then Jnana Yoga) is Acharya Ji's teaching, in line with Shankara's reading of Gita 3.4 and 6.3; Gita 3.3 also presents them as two paths, so do not present the sequence as a verse.
8. **Old age and money:** the chain experiment and the income/expense passage could be used for fear-based hooks ("You will end up alone and sick"). Keep snippets on planning and reflection (चिंतन), not on fear (चिंता); no health or financial promises.
