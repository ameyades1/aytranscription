# Was Ram Rajya Federal or Unitary?

We all dream of **Ram Rajya** (the ideal reign of Shri Rama). But have you ever asked whether the way our country is governed brings that dream closer or keeps it out of reach?

This discourse puts a simple and startling question on the table: can a federal government ever bring about Ram Rajya?

So was Ram Rajya federal or unitary? According to Acharya Ji, it was completely unitary: a single hand at the helm and a single voice. In a federal system the centre and the states may hold opposing views, so it cannot bring about Ram Rajya. The formula is surrender to the word of the centre, with each state free to serve its own people in its own language.

## What Is a Federal Government?

Our country has a **federal system** of government. This means it runs on more than one government: a central government and a state government.

When power rests in a single centre, we call it a **unitary system**.

Acharya Ji says that although we dream of Ram Rajya, a federal government can never deliver it.

## What Was Ram Rajya Like? History's Great Kingdoms

Shri Rama's kingdom was entirely unitary. So was the empire of Chandragupta Maurya. Foreign travellers described the rule of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj as not just unitary but **completely unitary**.

This means a single hand at the helm. Wherever that hand reaches, people hear one voice.

A federal system offers no such guarantee. One party may run the centre and another may run a state. The party that sits in opposition at the centre may well govern a state. Can a country really function well this way?

## One Organisation, Many Branches

A simple example makes this clear. Suppose the Antar Yog Foundation has its headquarters in Mumbai. That is its "central government". Its branches in states such as Rajasthan are like the "state governments" there.

The Guru cannot sit in every place himself, but his instructions can reach everywhere. A local head, secretary or president runs each branch.

Now imagine the headquarters teaching principles such as **Purusha** (the conscious Self), **Prakriti** (nature, or primordial matter) and the personal form of God, while the Rajasthan branch starts teaching from some other tradition altogether. What happens then? And what if a third branch announces that it will teach in its own way?

Picture Swami Vivekananda's branches teaching his message in one place, another religion in a second and something else again in a third. Would that work?

## Dosa at a Pizza Company!

Set spirituality aside and think about business. Take a pizza company with many branches. One branch is making **thalipeeth** (a Maharashtrian multigrain flatbread), another is making dosa and a third is making pizza.

The poor headquarters says, "Make pizza!" The branch replies, "No, we are in the opposition, so we will sell dosa." The burger chain protests, "My company sells burgers!" and its branch answers, "No, only **vada** (a savoury South Indian fritter) will sell here."

Acharya Ji explains that if you give freedom to two governments whose views may clash, the system cannot work.

*If every member of your family or organisation set their own direction, how far could it go?*

## Then Why Have State Governments at All?

Even Shri Rama could not be present in every kingdom himself. He needed a minister or soldier in each place who would govern according to his wishes.

So what role does a local government play? Whatever the headquarters teaches in Mumbai should be taught in Kolkata too. The head there may teach it in **Bengali**, though, and may give local people priority on the service team, because he understands the people of that place better.

But can he invent a new teaching of his own? Can he oppose the original one? No.

## The Khichdi of Our System

Today the village **sarpanch** (elected head of a village council) fights one battle, the mayor another, the chief minister another, and the nation yet another. A friend at one level is an enemy at the next, and an enemy there is a friend here.

Everything has turned into a **khichdi** (a dish of rice and lentils cooked together, used here for a muddled mix). This is the flaw where we keep falling short.

## How Can We Bring Ram Rajya? Surrender and Local Service

To bring about Ram Rajya, we need a **completely unitary system**. Everyone should listen to the person at the centre and follow his word exactly as given, without changing it in the slightest. The surrender must run that deep.

Here **sharanagati** (surrender) means complete self-offering: accepting the guide's word without altering it to suit yourself.

At the same time, each state is free to uplift its own people in its own language and by its own priorities. It has full permission to do so.

*Can you accept guidance in your own life without "editing" it to suit yourself?*

## Key Takeaways

- A federal government means more than one government, and their views may clash.
- The kingdoms of Shri Rama, Chandragupta Maurya and Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj were completely unitary.
- The examples of the organisation and the pizza company show that branches in conflict cannot run anything together.
- A local unit can be free in language, service and local employment, but not in the core teaching.
- The formula for Ram Rajya: surrender to the centre, and serve your own people in your own language.
