# Why a Mother's Bill Always Comes to Zero

Most of us love, yet somewhere inside we keep accounts. "I did so much for you. What did I get back?" The moment that ledger opens, love slowly begins to turn into control and expectation.

In this short discourse, Acharya Ji uses the example of a mother to show what love without accounting looks like, and why it is the highest spiritual state of all.

## Act, and Let Go of Your Demand for Results

The discourse opens with a plain teaching: do your work, and let go of your insistence on the fruit. Alongside it comes one of the founding lines of our tradition:

> **mātṛdevo bhava**
> **pitṛdevo bhava**
> **ācāryadevo bhava**
> "Regard your mother as God, regard your father as God, regard your teacher as God."
> (Taittiriya Upanishad, Shiksha Valli 1.11.2)

Why does the tradition call a mother divine? Because she lives **phala tyaga** (acting without desire for the outcome) quite naturally. Acharya Ji asks how much a mother charges for everything she does for her son.

## A Son Sends His Bill

There is a well-known story. A boy writes his mother a letter and puts a price on every chore. Bringing groceries from the market: twenty-five rupees. This task: twenty-five rupees. That one: fifty rupees. Hanging the clothes out to dry every day: twenty-five rupees.

His mother replies with a bill of her own. Raising you from a baby into a grown boy: zero. Nursing you: zero. Taking you to the hospital when you were sick: zero. Coming to your rescue when your friends scolded you and beat you: zero. Getting you vaccinated: zero.

*In your relationships, which bill do you write: the son's or the mother's?*

## Why Doesn't a Mother Ask for Anything in Return?

Why did the mother put zero against everything? Because she has no wish for a return at all. She wants nothing back because she feels that her child is not separate from her. The child is a part of her, a piece of her own being.

When a baby is born, the mother knows nothing about it. She does not know whether the child is dark or fair, beautiful or plain, or whether it has some physical defect. She cannot know whether this very child will one day turn against her. Still, love comes at once, because "this is a part of me."

In that moment her **matribhava** (the feeling and love of a mother) awakens, and with it her **heart chakra**, the centre of love and compassion. Just a day earlier, the same woman may have been living in the awareness of the lower chakras. Acharya Ji stresses the point: at birth a mother has no idea whether this child will grow up to be her enemy, and yet she loves it instantly, because it is a part of her.

## The College Friend Who Changed Overnight

Acharya Ji recalls an incident from his college days. He studied with a carefree, tomboyish girl who used to say she disliked small children and would slap any that came near her.

When someone asked what she would do with a child of her own, she said she would never put up with things the way her mother had. If the child disobeyed, it would get a slap.

Then she married and had a baby, and within a day she was a completely different person. The whole picture had changed. Why? Because nature, by its very design, awakens a mother's love and her heart chakra in that instant, at least towards her own child.

## When Expectation Creeps In

The fruit does come. Give your children good **samskaras** (deep impressions and values), and your son will be the support of your old age, and your daughter too will do everything for you. Yet the scriptures say that the day a mother starts expecting, she is no longer a good mother.

This is the fine line where love begins to turn into control. A true mother simply keeps giving, and Acharya Ji says that this is the highest state.

*Is some expectation quietly sitting somewhere inside your love?*

## From One Child's Mother to Mother of the World

Meditation, knowledge and **sadhana** (spiritual practice) all aim at reaching exactly this state. But what knowledge did a mother ever acquire? What meditation did she sit for? She just started doing it.

She did it for one child. Now extend that same way of being, little by little, to the whole world: to your neighbour, even to your enemy's son, everywhere. Acharya Ji says that whoever gradually treats the whole world this way becomes **Jaganmata** (the Mother of the whole universe) as well.

For now, we live without keeping score only for our own people. Sadhana is the work of widening that circle.

## Key Takeaways

- Act, and let go of your demand for results; this is the highest state.
- A mother bills zero for every service, because she wants nothing in return.
- Love flows naturally when you feel your child is part of you; in that moment matribhava and the heart chakra awaken.
- The day a mother begins to expect, she stops being a good mother.
- Meditation and knowledge aim at reaching this selfless state.
- Extend the feeling you have for one to the whole world, and you become Jaganmata.
