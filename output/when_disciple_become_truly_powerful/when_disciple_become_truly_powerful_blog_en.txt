# We Have a Guru, So Is Our Work Done?

Many of us assume that once we have found a Guru, he will handle everything. He will take our karma, give us shakti, and fulfil every wish in our life.

But is that really so? This discourse explains the Guru-disciple relationship through a very simple, direct example: the relationship between parents and children.

## The Guru Is a Guide, Not a Crutch

The Guru is a **mārgadarśaka** (guide). Taking on the disciple's karma is not his duty. He takes only as much as the **ṛṇānubandha** (karmic bond) from the last birth allows.

Only when he feels you are a **supātra** (worthy vessel) does he take something of yours. It is grace, not a contract.

> "Giving shaktipat is not his job either. That is the power within you."

**Shaktipat** means the transmission of spiritual energy from Guru to disciple. But the Guru is under no compulsion to give it. That power is already within you. That is why the Guru says, "Do sadhana."

## A Ninety-Year-Old Father and a Seventy-Year-Old Son

Parents raise a child, earning to feed him. Very good. But until when?

If you are ninety and your son is seventy or sixty-five, will you still be earning for him? Every parent wants strength to arise in the child, for him to earn himself and to be a support in old age.

So why should the same not apply to the Guru and the disciple?

> "How many times shaktipat? Why do you not become powerful yourself?"

Taught once, taught twice in a camp. Two sadhanas done, four done. And still, twenty-five years later, the same prayer: "Gurudev, give me a car, a horse, a bungalow." Will that do?

The one who understands this has gained intelligence.

*Am I growing through my sadhana, or am I still only asking?*

## Viveka: What Is the Real Distinction?

After intelligence comes **viveka buddhi**. Viveka means discrimination. But not between black and white, high and low, or the four varnas.

It is the distinction between **ātmā** (the Self) and **anātmā** (the not-Self). Mind, intellect and ego are all **jaḍa** (inert). The Self is **cetana** (conscious).

Consider a simple experiment. Someone shouted at you. Who shouted, his body or something unseen within him? And whom did he insult, your body, your mind, or you, that is, the Self?

*The next time someone is angry with you, ask: who got hurt, me or my mind?*

**Anātmā** means that which is not the Self. One who has learned this distinction has viveka buddhi. And one who finds bliss in this discernment is named **Vivekananda**.

The deity of viveka buddhi is **Ganapati**. It is a very high state.

## First, Subtle Intelligence

Before viveka, **sūkṣma buddhi** (subtle intelligence) should come, that is, the understanding of what is important. How much trouble should I give the Guru, and how much not?

My work is to make **yajña, dāna and tapas** (sacrifice, charity and austerity) my daily duty while staying aware, and in the way the scriptures prescribe.

> **yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma na tyājyaṁ kāryam eva tat**
> "Acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity are not to be given up; they must certainly be performed."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.5)

After this, my karma, knowledge, memory and **apohana** (forgetting, removal) are in the Guru's hands.

> **mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṁ ca**
> "From Me come memory, knowledge and their removal."
> (Bhagavad Gita 15.15)

## Why Is the Guru Above God?

Even God has no wish to take. The Guru too has no desire to take, yet he takes.

That is why in every religion the Guru's place is said to be above God, because he can take.

## A Sixty-Four-Year-Old Son and a Mother's Pride

How many mothers say with pride, "I do everything for my son. He is sixty-four, and still I cook his food and give him money. He is useless, he does no work."

Would any mother truly like this statement?

Support until fifteen, sixteen, eighteen is fine. But even then, one thing after another should gradually reduce. Money is also a strength. If you alone keep giving it forever, what will become of him after you are gone?

> "The Guru too should have the viveka buddhi to know what kind of person to make the disciple."

## When Ganapati Awakens in Both

The Guru should have the discernment of how to shape the disciple. And the disciple should understand how far to remain **dependent**.

When both reach this state, know that Ganapati has awakened in both.

Such stories are in the Ganesha Purana, and the **Ganesha Gita** comes within it. This discourse is the essence of that Ganesha Gita.

## The Atharvashirsha a Thousand Times, but Why?

Why the Ganesha Atharvashirsha a thousand times? There is no benefit in repeating the name of something imaginary a thousand times.

So why a thousand names? To help someone else? The answer is given right there: there is no other here at all.

*When I chant, for whom do I chant, and whom am I seeking?*

## Key Takeaways

- The Guru is a guide; taking karma or giving shaktipat is not an obligation for him.
- The real power is within you, and sadhana is the way to awaken it.
- Just as parents want their child to become self-reliant, the Guru wants the disciple to become powerful.
- Viveka buddhi is the distinction between the Self and the not-Self; its deity is Ganapati.
- Yajña, dāna and tapas should be daily duties, done as the scriptures prescribe.
- When the Guru has right discernment and the disciple right understanding, Ganapati awakens in both.
