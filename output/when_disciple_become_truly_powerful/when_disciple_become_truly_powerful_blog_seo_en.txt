# I Have a Guru, So Is My Work Done?

Many of us assume that once we have found the Guru, everything is in their hands now. They will take on our karma, give us spiritual power, and fulfil every wish we have in life.

Is that really how it works? This discourse explains the Guru-disciple relationship through a very simple, familiar example: the bond between parents and their children.

So when does a disciple become truly powerful? When they stop depending on the Guru for everything and awaken, through their own sadhana, the power that already lives within them. The Guru is a guide, not a crutch: just as parents want their child to stand on their own, the Guru wants the disciple to become powerful.

## The Guru Guides You; the Guru Is Not Your Crutch

The Guru is a **guide**. Taking on the disciple's karma is not their duty. They take on only as much as the **rinanubandha** (a karmic bond carried over from a past life) calls for.

They take something of yours only when they feel you are a **worthy vessel**. This is grace, not a contract.

Acharya Ji points out that giving shaktipat is not the Guru's job either.

**Shaktipat** is the Guru's transmission of spiritual energy into the disciple. Yet nothing obliges the Guru to give it. That power already lives within you. This is why the Guru tells you, "Do your **sadhana** (spiritual practice)."

## Why Don't We Become Powerful Ourselves?

Parents raise their children, earning to feed them. That is wonderful. But for how long?

Suppose you are ninety and your son is sixty-five or seventy. Will you still be the one earning for him? Every parent wants their child to grow strong, to earn on their own, and to become a support in their parents' old age.

Why shouldn't the same hold true between the Guru and the disciple?

Acharya Ji asks how many times shaktipat must happen. Why don't we become powerful ourselves?

The Guru taught you once, then twice more at a **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching). You did two sadhanas, then four. Yet twenty-five years later, the prayer is still the same: "Gurudev, give me a car, a horse, a bungalow." Can that go on forever?

Whoever grasps this has gained real intelligence.

*Am I growing through my own practice, or am I still just asking?*

## What Is Viveka Buddhi?

After intelligence comes the discerning mind, **viveka buddhi**. **Viveka** (discernment) means telling one thing from another. This has nothing to do with dark and fair skin, high and low status, or the four **varnas** (the traditional social classes).

It is the distinction between the **Atman (the Self)** and the **anatman** (what is not the Self). The mind, the intellect and the ego are all **jada** (inert). The Atman alone is **chetan** (conscious).

Try a simple experiment. Someone shouts at you. Who did the shouting: their body, or something unseen within it? And whom did the insult strike: your body, your mind, or you, the Atman?

*The next time someone gets angry with you, ask yourself: who was hurt, me or my mind?*

Anatman means whatever is not the Atman. Those who learn to draw this line possess viveka buddhi. And one who finds bliss in this discernment is called **Vivekananda**, the one whose joy is viveka.

The deity of the discerning mind is **Ganapati** (Lord Ganesha). This is a very high state.

## First Comes Subtle Intelligence

Before viveka, you need **sukshma buddhi** (subtle intelligence): a sense of what truly matters. How much should I burden the Guru, and how much should I spare them?

My part is to stay aware and make **yajna** (sacred offering), **dana** (charity) and **tapas** (austerity) a daily practice, performed the way the scriptures prescribe.

> **yajñadānatapaḥkarma**
> **na tyājyaṁ kāryam eva tat**
> "Acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity should never be given up; they must be performed."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.5)

Once I do this, my actions, my knowledge, my memory and **apohana** (forgetting, letting things fade from the mind) rest in the Guru's hands. Here, Shri Krishna's words about Himself are applied to the Guru.

> **mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṁ ca**
> "From Me come memory, knowledge and their loss."
> (Bhagavad Gita 15.15)

## Why Is the Guru Placed Above God?

God has no desire to take anything from us. The Guru has no such desire either, and yet the Guru does take.

This is why every tradition places the Guru above God: the Guru is able to take on what is yours.

## A Sixty-Four-Year-Old Son and His Mother's Pride

How many mothers say proudly, "I do everything for my son. He is sixty-four, and I still cook his meals and give him money. He is useless; he does no work at all."

Would any mother truly feel good saying that?

Supporting a child until fifteen, sixteen or eighteen is fine. Even then, you should withdraw that support gradually, one thing at a time. Money is a form of power too. If you keep supplying it forever, what will become of your child once you are gone?

In the same way, Acharya Ji says, the Guru must have enough discernment to know what kind of person the disciple needs to become.

## When Ganapati Awakens in Both

The Guru needs the discernment to know what to make of the disciple. The disciple needs the understanding to know how far to remain **dependent**.

When both reach this state, know that Ganapati has awakened in each of them.

Stories like this appear in the **Ganesha Purana**, and the **Ganesha Gita** forms part of it. This discourse distils the essence of that Ganesha Gita.

## Why Recite the Atharvashirsha a Thousand Times?

Why recite the **Ganesha Atharvashirsha** (a Vedic hymn to Lord Ganesha) a thousand times? Repeating the name of something imaginary a thousand times brings no benefit.

Why, then, a thousand names? To get help from someone else? The text itself gives the answer: there is no one else here at all.

*When I chant, whom am I chanting for, and whom am I searching for?*

## Key Takeaways

- The Guru is a guide, not obliged to take on your karma or give shaktipat.
- Real power lies within you, and sadhana is the way to awaken it.
- Just as parents want their child to stand on their own, the Guru wants the disciple to become powerful.
- Viveka buddhi is the discernment between the Atman and the anatman; its deity is Ganapati.
- Make yajna, dana and tapas a daily practice, done the way the scriptures prescribe.
- When the Guru has true discernment and the disciple true understanding, Ganapati awakens in both.
