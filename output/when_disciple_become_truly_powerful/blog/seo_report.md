## 1. Summary

A short talk (4:39 video, Ganesh Series episode 17, "When Does a Disciple Become Truly Powerful?") on the Guru–disciple bond, explained through the bond between parents and children. Having a Guru does not mean the Guru will take on all your karma, give shaktipat on demand or fulfil every wish: the Guru is a guide, not a crutch, and the power lies within the disciple, to be awakened through sadhana. A ninety-year-old father still earning for his seventy-year-old son, and a mother proud of feeding her sixty-four-year-old son, show what endless dependence looks like. The talk then moves from buddhi to sukshma buddhi (what matters; yajna, dana and tapas as daily duty, Gita 18.5) to viveka buddhi (discerning the Atman from the anatman), whose deity is Ganapati; when the Guru has the discernment to know what to make of the disciple, and the disciple the understanding of how far to depend, Ganapati awakens in both. The main intent is informational with a strong life-problem layer: seekers who feel stuck after years of practice, or who wonder what they can expect from a Guru ("shaktipat kya hai", "guru shishya sambandh", "when does a disciple become powerful"). Broad Guru queries are already targeted by other posts in this cluster, so the opportunity is the narrower self-reliance angle: what the disciple must do, and why the Guru wants the disciple to become powerful. Few pages answer this with a parent–child analogy; most shaktipat pages promise instant awakening, which this talk gently corrects.

## 6. FAQ

For the report only (not added to the page or schema).

1. **क्या गुरु शिष्य के सारे कर्म ले लेते हैं? / Does the Guru take on all the disciple's karma?**
   नहीं। गुरु उतना ही लेते हैं जितना पिछले जन्म का ऋणानुबंध है, और तभी जब शिष्य सुपात्र हो; यह कृपा है, अनुबंध नहीं। / No. The Guru takes on only as much as the rinanubandha from a past life calls for, and only when the disciple is a worthy vessel; it is grace, not a contract.
2. **शक्तिपात क्या है, और क्या गुरु को देना ही होता है? / What is shaktipat, and must the Guru give it?**
   शक्तिपात गुरु द्वारा शिष्य में आध्यात्मिक ऊर्जा का संचार है, पर गुरु पर इसकी कोई बाध्यता नहीं; वह शक्ति शिष्य के भीतर ही है, इसलिए गुरु साधना करने को कहते हैं। / Shaktipat is the Guru's transmission of spiritual energy into the disciple, but nothing obliges the Guru to give it; the power already lives within, which is why the Guru says, "Do your sadhana."
3. **विवेक बुद्धि क्या है? / What is viveka buddhi?**
   आत्मा और अनात्मा का भेद करने की क्षमता: मन, बुद्धि और अहंकार जड़ हैं, आत्मा चेतन है। इसके देवता गणपति हैं। / The ability to tell the Atman from the anatman: the mind, intellect and ego are inert, the Atman alone is conscious. Its deity is Ganapati.
4. **शिष्य का अपना काम क्या है? / What is the disciple's own part?**
   जागरूक रहकर यज्ञ, दान और तप को शास्त्रोक्त विधि से नित्य कर्म बनाना (गीता १८.५)। / To stay aware and make yajna, dana and tapas a daily practice, done the way the scriptures prescribe (Bhagavad Gita 18.5).

## 10. Authenticity flags

Review issues carried from `tools/review/when_disciple_become_truly_powerful.md` (verdict: Needs correction):
1. **Gita 15.15 applied to the Guru without a bridge in Hindi** (major): the English says Krishna's words are applied to the Guru; the Hindi does not. Recommendation: add the review's bridge sentence to the Hindi blog. No SEO text quotes 15.15.
2. **"A thousand recitations" of the Atharvashirsha confused with "a thousand names"** (major): the Sahasranama is a separate text. Recommendation: fix in the blog as the review suggests; no title, meta, FAQ or keyword uses this section, and the in-text links avoid it.
3. **"Every tradition places the Guru above God"** (major, sensitivity): an overgeneralisation. Recommendation: change to "our Guru tradition". The "Also read" box sits after this heading because the linked post extends it; no SEO text, FAQ or keyword repeats the claim ("why is the Guru above God" was deliberately not used as a keyword).
4. **"This discourse distils the essence of the Ganesha Gita"** (minor): the examples are not from the Ganesha Gita. "गणेश गीता" / "Ganesha Gita" is used only as a tag and a low-priority Hindi keyword (it is the talk's context, which the review confirms); the schema's `about` names it without claiming the examples come from it.
5. **अपोहन gloss** (minor): Gita Press reads "removal of doubt"; the blog gives Shankara's "forgetting". Editorial; not in SEO text.
6. **Partial verse quotations not marked** (minor): 18.5 first half, part of 15.15. The FAQ cites "Bhagavad Gita 18.5" only for the yajna–dana–tapas line, which is in the quoted half.
7. **IAST anusvara ṁ vs ṃ** (minor): house-style choice.
8. **"Vedic hymn" for the Atharvashirsha** (minor): better "Upanishadic hymn"; not in SEO text.

SEO-specific:
9. **"Truly powerful" in the video title vs the body**: the body never says "truly powerful"; titles and metas say "powerful" to match the body and avoid promising more than the talk delivers.
10. **Shaktipat intent**: many "what is shaktipat" searchers want instant awakening. The meta and FAQ state the talk's actual teaching (not the Guru's obligation; the power is within; sadhana), with no promise of awakening.
