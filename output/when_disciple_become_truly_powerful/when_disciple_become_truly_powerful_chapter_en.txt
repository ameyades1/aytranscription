# Chapter: When Will the Disciple Become Truly Powerful?

## The Guru Is a Guide, Not Merely a Crutch

Many people think, "We have a Guru, so our work is done." But the Guru is a guide. Taking on the disciple's karma is not his duty. He will take on only as much karma as the ṛṇānubandha (karmic bond) from the last birth allows. When he feels that you are a worthy vessel, that you are deserving, then he takes something of yours.

Giving shaktipat is not his job either; there is no compulsion on him. That shakti is the power within you. That is why he will say, "Do sadhana." What he did was not done just so that he could keep giving to you.

## The Example of Parents and Children

The mother earns; fine, very good. The father and everyone together raise the son. But until when? For how many years? You have turned ninety, and by then the son must be seventy or sixty-five. Will you keep earning for him? Don't you feel that strength should arise in him too, and that he should earn for himself? We even say that in old age he should be our support.

So should the same not hold between the Guru and the disciple? How many times shaktipat? Why do you not become powerful yourself? Once it was childhood, and until you grew up it was given. Once you were taught, twice taught in a camp; you did two sadhanas, four sadhanas. Even after that it is still the same: "Gurudev, twenty-five years have passed, give me a car, a horse, a bungalow." Will that do? The one who understands this has gained intelligence.

## Viveka Buddhi: Discerning the Self from the Not-Self

After this comes viveka buddhi. Viveka means to discriminate. But this is not discrimination between black and white, high and low, or the classification of the four varnas. It is discerning what is ātmā (the Self) and what is anātmā (the not-Self). As said earlier, mind, intellect and ego are all inert; the Self is consciousness. Knowing the difference between these two is viveka.

Someone shouted at me. Did his body shout, or something within him that I cannot see? And why did he shout at me? Which thing within me did he insult? My body, my mind, or me, that is, the Self?

Finally, to understand what is the Self and what is the not-Self. Anātmā means that which is not the Self. One who has learned this discernment has what is called **viveka buddhi**. And one who finds bliss in that discernment is named **Vivekananda**. That is a very high intelligence indeed. Ganapati is the deity of viveka buddhi, and that state is very high.

## First, Subtle Intelligence

First let subtle intelligence come: what is important. How much trouble should I give the Guru, and how much not. What is my work? Staying aware, yajña, dāna and tapas should be my daily duty, but done in the way the scriptures prescribe.

> **yajña-dāna-tapaḥ-karma na tyājyaṁ kāryam eva tat**
> "Acts of sacrifice, charity and austerity are not to be given up; they must certainly be performed."
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.5)

After that, my karma, knowledge, memory and apohana are in the Guru's hands.

> **mattaḥ smṛtir jñānam apohanaṁ ca**
> "From Me come memory, knowledge and their removal (forgetting)."
> (Bhagavad Gita 15.15)

## Why Is the Guru Placed Above God?

Even God has no wish to take. Then why would the Guru take? Yet he takes. That is why in every religion the Guru's place is said to be above God, because he can take. He has no desire to take, and still he takes.

## A Mother's Pride and a Son's Dependence

Not every mother wishes to keep giving strength to her son forever. Think about this. All the mothers will understand. How many women say with ego and pride, "Oh, I do everything for my son. He is sixty-four, and I still cook his food. I still have to give him money. He is useless, he does no work."

How many mothers would like this statement? Up to the age of fifteen, sixteen, eighteen it is fine. Even then, up to eighteen, one thing after another should gradually reduce. "I still have to feed him, I still do everything for him, I alone give him strength. Money is also a strength, and I keep giving that too." And one day, after you are gone, what will become of him?

## Ganapati Awakened in Both

You too should have this much understanding. The Guru too should have the viveka buddhi to know what kind of person to make the disciple. And the disciple, like the child, should understand how far to remain dependent. When both reach this state, know that Ganapati has awakened in both.

All such stories are in the Ganesha Purana and others, and the Ganesha Gita comes within it. What I am telling you is the essence of the Ganesha Gita. So did you gain some new knowledge or not?

## The Purpose of the Atharvashirsha and the Thousand Names

Why recite the Ganesha Atharvashirsha a thousand times? There is no benefit in repeating the name of something imaginary a thousand times. Why take a thousand names? Why must I do this first? To help someone else? It is said right there that there is no other here at all.
