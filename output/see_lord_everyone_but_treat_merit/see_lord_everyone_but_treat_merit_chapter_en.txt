# Chapter: See the Lord in Everyone, but Treat Each by Their Merit

## Seeing God Everywhere

The eighteenth verse of the fifth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita clearly says that we should see God in everyone.

> **vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini**
> **śuni caiva śva-pāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ**
> "The wise see with an equal eye a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste."
> (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

Whether it is a dog, a cat, an outcaste, a cow or a learned brahmin, the same God should be seen in all. That is right. But if we also start to treat everyone the same way, destruction too will come quickly.

## One Vision, Conduct by Merit

The Lord only asked us to see equally. He did not ask us to behave the same way with everyone. Conduct should be based on merit: we treat each according to their worth.

There is God in a dog and there is God in a minister. Now invite both home and serve both the same plate: a bone. Will that do? Why not, God is the same in both! But God was only meant to be seen as the same. Conduct follows merit.

> "We were told only to see that way, not to behave that way."

## As You Worship, So the Answer

There is God even in an asura. But the Lord says:

> **ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "In whatever way people approach Me, in that same way I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

So the asura is answered in his own way.

But does this mean that God or an avatar will come down to such a low level that if you hit me, I will hit you back? That is never the purpose. Yes, the answer comes in the same language. As the saying goes, those who understand only kicks do not listen to words. Yet God never loses His purpose. The purpose is your upliftment.

## Goddess Durga's Answer

Goddess Durga fights such a long battle with Mahishasura and with the asuras. The gods ask her, "You could have destroyed them in a single stroke. Why such a long war? And why do you let their impure, unclean hands touch you?" For after the weapons were thrown, there was wrestling and fist fighting. All of this is described.

Goddess Durga's answer is wonderful. She said, "The more I fight, the more he will call my name, even if in enmity. The enemy will still be saying my name. 'Durga! Durga! You wicked Durga!' Whatever he says, he will say my name. In this way he does naam jap. Second, when there is touch, he must be receiving sparsha diksha, initiation by touch. And in the end, I want him to attain moksha."

In the same way, Shri Krishna also wants the other to be liberated.

## To Kill the Enemy, Love the Enemy

> "To kill the enemy, you have to love the enemy."

Remember this as the first principle.

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> "For the protection of the good and the destruction of the wicked."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

Only one who also loves the other can destroy the wicked. He must see the Supreme in him.

## Sama, Dama, Danda, Bheda

For upliftment, the order is first sama, dama, danda, bheda: persuasion, offering, punishment, division. Shri Krishna first tried sama. He even showed his Vishwaroop, the cosmic form, but he did not understand. Dhritarashtra did not understand either. Then came dama: only five villages were asked for, and even that was not heard. Only then did the war begin.
