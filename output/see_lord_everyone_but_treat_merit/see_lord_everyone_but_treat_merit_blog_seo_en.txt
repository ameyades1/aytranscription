# See God in Everyone, but Treat Each on Merit

What does seeing God in everyone mean? Does it mean treating everyone the same way? People often get caught in this confusion when they read the Gita's teaching of equal vision. Acharya Upendra Ji untangles it with one plain principle: see the same One in all, but deal with each person according to their merit.

In short, no. Bhagavad Gita 5.18 calls the wise people of equal vision, who see the same Supreme Being in all. As Acharya Ji explains, this is about how you see, not how you behave. God dwells in a dog and in a minister, yet you would not serve both the same plate. Behaviour follows merit.

## What Does Gita 5.18 Mean by Equal Vision?

The eighteenth verse of the fifth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita speaks of **samadarshan** (equal vision).

> **vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini**
> **śuni caiva śva-pāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ**
> "The wise see the same Supreme Being in a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste."
> (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

This is true. But Acharya Ji warns that if you also start treating everyone alike, ruin will follow, and it will follow quickly.

## Does Seeing God in Everyone Mean Treating Everyone Alike?

The instruction, Acharya Ji points out, was to see in this way. Nobody was told to behave in this way.

The message of the Gita is **treat on merit**: deal with each person according to what they are capable of and what they deserve.

He offers a simple example. God dwells in a dog, and God dwells in a government minister. Invite both of them home and serve them the same plate: a bone. Would that work? After all, the same God lives in both of them!

That settles the matter. You were meant to **see** God equally in all. How you behave will always depend on merit.

*Does discernment walk alongside your compassion, or have you mistaken the two for one?*

## God Lives Even in the Demon, So How Does He Respond?

God lives even in the asura, the demon. But keep in mind this principle from the Gita as well:

> **ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "In whatever way people approach Me, in that same way I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

Does this mean God would stoop so low as to say, "You struck Me, so I will strike you"? No, that is never His aim. He does answer in the language a person understands, though. As the Hindi saying goes, a ghost that responds only to kicks will not listen to words.

Yet God never loses sight of His purpose, and that purpose is your **uddhara** (upliftment and liberation).

## Why Does Devi Durga Fight Such a Long Battle?

Devi Durga's battle against Mahishasura, the buffalo demon, and his fellow demons went on for a very long time. After the weapons had been hurled, she fought them in wrestling and then with bare fists. The scriptures describe every stage of it.

The gods asked her, "You could have destroyed them in a single stroke. Why fight for so long? And why let their impure, filthy hands touch you?"

Her answer touches the heart:

- **Nama japa** (repeating the divine name): The longer the battle lasts, the more the demon calls out My name, even if it is out of hatred. "Durga! Durga! You wicked Durga!" Whatever he shouts, it is My name he is taking.
- **Sparsha diksha** (spiritual initiation through touch): Whenever he touches Me, he must be receiving initiation through that touch.
- **Moksha** (liberation): And in the end, I want him to be set free.

Shri Krishna wants the same thing: that the one standing against Him should be liberated.

## Love the Enemy to Overcome the Enemy

Acharya Ji explains that to slay an enemy, you have to love that enemy. This is the first principle to remember.

The Gita says:

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> "For the protection of the good and the destruction of the wicked."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

Only someone whose heart also holds love for the wrongdoer can truly destroy wickedness. That person must see God in the very one they oppose.

*Can you see God even in those you stand against?*

## First Persuasion, Then an Offer, and Only Then War

The path to upliftment follows a set order: **sama** (persuasion), **dana** (offering a gift or concession), **danda** (punishment) and **bheda** (sowing division).

Shri Krishna tried persuasion first. He even revealed His Vishvarupa, the Cosmic Form, yet Duryodhana refused to understand. Dhritarashtra, the blind Kuru king, did not understand either. Then Shri Krishna made an offer and asked for only five villages, but no one listened to that either.

Only then did the war begin.

## Key Takeaways

- Equal vision means seeing the same God in all, not treating everyone alike.
- Behaviour should always follow merit: treat on merit.
- God answers people in the way they approach Him, but never abandons the purpose of their upliftment.
- Devi Durga's long battle gave the demon nama japa, sparsha diksha and liberation.
- Only those who also love the enemy can destroy the enemy.
- War is the last resort, after persuasion and an offer have been tried.
