## 1. Summary

A short discourse (3:23) in which Acharya Upendra Ji answers a common confusion about Bhagavad Gita 5.18 (samadarshan, equal vision): does seeing God in everyone mean treating everyone the same? His answer is "see the same One in all, but treat each on merit", illustrated with the dog-and-minister example, Gita 4.11 (God answers people the way they approach Him, yet never gives up their uddhara), Devi Durga's long battle with the asuras (nama japa, sparsha diksha, moksha), loving the enemy one must defeat (Gita 4.8), and sama-dana before war in the Mahabharata. The main intent is informational ("what does seeing God in everyone mean", "Gita 5.18 meaning") with an emotional/practical layer (people who feel spirituality means being a doormat, or who confuse compassion with lack of discernment). The opportunity: most results for "see God in everyone" are quote lists or devotional platitudes; this talk gives a clear, practical distinction (seeing vs behaving) that answers the reader's real doubt.

**English (≈55 words):** Seeing God in everyone does not mean treating everyone the same way. Bhagavad Gita 5.18 says the wise have equal vision: they see the same Supreme Being in a learned brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste. As Acharya Upendra Ji explains, the vision is equal, but behaviour must follow each person's merit.

## 6. FAQ

1. **सबमें भगवान देखने का अर्थ क्या है? / What does seeing God in everyone mean?**
   सबमें एक ही परमात्मा को पहचानना (गीता ५.१८)। आचार्य जी के अनुसार यह देखने की बात है, सबसे एक जैसा व्यवहार करने की नहीं। / Recognising the same Supreme Being in all (Gita 5.18). According to Acharya Ji, this is about how you see, not an instruction to treat everyone alike.
2. **क्या सबके साथ एक जैसा व्यवहार करना चाहिए? / Should we treat everyone the same?**
   नहीं। आचार्य जी का उदाहरण: कुत्ते और मंत्री, दोनों में भगवान है, पर दोनों को एक ही थाली नहीं दी जाती। व्यवहार योग्यता से होता है। / No. Acharya Ji's example: God dwells in a dog and in a minister, yet you would not serve both the same plate. Behaviour follows merit.
3. **भगवान उन्हें कैसे उत्तर देते हैं जो उनके विरुद्ध हैं? / How does God respond to those who oppose Him?**
   गीता ४.११ के अनुसार जो जिस रूप में भजता है, भगवान उसे उसी रूप में उत्तर देते हैं; पर आचार्य जी बताते हैं कि उनका उद्देश्य सदा उसका उद्धार रहता है। / By Gita 4.11, God responds to people in the way they approach Him; but, as Acharya Ji explains, His aim always remains their upliftment.
4. **आचार्य जी के अनुसार दुर्गा देवी ने इतना लंबा युद्ध क्यों किया? / In Acharya Ji's telling, why did Devi Durga fight so long?**
   ताकि असुर शत्रुता से ही सही, उनका नाम ले (नाम जप), उनके स्पर्श से दीक्षा पाए, और अंत में उसका मोक्ष हो। / So that the demon, even in hatred, would keep taking her name (nama japa), receive initiation through her touch, and finally be liberated.
5. **युद्ध कब उचित है? / When is war justified?**
   अंतिम उपाय के रूप में: श्रीकृष्ण ने पहले समझाया (साम) और प्रस्ताव दिया (दाम); जब कोई नहीं माना, तब युद्ध हुआ। / Only as the last resort: Shri Krishna first tried persuasion and then an offer; only when neither was heard did the war begin.

FAQ 4 must stay attributed to Acharya Ji (see flag 1). FAQ 5 keeps the blog's wording; see flag 4 for the five-village detail.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Devi Durga's dialogue presented as scripture (Major, from the Sanskrit review).** No dialogue in which the gods ask why the battle is long and Devi answers with nama japa, sparsha diksha and moksha is found in the Gita Press Durga Saptashati; the moksha point matches the Shakradi Stuti (chapter 4: enemies purified by her weapons reach the higher worlds), while nama japa and sparsha diksha are Acharya Ji's own teaching. Recommendation: attribute the episode to Acharya Ji in the blog ("आचार्य जी इसे एक प्रश्न के रूप में रखते हैं..." / "Acharya Ji frames it as a question..."). In this report the FAQ says "in Acharya Ji's telling", the meta only says "why Durga fought so long", and Durga Saptashati is deliberately not used as a tag or keyword. Do not cite the Saptashati for this dialogue in any snippet.
2. **Wrestling and fist fight placed in the Mahishasura episode (Minor, from the review).** In the Saptashati the hand-to-hand fight is with Shumbha (chapter 10), not Mahishasura. Recommendation: the review's correction ("महिषासुर, शुम्भ-निशुम्भ और अन्य असुरों से..."). Snippets here do not mention Mahishasura.
3. **"साम, दाम, दंड, भेद" called a sequence (Minor, from the review).** The classical order is साम, दान, भेद, दण्ड, with force last, which fits the blog's own point. Recommendation: use the classical order in the blog and note the colloquial one. The secondary keyword "साम दाम दंड भेद" / "sama dana danda bheda" is kept because that is how people search; the FAQ only says "persuasion, then an offer, then war", which holds in either order.
4. **Five villages and "वह" (Minor, from the review).** The Hindi "वह" has no antecedent (English: Duryodhana), and in the Mahabharata the five-village demand is made by Yudhishthira through Sanjaya; that Krishna himself asked is the popular telling. Recommendation: the review's correction ("पांडवों ने केवल पाँच गाँव माँगे"). FAQ 5 does not repeat the five-village detail.
5. **"Treat on merit" as the Gita's own words (Minor, from the review).** No Gita verse says this; it is Acharya Ji's reading of 5.18 (sama-darshana, not sama-vartana). Recommendation: "आचार्य जी के अनुसार गीता का संदेश है..." / "As Acharya Ji reads it...". The titles pair "treat on merit" with Gita 5.18; the direct answer and FAQ 1 attribute the merit principle to Acharya Ji. If the editor prefers, use title option 2 to avoid any implication that the phrase is in the verse.
6. **Partial verses 4.8 and 4.11 (Minor, from the review).** Both are first halves; the 4.8 meaning is an incomplete sentence. Recommendation: mark "(पूर्वार्ध)" / "(first half)" and complete the 4.8 meaning as the review suggests.
7. **Gita 5.18 rendering (Minor, optional, from the review).** Keep "समदर्शी" in the meaning to tie it to the term explained. The direct answer here already says "समदर्शी" / "equal vision".
8. **"Treat on merit" and caste (SEO pressure).** The verse names a brahmin and a चांडाल / outcaste; a snippet like "treat people according to merit" next to that list could be read as endorsing caste hierarchy. The talk's example is a dog and a minister, i.e. treatment according to role and capacity, not birth. Recommendation: never place "treat on merit" next to the caste terms in titles, meta or social snippets; keep the dog-and-minister example when explaining it.

Flags 1 to 7 require edits to the blog text, which this report does not make; they are left to the editor.
