# God in Everyone, Conduct by Merit

Does seeing God in everyone mean treating everyone the same way? The Gita's idea of equal vision often gets caught in this confusion. Acharya Upendra Ji untangles it with one simple point: see the One in all, but behave according to merit.

## What the Gita Says: Equal Vision

The eighteenth verse of the fifth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita speaks of **samadarshan** (equal vision).

> **vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini**
> **śuni caiva śva-pāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ**
> "The wise see with an equal eye a learned and humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste."
> (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

The wise see the same Supreme in a learned, humble brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste.

This is right. But Acharya Ji warns that if our conduct also becomes the same with everyone, destruction will come quickly.

## Seeing and Behaving Are Two Different Things

> "We were told only to see that way, not to behave that way."

The Gita's message is: **treat on merit**, meaning conduct according to each one's worth.

Acharya Ji gives a simple example. There is God in a dog and God in a minister. Invite both home and serve both the same plate: a bone. Will that do? God is the same in both, isn't He!

Here it becomes clear. God was only meant to be **seen** as the same. Conduct follows merit.

*Does discernment walk alongside your compassion, or have you taken the two to be one?*

## God Is in the Asura Too, So How Does He Answer?

There is God even in an asura. But remember this teaching of the Gita too:

> **ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham**
> "In whatever way people approach Me, in that same way I respond to them."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.11)

So will God come down so low as to say, "You hit me, so I will hit you"? No, that is never His purpose. Yes, the answer comes in the same language. As the saying goes, those who understand only kicks do not listen to words.

But God never loses His purpose. And that purpose is your **uddhar** (upliftment, liberation).

## Why Does Goddess Durga Fight So Long?

Goddess Durga's battle with Mahishasura and the asuras was very long. After the weapons were thrown, there was wrestling and fist fighting. All of this is described.

The gods asked, "You could have destroyed them in a single stroke. Why such a war? And why let their impure, unclean hands touch you?"

Goddess Durga's answer touches the heart:

- **Naam jap:** The longer the battle, the more he will call my name, even in enmity. "Durga! Durga! You wicked Durga!" Whatever he says, it is my name he takes.
- **Sparsha diksha:** When there is touch, he must be receiving sparsha diksha (spiritual initiation by touch).
- **Moksha:** And in the end, I want him to attain liberation.

Shri Krishna too wants the other to be liberated.

## Love the Enemy, Win Over the Enemy

> "To kill the enemy, you have to love the enemy. Remember this as the first principle."

The Gita says:

> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> "For the protection of the good and the destruction of the wicked."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.8)

But only one who also loves the other can destroy the wicked. He must see the Supreme in him.

*Can you see the Supreme even in those you oppose?*

## First Sama, Then Dama, Then War

The order of upliftment is: **sama, dama, danda, bheda** (persuasion, offering, punishment, division).

Shri Krishna first tried sama. He even showed his cosmic form, but he did not understand. Dhritarashtra did not understand either. Then came dama: only five villages were asked for, and even that was not heard.

Only then did the war begin.

## Key Takeaways

- Equal vision means seeing one Supreme in all, not treating everyone the same way.
- Conduct should always be by merit: treat on merit.
- God answers in the language in which He is approached, but never abandons the purpose of upliftment.
- Goddess Durga's long battle was to give the asura naam jap, sparsha diksha and moksha.
- Only one who also loves the enemy can destroy the enemy.
- War is the last resort: sama and dama are tried first.
