# Chapter: Veda and Vedanta, the Journey of Knowing and Its End

## Is the Veda a Book?

If someone asks us about our Sanatan Dharma, our Hindu scriptures, and which text is ours, we immediately say: the Veda. Today I want to talk a little about the Veda.

What do you think, is the Veda a book? Is there one book called the Veda? How many of you think the Veda is a book, a scripture? All right. Let me tell you today that this belief too is mithya, a misconception.

## The Veda Is Not a Name but an Action

First understand this: is Veda a name or an action? In the language of grammar, is Veda a noun or a verb? We call ourselves Sanatanis, we call ourselves Hindus, so at the very least we should know our own scripture properly.

The Veda is not a name, not a book. The Veda is an action.

> "Aham veda, I have known. The process of knowing is called Veda."

So Veda means: to know. To know what? The knowledge of this world and the world beyond. All the knowledge that is here, which I come to know slowly. And while knowing, also this: what will happen after death, what I will become next, whether there will be moksha or something else, what the next birth will be like. All the spiritual knowledge up to that point and all the knowledge of this world, the name for all of it is **Veda**.

## Apaurusheya and the Voice of the Divine

The Veda is called **apaurusheya**, meaning it was not written by any one person. And it is also called **devavani**, the voice of the divine.

Now think: is not every person divine? Is there not Brahman within each one? "Aham brahmasmi", that is exactly what we learned yesterday. There is Brahman within every person. So knowledge that came forth through many people is itself devavani.

## A Vessel of Infinite Size

Understand the Veda as a very large vessel, a big container, whose size is infinite. For this, look at the forty-fifth verse of the second chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, which the Lord spoke to Arjuna:

> **traiguṇya-viṣayā vedā nistraiguṇyo bhavārjuna**
> **nirdvandvo nitya-sattva-stho niryoga-kṣema ātmavān**
> "The Vedas deal with the subjects of the three gunas; O Arjuna, rise above these three gunas, be free of the pairs of opposites, ever established in sattva, free from concern for acquiring and preserving, and be established in the Self."
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.45)

Which are the three gunas? Sattva, rajas and tamas. There are three kinds of people in the world.

## Chapters for the Tamasic Too

Tamasic people must also be saying something, and there is Brahman within them too. So someone would have said: eat plenty, eat till your stomach is full, eat this, eat that, eat pizza, eat everything, and you will find joy. Because ultimately everyone is searching for joy. Every person is seeking happiness. So somewhere in the Veda such a chapter will also appear, along with its phalashruti.

Someone will write: drink liquor, keep drinking this much, then joy comes; not four glasses, you must drink five glasses every day. Someone's article will also say what kind of medicine is in it and how it works. Somewhere in the Atharvaveda you will find such chapters, along with their phalashruti.

After that someone will say: no, not one, there should be four or five women, many relationships, lust, that will bring joy. Chapters with such desires too will be found somewhere in the Vedas. Because that part fulfils the wishes of tamasic people. Some tamasic Brahman, that is, a person in a tamasic state, would have said it, and it too became part of the apaurusheya knowledge.

## The Rajasic and Sattvic Voices

There are also rajasic people in the world, and sattvic people too. So then a physician will also write in it: yes, but if you drink this much liquor, the liver gets damaged too, do not do this, your diet should be like this. That too will then come into the Vedas.

Do you understand how the Veda developed? People wrote that if you do this, you will find joy. Going on like this, the knowledge of the whole world came into it. Pandits too would have written something.

## The Veda Never Ends

As I said, the Veda is not a book. For all the knowledge that was gathered, one can only say: "Kalaya tasmai namah."

> "The Veda never ends."

Has the action of knowing come to an end even today? Today you are doing something on a computer, and that new knowledge too is actually a part of the Veda. But to understand something, we read its essence in a book, with names like the Yajurveda and the Atharvaveda. Only an extract of it has been given in them. Otherwise the Veda never ends, it keeps on growing, however much you learn. Today some new invention will come, and from that angle too some people will benefit. So everything is a part of the Veda.

## Vedanta: Where Knowing Ends

So then, at the end, some rishi or muni would also have written, because they too are part of this universe. There would have been avatars, and they too would have said something. So would their word not be final? Their word is final.

What would they have written? Leave all of this above, all this is moha-maya, do only this. So the sentences the rishis and munis wrote at the very end, that is where the Veda ends. There is no teaching above it.

That part is called **Vedanta**, meaning the end of the Vedas. Here the process of knowing comes to an end. Beyond this, nothing remains to be understood.

## The Only Scripture That Says, Do Not Read Me

> "The Veda is the only scripture that calls out and says: do not read me."

You will not believe it, but it is true. It means that about everything read so far, Shri Krishna himself said: "nistraiguṇyo bhavārjuna". Leave all that earlier part of the three gunas and read what lies beyond it, that is, the final part. If you must read, what should you read? Vedanta.

## Moksha for the Jnani

Vedanta is called the **jnana kanda**. The scriptures say that the jnani, the one who knows, attains moksha. But what does moksha for the jnani mean? It does not mean going to school and becoming a pandit.

There are four paths: **karma yoga, bhakti yoga, jnana yoga and dhyana yoga**. And on each path, knowledge is essential.

- **Karma:** You want to do karma? Work is worship, fine, but do karma filled with knowledge. Understand the result of what you are doing, understand why you are doing it. Get knowledge from the guru.
- **Bhakti:** You want to do bhakti? Do not have blind faith, do bhakti filled with knowledge. First understand the Narada Bhakti Sutra and the like properly. Who were the gopis, who was Radha, first know all this. First understand what bhakti is, then do it. Do not do bhakti any way you please, according to the three gunas.
- **Jnana:** If it is knowledge you want, jnana yoga, then gain it too like a jnani.
- **Dhyana:** If you want to meditate, do not go and sit silently in the Himalayas to get your photo taken. On what to meditate, do that too with knowledge.

That is why it is said that moksha comes to the jnani, the tattvajnani. First try to understand tattvajnana.

## Karma Kanda, Upasana Kanda and Jnana Kanda

Vedanta is nothing but the jnana kanda. What will the rest be? Before it, the upasana kanda, and before that, the karma kanda. The karma kanda contains only puja-path, yajnas and the like.

## Upanishad: Sitting Close to the Guru

This very jnana kanda turned into a word called **Upanishad**. Upa, ni and shad, meaning to sit close to the guru, at his feet.

The Upanishad is that knowledge which is gained by going near the guru, sitting below him, in a sattvic state. And being near means being near through **sharanagati**, surrender, that is, learning while understanding his words with the heart.

Have you understood this much?
