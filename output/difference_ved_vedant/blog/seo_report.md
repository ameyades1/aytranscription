## 1. Summary

The talk answers the question in the video's title: what is the difference between Veda and Vedanta? Acharya Ji starts by asking whether "veda" is a noun or a verb, and says the Veda is not a book but the act of knowing ("अहं वेद"), covering worldly and spiritual knowledge alike. Because Brahman is in everyone, the Veda is apaurusheya and holds the voices of tamasic, rajasic and sattvic people (Gita 2.45, "त्रैगुण्यविषया वेदा"); it never ends, and the four Vedas are only its essence. Vedanta is where the final words of the rishis were recorded: the end of the Veda, where knowing is complete. Liberation comes to the knower of tattva, and every path (karma, bhakti, jnana, dhyana) must rest on knowledge. It closes with the three kandas (karma, upasana, jnana) and Upanishad as sitting near the Guru in surrender. The main intent is **informational**: "वेद और वेदांत में अंतर" / "difference between Veda and Vedanta" is an evergreen query asked by students, exam takers and curious seekers, and so are "वेद का अर्थ", "वेदांत क्या है" and "meaning of Upanishad". Ranking pages are mostly encyclopedic summaries (Samhitas, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads; the six darshanas). This post's angle is experiential and memorable: the Veda as a verb, "the scripture that says don't read me", and knowledge as the basis of every path. There is no festival peak; Guru Purnima (Vyasa, who arranged the Vedas) is a natural moment to refresh it alongside `real_meaning_guru`.

## 6. FAQ

1. **वेद और वेदांत में क्या अंतर है? / What is the difference between Veda and Vedanta?**
   वेद जानने की पूरी प्रक्रिया है, जिसमें संसार और अध्यात्म का सारा ज्ञान आता है। वेदांत उसका अंतिम भाग है, जहां ऋषियों के अंतिम वचन हैं और जानना पूर्ण होता है। / The Veda is the whole process of knowing, worldly and spiritual. Vedanta is its final portion, holding the last words of the rishis, where knowing reaches completion.
2. **क्या वेद एक किताब है? / Is the Veda a book?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार नहीं: वेद मूल रूप से जानने की क्रिया है ("अहं वेद")। ऋग्वेद, यजुर्वेद जैसे ग्रंथ उस अनंत ज्ञान का केवल सार हैं। / According to Acharya Ji, no: at root the Veda is the act of knowing ("ahaṃ veda"). Texts such as the Yajurveda are only an essence of that infinite knowledge.
3. **वेद के तीन कांड कौन-से हैं? / What are the three kandas of the Veda?**
   कर्मकांड (पूजा-पाठ, यज्ञ), उपासना कांड और ज्ञान कांड। ज्ञान कांड ही वेदांत है। / Karma kanda (worship, recitation, yajnas), upasana kanda and jnana kanda. The jnana kanda is Vedanta.
4. **उपनिषद का अर्थ क्या है? / What does Upanishad mean?**
   उप + नि + षद्, यानी गुरु के समीप बैठना: सात्त्विक अवस्था में, शरणागति के भाव से, हृदय से सीखी गई विद्या। / Upa + ni + shad: sitting near the Guru, wisdom learned in a sattvic state, with surrender, from the heart.
5. **ज्ञानी को मोक्ष का क्या अर्थ है? / What does "liberation for the wise" mean?**
   ज्ञानी का अर्थ पंडित बनना नहीं। कर्म, भक्ति, ज्ञान और ध्यान, हर मार्ग समझ पर टिका हो; तत्त्वज्ञान ही मोक्ष की ओर ले जाता है। / Being wise does not mean becoming a scholar. Every path, whether action, devotion, knowledge or meditation, must rest on understanding; knowledge of tattva leads to liberation.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Tamasic "chapters" in the Vedas (Sanskrit review issue 1, Major, not yet applied).** The blog states as fact that the Vedas, including the Atharvaveda, contain chapters urging heavy drinking and many women; no passage is cited, and the review knows of none. The English adds a disclaimer the Hindi lacks (review issue 7). Apply the review's corrections (frame it as Acharya Ji's illustration; mention the Atharvaveda's healing and desire-fulfilling hymns). In SEO text never use "alcohol in the Vedas", "polygamy in the Vedas" or similar queries, and keep these examples out of titles, metas, snippets, social cards and the FAQ (none of them uses it).
2. **Apaurusheya gloss (review issue 2, Major).** "No single person wrote it" reverses the traditional meaning (not composed by any person; the rishis saw and heard it). Apply the correction. The SEO text does not define apaurusheya; the low-priority keyword "अपौरुषेय वेद का अर्थ" should only be targeted once the correction is in.
3. **Veda as a verb; "अहं वेद" = "मैंने जाना" (review issue 3, Major).** As the scripture's name वेद is a noun (knowledge, from विद्); "अहं वेद" means "I know". The FAQ and snippet say "at root the act of knowing" and "the process of knowing", which stay true either way, and the English FAQ uses "ahaṃ veda" without a translation. Apply the review's correction to the blog.
4. **Gita 2.45 as "the Veda says don't read me" (review issue 4, Major, Hindi).** The Hindi presents Acharya Ji's reading as what the verse says; the English correctly says "this is how he reads". Apply the Hindi correction. Do not use "the Veda says don't read me" or "Krishna said don't read the Vedas" as a title, snippet or social hook; if used at all, attribute it ("Acharya Ji reads the Gita's call to rise above the three gunas as…"). Mundaka Upanishad 1.1.4-5 (apara and para vidya) is the Veda's own statement of the idea if the editor wants a citation.
5. **"Liberation for the wise" source (review issue 5, Minor).** "तत्वज्ञानी को मोक्ष" is a summary, not a quotation. The FAQ gives the teaching without citing a verse; if a source is added to the blog, use Gita 4.37 as the review suggests.
6. **सत्त्व / तत्त्व / सात्त्विक spelling (review issue 6, Minor).** The SEO text (snippet, FAQ) uses सत्त्व-family spellings. The Hindi also_read heading "फिर राजसिक और सात्विक स्वर आते हैं" is copied from the current blog; **if the editor applies the सात्त्विक correction, update that heading in seo.json (and section 11) and rebuild**, or the box will not be placed. The five Hindi link anchors avoid these words.
7. **"The original name of the Hindu tradition" (review issue 8, Minor, English only).** Contested historical claim; apply the correction. Not used in SEO text.
8. **देववाणी as a name for the Veda (review issue 10, Minor).** Mainly a name for Sanskrit; श्रुति is the standard term. Not used in SEO text.
9. **"The Veda keeps growing; what you learn on a computer is Veda" (attributed interpretation).** The review accepts it as Acharya Ji's view. Do not turn it into "modern science is in the Vedas" or "the Vedas predicted computers" headlines; that would misstate the talk, which says new knowledge is part of the process of knowing, not that old texts contain it.
10. **"Ved" vs "Veda" spelling and IAST (review issue 9, Minor).** The video says "Ved and Vedant" (Hindi pronunciation); the English SEO text uses "Veda" and "Vedanta", which most English searches use, and the Hinglish keywords "ved aur vedant mein antar" cover the other form. For YouTube, a title such as "Difference Between Veda and Vedanta (Ved vs Vedant) | Acharya Upendra Ji" covers both. Apply the ṃ IAST correction ("ahaṃ veda") to the blog; the FAQ already uses ṃ.

Category note: `gallery_topics.txt` places the talk under `dharma`, copied as is. "Vedas" / "वेद" and "Upanishads" / "उपनिषद" are new tags; both fit at least `true_power_of_scriptures_decoded`, `evolution_of_dharma_sanatan_dharma` and `shreya_vs_preya_vedantic_wisdom`, so reuse them there.

## 12. SEO checklist

### Keywords
- [x] Hindi and English each have a focus keyword
- [x] The focus keyword isn't used by another post (no cannibalization)
- [x] 2–4 secondary keywords per language
- [x] Hindi and English keywords researched separately, not translated; Hinglish variants considered
- [x] Focus keyword taken from wording the article already uses
- [x] If people search a different wording, it is in the secondary keywords and the mismatch is flagged
- [x] Priority given as High / Medium / Low, based on intent and competition; no invented numbers

### Focus keyword placement (SureRank)
- [x] In the SEO title
- [x] In the meta description
- [x] In the body text
- [x] In the URL slug (English keywords)
- [x] Within the first 100 words (preferred)

### SEO title
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 60 characters
- [x] Keyword near the start
- [x] Faithful to the talk, no clickbait

### Meta description
- [x] Present in both languages, ≤ 160 characters
- [x] English is 140–155 characters
- [x] Gives a clear reason to click

### URL slug
- [x] Lowercase Latin, hyphenated, unique across posts
- [x] Short enough that the full URL stays ≤ 90 characters

### Headings
- [x] At least one subheading (H2)

### Images
- [x] Every image has alt text in both languages
- [x] Alt text is descriptive, in the post's language, based on looking at the image
- [x] Captions add meaning (not a repeat of the alt text or title)

### Internal linking
- [x] Links point only to real posts, never to the same post
- [x] Anchor text is a natural phrase from the body (not a heading or verse)
- [x] 3–5 in-text links per language
- [x] One "Also read" link, placed after the section it extends best
- [x] 3 related posts at the end, the ones that suit this reader best

### Category and tags
- [x] One category, from the approved 14
- [x] 3–6 tags per language
- [x] No tag repeats a category name
- [x] Existing tags reused; no near-duplicates
- [x] Each tag can be shared by at least 3 posts
- [x] No broad labels that only restate a category
- [x] Series tags named exactly as the series
- [x] `festivals` only when the post is about the festival itself; Pitru topics in `pitru`

### Structured data (in this report, not on the page)
- [x] One JSON-LD block per language: BlogPosting, Person, Organization, VideoObject (if there is a video), BreadcrumbList
- [x] Hindi and English versions linked as translations

### Content trust
- [x] Titles, metas and snippets faithful to the talk
