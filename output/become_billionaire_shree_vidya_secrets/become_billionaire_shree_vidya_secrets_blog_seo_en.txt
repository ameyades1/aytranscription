# Shri Vidya: The Ancient Secret of Wealth, Dharma and Liberation

Does earning money go against a spiritual life? Is it a sin to want wealth?

In this discourse, given at the Antar Yog Shri Vidya **Shibir** (a gathering for spiritual practice and teaching), Acharya Ji breaks down this old belief. He explains that **Shri Vidya** (also written Shree Vidya) is a rare sadhana that awakens the power to create wealth within the seeker as a **siddhi** (a spiritual capability).

What is the link between Shri Vidya and wealth? According to Acharya Ji, there is nothing shameful about needing money, because Chanakya's sutra says the root of dharma is artha, wealth. Shri Vidya teaches more than earning: it teaches how to plan wealth and set aside part of it for dharma and charity, turning worldly enjoyment into material for yoga that leads to liberation.

## Why Are Some People So Rich and Others So Poor?

Earning money does not come easily to everyone. Many people work hard all day and earn only a little, and they get by on that.

At the other end stand the billionaires and the multi-billionaires. Acharya Ji says he has seen deep inequality in our country: a few people are extremely rich, while many are extremely poor.

## Dreaming Beyond a Job

Our education system does not teach this knowledge at all. The first thing a young person leaving school or college thinks about is where to find a job.

Even the brightest students think only about how to land a job abroad. Acharya Ji wants them to become great entrepreneurs and build something big. And if you want to do any work of **dharma** (righteous duty), you need money for that too.

*When did you last tell yourself that you were made to create something, not just to hold a job?*

## Is Earning Money a Sin? Why Feel Ashamed of Needing Money?

A sutra of Acharya Chanakya makes this truth clear:

> **sukhasya mūlaṃ dharmaḥ |**
> **dharmasya mūlam arthaḥ |**
> **arthasya mūlaṃ rājyam ||**
> "The root of happiness is dharma, the root of dharma is **artha** (wealth), and the root of wealth is the state."
> (Chanakya Sutra 1-3)

A state, therefore, must prosper, and there is nothing shameful about needing money.

Lord Vishnu sustains the world, and Maa Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, is always at his side to make that possible. In the Vedic age, people called India "the golden bird" because of its riches. Neither Shri Krishna, the king of Dwarka, nor Shri Rama ever lacked wealth.

We have been taught that money is bad, that the rich are bad, that wealth itself is bad and that it corrupts the mind. Acharya Ji says none of this is true.

## What Does Shri Vidya Teach? More Than Making Money

The real art lies in knowing how to plan your wealth, how to manage it, and how to set aside part of it for dharma and charity. Only those who learn this properly understand Shri Vidya.

Tradition says Shri Vidya grants both **bhoga** and **moksha** together. Here bhoga means the pleasures and comforts of the world, and moksha means liberation.

Acharya Ji explains that Shri Vidya turns worldly enjoyment into material for yoga and, through it, leads you to liberation.

In other words, you do not run away from the world. You make the world's resources themselves the means of your practice.

*Is your wealth binding you, or is it carrying you toward freedom?*

## The Same Knowledge Abroad

Acharya Ji points out that people abroad are using this very knowledge today. On one hand, foreign powers looted India's wealth; on the other, they carried away our Shri Vidya. Many of the great industrialists there learned it from here.

## The Lineage of Masters and the Shri Yantra Sadhana

The tradition of Shri Vidya is very old. Adi Shankaracharya held the **Saundarya Lahari** (a celebrated hymn to the Divine Mother) along with Shri Vidya. The same knowledge runs through the lineage of Agastya Muni, Dattatreya, Machhindranath and Gorakhnath. Acharya Chanakya's **Arthashastra**, also known by his other name Kautilya, is famous across the world today.

The Shibir also offers rare practices from this tradition. The sadhana of the **Shri Yantra**, which is the worship of Maa Lakshmi, is taught through a special method. It uses a particular **vigraha** (a sacred form or image for worship), designed according to precise science. This is an ancient, rare and secret sadhana.

## A Resolve to Reach Thirty Million People

This is not a one-day event. Acharya Ji wants to take it to every city and every village in India. The resolve is to bring Shri Vidya to at least two percent of the country, roughly 27 to 30 million people.

India's economy is growing toward five trillion dollars, and that is a matter of pride. Yet Acharya Ji has noticed that only a handful of people are becoming very rich, while everyone else stays where they were. He believes that at least 30 to 40 million people should become entrepreneurs themselves.

Today this knowledge reaches only the sons of the rich, just as only a politician's son gets to learn politics. Everyone else either never receives it in school or college, or receives it in a form that never takes hold.

## Save the Nation, and Dharma Survives

Acharya Ji calls on the Education Minister, the Finance Minister and everyone concerned to bring this knowledge to the whole country, so that India can soon become an **economic superpower**. That wealth will support the work of dharma, the defence of the nation and the care of our armed forces.

If the nation survives, Acharya Ji says, dharma survives; and where dharma survives, culture can flourish. Only then does wealth bring its true benefit. Even keeping your own body healthy takes money. Acharya Ji invites industrialists and every citizen of the country to join this programme.

## Key Takeaways

- Needing money is nothing to be ashamed of; for Chanakya, wealth is the root of dharma.
- Lakshmi stands beside Vishnu, and neither Shri Krishna nor Shri Rama lacked wealth.
- Shri Vidya teaches more than earning: it teaches planning wealth, dharma and charity.
- Shri Vidya turns worldly enjoyment into yoga and leads to liberation.
- Adi Shankaracharya, Agastya Muni, Dattatreya, the Nath lineage and Chanakya all belong to this tradition.
- The goal is to turn millions of job seekers into entrepreneurs and make India an economic superpower.
