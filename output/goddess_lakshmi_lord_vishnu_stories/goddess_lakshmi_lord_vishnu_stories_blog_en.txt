# Creation Takes a Moment, Sustenance Takes a Lifetime

We often treat beginnings as the biggest thing: birth, first love, a new business. But Acharya Upendra Ji asks a simple question: how long did that beginning last? And who took care of the long stretch that came after it?

This is where we understand why **Vishnu and Lakshmi** hold such a central place in Sanatan Dharma.

## How Long Was the Moment of Birth?

When you were born, how many minutes did that moment of creation last? Only a fraction of a second. And from the very next moment you were already born.

Death too will come one day. How long will that moment be? Very short.

It is the same in relationships. You saw someone for the first time, and there was love at first sight. Then a second sight, a third sight, and it continues. But that first sight, that **utpatti** (creation), was complete in just a few moments. The feeling that arose within for the first time also lasted a moment.

It is the same in business. You applied for the papers, completed the registration, and the beginning was done. And ending does not take long either.

> "The moment of creation is very short."

## So Where Does the Time Go?

Now the question is: how much **Brahma and Saraswati** (the power of creation and knowledge) do you need in life, how much **Kali Mahakali** (the power of ending and transformation), and how much **Vishnu Lakshmi** (the power of sustenance and prosperity)?

The answer is clear. **Most of the time goes into sustenance.**

Whether it is the body, a business, running a kingdom or a family, at whatever level you go, everywhere the **Lakshmi principle and the Vishnu principle** are needed a great deal.

*What in your life today takes most of your energy simply to sustain?*

## Small Stories Within the Great Story

But this does not mean Brahma comes only once. In every moment something or other new keeps arising.

In a business, first a small branch began. Then a second branch will open, a new unit will form, someone new will be appointed. Within the great creation, small creations keep happening.

It is the same in life. You were born, grew a little, then went to school. Something new began in life from there, and once again **Saraswati** was needed.

But school should end at some point. Acharya Ji asks with a smile: at the age of seventy-two, what are you doing? Still in the fourth grade? Every small cycle also has an end.

> "Within the great story, small things arise again and come to an end."

So do not think that Kali or Saraswati are no longer needed. New cycles will keep coming in between.

## Marriage: Building a New World

Then at some point there is a marriage. Between birth and death, this is a new **chapter**. Something new was created, and for it Brahma, Saraswati and intellect are needed again. From there a new world is built.

That too will end some day. Through death, a quarrel, an accident, a hero's death in battle, or by going to different places for work. We do not know how it will happen. One may go first, but the other still lives on.

*What new chapter has begun in your life right now, and what are you giving to sustain it?*

## That Is Why Mahalakshmi Matters Most

So the essence is this: you cannot say that you do not need Saraswati right now, or that you have no need for Kali. Creation and ending both come again and again.

But in every small story, what is needed to sustain what has arisen? The same **Vishnu** and the same **Lakshmi**.

> "In our Sanatan Dharma, Mahalakshmi has been given the greatest importance."

## Key Takeaways

- The moments of creation and of ending are both very short.
- In life, the body, business, family and kingdom alike, most of the time goes into sustenance.
- Sustenance needs the **Vishnu principle and the Lakshmi principle** most of all.
- Small cycles keep running within the great story: school, a new branch, marriage. Brahma, Saraswati and Kali each have their role in every cycle.
- That is why **Mahalakshmi** is given the greatest importance in Sanatan Dharma.
