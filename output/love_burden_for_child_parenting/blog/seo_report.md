## 1. Summary

The talk describes **rajasic parenting**: parenting driven by rajas, the quality of desire, ambition and "I". Acharya Ji says "mother" here includes the father, and asks the reader to check themselves as they read. Its signs: constant pressure and timetables, very high expectations tied to the parent's own pride ("my son came first"), making the child carry the parent's unfinished dream, endless classes and comparison with other children, careers chosen for money and fame rather than the child's passion, and children dressed up for photos and social media. The talk's images are strong (a goat dragged to the butcher), and its conclusion is that rajas ends in suffering: such children leave home and look for love in the wrong places, and the parent rarely gets what they wanted. A sattvic parent lets a child be a child, and a home with more of God and less show is the answer. The main intent is **emotional / life problem** from parents ("बच्चों पर दबाव", "parental pressure on children", "am I a toxic parent"), with an **informational** layer around the gunas ("राजसिक परवरिश", "rajasic parenting"). Ranking pages are mostly psychology listicles and parenting-tip blogs; this post's angle is the guna lens (rajas versus sattva) and the parent's self-examination, which no content farm offers. Searches are steady through the year, with peaks around board-exam and results season (February to May) and admissions; refresh the post by January. The H1 "सपनों का बोझ और राजसिक परवरिश" / "The Burden of Dreams and Rajasic Parenting" is good but "rajasic" is a low-volume word, so the `<title>` leads with the parents' own search phrase.

## 6. FAQ

For the report only; not added to the page.

1. **राजसिक परवरिश क्या है? / What is rajasic parenting?**
   रजोगुण से प्रेरित परवरिश, जो इच्छा, महत्वाकांक्षा और "मैं" से चलती है: ऊंची अपेक्षाएं, दबाव और बच्चे के माध्यम से अपने सपने पूरे करवाना। / Parenting driven by rajas, the quality of desire, ambition and "I": high expectations, pressure, and living out one's own dreams through the child.
2. **क्या यह केवल माताओं की बात है? / Is this only about mothers?**
   नहीं। आचार्य जी कहते हैं कि यहां "माता" में पिता भी शामिल हैं। / No. Acharya Ji says that "mother" here includes fathers too.
3. **बच्चों पर अपेक्षाओं के बोझ का क्या परिणाम होता है? / What happens to children under this pressure?**
   आचार्य जी के अनुसार ऐसे बच्चे बड़े होकर घर छोड़ देते हैं और बाहर प्रेम ढूंढते हुए गलत जगह पहुंच जाते हैं; और माता-पिता को जो चाहिए था, वह अधिकतर नहीं मिलता। / According to Acharya Ji, such children leave home when they grow up and look for love in the wrong places, and the parents rarely get what they wanted.
4. **सात्त्विक माता-पिता क्या करते हैं? / What does a sattvic parent do?**
   वे बच्चे को स्वाभाविक रहने देते हैं: थोड़ा शालीन और प्रस्तुत करने योग्य, इतना पर्याप्त है; और घर में परमेश्वर अधिक, दिखावा कम रखते हैं। / They let the child be natural: reasonably neat and presentable is enough, and the home holds more of God and less show.
5. **क्या बच्चों को सजाना गलत है? / Is it wrong to dress children up?**
   नहीं। प्रश्न यह है कि घर में क्या भरा है: संसार और भौतिकता, या परमेश्वर। / No. The real question is what fills the home: the world and material things, or God.

## 10. Authenticity flags

1. **Sanskrit spelling (from the Sanskrit review).** "सात्विक" is written "सात्त्विक" by Gita Press; "महत्वाकांक्षा" may likewise be standardised to "महत्त्वाकांक्षा". This is a standardisation point, not an error of meaning. Recommendation: the editor decides one form for all blogs; SEO keywords and tags here use the common "राजसिक"/"सत्त्व गुण" forms already used on the site.
2. **"Rajas ends in suffering" is Acharya Ji's statement, not a quoted verse.** It agrees with Bhagavad Gita 14.16 ("रजसस्तु फलं दुःखम्", Gita Press), as the review notes, but the blog does not cite the verse. Recommendation: do not write "the Gita says" in titles, snippets or the FAQ unless the editor adds the reference to the blog; until then attribute the line to Acharya Ji, as sections 5 and 6 do.
3. **The video says "Parenting Tips", but the talk is diagnostic.** It describes rajasic parenting and its cost; it does not give a step-by-step list of what to do. Recommendation: do not promise "tips", "ways" or "how to reduce pressure" in the title or meta; the recommended titles promise "signs", which the post delivers. Hinglish "kaise kam kare" queries are kept at Low priority for this reason.
4. **The goat-and-butcher image and the consequences are strong.** Used out of context ("This is why YOUR child suffers"), they could read as fear or guilt bait for anxious parents. Recommendation: keep them in the body as the talk's own image, but do not use them as headline hooks or social cards; frame the post as self-examination, as Acharya Ji does ("पढ़ते हुए अपने भीतर भी जांचते चलिए").
5. **Outcomes are the teacher's observation, not research findings.** "Children leave home, fall into infidelity, marry wrongly" and "stress and anxiety" are general statements in the talk. Recommendation: do not present them as psychological or medical facts, add statistics, or position the post as mental-health advice.
6. **"Mother" includes fathers.** The talk says "माता" here means both parents. Recommendation: titles, meta and tags say "parents" / "माता-पिता" / "children", never "mothers" alone, so the post is not read as blaming mothers.
