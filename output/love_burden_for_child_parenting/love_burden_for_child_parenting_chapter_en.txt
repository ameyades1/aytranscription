# Chapter: The Rajasic Mother and the Burden of Expectations on Children

## Raising Children with a Rajasic Disposition

Now we speak of mothers with a rajasic disposition. A mother who raised children twenty years ago remains a mother, whether thirty years pass or forty. Her nature does not change. So how does she raise her children with a rajasic disposition? Listen carefully, and check within yourself whether you too are doing this.

Here, "mother" does not mean only the mother; the father is included too. This is about a disposition.

## Pressure, Expectation and the "I"

What will a rajasic mother do to her children? Put all the pressure on them. There will be a timetable, inspirational pictures on the wall saying "work hard". School, this to do, that to do. Such a mother sits constantly on the child's head.

And when she says, "My son is the topper", it is the "I" that is speaking. Such mothers' expectations are very high. They want some fruit. And if the children do not meet those expectations, they become upset.

They impose their ideas on their children: "I could not do it, son; you must fulfil this dream." "It was my childhood dream; my son will fulfil it." And then they are after the son. The son's passion may lie in some other direction, but by force he must do this.

## A Child Like a Goat Led to Slaughter

Now with how much interest will that child do the work? He sees his death there. It is like a goat being dragged by a rope, and the butcher telling it, "Now I am going to run a knife over your neck." With how much enthusiasm will it go? With that same enthusiasm these children listen to their parents, because, being small, they can do nothing.

## The End of Rajoguna Is Sorrow

But these same children, when grown, leave these mothers or fathers and go away. Then, searching outside for that same love, they either fall into immorality or marry in the wrong place. This happens often. Or they rebel and simply run away from home. They wait only to grow up so that they can be free.

So the end of rajoguna is sorrow. In their kingdom too, what happens in the end? What the mother wanted, she mostly does not get. And even if she gets it, it comes in the wrong form.

## Comparison and Endless Classes

Such mothers' expectations are very high. Whether the child has that much capacity or not, they do not know, yet: enrol in classes, two of them, three of them.

Such mothers always compare their children with others: "He sat for that exam; next time we will get ahead of him. He spoke at that function; at the next function we will do something special to show."

## The Race for Money and Fame

Such mothers push their children towards material success and fame, which leads to stress and anxiety in children. "Son, you must come first." "Become an engineer, because this profession pays the most."

Their upbringing is just like this: "This profession has the most money, so do this, do an MBA, or do that." Not because the child has a passion for it, but because it brings the most money, or name and fame. That alone is their reason for choosing a profession.

## A Child Dressed Up for Show

Rajasic mothers encourage their children to look perfect on social media or in public, just for photos. A sattvic mother, by contrast, is simple. She says, "Let them be natural; it is a small child, let them be as they are." "As they are" does not mean completely untidy; a little decent, presentable, that is fine. But a child is a child; let the child be like a child.

Rajasic mothers, though, give the child full makeup. I have seen so many mothers, from far, from near, everywhere; they do this. Full makeup, just like film heroes and heroines, because the child has to appear in a photo, on social media, at a function. Even real heroines do not do as much as these mothers make their children do.

I am not saying do not dress up your children, or do not give them good clothes. But making them dance to film songs from now on, is that even their age? Do they even understand what the words mean? Those love songs, and children of eight or nine are told to dance to them.

Because these mothers' own lives are filled with exactly that: the world, materialism. There is little of God and much of this. So from childhood the influence of TV serials has already settled on the child: the same dialogues, the same dances, the same clothes. All this happens in the homes of rajasic mothers.
