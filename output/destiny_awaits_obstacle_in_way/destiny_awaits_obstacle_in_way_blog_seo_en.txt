# Your Destiny Is Already Flowing: Just Clear the Obstacle

Do you feel that to achieve something great in life, you have to push harder, run faster and ask for more? Acharya Upendra Ji turns this way of thinking on its head with one simple example.

His message is this: what you are searching for already lies within you. Your only task is to remove whatever is blocking the way.

So what is blocking your destiny? Acharya Ji explains that the water of knowledge and the channel of your destiny already exist. The block is the debris of maya: desire, ego and selfishness. As Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (4.3) teach, our efforts do not drive nature; they only remove the obstruction. Clear it, and the flow takes care of itself.

## A Field, a Channel and a Stone

In the Yoga Sutras, Maharshi Patanjali offers an illustration. Picture a field with a channel dug to water it, and a tap that runs without stopping. Naturally, the water should reach every corner of the field.

But right at the mouth of the channel, a clump of debris or a stone has lodged itself. The water cannot move forward.

What should you do? Open the tap wider? Dig the channel broader? No. Just pull out the debris.

> **nimittam aprayojakaṃ prakṛtīnāṃ**
> **varaṇabhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat**
> "The instrumental cause does not set nature in motion; it only removes the obstruction, just as a farmer breaks open the embankment and the water flows into the field by itself."
> (Yoga Sutras 4.3)

This is the very sutra in which the image of the farmer, the **kshetrika** (one who tends a field), appears. Our efforts do not drive nature forward. They clear away the covering that blocks it, and the flow then takes care of itself.

As Acharya Ji explains, the water was there all along, and so was the path. The debris was the only problem.

## Do You Need to Keep Repeating Your Goal?

Acharya Ji says you do not need to repeat the aim of **Nar se Narayan** (rising from the human to the Divine) ten times, or a thousand times. Nor do you need to chant "I must do something for the nation, I must, I must" over and over.

All of this already exists within you.

In this illustration, the water stands for **tattvajnana** (knowledge of the ultimate truth). The channel is your **destiny**, the entire script of your life. In the light of knowledge, everything was already in place.

What, then, is the blockage? It is the debris of **maya** (the illusion that veils reality): "I just want money," desire after desire after desire, all that "me, me" and selfishness. That is what you need to remove. **Brahman** (the supreme reality) already dwells within everyone.

*Which stone is stuck in your channel today: a desire, your ego, or some selfish motive?*

## The Sun Has Good Manners

Now a second example. Outside, the sun is already shining. You do not have to fetch it from anywhere. Your house is dark only because you have drawn the curtains.

With a smile, Acharya Ji calls the sun "a very decent person." It will never force its way into your home. If you keep the curtains closed, it stays outside and falls no further than the curtain.

The wider you open the curtains, the more light comes in, because the sun was there all along.

The sun here represents your **brahma bhava** (awareness of the Divine within) and **atma bhava** (awareness of your true nature). It is radiant by nature, and the thicker the curtain of **jada buddhi** (a dull, inert intellect), the more of it stays hidden.

You never have to light it with a matchstick. It is already there.

## How Do Sattvic, Rajasic and Tamasic Minds Handle Obstacles?

The real question is what we do with this debris. Here the difference between the **trigunas** (the three qualities of nature: **sattva**, purity; **rajas**, restless activity; and **tamas**, inertia) becomes clear.

**A sattvic mind** prays: "May this debris never enter me. O Lord Ganesha, remove every obstacle." The obstacles in this prayer are exactly this debris.

**A rajasic person** does the opposite and asks for more obstacles: a little more money, a little more status, a little more recognition. In other words, they ask for even more debris.

**A tamasic person** not only piles up their own debris but goes and dumps it into other people's channels too. At times they even try to shut off the tap altogether. They never succeed, but they keep trying.

Such people mistake **adharma** (unrighteousness) for **dharma** (righteousness) and dharma for adharma. They turn against dharma and against the Guru. Otherwise, Acharya Ji asks, why would every story of a Guru include an enemy? This is tamas at its extreme, and after all, every epic needs its Duryodhana, the scheming prince who opposed the Pandavas in the Mahabharata.

*When an obstacle appears in your life, do you pray for it to be removed, or do you unknowingly make it bigger by asking for "just a little more"?*

## Key Takeaways

- The water (tattvajnana) and the channel (destiny) already exist; you only need to clear away the debris of maya.
- You do not need to keep repeating the goal of "Nar se Narayan"; it already lives within you.
- The sun never forces its way in; the wider you open the curtain, the more light enters.
- A sattvic mind prays for obstacles to be removed.
- A rajasic mind adds to the debris by asking for more money, status and recognition.
- A tamasic mind dumps debris on others' paths and turns dharma and adharma upside down.
