# Chapter: Destiny Awaits, Only the Obstacle Must Be Removed

## Maharishi Patanjali's Field and Canal

In his Yoga Darshan, Maharishi Patanjali gives a beautiful example. There is a field, and a canal has been made to bring water to it. The tap has been left running continuously, and water is flowing through it. Now tell me, will the water reach the whole field or not?

But at one spot, right at the beginning, some debris has fallen into the channel. What we call a *roda*, some rubbish or a stone, has got stuck there. Now the water is not moving ahead. So what should be done? Should the tap be opened with more force, or should that debris simply be removed? What is really needed for the water to move forward?

> **nimittam aprayojakaṁ prakṛtīnāṁ varaṇabhedas tu tataḥ kṣetrikavat**
> "The instrumental cause does not set nature in motion; it only removes the obstruction, like a farmer."
> (Patanjali Yoga Sutra 4.3)

The farmer's example is found in this very sutra of the Yoga Sutras: external means do not drive nature, they only remove the obstruction, just as a farmer breaks the ridge of the field and lets the water flow on its own.

The answer is simple: removing the debris is enough. The water was already present. The path was already present. The only problem was that debris. There is no need to release the water more forcefully, nor to widen the canal.

## From Nar to Narayan: The Goal Is Already Within

That is why it is said that the aim of "from *nar* to *Narayan*" (from man to the Divine) need not be recalled ten times. Do you understand? There is no need to repeat this aim a thousand times. Not this chanting of "I must do something for the nation, I must, I must." All of this is already present within you.

That water is *tattvajnana*, the knowledge of truth. It is present just as it always was. That canal is your destiny, the entire script of your life. In knowledge, everything was already as it is. You have placed the debris of *maya* in between. Only that veil of maya has to be removed.

"I want only money, money; I want desire, desire and more desire": all this sense of "I, I", this selfishness, removing it is the only work. As for the rest, Brahman is already within everyone.

## The Sun and the Curtain

The sun is already present outside. You do not have to bring a sun separately. You have hung a curtain, and that is why there is darkness in your home. Is this clear?

And the sun is very civil and gracious. It will not force its way into your home. It will say:

> "You have closed the curtain, haven't you? I will not come in. I will wait outside. I will fall only up to the curtain. I will not enter the house. You do not wish it, so I will not come."

The sun will not say, "No, I will come inside no matter what." You do not want it, and that is exactly why you closed the curtain. The sun says:

> "As much as you open, that much I will come in. I am already present."

This sun is the symbol of the **sense of Brahman**, the **sense of the Self**, within you. It is already there, full of light. It shines according to how much of the curtain of dull intellect you have drawn. The sun does not need to be lit with a matchstick. It is within, it is already there. The only work is to remove the debris.

## The Resolve of the Sattvic Intellect

So what should our resolve be? The **sattvic intellect** says, "May none of this debris enter me. O Ganapati, may all obstacles go away; I want no obstacles." And which obstacles? All these very obstacles, this very debris. Is the point clear?

## The Rajasic Person: Asking for More Debris

Now the **rajasic** person, on the contrary, asks for more obstacles: a little more money, a little more honour, a little more respect. That is, they talk only of increasing the debris further.

## The Tamasic Person: Debris in Others' Canals Too

And the **tamasic** person even thinks of going and dumping debris into other people's canals. They increase their own debris anyway, and they go and put some into others' as well. Sometimes they even try to shut off the tap. They will not be able to shut it, but they certainly try.

On the contrary, they take dharma to be adharma and adharma to be dharma. They do not remove the debris, they double it, and on top of that they try to shut off the tap. Such people go against dharma and against the Guru. Such people exist, don't they, who stand in opposition to the Guru? If it were not so, an enemy camp would never appear in the Guru's story. This is extreme tamas. You need a Duryodhana too, don't you? Is the point now properly clear?
