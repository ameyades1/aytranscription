# Do You Act, or Does Nature Make You Act

## Am I Really the Doer?

We all assume that whatever we do is our own decision. But is that really so?

This whole world is divided into three **gunas** (qualities of nature): **sattva** (purity and peace), **rajas** (movement and the desire for enjoyment) and **tamas** (inertia and darkness). Place, person, object, everything is divided according to them.

Acharya Ji says that everything has been divided according to sattva, rajas and tamas, yet in our ego we keep believing that we are the ones performing action.

## The Combination of Gunas Creates Action

In every human being, at any moment, some guna or other is dominant. Every place also has its own guna. And so do the objects around us: those in our hands, those on display, those we see in the world.

Action happens from the combination of all these. The Bhagavad Gita says exactly this:

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ**
> **ahaṅkāra-vimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate**
> "All actions are performed in every way by the gunas of nature; yet one whose mind is deluded by ego thinks, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

All actions happen through the gunas of nature, but one whose **antahkarana** (inner instrument: mind, intellect, memory, ego) is deluded by ego believes, "I am the doer."

*Which guna is dominant within you at this very moment?*

## The Goa Beach: A Rajasic Stage

The atmosphere of the Goa beach (not the sea, the mood of the beach) is **rajasic**. Now a rajasic person arrives there.

At the office he may be a senior executive in a suit and boots. But here? Instantly a shiny, flowery silk shirt, bright green or yellow shorts, and multicoloured sunglasses.

Why did this thought arise in his mind? Why didn't he go into the sea in a suit and boots?

Acharya Ji asks whether he really performed that action, or whether nature made him do it.

## The Meeting of Within and Without

This was not the work of outer nature alone. The **guna within** him and the **guna without** met, and the action happened.

But what if a saint, a mahatma in saffron robes, who has reached **kaivalya** (the state of ultimate liberation), goes there? It will make no difference to him at all. This too is an action, and this too is already decided.

## The Liquor Den: A Gateway of Tamas

A liquor den is a **tamasic** place. If a sattvic person arrives there, he will cover his nose with a cloth and walk away.

And the tamasic person? He will run in like an animal with his tongue hanging out, hoping to get something to eat and drink.

There is a fixed combination here, a fixed formula. As the combination, so the behaviour.

## What Is in Your Hands?

Society is not in your hands. Nature is both outside and within. You cannot change the nature outside, but the one within?

When these combinations begin to make sense, **changing your own gunas becomes your duty**. If you do not change them, your surroundings will keep making you act, and in your ego you will keep believing, "I am doing it."

Acharya Ji explains that the one who dies believing "I perform action" is the one who becomes a pitar.

What it means to become a pitar is the subject of a later discussion.

*How many of your decisions are truly yours, and how many are formed by the combination of your gunas and your surroundings?*

## Key Takeaways

- Places, people and objects in the world are all divided into sattva, rajas and tamas.
- The combination of inner and outer gunas creates action (Gita 3.27).
- A rajasic person in a rajasic place, or a tamasic person in a tamasic place, behaves according to a fixed formula.
- The surroundings have no effect on a saint who has reached kaivalya.
- Outer nature is not in your hands; changing your own gunas is your duty.
- One who dies with the ego "I am the doer" becomes a pitar.
