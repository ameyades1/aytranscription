# Chapter: Who Performs Action? The Play of the Gunas

## Everything Is Divided by the Gunas

This whole world is divided according to three gunas: sattva, rajas and tamas. Place, person, object, everything has been divided according to these qualities. And a human being, steeped in ego, keeps believing, "I perform action." So whose play is this? First understand this.

Each one forms its own combination. A human being too, at any given moment, will have some guna or other. Can you recognise this guna within yourself right now or not? In the same way, every place has its own guna. The object in your hand, the objects you find or see there, those on display, or those visible out in the world, it is from the combination of all these that action happens.

## Who Is the Doer?

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ**
> **ahaṅkāra-vimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate**
> "All actions are performed in every way by the gunas of nature; yet one whose mind is deluded by ego thinks, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

In truth, all actions are performed in every way by the qualities of nature. Yet the ignorant one, whose inner instrument is deluded by ego, believes, "I am the doer."

## The Example of the Goa Beach

Suppose you went to Goa. What is the guna of the Goa beach? Here I am speaking not of the sea but of the atmosphere of the beach. It is rajasic.

Now, if a rajasic person goes there, what will happen? At the office he may be a suit-and-boot wearing chief executive of a big organisation, yet here he will immediately put on a flowery shirt and flowery silk shorts, won't he? Shiny, silky, flowery fabric, shorts in silver or bright green, then dark blue or yellow, and multicoloured sunglasses too.

How did his attire become rajasic so instantly? Do you think he did it, that it happened because of him? Why did such a thought come to his mind? What made him think that way? Why didn't he go there in a suit and boots? Does anyone go to bathe in the sea in a suit and boots?

So tell me clearly: did he perform that action, or did nature make him do it?

## The Guna Within and the Guna Without

It was not only nature outside; the nature within him was also at work. The guna inside him and the guna outside, from the combination of these two, this action was formed.

But if there is a saint, a mahatma, who wears saffron robes and has reached the state of kaivalya, with the guna that is his, if he goes there, how will he behave? It will make no difference to him at all. When this combination forms, nothing changes. This too is an action, and this too is already decided. Whenever you see such a combination, this same action will happen.

## The Example of the Liquor Den

A liquor den comes into view, that is, a tamasic place. If a sattvic person goes there, what will happen? He will cover his nose with a cloth and walk away, won't he?

And the tamasic person? He will run there with his tongue hanging out like an animal, with the feeling, "Will I get something to eat and drink even now?"

So whose play is this? First understand this. There is a fixed combination, a fixed formula, and the behaviour will be exactly that.

## Changing Your Gunas Is Your Duty

Now the question is: is society in your hands, or are you yourself in your hands? Nature is both outside and within. Is the nature outside in your hands, or the one within you, your own?

As these combinations become clear to you, changing your own gunas becomes your duty. If the gunas are not changed, actions will keep being performed through you according to your surroundings, and you, steeped in ego, will always keep believing, "I perform action."

> "And the one who dies thinking 'I perform action' is the one who becomes a pitar."

What it means to become a pitar will come later. Have you understood this?
