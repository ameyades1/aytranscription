# Are You the One Acting, or Is Nature Acting Through You?

## Am I Really the Doer?

We all take it for granted that everything we do is our own decision. But is that actually true?

The three gunas and human behaviour are linked like this: at any moment one guna dominates each of us, each place and object has its own quality too, and action arises when the two meet. The Bhagavad Gita (3.27) says every action is carried out by the gunas of nature, yet the ego-deluded person believes, "I am the doer."

The whole world is made up of three **gunas** (qualities of nature): **sattva** (purity and calm), **rajas** (restless activity and the craving for enjoyment) and **tamas** (inertia and darkness). Every place, every person and every object falls under one of them.

Acharya Ji points out that although everything is divided along the lines of sattva, rajas and tamas, ego keeps us convinced that we are the ones who act.

## How Do the Gunas Influence Our Actions?

At any given moment, one guna or another dominates each of us. Every place carries a quality of its own too, and so do the things around us: what we hold in our hands, what is laid out in front of us, what we see in the world.

Action arises when all of these come together. The Bhagavad Gita says exactly this:

> **prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ**
> **ahaṅkāravimūḍhātmā kartāham iti manyate**
> "Every action is carried out entirely by the gunas of nature, yet a person whose **antahkarana** (inner instrument: mind, intellect, memory and ego) is deluded by ego believes, 'I am the doer.'"
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.27)

*Which guna is strongest in you right now?*

## A Beach in Goa: A Stage Set for Rajas

The atmosphere of a Goa beach (the scene along the shore, not the sea itself) is **rajasic**. Now picture a rajasic person arriving there.

At the office, this might be a senior executive in a suit and polished shoes. But here? Out comes a shiny silk shirt covered in flowers, bright green or yellow shorts and a pair of multicoloured sunglasses.

Where did that idea come from? Why didn't this person walk into the sea in a suit and shoes?

Acharya Ji asks: did they really choose to act this way, or did nature make them do it?

## Where the Inner Meets the Outer

Nature outside was not working alone. The quality **within** the person met the quality **around** them, and the action followed.

But what if a saint in saffron robes, someone who has reached **kaivalya** (the state of ultimate liberation), went to the same beach? It would make no difference to them at all. Their response is an action too, and it is just as predetermined.

## The Liquor Den: A Doorway to Tamas

A place where people gather to drink is **tamasic**. A sattvic person who wanders in will hold a cloth over their nose and leave.

And a tamasic person? They will rush in like an animal with its tongue hanging out, hoping to find something to eat and drink.

There is a fixed pattern at work here, a set formula: the combination decides the behaviour.

## What Lies Within Your Control?

Society is not in your hands. Nature exists both outside you and inside you. You cannot change the nature outside, but what about the nature within?

Once you begin to see how these combinations work, **changing your own gunas becomes your duty**. If you don't, your surroundings will keep making you act, while ego goes on telling you, "I am the one doing this."

Acharya Ji explains that those who die believing "I am the doer" become a **pitar** (an ancestral spirit).

What it means to become a pitar is a subject for a later discussion.

*How many of your decisions are truly yours, and how many grow out of the meeting between your gunas and your surroundings?*

## Key Takeaways

- Every place, person and object in the world falls under sattva, rajas or tamas.
- Action arises when the gunas within you meet the gunas around you (Gita 3.27).
- A rajasic person in a rajasic place, like a tamasic person in a tamasic place, behaves according to a fixed formula.
- Surroundings have no hold over a saint who has reached kaivalya.
- You cannot control nature outside you, but changing your own gunas is your duty.
- Those who die with the ego of "I am the doer" become pitars.
