# The Company You Keep Shapes Your Destiny

## The Simple Secret of Staying Sattvic

Have you ever wondered why the peace you gain from your practice fades within a few days? Acharya Ji answers this very simply: the company you keep and spiritual growth go hand in hand. Your thoughts are shaped by the people you live among, and your conduct follows your thoughts. Time with sattvic people and with the Guru keeps your sattva strong, while rajasic company slowly pulls it down.

Keeping alive your inner **sattva** (the quality of purity, clarity and peace) is not difficult. It comes from staying close to people of a sattvic nature, and from keeping the company of those who lead you towards truth.

If the people around you are **rajasic** (driven by restlessness, show and desire), your inner state will slowly begin to slide. Your **samskaras** (deep impressions that shape your character) form from the surroundings you live in and the people you spend time with.

Acharya Ji explains that your conduct follows your thoughts.

And what shapes your thoughts? The people you live among.

*Are the five people you spend the most time with today leading you towards truth, or away from it?*

## What Should You Do When You Outgrow Your Family?

As you rise in life, you may feel that you have moved far ahead of your brothers, sisters and relatives. Acharya Ji says that this is the moment to understand that their world and yours are now different. You do not need to meet them very often.

When you go to a family function, come down to their level. Talk about whatever they enjoy at that time. Do not go on about how this happened for you and that worked out for you.

If you must say something, speak in a way that lifts them up: "You could start a business too." If they like it, good. If not, settle back easily to their level.

## "It Is the Company You Keep"

Acharya Ji says that the company you keep is what decides everything.

The people you surround yourself with set the direction of your life. Having come this far, you will need to practise a little **tyaga** (letting go) with some of your old ties.

Friends of a new level need to come into your life. Then your whole circle will change. Visit your relatives by birth from time to time. This is how your sattva stays intact.

## Where Does the Fall Begin?

By the grace of the Guru, you rise. Happiness begins to enter your life, and you get bound up in that very comfort. Along with it comes the pull of **tamas** (the quality of inertia and attachment).

That pull draws the mind back towards the same old relationships. You step down and start showing them your restless ambition and your achievements.

Yet they have no interest in how high you have climbed. You begin to resent them, they speak ill of you, and slowly your sattva starts to fall.

*Have you ever gone to show your progress to people who had no interest in it at all?*

## What Choices Do You Have?

Acharya Ji lays out clear paths:

- Either bring them up to your level.
- Or bring your new friends into their circle.
- Otherwise, come down to their level for a while to meet them, knowing that the time will be wasted.

So bring new people into your life at the level you have reached: new friends, new everything, a new family.

## Why Do Great Souls Leave Home?

This is why great souls often leave home. You can have only one of the two. If you achieve something truly great in the world outside, home is bound to slip away little by little.

Very few people have their whole family rise along with them. To keep their sattva intact, they have no other choice. They need to spend as much time as possible with the Guru.

Acharya Ji points out that nature itself is built this way.

At home, you will often find people of rajas and tamas, caught in **maya** (the illusion that binds us to the world). That is how nature is designed.

## A Reference from the Ganesha Purana

Referring to the Ganesha Purana, a scripture devoted to Lord Ganesha, Acharya Ji explains that most relatives are there for their own gain. He sees this as a **tattva** (fundamental truth), one that applies to every family.

## Even Avatars and Great Souls Faced It at Home

What happened in Shri Rama's own home? "Send him to the forest for fourteen years." This happened in the household of Shri Rama himself.

Shri Krishna's family had Kamsa, his own maternal uncle. There was a demon in his very home.

Even in the household of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, his queen Soyarabai would say, "Keep this kingdom for my son. Give your Sambhaji Raje the lower south, and give Rajaram the upper part."

When this happens in the homes of such great avatars and great souls, how can you claim that your home has no rajasic or tamasic people?

## The Choice of Discernment

A person with **viveka** (the ability to tell right from wrong) understands that most people nearby, and most relatives, will be of rajas and tamas. Such a person then sees clear paths ahead:

- I am doing great work, so I will have to rise above them.
- Or I will have to lift them up and bring them along.
- Or I will have to come down to their level and talk about what they enjoy.
- Or I will have to build a new family, a new everything.

And if you cannot break the relationship, go and meet them now and then, on their level.

This is how you can keep your sattva intact, and there is no other way.

*Which relationships in your life lift you up, and after meeting whom do you feel yourself sinking inside?*

## Key Takeaways

- The simple way to stay sattvic is to keep the company of sattvic people who lead you towards truth.
- Your conduct follows your thoughts, and your company shapes your thoughts.
- Showing off your achievements to old relationships after you rise pulls your sattva down.
- Meet old relatives occasionally on their level, and bring friends of a new level into your life.
- Citing the Ganesha Purana, Acharya Ji explains that most relatives are there for their own gain, and the homes of Shri Rama, Shri Krishna and Shivaji Maharaj were no exception.
- Spending as much time as possible with the Guru is essential to keep your sattva intact.
