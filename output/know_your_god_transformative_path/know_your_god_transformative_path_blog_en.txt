# Know God, Do Not Merely Believe

Many of us say we are devotees of Ganapati, of Shiva, of the Goddess. But Acharya Upendra Ji asks a simple question: do we even know them?

## Why Were Idols and Images Given?

The scriptures say the formless Supreme is hard to understand, so a form was created for seekers.

> **sādhakānāṁ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the benefit of seekers, a form of Brahman is conceived."
> (Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad)

The form is a means, not the destination. Acharya Ji warns that if we drown in that form and stay there, we will not move forward.

## Could You Marry a Photograph?

Acharya Ji gives an example. Suppose someone finds a stranger's picture in a newspaper. He does not know the person, nor where they live. Yet he says, "I have fallen in love with this person, this is my life partner," and talks to the picture every day.

> "If you keep doing that, will people think you are mad or not?"

Acharya Ji says the form of religion is slowly becoming just like this. An idol has taken the place of the picture, but the knowing still has not happened.

*The God you worship: how much do you truly know about Him?*

## Devotion, Love or Self-Interest?

The first step of **bhakti** (devotion) is **love**. Acharya Ji asks whether there was really love before our devotion, or only demands: "O God, give me this, do my work, remove my obstacles."

> "A person connects either through love or through self-interest."

And if there is love, there will also be a wish to know. Love without knowing becomes **andhashraddha** (blind faith).

## What Does the Gita Say?

The Bhagavad Gita and the Ganesh Gita share one message: know God, reach God. Merely believing is not enough. According to Acharya Ji, our rishis say that believing without knowing is just like marrying a photograph.

So the questions must come first. Why does Ganapati have this form? If "the Lord of all is one", why so many forms? Is my God limited to my religion?

*Have you ever had the courage to question your own faith?*

## When Even Names Were Divided

Acharya Ji tells a story. At Diwali an acquaintance served him **shankarpale**, but called them only "pale", because his guru had forbidden saying the word "Shankar". He was a devotee of Vishnu.

Yet Shankar is the one Vishnu worships, and Vishnu the one Shankar worships. And Ganapati is the son of that very Shankar.

Another person fell into the same confusion. Seeing the Bhagavad Gita taught, he thought these were **Vaishnavas**; seeing Navratri, he thought they were **Shaktas**; and seeing Shivaratri, he said they were **Shaivas** and this could not be a real guru.

## The Ganesh Gita's Answer: Non-Difference Is Yoga

Shri Ganesha himself answers this confusion in the Ganesh Gita.

> **aham eva mahāviṣṇur aham eva sadāśivaḥ**
> **aham eva mahāśaktir aham evāryamā priya**
> "I alone am Mahavishnu, I alone am Sadashiva, I alone am Mahashakti, and I alone am the Sun."
> (Ganesh Gita, Chapter 1)

> **śive viṣṇau ca śaktau ca sūrye mayi narādhipa**
> **yā'bhedabuddhir yogaḥ sa samyag yogo mato mama**
> "The understanding that sees no difference between Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and me is true yoga."
> (Ganesh Gita, Chapter 1)

Seeing no difference, **abheda buddhi**, between Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and Ganesha is true **yoga**.

## Key Takeaways

- A form is a means for seekers; getting stuck in it is not the goal.
- Believing in God without knowing Him is like marrying a photograph.
- True devotion begins with love, not self-interest, and love awakens the wish to know.
- The Bhagavad Gita and the Ganesh Gita both say: know God.
- Seeing no difference between Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and Ganesha is yoga.
