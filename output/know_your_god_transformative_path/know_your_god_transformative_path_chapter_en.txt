# Chapter: Know God, Do Not Merely Believe

## The Purpose of the Form

Maharshi Vedavyasa himself says, and he too portrayed many divine forms, that:

> **sādhakānāṃ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the benefit of seekers, a form of Brahman is conceived."
> (Traditional saying; cf. Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad 1.7)

That is, for the benefit of seekers, a form of Brahman has been conceived. This form has been given so that we may attain and understand the real Supreme Being. The Supreme is formless, and the formless does not come easily to our understanding. So, to explain it, we were given a form.

This much is understood. But if you drown in that form and remain there, not moving further, it will not do.

## Love for a Photograph

I always give an example. Someone has a picture of Ganapati. Now suppose someone has a picture of a girl who has not yet even become his friend. I could also give the example of a mother, but we know our mother. So I am saying: a picture from some random newspaper, or found somewhere else, has been given to you. If you are a woman, consider it a picture of a man.

You do not know him, you do not recognise him, you have no idea where he lives. And you say straight away, "I have fallen in love with this person." How many people would say that? Yes, seeing someone in a magazine can certainly attract you, but can anyone say, "I have fallen in love"?

And then he says, "This is my wife," or "This is my husband, our marriage is fixed." After that he talks to that same picture every day, "I love you, I love you." How would that look? Would people think he is mad or not? People would say nothing else.

## The Same Is Happening in Religion

Slowly, the shape of our religion is becoming the same. In place of the picture we were given an idol, as if the picture had become three-dimensional, like in a wax museum. Such a form was given to you. You do not even know it, do not even recognise it, have no idea of it, and you say, "I am connected to it, I have fallen in love with you."

## Love or Self-Interest?

The first step of devotion is love. We say, "My devotion is to Ganapati," or "to Shiva," or "to the Goddess." So, was there love before the devotion? If not, it is self-interest. A person connects either through love or through self-interest.

So, on what has your relationship rested until now? On self-interest. "O God, give me this." God gives, does my work, removes obstacles; that alone was the reason.

And if there truly is love, then you should know Him. Otherwise this is a false love, a love of blind faith. Does anyone marry a photograph?

## To Know, Not Merely to Believe

This very thing is said in the Bhagavad Gita, and the same in the Ganesh Gita. Both clearly say that God is to be **known**, not merely **believed in**.

We are merely believing in Ganesh, as if someone had imposed the form of Ganapati on us. Our own rishis say this will not do, it is wrong. If we do this, exactly the same thing will happen: marriage with a photograph, love with a photograph. Who she is, we do not know; where she lives, we do not know.

In the same way, where is this God? We have not seen. Does anyone know where He is? Has anyone seen Him? Just as the girl in that picture may already be married to someone else while you sit holding the photograph, so too there must be some true devotee who has actually attained Ganesh. The picture is real, it is of some girl, but whether she is for me or not, that is the question.

I said this only as an example. Ganapati can belong to thousands, whereas a wife belongs to only one person. I am not comparing that; I am only giving an example. You must have understood what I want to convey.

## Questions Should Arise in the Mind

The Ganesh Gita and the Bhagavad Gita say: know God, do not merely believe. Reach Him. So the first question that should arise in your mind is: why is it so? Why the elephant face?

The second question should arise: how did there come to be so many forms, when it is said "the Master of all is One"? If the Master of all is One, then why is Ganapati not among those of other religions? Is my God limited to my religion? And if we say He is in everyone, why do they not accept that Ganapati is in everyone?

We say, "Shiva is ours, we feel devotion for Him." And here another devotee is saying, "Yours is different, ours is different."

## The Story of the Shankarpale

There was a gentleman we knew. It was Diwali time, and we had gone to his house. At Diwali, faral and such snacks are made, are they not? He brought a diamond-shaped snack. You will understand which one I mean.

He said to me, "Have some pale, pale." I asked, "What is that? I know what it is, it is called shankarpale." He replied, "Our guru has forbidden us to say Shankar. We are devotees of Vishnu."

What kind of thing is this! Shankar is the one Vishnu worships, and Vishnu is the one Shankar worships. And Ganapati is a form of Vishnu. If he read the story of Ganapati, he would understand that this Ganapati is the son of that very Shankar.

## Vaishnava, Shakta or Shaiva?

Another incident. One of our seekers encouraged a friend of his to come here: "Come, come here. Here they teach everything about Ganesh; first they teach the Bhagavad Gita." The friend decided that these must be Vaishnava people.

A few days later the seeker said, "Navratri has come, now they teach about the Goddess." Now he was confused: they teach Navratri too, the worship of Durga, so they are Shaktas, not Vaishnavas. For a while he attended both discourses, but the judging in his mind kept going.

A few days later he was invited for Shivaratri, and he felt these must be Shaivas. In the end he concluded that this cannot be a guru at all, that he is not a true guru.

## The True Yoga of the Ganesh Gita

Yet in the very first chapter of the Ganesh Gita there is a verse describing what is called yoga. If you have the Ganesh Gita, in it Lord Ganesh Himself says that I am Shiva, I am the Goddess, I am everything: Vishnu, Rudra, Brahma. These are the two verses:

> **aham eva mahāviṣṇur aham eva sadāśivaḥ**
> **aham eva mahāśaktir aham evāryamā priya**
> "O dear one, I alone am Mahavishnu, I alone am Sadashiva, I alone am Mahashakti and I alone am the Sun."
> (Shri Ganesh Gita 1.19)

O dear one, I alone am Mahavishnu, I alone am Sadashiva, I alone am Mahashakti, and I alone am the Sun.

> **śive viṣṇau ca śaktau ca sūrye mayi narādhipa**
> **yā'bhedabuddhir yogaḥ sa samyag yogo mato mama**
> "O king, the understanding of non-difference between Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and Me: that is yoga, and I hold it to be true yoga."
> (Shri Ganesh Gita 1.21)

O king, the understanding of non-difference among Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and Me, that is, Ganesh, is yoga, and that alone I consider true yoga.

When this vision of non-difference awakens, the division between "your God" and "my God" disappears, and the journey of going beyond merely believing in God to truly knowing Him begins.
