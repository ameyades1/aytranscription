# Chapter: Know God, Do Not Merely Believe

## Why a Form Was Given

Vedavyasa Ji himself says, for he too created many such images, that a form of the Supreme was conceived for the sake of seekers.

> **sādhakānāṁ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the benefit of seekers, a form of Brahman is conceived."
> (Rama Purva Tapaniya Upanishad)

To attain the real Supreme, to understand it, we were given a form, because the formless is not understood at once. That much is clear. But if you drown in that form and stay there, it will not work.

## The Example of Loving a Photograph

I always give an example. Someone has a picture of Ganapati. Now suppose someone has a picture of a young woman he does not even know. I could have given the example of a mother, but we know our mother. Someone hands you a picture found in a newspaper or somewhere else, of a woman, or of a young man. You do not know them, do not recognise them, have no idea where they live.

And you say straight away, "I have fallen in love with this person!" How many people would say that? You may be attracted by seeing someone in a magazine, but can you say, "I have fallen in love"? And then say, "This is my wife, this is my husband, our marriage is settled," and talk to that picture every day: "I love you, I love you not." If you keep doing that, will people think you are mad or not? They will say nothing else.

## Religion Has Come to the Same Point

Slowly, the form of our religion is becoming just like this. In place of a picture we were even given an idol, a three-dimensional version, as in a wax museum. But you do not know it, do not recognise it, know nothing about it, and you say, "I am connected to it, I have fallen in love with you."

## Love Before Devotion, and Knowledge Before Love

The first step of devotion is love. We say, "My devotion is to Ganapati, or to Shiva, or to the Goddess." So was there love before the devotion? If not, it is self-interest. A person connects either through love or through self-interest. What has yours been so far? Self-interest. "O God, give me this." God gives, does my work, removes obstacles; that was the reason.

And if there is love, you should know the one you love. Otherwise it is a blind-faith love in which nothing is known. Does anyone ever marry a photograph?

## The Message of the Gita: Know, Do Not Merely Believe

The Bhagavad Gita and the Ganesh Gita both say the same thing: God is to be known. Nowhere does it say God is merely to be believed. We merely believe that Ganesha has such a form, as if someone has imposed Ganapati on you. Our own rishis say this does not work, it is wrong. If you do this, exactly the same thing will happen: marriage with a photograph, love with a photograph. Who she is, you do not know; where she lives, you do not know.

Where is this God, anyway? We have not seen. Has anyone seen? In the photograph example, that young man would long since have married someone else, and you are sitting there holding his picture! There must also be some true devotee who has actually attained Ganesha. That is why this path is wrong. The photograph is real, it is of some young woman, but whether she is meant for me, that I need to know.

I told this only as an example. Ganapati can belong to thousands; a wife belongs to only one person. I am not talking about that; it is just an example. Do you understand what I want to convey?

## The Questions Must Come First

Ganesha says, and the Bhagavad Gita says, know God, reach God. It does not say believe. So the first question in your mind should be: why so? Why the form with an elephant's face?

The second question should be: how did there come to be so many forms, when it was said only once? People say "the Lord of all is one", don't they? If the Lord of all is one, why is there no Ganapati among those of other religions? Is my God limited to my religion? And if we say He is in everyone, why do they not accept that Ganapati is in everyone? "Shiva Ji is ours, we feel devotion." Why, here the other devotee is also saying, "Yours is different, ours is different."

## The Story of the Shankarpale

There was a person we knew. It was Diwali time and we had gone to his home. At Diwali faral is made, so he brought a diamond-shaped sweet, you will know the one. He said to me, "Have some pale, pale!" I knew what it was; it is called shankarpale. He said, "Our guru has forbidden us to say 'Shankar'. We are devotees of Vishnu."

Why, Shankar is the one Vishnu worships, and Vishnu is the one Shankar worships! Ganapati is a form of Vishnu; read his story and you will understand. And Ganapati is the son of that very Shankar.

## A Seeker's Confusion

Once one of our seekers encouraged a friend: "Come, come here. Here they teach everything about Ganesha, and first they teach the Bhagavad Gita." The friend concluded that these must be Vaishnavas. A few days later the seeker said, "Navratri has come, they teach about the Goddess here." Now he was confused. Navratri too, and Durga too, so these are Shaktas, not Vaishnavas. For a while he came to both kinds of discourse, but his mind kept reaching verdicts.

Then a few days later he was invited for Shivaratri. "These seem to be Shaivas! This cannot be a guru," was his conclusion. In the end he declared that this guru is not genuine, he is wrong.

## The Yoga of Non-Difference in the Ganesh Gita

Yet Shri Ganesha himself, in the very first chapter of the Ganesh Gita, explains what yoga is. If you have the Ganesh Gita, he himself says in it that I am Shiva, I am the Goddess, I am everything: Vishnu, Rudra, Brahma. These are the two verses:

> **aham eva mahāviṣṇur aham eva sadāśivaḥ**
> **aham eva mahāśaktir aham evāryamā priya**
> "O beloved, I alone am Mahavishnu, I alone am Sadashiva, I alone am Mahashakti, and I alone am the Sun."
> (Ganesh Gita, Chapter 1)

Why does he say this?

> **śive viṣṇau ca śaktau ca sūrye mayi narādhipa**
> **yā'bhedabuddhir yogaḥ sa samyag yogo mato mama**
> "O king, the understanding that sees no difference between Shiva, Vishnu, Shakti, the Sun and me is what I hold to be true yoga."
> (Ganesh Gita, Chapter 1)

"Me" here means Ganesha.
