# Chapter: The Fear of Sin, the Knot of the Heart, and the Journey Beyond Worship

## Correcting Mistakes Without Fear

In India, all I did was give more of this knowledge. Abroad, this knowledge was never given, so people there are not even afraid of committing sin. When a sin happens at their hands, its result also comes. And when they are told that this was wrong, do you know what they say?

> "Oh, Guruji, oh, Swamiji, was that a sin? I did not even know. From now on I will not do it."

And immediately their impressions are cleansed too. They commit sins, and they are also corrected, because no unnecessary fear has been placed inside them.

## A Fear Sown in Childhood

Our problem begins in childhood itself. "This is God, this is Bappa. Bappa is watching you. Bappa sees everything from above. Behave yourself." Even before a child can say "Should I drink water or eat?", the idea of sin and merit is already placed in their mind.

Going on with sin and merit, sin and merit, a person never becomes fully pure within. They still commit sins, but one thing keeps playing in their heart constantly, like a tape on repeat: "What you did is a sin, you should not have done it, you have done wrong."

## The Knot of the Heart: What Prevents Liberation

This is how the **knot of the heart (hridaya granthi)** forms. The knot of the heart means this: sattva wants to take a person upward, while rajas and tamas, the rajasic and tamasic tendencies, pull them down. In this tug of war a knot forms in the heart, and the person cannot become free.

## The Right to Sin and the Duty to Come Out of It

The scriptures say:

> "You have full right to commit sin, provided you think it is your duty to come out of it before it harms you and others."

Everyone can make a mistake; that is natural. But then it also becomes your duty to save yourself and others from it before its terrible results arrive. A mistake happened, fine. But right after, you should realize that something went wrong. If you correct it before the results come, it is fine. And next time you must not repeat that sin.

Foreigners do this quickly. Here, this problem has arisen because of **wrong teachings**.

## Either Teach Everything, or Do Not Teach Fear

Either teach the child everything from childhood. What does Ganapati mean? What do his four hands mean? We circled the plate and did the aarti; what does that mean? The silver placed on the plate, the gold, what do they mean? The betel nut placed there, what does it mean? The lamp placed there, what does it mean?

If you do not teach this, then do not teach them the fear of sin and merit either. Otherwise you will spoil their life.

## Knowledge With References

I say this on the basis of the Upanishads. This will seem wrong to you, but it is not wrong; this is the reality. When I take you to that stage, you will gradually understand it logically.

Here at Antar, something different from all other institutions will be taught, and it will be taught with references. There will be no empty talk in the air. Everything, with its reference, will compel you to think.

## Not Knowledge, but Results Matter

In the end, what matters is not knowledge. The question is: do you get its result? That is why my guarantee is that before the camp ends, everyone receives some good news, and stalled work gets resolved on its own. In Shri Vidya too I had said that something will happen on its own in your business or income. When there is a health camp, illnesses go away before the camp ends.

If it happens to one person, it is a coincidence. If to two, still a coincidence. But when it happens to everyone sitting there, it cannot be a coincidence.

## Not Miracles, but Thought

> "I never claim that I perform miracles. I compel you to think."

As you think, so your karma is formed, and that karma makes you perform that magic. Offering sandalwood there will never get you a job here.

## Worship: The Beginning, Not the End

Fifty people who give discourses will keep teaching the same thing in fifty different ways. Now apply it in life. Puja and ritual worship was the first step, not the last.

Even a two-year-old child is taught, "Say Bappa, bow down." And from there until the age of ninety-eight, a person keeps doing the same. The ordinary person wastes their whole life in this alone.

> "Worship is not the end; it is the beginning."
