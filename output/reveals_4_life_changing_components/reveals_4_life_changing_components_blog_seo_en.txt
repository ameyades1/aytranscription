# To Change Your Destiny, You Need These Four Things

## Everyone Talks, but Who Has a Plan?

"India's future must be bright." How many times have you heard that? People have been saying it for ages, and by now even the slogan has grown old.

Everyone repeats it, and everyone plays the Guru and explains what should happen. Yet one question remains unanswered: **who actually has a plan?**

Acharya Ji explains that changing your destiny takes four things. Whether it is one person or an entire nation, these four principles stay the same.

In short, this is how to change your destiny, according to Acharya Ji: a clear plan and resolve (sankalpa); the support of prarabdha, the destiny shaped by past actions; self-belief in both your plan and your destiny; and, above all, the Guru's grace. Destiny can be changed, but the road is far easier when prarabdha supports your plan. Let us look at them one by one.

## The First Principle: A Plan (Sankalpa)

First of all, you need a **plan**. The mind needs an **adhishthana** (a firm foundation) and a **sankalpa** (a resolute intention) that says, "This is what I must do."

It might be a modest job or a business, or it might be transforming a whole country so that it becomes a teacher to the world. The first step is always the same: a plan, a **purushartha** (one's own effort) that you commit to making.

*Do you have a clear plan for your life, or only a list of wishes?*

## The Second Principle: Is Destiny on Your Side?

You can have any number of wishes. Purushartha concerns the new actions you take. The real question is whether your old actions, including those from past lives, support the new ones.

This is called **prarabdha** (the destiny shaped by past actions). Does your destiny support your resolve?

This does not mean you can never rise above destiny. Destiny can be changed. But when your plan has the backing of prarabdha, how much easier the road becomes!

## The Third Principle: Self-Belief

Even when you have a plan and destiny is on your side, you need a third thing: **self-belief**. It takes two forms:

- Faith in your plan
- Faith in your destiny, the trust that this truly is what life holds for you

You might learn your destiny from a Guru, from **Nadi Shastra** (an ancient system of palm-leaf astrology), from a birth chart or from palmistry. And if you have never consulted any of these, that is fine too. Your past actions will still play out. Even an atheist lives according to the same prarabdha.

Self-belief is exactly what many people lack. Even when the Guru tells them, they think, "Oh, what will come of it? How could it ever happen?" And so, even though success is written in their destiny, their work never gets done.

Others, meanwhile, dream big and draw up plans, yet never manage to act on them.

*When someone tells you that you can do something, is the first voice inside you saying "yes" or "how could I?"*

## The Fourth Principle: The Guru's Grace

That makes three. The fourth principle stands above them all: **Guru kripa** (the grace of the Guru).

In short, here are the four principles for changing your destiny:

1. **A plan:** a clear vision for your future.
2. **Prarabdha:** does destiny support you? If it does not, make a plan strong enough to carry you beyond it.
3. **Self-belief:** trust in your plan and in your destiny.
4. **Grace:** the blessing of the Guru.

## Why Look at Your Destiny at All?

A natural question arises here: why should I consult the Nadi readings of Agastya Muni, the ancient sage, or look at my own birth chart?

Set the ancient sages aside for a moment. A saying is popular among today's highly successful people: millionaires do not need astrologers, but billionaires do.

The higher you rise and the closer you come to God, the more you realise how important **Jyotisha** (Vedic astrology) really is.

Astrology is not about asking, "When will I get my car and my bungalow?" The Vedas have six limbs, called the **Vedangas** (the six auxiliary disciplines of the Vedas). Among them, Jyotisha is called **the eye of the Vedas**. Our sages taught that with it, you can plan your entire life with ease.

This is why sending a child to the **gurukul** (a traditional residential school with the Guru) early in life was so essential. There the child received a roadmap in advance, showing what their destiny held. Only after that comes the decision: accept what life brings, or change it.

## Stop Today the Suffering That Has Not Yet Come

Maharshi Patanjali states it plainly in the Yoga Sutras:

> **heyaṁ duḥkham anāgatam ||**
> "The suffering that has not yet come is what can be avoided."
> (Yoga Sutras 2.16)

- **heyam:** to be destroyed, to be given up
- **duhkham:** suffering
- **anagatam:** that which has not yet come

In other words, **you are meant to destroy the suffering that has not yet arrived.**

You may ask how you can destroy something that has not even come. Acharya Ji answers with everyday examples:

- If you get angry today, lack selfless love and watch your blood pressure climb, a heart attack tomorrow is all but certain. Take control today, and you protect yourself.
- If you eat very hot, spicy food today, some trouble with the **Muladhara chakra** (the root energy centre at the base of the spine) and the stomach is bound to follow.
- If you eat a lot of greasy food, you will inevitably struggle with obesity one day.

If you want healthy teeth tomorrow, you have to brush them today. In the same way, you have to plan your life. When someone describes the signs of what lies ahead, they are restating this very principle. Your duty is to remove the coming suffering through the right remedy, applied in the proper way.

*Which "suffering yet to come" are your habits preparing for you today?*

## From One Person to a Nation: India's Destiny

If one person needs a plan to change their destiny, how great a plan must a whole country need? Acharya Ji explains that India's plan, the one nobody was talking about, is the resolve of Antar Yog. Here is how the four principles apply to it:

- **A plan:** drawing it up poses no difficulty, because the plan reveals itself.
- **Prarabdha:** it appears in the chart of Antar Yog and in Acharya Ji's own chart, so destiny is certainly on its side.
- **Self-belief:** this is the sadhana (spiritual practice) of the Self itself. Embrace the truth today, and self-belief is bound to follow.
- **The Guru's grace:** this is the Guru's own work, so that grace will surely be there.

## Key Takeaways

- Changing your destiny rests on four principles: a plan, prarabdha, self-belief and the Guru's grace.
- Destiny can be changed, but the path is easier when it supports your plan.
- Without self-belief, the work fails even when success is written in your destiny.
- Jyotisha is the eye of the Vedas: a tool for planning life, not for predicting cars and bungalows.
- **Heyam duhkham anagatam:** your duty is to stop the suffering to come today, through the right remedy applied properly.
- The principles that apply to a person apply equally to a nation.
