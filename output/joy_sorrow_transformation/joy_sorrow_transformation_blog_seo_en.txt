# A Horse in the Dung, Tears Among the Toys: The Secret of Dharana Shakti

Have you ever noticed how some people find a reason to smile whatever happens, while others find something to complain about even when they have everything? Through a short story, Acharya Ji shows that the difference lies not in outer circumstances but in an inner **dharana shakti** (the mind's power to hold on to a feeling until it becomes its nature).

In short, the answer to why some people are always unhappy lies within, not in what happens outside. Whatever the mind accepts again and again becomes its nature. A mind that keeps accepting pain finds it even among gifts; a mind that holds on to joy finds the promise of a horse even in the dung.

## Does Suffering Come from Outside?

People who keep saying "I'm miserable, so miserable" have come to play the game of suffering. And this game is played within, not outside.

Such a person finds pain in everything. Bring them the finest thing you can, bring them anything at all, and they will still find something to hurt them.

Acharya Ji explains that the game of suffering begins inside, and that is exactly why these people see misery wherever they look.

## A Father, Two Sons and an Experiment

A father had two sons. One was always happy, the other always unhappy, and the father could not understand why.

At festival time, he decided to try an experiment. He would choose gifts that would make the happy son unhappy for once, and give the unhappy son at least one happy day.

That night, while both boys slept, the father spread horse dung across the whole floor of the happy son's room, so that his feet would land in it the moment he got out of bed. In the unhappy son's room, he set out brand-new remote-controlled cars and toys in every corner.

## A Morning Nobody Expected

The father was sure that everything would turn upside down that day. Something quite different happened.

The happy son woke up first, because his mind is always fresh and free of **tamas** (dullness and inertia). His father waited, expecting him to burst into tears so that he could comfort him.

Instead, the boy jumped right into the dung and began dancing, the way children dance in mud puddles. Dung flew in every direction, even up the walls, and he did not care in the least. It was the father who ended up worried.

Meanwhile, the unhappy son woke to the sound of his brother's shouting. Seeing all those cars, he first thought, "Santa Claus must have come." A moment later, his eyes filled with tears.

## "There Must Be a Horse Somewhere!"

The father asked the happy son, "Why are you dancing?"

His answer was worth hearing: "Father, with this much horse dung, there has to be a horse close by. Even if you didn't bring a big one, you must have brought at least a pony. Tell me where it is. I want to ride it right now!"

Acharya Ji points out that this boy is a thoroughly positive thinker. He has carried this outlook with him from past lives, and his mother taught him the same thing.

These are what the tradition calls **samskaras** (deep impressions on the mind). They travel with us from life to life and shape the way we see the world.

## "Who Will Buy the Batteries?"

Next, the father asked the unhappy son, "Why are you crying?"

The boy answered at once: "Who is going to run all these cars? When the batteries die, you'll tell me to go to the shop and buy new ones. Or you won't bring them for four days because you're busy. Then how will I play?"

One boy's tears had just begun, while the other was lost in joy.

*Pause for a moment: when life hands you a gift, do you look for the horse, or for the batteries that will run out?*

## Dharana Shakti: The Real Root of Happiness and Suffering

Dharana shakti is the mind's ability to take hold of a feeling and make it part of its own nature. One son had taken hold of happiness, the other of suffering.

Acharya Ji explains that suffering takes root naturally, from within. Nobody has to go out and fetch it.

Every time, one son's mind accepted pain, more pain and still more pain. The other son's mind kept returning to joy, delight and a positive outlook.

The situation stays the same. What changes is the eye you look at it with.

*Ask your mind today: what have I been holding on to all this time, happiness or suffering?*

## Key Takeaways

- The game of suffering is played within, not outside; an unhappy mind finds pain in everything.
- The same father gave both sons the opposite of what they expected, yet the happy son stayed happy and the unhappy son stayed unhappy.
- The happy son saw the promise of a horse even in the dung; the unhappy son saw dying batteries even among his toys.
- The difference lies in **dharana shakti**: whatever the mind accepts again and again becomes its nature.
- A positive outlook comes from **samskaras**, shaped both by past lives and by upbringing.
