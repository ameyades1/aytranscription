# Chapter: The Power of Dharana, the Inner Play of Joy and Sorrow

## The Game of Sorrow Is Played Within

Those who say "I am unhappy, very unhappy" come into this world to play the game of sorrow. This game is not played outside; it is played within. Such a person sees only sorrow in everything. However good a thing you bring them, whatever you bring them, they will still see only sorrow in it.

## The Story of Two Sons

A father had two sons. One of them was always happy and the other was always unhappy. The father himself could not understand how one son stayed happy every time, and how the other became unhappy no matter what he was given.

So the father thought that on some festive occasion, when a festival came, he would give the two of them gifts. And the gifts would be such that the one who is always happy would, for once, be seen unhappy; and he would also see whether the one who is always unhappy ever becomes happy.

While both sons were asleep, the father spread horse dung, like cow dung, all over the floor of one son's room, so that when he got up his feet would land right in it. And in the other son's room he placed new remote-controlled cars and all kinds of toys everywhere, so that he would wake up and be delighted to see so many gifts.

## The Secret of Dharana Shakti

The next day the father expected everything to turn out the opposite way. But this is where the secret of **dharana shakti** (the power of inner holding) lies. One had taken joy as his inner disposition, and the other had taken sorrow.

What happened? The next morning the happy son woke up first, because his mind is fresh; there is no tamas in it. The unhappy son woke up later. The father was watching and thinking: now he will cry a little, then I will console him and explain something to him.

But as soon as the boy set his foot down and jumped into all that dung, he became even happier. Just as people dance in mud, he began dancing even more. The dung was flying here and onto the walls there, but he had no worry at all. He jumped in it so much that the father became worried.

Now it was time to see what was happening in the other room. That son woke up later, roused by his brother's noise. On waking he saw the cars and all the toys. He thought, "Who brought so many remote-controlled cars? Santa Claus must have come." But as soon as he understood everything, tears came to his eyes at once.

The very next moment the father's worry grew. The one whose dharana shakti holds crying is crying; and the one whose dharana shakti holds joy is happy. How did this happen?

## The Happy Son's Answer

To find out what was going on, the father went to the happy son and asked, "Why are you dancing?"

He answered, "Father, there is so much horse dung lying here, which means there must surely be a horse nearby. If not a big horse, you must at least have brought me a small one. Where is it? Tell me, I want to ride it right now. I am a fully positive-minded person. This has been my samskara in everything from past lives, and my mother also taught me this. Where is the horse? Show me. I have come for that horse, and that is why I am dancing."

## The Unhappy Son's Answer

Then the father asked the unhappy son, "Why are you unhappy?"

He replied at once, "Oh, who is going to run so many cars? Then their batteries will run out, and you will tell me to go to the shop and get them. Or else you will not bring them for four days, saying you are busy. Then how will I play, what will I do?"

One began crying and the other rejoicing.

## The Lesson of This Story

What did you learn from this story? About dharana shakti. This is a very big lesson about dharana shakti.

What did you understand? This sorrow is, by its very nature, held from within. It does not have to be brought in from outside.

> "His mind accepted, every time, sorrow, sorrow, sorrow; and in this one's mind, joy, joy, positivity."

Joy and sorrow do not live in outer things but in our inner disposition. A mind that has taken hold of sorrow will find sorrow even in a heap of gifts; and a mind that has taken hold of joy will see the hope of a horse even in a heap of dung.
