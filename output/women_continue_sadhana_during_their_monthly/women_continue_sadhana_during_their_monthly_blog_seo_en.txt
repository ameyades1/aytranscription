# Why Your Sadhana Need Not Stop During Your Monthly Cycle

## The Rules We Have All Heard

Don't go to the cremation ground. Don't do this in the presence of the Guru. Don't recite too many prayers. Don't visit the temple of Shani Dev too often.

You have probably heard prohibitions like these for women somewhere along the way. Acharya Upendra Ji calls them all **misconceptions**. They are not entirely false, yet they are not entirely true either. Among them is the question of sadhana during menstruation: should it stop? Before going further, let us understand what lies behind them.

The short answer: according to Acharya Ji, your personal sadhana should continue on all thirty days of the month, because God pervades everything. The rule of keeping away from public places on those days came from the hygiene and social conditions of an earlier time. If you can follow it, that is fine; your own practice is another matter.

## The Fear of Cremation-Ground Detachment

A woman's mind is emotional by nature, and she is the one who holds the home and family together. If she were suddenly overtaken by **vairagya** (dispassion, a turning away from the world), what would become of the household? A male-dominated society framed these rules out of exactly this worry.

The brief detachment that stirs in someone who visits a cremation ground is called **shmashan vairagya** (cremation-ground dispassion). People feared that if a woman felt it, the home would struggle afterwards.

> **annamayaṃ hi somya manaḥ**
> "The mind, dear one, is made of food."
> (Chandogya Upanishad 6.5.4)

In the home, it is the woman who prepares the food; she is **Annapurna** (the goddess who nourishes all with food). If her thoughts turned negative, the whole family could feel it.

## Visiting the Guru: What Was the Fear?

Yet if a woman is strong and spiritual, like the Devi herself, this rule does not apply to her at all. There is never anything wrong in going to the Guru. The only fear was that she might abandon her worldly life.

The answer is simple: don't go to a guru who tells you to give everything up. Acharya Ji says, go to the Shri Vidya Guru, who tells you that you can have bhoga plus moksha together.

The tradition of **Shri Vidya** carries **bhoga** (worldly enjoyment) and **moksha** (liberation) side by side. A Guru of this path teaches you to balance all three: Lakshmi, Parvati and Saraswati.

*Is your spiritual path pulling you away from life, or teaching you to live it in balance?*

## Shani Dev's Temple: The Pull of the Atmosphere

In a temple of Shani Dev (Saturn, the deity of discipline and karmic justice), the mantras belong to Shani and so does the whole atmosphere. The prayers of troubled people, together with that setting, awaken a mood of detachment and humility. Society worried that if a woman absorbed this mood, no one would run the household.

Speaking as a Guru, however, Acharya Ji is clear: go whenever you like, pray as much as you wish, offer as much oil in **abhisheka** (ritual pouring of offerings over the deity) as you want. All of it is fine.

This freedom belongs to the woman who is strong and whose family supports her. She need not worry about it at all. What Shani Dev loves is someone who keeps their **balance**.

## Can Women Do Sadhana During Menstruation?

Many women ask: should we do our **sadhana** (daily spiritual practice) for twenty-five days, or for thirty?

Spiritual life has nothing to do with counting days. Practise on all thirty days, and on all thirty-one in a month that has them. **You never have to give up your sadhana.**

Why? Because God pervades everything. Just think about it:

- Did you stop eating during those five days? Yet Annapurneshwari Devi dwells in food.
- When your salary arrived, did you refuse it? Yet Lakshmi dwells in money.
- The **Vastu Devata** (the deity who presides over a dwelling) lives in your house. Did you stop going home?

Wherever you turn, you simply cannot leave God behind.

*If God is everywhere, can there ever be a day when I am far from Him?*

## Then Where Did This Rule Come From?

The rule arose for **social reasons**. In those days, women had none of the hygiene products available today. An accident in a public place could disturb others' concentration and invite improper thoughts and gossip. So women were told to keep their distance.

If you are able to follow that rule, that is fine. Your own personal practice is another matter. Acharya Ji explains that you should keep up your own sadhana on all thirty days, and Shani Dev is pleased by it.

## Key Takeaways

- Many prohibitions placed on women are misconceptions: neither entirely false nor entirely true.
- A male-dominated society made these rules, fearing that dispassion in a woman would unsettle the home.
- These rules do not bind a strong, spiritual woman, especially when her family supports her.
- Choose a Guru who teaches the balance of bhoga and moksha, such as a Guru of Shri Vidya.
- God is everywhere, so continue your personal sadhana on every day of the month.
- The restriction linked to menstruation came from the hygiene and social conditions of an earlier time.
