# The Power of Speech and the Mahakali Asleep Within Every Woman

If women are to lead this country and govern it in the years ahead, what will they need to learn? Which qualities does a woman need to become Mahakali?

In this discourse, the answer begins with a single word: **vak** (the power of speech). The practice belongs to Saraswati, and through it she connects with Mahakali.

Why is the power of speech for women leaders so important? According to Acharya Ji, the first quality a woman who leads needs is vak, a strong voice. In Bhagavad Gita 10.34, Shri Krishna says that among women he is fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness. Whoever has vak has fire within, and that vak brings Mahakali forth. Yet speech only opens the door; work is what keeps you there.

## Can Anyone Lead Without a Voice?

If you want power, you need a strong voice. Have you ever seen a leader who never speaks, or whose voice is so feeble that nobody listens?

There may be an exception somewhere, but we are not talking about exceptions. When a speaker's voice gives out in the middle of a speech, people start laughing: "What is this one going to achieve?"

Acharya Ji says a leader's voice must be powerful. It will at least win votes, but the leader will last only by working sincerely alongside it.

In other words, speech opens the door, but only work keeps you in the room. Speeches alone are not what we need.

## Fierce and Gentle: The Nature of a Woman

Who gives the better speech, a man or a woman? In a male-dominated society, we have grown used to hearing only male ministers with harsh voices.

Yet the scriptures describe a woman as **ati raudra and ati saumya** (extremely fierce and extremely gentle). The fierce side she shows at home, she must also learn to show in the world outside.

This does not mean becoming shrill. It means recognising your own strength.

*Do you keep the power of your voice locked within the four walls of your home?*

## What Does the Gita Say About Women?

In the tenth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, **Vibhuti Yoga** (the Yoga of Divine Manifestations), Shri Krishna himself describes the forms in which he appears in women.

> **mṛtyuḥ sarvaharaś cāham udbhavaś ca bhaviṣyatām**
> **kīrtiḥ śrīr vāk ca nārīṇāṁ smṛtir medhā dhṛtiḥ kṣamā**
> "I am all-devouring death, and I am the source of all that is yet to be born. Among women, I am fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness."
> (Bhagavad Gita 10.34)

You can understand these seven qualities in two groups:

- **Outer qualities:** kirti (fame), shri (prosperity) and vak (speech). These show themselves in the world.
- **Inner qualities:** smriti (memory), medha (intelligence), dhriti (the strength to hold steady) and kshama (forgiveness). These arise from within.

Tradition calls Shri the daughter of the sage Bhrigu, and Vak the daughter of Lord Brahma. Kirti, Smriti, Medha, Dhriti and Kshama are called the daughters of **Daksha Prajapati** (a progenitor of creation in the Puranas).

## Which Power Is Strongest in a Woman?

Look closely, and most of these qualities belong to Saraswati. Memory is Saraswati herself. Intelligence is her role, and steadfastness is hers too.

Forgiveness also comes only to those who have intelligence and **viveka** (discernment). Yes, it takes heart and courage as well, and there Lakshmi and Kali lend their help. But Saraswati remains at the centre.

Shri is Lakshmi. Fame belongs to those who have Mahakali within them, because tales of courage are the ones people tell. All three goddesses appear in the verse, but Saraswati appears most of all.

That is why Acharya Ji says women should carry **sattva guna** (the quality of purity, clarity and balance) above all.

Sattva is Saraswati's quality, and it is the root strength of a woman.

## What Is Vak, Really?

Vak is Saraswati's very name. The vak that a leader possesses is what brings Mahakali forth.

The scriptures call Saraswati both Brahma's daughter and his wife. How can she be both? Because these figures are a **rupa-kalpana** (a form imagined to help the seeker), and that imagined form lives within us.

> **sādhakānāṁ hitārthāya brahmaṇo rūpakalpanā**
> "For the good of seekers, the formless Brahman is imagined in a form."
> (Traditional saying)

## The Story of Brahma and Saraswati That People Laugh At

The story goes that when Brahma saw Saraswati, his seed was released, and she became his wife. People laugh at this.

Here, though, nothing physical is being described. No human being has four faces. Brahma stands for the **brahmabhava** within us (the state of the creator awakening in us), which arises when we begin to seek knowledge and rise above the four **varnas** (the traditional social classes).

The release of seed means the **manifestation of tejas** (radiant inner energy). Brahma is the creator, and he shapes destiny by beholding knowledge.

Knowledge is born in those who have something to create. Necessity is the mother of invention. Invention needs intelligence, but the need has to come first. That is why knowledge is both the daughter and the wife of the creator.

People of other faiths laugh at these stories, and we start seeing them the same way. Let the wisdom of Lord Ganesha awaken within you, and from today, do not make this mistake again.

*Do you read a story literally and miss its deeper message?*

## How Does Speech Bring Forth Mahakali?

Vak lives in women, which is why ideal leaders can also come from among them, since the defining quality of a leader is Mahakali. Whoever has vak within has fire within.

Soft, loving words are the domain of Lakshmi. Lord Vishnu's work is different again: Vishnu wielding the **Sudarshana Chakra** (his divine discus) is in one state, and Narayana, his serene form, is in another. Each power has its own place.

## The Next Mark of Power: The Ability to Punish

If you want to awaken Mahakali, which means you want power, the next quality you need is **knowing how to punish**.

Acharya Ji points out that the kingdom of the one most capable of punishing is the one that expands.

## Key Takeaways

- Leadership and power demand a strong voice (vak), but only those who also work will last.
- The scriptures describe a woman as both fierce and gentle; she must learn to show her fierce side outside the home too, without becoming shrill.
- According to Gita 10.34, fame, prosperity, speech, memory, intelligence, steadfastness and forgiveness in women are manifestations of God.
- Saraswati dominates these qualities, so women should carry sattva guna above all.
- The story of Brahma and Saraswati is symbolic: the wish to create brings forth the radiance of knowledge.
- The next quality for awakening Mahakali is the ability to punish.
