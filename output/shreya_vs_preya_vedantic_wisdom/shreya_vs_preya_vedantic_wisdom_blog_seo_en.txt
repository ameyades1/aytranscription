# Shreya or Preya: Where Are Your Actions Taking You?

Every day we perform countless actions. Have you ever stopped to ask whether they are lifting you up or pulling you down?

In this discourse, Acharya Upendra Ji turns to a profound teaching of the Katha Upanishad, the contrast between **shreya** (the good) and **preya** (the pleasant). Through it he explains what noble action is, when the desire for it awakens, and how it transforms a life, and even a business.

Shreya vs preya is the choice the Katha Upanishad (1.2.2) places before every person: shreya, the good, and preya, the pleasant. Shreya is what the scriptures hold up as the ideal and your duty; preya is what you like. People driven by rajas and tamas chase preya, while a sattvic person chooses shreya, whose fruit is happiness, knowledge and detachment.

## What Does Noble Action Bear?

The scriptures call the fruit of noble action **sattvic** (pure, harmonious). A sattvic fruit means happiness, knowledge and detachment.

> **karmaṇaḥ sukṛtasyāhuḥ sāttvikaṁ nirmalaṁ phalam**
> "The fruit of good action is said to be sattvic and pure."
> (Bhagavad Gita 14.16)

When you act nobly, joy comes to you, understanding comes to you, detachment comes to you, and a great deal more arrives along with them.

## Shreya vs Preya: What Is Right Versus What Feels Good

The Katha Upanishad says that two paths open up before every human being.

> **śreyaśca preyaśca manuṣyametas**
> "Both the good and the pleasant present themselves to a person."
> (Katha Upanishad 1.2.2)

Shreya is what is truly higher. Whatever the scriptures hold up as the ideal and as your duty, that is shreya.

Preya is whatever I happen to like.

People driven by **rajas** (restless passion) and **tamas** (inertia and dullness) chase preya. A sattvic person chooses shreya, the path of noble action.

*Of the decisions you made today, how many were shreya, and how many were merely preya?*

## When Does the Desire for Noble Action Awaken?

Acharya Ji explains that the desire for noble action arises when your very purpose in life is to rise from **nara to Narayana** (from the human to the Divine). Noble action becomes possible only when this is what you long for: to move along the path that passes through heaven, toward the Self, toward **liberation** (mukti).

And if that purpose is missing? Then rajas is bound to lead you downward. A fall needs no goal at all. It takes care of itself, and the mind drifts toward preya on its own.

It all depends on how I think, Acharya Ji says, and my thought is this: I must rise from nara to Narayana.

When your thinking takes that shape, the body turns toward noble action by itself. Qualities such as happiness, knowledge and detachment begin to grow within you.

## Can Business Also Be Noble Action?

Acharya Ji offers his own life as an example. He ran a business too, but he joined it with **Shri Vidya** (a sacred tradition of worship and inner knowledge centred on the Divine Mother), so it became a business guided by knowledge.

Running a business this way means you never stop learning: the psychology of customers, the psychology of marketing, positioning a product well, management, the psychology of employees, and the art of communication.

Where does your attention go? Toward understanding. Your product should be the very best. You also learn the practical wisdom of how to stay in the competition and how to live through it.

Such a person wants to learn from experience, not to take revenge.

Even bitter experiences are no problem. Instead of filing fifty lawsuits, you improve your own terms and conditions, befriend a lawyer and learn the law from them. That is the path of knowledge.

*When someone harms you, is your first instinct to take revenge or to learn?*

## What Changes as Sattva Grows?

In this way, little by little, your understanding deepens and **sattva guna** (the quality of purity and harmony) grows, because Lord Ganesha belongs to the realm of sattva. Your attention stays on knowledge and happiness.

Your actions begin to climb, one level at a time, and you taste the joy they bring. Yet even with so much wisdom and contentment, such a person does not get stuck in **ego** or in **rajo guna** (the quality of restless activity and desire). Only when knowledge, happiness and detachment travel together does someone keep moving along the higher path.

## "Not Everything Is Mine": Toward Karma Yoga

Gradually a new feeling takes root: on the path of **dharma** (righteousness), I must give to others as well, because not everything is mine.

A **chaitanya bhava** (living awareness) awakens in this person, and they become full of energy. They are not agitated about their work but enthusiastic about it. **Prana shakti** (life force energy) flows into them.

They make this the aim of their life, and their path upward, toward liberation.

For them, work itself is worship.

In this way they move toward **Karma Yoga** (the path of selfless action), acting from a sattvic spirit.

## Key Takeaways

- The fruit of noble action is sattvic and pure: happiness, knowledge and detachment.
- Shreya is what the scriptures call higher and your duty; preya is what merely feels pleasant.
- The desire for noble action awakens when your purpose is to rise from nara to Narayana.
- Without that purpose, the mind drifts toward preya and decline on its own.
- Guided by knowledge, even business can become noble action.
- Learn from experience; do not seek revenge.
- Staying free of ego amid knowledge and happiness is the condition for the higher path.
- The feeling "not everything is mine", along with enthusiasm and prana shakti, leads a person toward Karma Yoga.
