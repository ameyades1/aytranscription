# One Realisation That Frees You from Suffering: Seeking the Stable in an Unstable World

> "Either you have to leave the world, or the world will leave you."

Either you will leave the world, or the world will leave you. Acharya Upendra Ji says, "This much is certain."

The sentence sounds harsh, but it holds life's plainest truth. Let us open this truth slowly and look at it.

## Does Anything Last Forever?

You bought a new car. Slowly it too grows old. Objects, people, places, everything changes. And one day comes when we ourselves have to leave everything and go.

Relationships are the same. A son grows up, goes to another country to fulfil his desires, goes out with his friends. He is not going to stay with us for that long.

And yet we treat everything as **permanent** and pour ourselves into it. Acharya Ji's principle is simple: **do it as a duty, and let go.** Failing to do this is the problem that makes a person become a **pitar** (a soul that wanders unfulfilled after death).

*Which thing or relationship have you quietly decided is "forever"?*

## The Ninety-Nine-Year Lease

A ninety-nine-year-old man happily signs a ninety-nine-year lease on land. As if he is going to live another ninety-nine years to enjoy it.

Acharya Ji asks, where does this patience within him come from? There is no guarantee of his own life, yet he runs after things looking for guaranteed happiness.

## So Should We Buy Nothing?

No. Acharya Ji makes it clear that no one said, do not acquire things. **Buy, but understand their nature: they are unstable.**

The first verse of the scripture had already said that **bhoga** (worldly pleasures and experiences) is fine, but convert it into **material for yoga**, that is, material that helps the inner journey. The second verse describes the nature of enjoyment.

The yoga part within enjoyment leads towards liberation. But if enjoyment remains only enjoyment and is not turned into material for yoga, it will never reach **moksha**.

The second verse says plainly: all of this is unstable. Nothing is stable, not even your problems. And to give up your life chasing this unstable thing is the road to becoming a pitar.

## From Six-Pack to One-Pack

Here Acharya Ji explains with his usual humour. At the time of marriage you may have had a six-pack. Then age increased, and that six-pack became one big "one-pack".

A few years after marriage the wife looks pregnant, and a few years later the husband starts looking pregnant! It is funny, but the message is deep: **the body changes.**

Fortunes change, finances change. Hair turns from black to white. Wrinkles keep coming. Nothing stays stable.

## The Lens of Ego

The one who sees this unstable world and still takes it to be stable is the first to become a pitar, because his desires and cravings remain of a different kind.

He shouts at his wife, "You are not as beautiful as before." Acharya Ji says, oh, but you have become an uncle too! Yet his gaze is still stuck in youth.

He feels everything else is changing, because **ego** keeps him from seeing himself. Sometimes sade sati comes, sometimes an exalted period, sometimes a low one. All of it is unstable.

*Do you also notice the changes in others while looking away from your own?*

## What Is Stable Amid Instability?

First know that **maya** (the ever-changing world) is unstable. Then, within that instability, you must find something stable. And that stable thing is **the Supreme**.

There was a wife, and she was a goddess. There was a husband, and he was Shiva. In every up and down, **love is God.**

> "Love was God, forgiveness was God, peace was God. That God was what I had to seek."

Ask yourself: what was my love resting on? Money, or the person? If money, then the relationship will break as soon as circumstances worsen. Only the things equal to God are eternal. Everything else is changing.

## From Hair Dye to a Wig

The more you dye your hair black, one day that very hair will be gone. Then you will not dye the bald head black! Then comes the wig. After some time even the wig has to be coloured, because dust spoils it too.

First treatment for the teeth, then dentures. Everything is unstable. The only question is: will we **accept** it or not?

## Why Am I Not Liberated?

The one who takes the unstable to be stable does not even understand that his own **mind** is unstable. Such people become pitar quickly, because even at the time of death they cannot accept themselves.

The lesson of the second verse is clear: hold on to what is stable. Do not run too much after what is unstable. As long as a companion is with you, they are your companion; if they are gone, they are gone.

## Vanaprastha: Not a Jungle, but a Way of Living

Our tradition describes four **ashramas** (stages of life): brahmacharya, the householder stage, vanaprastha and sannyasa. After brahmacharya comes the householder stage, and after the householder stage, vanaprastha.

Acharya Ji explains that **vanaprastha** does not mean going to the jungle. It means beginning to live at home with the same detachment a person has in the forest.

Whether the curtain is red, green or blue, leave it to the daughter-in-law. Let her keep the sofa whatever colour she likes. I have lived the way I wanted. **Let her live the way she wants.**

## Why Slowly Detach After Fifty?

Because one day, at sixty, seventy or eighty, one of the two who live together will go. Then there will be a great shock, because there was always the habit of clinging, and **virakti** (gradual freedom from attachment) never came.

This bond too was unstable. For one who does not understand this, the chances of becoming a pitar increase, and moksha does not happen. And at seventy-five the crying begins: "What will become of me now?"

## A Bitter Question on the Fiftieth Anniversary

People joyfully celebrate their fiftieth wedding anniversary. Acharya Ji says that from the spiritual point of view this is nonsense. Married at twenty-five, you are seventy-five now. What kind of householder are you even now?

What should have been is: "After fifty, I had already moved towards detachment."

Note that this **does not mean leaving your wife.** Acharya Ji did not say that. It is about the inner feeling. If the feeling is still that of a householder and at seventy-six or seventy-eight one of them goes, the one left will be the first to become a pitar, because he had begun to feel the relationship was permanent.

> "One day or another, it has to go."

If this much attachment remains until the end, this very grandfather will later become a pitar and trouble the family.

*If you are past fifty, is your inner feeling still only that of a householder, or has the journey of detachment begun within?*

## An Important Truth

Acharya Ji calls this "a very, very important revelation". If after fifty you do not move towards vanaprastha, one day something inside will break, and it will cost very dearly.

If the spiritual life has not been started even a little by now, there is great suffering later. And often, after that, the other partner too passes away soon.

## Key Takeaways

- Either you will leave the world or the world will leave you; nothing lasts forever.
- Buying or enjoying things is not wrong, but understand their unstable nature and turn enjoyment into material for yoga.
- Body, wealth, fortune, relationships and mind are all unstable; ego keeps us from seeing our own change.
- Amid instability only the Supreme is stable: love, forgiveness and peace are equal to God and eternal.
- Vanaprastha does not mean going to the jungle; it means slowly becoming detached while living at home.
- After fifty, move towards detachment and begin the spiritual life, or attachment at the end makes a person a pitar and blocks liberation.
