# Chapter: Seeking the Stable in an Unstable World

## Leave the World, or the World Will Leave You

> "Either you have to leave the world, or the world will leave you."

This much is certain. Think about it: does any object last forever? You buy a car, and slowly it too grows old, older and older. Objects, people, places, everything changes little by little, and one day comes when you yourself will die and will have to leave everything behind.

There is some relationship, there is a son. He keeps growing up. To fulfil his own desires he goes to another country, goes abroad, goes out with his friends. He is not going to stay with you for that long. And yet a person invests so much in everything, taking it to be permanent and stable. What was to be done was this: do it as a duty, and then let go. This is the second problem that makes a person become a *pitar*. Is it clear now where the problem lies?

## The Ninety-Nine-Year Lease

A ninety-nine-year-old man happily signs a ninety-nine-year lease on a piece of land, thinking that he is going to live another ninety-nine years to enjoy it. Where does this patience within him come from? This is exactly what that verse is telling us.

This second verse is relatively easy to understand. There is no guarantee of one's own self, yet a person chases after things in search of guaranteed happiness.

## The Nature of Enjoyment: Impermanence

So does this mean one should not acquire things, should not buy anything? Was that said? No. Buy, but understand its nature: it is unstable.

About enjoyment (*bhoga*), things had already been made clear. The first verse said that enjoyments are fine, but convert them into material for yoga. The second verse describes the nature of enjoyment. For the part of enjoyment that belongs to liberation, the part that belongs to yoga, will free you; it will take you towards *moksha*. But if enjoyment remains only enjoyment, is that not a problem? If enjoyment is not being converted into material for yoga, it will never reach liberation.

So what is the nature of enjoyment? The second verse tells us. What did it say? It is unstable. Nothing is stable, not even your problems. They too are not stable. And to give up your life for that very unstable thing, this is what will make you a *pitar*.

## The Body Changes, and So Does Fortune

When you got married, you may have had a toned body, a six-pack. As age increases, no girlfriend, no boyfriend, no worries, there is just the one, and slowly you shout at that very person, quarrel with them. Then the six-pack merges into one big belly. A few years after marriage the wife looks as if she is pregnant, and a few years later the husband looks as if he is pregnant! Something like this does happen. Changes come in them.

Nothing will remain stable. Your fortunes will not remain stable, your financial condition will not remain stable. Your hair will slowly turn from black to white. Wrinkles will keep coming. Is this right or not?

## The One Who Takes the Unstable to Be Stable

The one who sees this unstable world and still thinks everything will remain stable, he is the first who will become a *pitar*, because his desires and cravings will be of a different kind. He shouts at his wife, "You are not as beautiful as before." Oh, but you too have become old! Yet his eyes remain stuck in youth. He thinks everything else is changing, because, owing to ego, he cannot see himself.

His financial situation too keeps changing. Sometimes *sade sati* comes, sometimes an exalted period, sometimes a low one. All of this is unstable.

## The Stable Divine Amid Instability

First know that *maya* is unstable. Within that instability I must see something that is stable. What is stable in it is the Supreme. There was a wife, and she was a goddess; there was a husband, and he was Shiva.

In every condition, in every up and down, **love is God**. Did my love endure? Was I loving money, or was I actually loving the person? If I was loving money, then as soon as their circumstances worsen, I am headed for divorce.

Whatever was equal to God was eternal. Love was God, forgiveness was God, peace was God. That God was what I had to seek. Everything else is changing.

## Black Hair, False Hair and False Teeth

The more I go on dyeing my hair black, black, black, one day that very hair will be gone. Then what will you do, dye the bald head black? No. Then he will wear a wig. After some time even the wig will have to be coloured, because dust will spoil it too. First all kinds of treatment for the teeth, then come the dentures.

Everything is unstable. Will a person accept it or not? So the nature of *prakriti* has now been understood.

## Why Am I Not Liberated?

The one who takes the unstable to be stable even when it plainly looks unstable does not understand that his own mind too is actually unstable. Such people will become *pitar* very quickly, because even at the time of death they will not be able to accept themselves. Is the problem clear up to here? Why am I not liberated?

The second verse is completely clear. That fire has been kindled. What is its lesson? Whatever is stable in this, I had to hold on to it. I had to let go of unstable things, or at least not run after them too much. As long as it is there, it is my companion; if it is gone, it is gone.

## The True Meaning of Vanaprastha

So why would it have been said that after the *brahmacharya* stage one is to enter the householder stage, and after the householder stage, *vanaprastha*? *Vanaprastha* does not mean going to the jungle. The way a person lives after going to the forest, that is how he should begin living at home.

Now whether the curtain is red, green or blue, leave it in the hands of the daughter-in-law. I have lived the way I wanted to live. Let her keep the sofa whatever colour she likes. Let her live the way she wants.

Do you know why it is necessary, after the age of fifty, to become detached from all this slowly and steadily? Because one day, one of the two in this companionship will go, at sixty, seventy or eighty. When that happens, there will be a great shock, because there was always the habit of clinging, and detachment never came. This bond too was unstable. One who does not understand this may face trouble again. The chances of becoming a *pitar* increase, and liberation will not happen. At the age of seventy-five the crying will begin: "What will become of me now?"

## A Question on the Fiftieth Anniversary

Those who joyfully celebrate, "Today is our fiftieth wedding anniversary!", spirituality says this is nonsense. If you have been married for fifty years, you must have married at around twenty-five. That means you are seventy-five now. What kind of householder are you even now?

What should have been is this: "After fifty, I had already moved towards detachment." This does not mean leave your wife. That has not been said. But if the feeling is still that of a householder, and at seventy-six or seventy-eight one of them passes away, then the one left will be the first to become a *pitar*. He has begun to feel this relationship is permanent. It was not permanent. One day or another, it has to go.

If this much attachment remains until the end, then this very old grandfather will later become a *pitar* and trouble you. Is that understood or not?

## A Very Important Revelation

> "This was a very, very important revelation. I am telling you the truth; this is the truth."

Detachment after fifty. If a person does not come out of that state into *vanaprastha*, one day he will break. And the day that happens, it will cost very dearly; it will feel very hard. After that, problems arise in life. If the spiritual life has not been started even a little by now, there is great suffering later. And then, after that, the other partner too passes away soon.
