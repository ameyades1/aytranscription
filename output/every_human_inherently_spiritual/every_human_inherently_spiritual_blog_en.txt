# Are Spiritual and Worldly Life Really Separate?

## The Illusion of Two Worlds

Many of us believe life has two halves, one spiritual and one worldly. On one side stand the temple, meditation and **sadhana** (spiritual practice); on the other, the office, the family and the business.

Acharya Ji challenges this belief head-on. In his view, there are not two worlds at all. There is only one world, and it is a spiritual one.

## What Does It Mean to Live Two Hundred Percent?

Acharya Ji puts it this way: if you are going to live, live two hundred percent. Be one hundred percent spiritual and one hundred percent worldly.

This does not mean twelve hours at the office and twelve hours at the temple. The two hundreds lie one on top of the other; they overlap completely. Whatever is worldly is, at the same time, wholly spiritual.

## Finding God in Marriage and Business

Take marriage. What we call worldly life is itself spiritual. A goddess has entered your home, and the two of you are Lord Shiva and Devi Parvati. Seen this way, everything you do in that home becomes service to some form of God.

Business calls for the same vision. The customer is God. He arrives bringing **Lakshmi** (the goddess of wealth), and he is Lord Vishnu himself. How, then, should you treat him? Acharya Ji calls this the practical form of **Shri Vidya** (the sacred tradition of worshipping the Divine Mother), Shri Vidya brought down into daily life.

From this vantage point, life is spiritual twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week.

*If you saw God in the tasks you now dismiss as "just worldly", how would those tasks change?*

## Your Duty Is Your Dharma

**Dharma** (righteous duty) means doing your duty rightly. A doctor who treats a patient is practising dharma. A doctor who exploits that patient is practising its opposite, **adharma** (unrighteousness).

Whether you realise it or not, every action you take is weighed on this scale of dharma and adharma.

## What Does Even a Lustful Person Truly Desire?

Here Acharya Ji offers a bold example. Even a deeply lustful man, gripped by desire, is unknowingly longing for the **Atma tattva** (the principle of the Self) when he desires a woman.

It is not her body alone that draws him. He wants the **shakti** (living power) that lets her move, speak and respond to his wishes. Acharya Ji says that even such a man, out of ignorance, loves the Atman (the Self), not the body.

## A Simple Test

The test is easy. If that power departs, could he love the lifeless body? He could not. Nor could a wife love her husband's corpse. Adi Shankaracharya makes this very point in **Bhaja Govindam** (his hymn urging the mind to turn to God).

Acharya Ji makes it even plainer. Suppose her flesh and bones were laid out before you, a bone placed in your hand, her waste sealed in a box and her blood poured into seven or eight bottles. Could you love her now?

Suppose her bones were assembled into a skeleton and hung beneath a ceiling fan, swaying in the breeze. Picture it: the same beloved, now nothing but a frame of bones. Could you love her now?

The image is harsh, and deliberately so. It is meant to drive the point all the way home.

## The Play of Love and Aversion

Shankaracharya teaches that human beings only ever love the living power, the Self. In their ignorance, they call it love when things go their way and aversion when they do not. Even the lowest of people keep listening to the voice within, and without knowing it, they keep living a spiritual life.

No human being, then, stands outside the spiritual. The only difference lies in awareness.

*When you love someone, what are you really loving: the body, or the living consciousness within it?*

## Key Takeaways

- Spiritual and worldly life are not two separate worlds; there is only one world, and it is spiritual.
- Living two hundred percent means being one hundred percent worldly and one hundred percent spiritual, both at once and fully overlapping.
- Seeing Lord Shiva and Devi Parvati in a marriage, and Lord Vishnu in a customer, is Shri Vidya brought into daily life.
- Doing your duty rightly is dharma: a doctor who heals practises dharma, while one who exploits practises adharma.
- Even a lustful person, in ignorance, loves the Self and its living power, not the body; no one can love a corpse.
- Every human being, even the lowest, is unknowingly living a spiritual life; the only difference lies in awareness.
