# Are Spiritual and Worldly Life Really Separate

## The Illusion of Two Worlds

Many of us believe life has two parts: one spiritual, one worldly. On one side the temple, meditation, sadhana; on the other the office, family, business.

Acharya ji challenges this belief directly. According to him, there are no two worlds at all.

> "There are no two worlds; there is only one world, and that is the spiritual world."

## What Is a Two Hundred Percent Life?

Acharya ji says: if you live, live a two hundred percent life. One hundred percent spiritual and one hundred percent worldly.

But this does not mean twelve hours in the office and twelve hours in the temple. These two hundred percents lie one over the other (overlap). What is worldly is also completely spiritual.

## God in Marriage and Business

Take marriage. What we call the world is itself spirituality. A goddess has come into the home; this is the pair of Shiva and Parvati. Then whatever we do becomes service to some form of the Lord.

The same vision applies in business. The customer is God. He has come bringing Lakshmi; he is Vishnu himself. So how should he be treated? Acharya ji calls this the practical form of **Shri Vidya**, that is, Shri Vidya applied in life.

Seen this way, twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, life is nothing but spirituality.

*If you saw God in the tasks you now do as "merely worldly", how would they change?*

## Duty Is Dharma

**Dharma** means dutiful action (duty performed rightly). When a doctor treats a patient, he is doing dharma. And if he loots people, he is doing adharma.

Whether a person is aware of it or not, every action stands on this test of dharma and adharma.

## Whom Does Even the Lustful Person Seek?

Here Acharya ji gives a bold example. An extremely lustful person, gripped by desire, when he longs for a woman, is unknowingly longing for that same **principle of the Self** (atma tattva).

It is not merely the body he likes. He desires the **power** (shakti) by which that woman moves, speaks and behaves as he wishes.

> "Even the lustful person, out of ignorance, loves the soul and not the body."

## A Simple Test

The test is very simple. If that power departs, could he love the dead body? No. Nor could a wife love her husband's dead body. Adi Shankaracharya says the same thing in **Bhaja Govindam**.

Acharya ji makes it even clearer. Place that woman's flesh and bones before him, put her bone in his hand, her excrement and urine in a box, her blood in seven or eight bottles. Can you love now?

Join her bones into a skeleton and hang it under the fan, and let it sway in the breeze. Imagine it: that same girlfriend, now only a frame of bones. Can you love now?

The image is harsh, but it is shown deliberately, so that the point sinks in deep.

## The Play of Love and Hatred

Shankaracharya says that a person loves only the living power, the soul. Only out of ignorance, when things go according to his wishes he calls it love, and when they do not he calls it hatred.

> "Even the lowest person keeps listening only to the inner self, and unknowingly keeps living a spiritual life."

This means no human being is outside spirituality. The only difference is awareness.

*In the one you love, what do you truly love: the body, or that living consciousness?*

## Key Takeaways

- The spiritual and the worldly are not two separate worlds; there is only one world, and it is spiritual.
- A two hundred percent life means one hundred percent worldly and one hundred percent spiritual, together, overlapping.
- Seeing Shiva and Parvati in marriage and Vishnu in the customer in business is Shri Vidya applied in life.
- Dutiful action is dharma; a doctor who heals does dharma, one who loots does adharma.
- Even the lustful person, out of ignorance, loves not the body but the soul and its power; no one can love a dead body.
- Every human being, even the lowest, is unknowingly living a spiritual life; the only difference is awareness.
