# Chapter: The Source of All Religions

*(The experience of sadhika Geeta Dangi)*

Heartfelt greetings to Neeta and all the other seekers. I am Geeta Dangi. Please forgive me, I am not used to speaking, so to make sure no point is missed, I am reading out what I want to say.

## The Hidden Prejudice in the Mind

Before coming to Antar Yog, I held a very wrong notion about religion. Let me give an example. During a cricket match between India and Pakistan, even when their players were playing very well, instead of feeling admiration for their skill, a bias already sat in my mind: "They are Pakistanis." The feeling of religion mattered more to me than the joy of the game.

"All religions are equal," I would say with my lips, yet the discrimination in my mind never went away.

## The Common Ground in the Scriptures

Then, in one session, Guruji read out passages from the Bhavishya Purana, the Bible and other Puranas. What is written in the Puranas, what is written in our Bhagavad Gita, and what is written in the scriptures of all religions, how much they share, how similar they are even at the level of essential truth: he taught and explained this so beautifully that my whole outlook changed.

Later, while we watched a film about Jesus, Guruji explained in great detail how the principles of the Bhagavad Gita are reflected in the events of Jesus's life. After seeing all this, all my wrong notions about religion were removed, and the wrong impressions in my mind also left. Now I feel deep equanimity toward all religions, and great respect as well.

## I Am Not Hindu; I Belong to Sanatan Dharma

The biggest thing I came to understand clearly was that I am not Hindu. Our dharma is Sanatan Dharma, which is based on the principles of the Vedas. It is the most ancient religion in the world; it is the first religion. Later, according to the needs of place, time and circumstance, all the other religions gradually arose.

For example, some selfish people performed yajnas to fulfil their own desires, and they made animal sacrifice an established custom in them. Violence grew so much that, to stop it, Gautama Buddha founded Buddhism on the principle of non-violence.

> **ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ**
> "Non-violence is the highest dharma."
> (Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva)

So Buddhism too came from Sanatan Dharma, and many of its principles are the same. In the same way many more religions came into being, but the root source of all of them is Sanatan Dharma.

## When Religion Itself Became the Cause of Conflict

With so many religions arising, society should have progressed, but instead it only declined. The reason was that great discrimination crept in: I am superior, you are inferior. From this began the vicious cycle of hatred, violence and war.

Religion should truly exist for the welfare of humanity, but instead it became the cause of humanity's destruction and downfall.

## Jesus: The Story of an Avatar

In the Bhagavad Gita, the Lord says:

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> **abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmy aham**
> "O Bharata, whenever there is a decline of dharma and a rise of adharma, I manifest Myself."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

When such a time comes, an avatar takes birth. In the same way, at that time Jesus Christ was born; he came as an avatar.

He kept giving people knowledge and showing them the right path, yet people did not understand. Only when he performed some miracles did people feel that he was someone great, that there was something in him, and then people began to follow him.

Our Guruji also always says:

> "Chamatkārāśivāy namaskār nāhī."

That is, without a miracle, no one bows. But this tendency is very wrong. Performing miracles does not prove any good thing or any truth.

## How Rare a Surrendered Disciple Is

Jesus lived his whole life beautifully, teaching people the principles of dharma and showing them a good path. Yet who Jesus truly was, his greatness and his significance, people did not understand until the very end. His chosen disciples were only twelve. Even among them, we can understand how rare a truly **surrendered disciple (sharanagat shishya)** is.

## Forgiveness on the Cross and Remembrance at the End

When Jesus was crucified at the end, he was in unbearable agony. Yet even in that state he forgives everyone, and even there he sees only God.

This very principle is in the fifth verse of the eighth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita:

> **antakāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram**
> **yaḥ prayāti sa madbhāvaṃ yāti nāsty atra saṃśayaḥ**
> "Whoever leaves the body at the time of death remembering Me alone attains My being; of this there is no doubt."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.5)

That is, the person who remembers Me alone at the end attains Me directly. This is the very verse that Jesus taught by living it himself.

## The Root of Religion and Its Distortion

Society did not understand the worth of a guru like Jesus even then, and today too the same picture is seen everywhere.

If we look truly, the original teachers of all religions give the same kind of knowledge, because the principles of all religions are the same. No religion is good or bad, greater or lesser. But the followers and disciples who come later distort religion according to their own convenience and their own self-interest. Gradually that religion slides to a lower stage, and the ordinary person puts their faith in that. Moving far away from the truth taught in the original scripture, people go on behaving wrongly.

## Learn from a Shrotriya and Brahmanishtha Guru

From all this I deeply felt that the true knowledge and truth in our original scriptures must be learned only from a **shrotriya and brahmanishtha guru** (one versed in the scriptures and established in Brahman). This is the right path for everyone.

Only when this happens will the wars, quarrels and discrimination in the name of religion stop, and only then will society and the nation progress. In the same way, the real purpose of life, **liberation (mukti)**, will also become attainable only then.

## Gratitude and Resolve

When this thought came to me, I truly felt what great work our Guruji, Acharya Upendra Joshi ji, is doing in these difficult times, in this Kali Yuga, and what good fortune we have to be with him.

My wish too is to take part in Guruji's work as much as I can, giving my full hundred percent. With this I end my few words. Sashtang dandavat to Guruji.
