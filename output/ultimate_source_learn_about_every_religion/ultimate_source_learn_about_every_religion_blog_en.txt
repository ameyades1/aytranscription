# All Religions Spring from One Source

*The story of a seeker, Geeta Dangi: how a prejudice hidden in her mind gave way to equanimity and respect.*

Geeta Ji admits that she is not used to speaking in public. So that nothing gets left out, she reads her experience aloud. Yet her plain, honest words reveal a deep change.

## Do I Carry a Hidden Prejudice Too?

Before she came to Antar Yog, she held a badly mistaken idea of religion.

Whenever India played Pakistan at cricket and the opposing players performed brilliantly, she could not bring herself to admire their skill. A refrain was already playing in her mind: "They are Pakistanis." Religious feeling mattered more to her than the joy of the game.

She would say that all religions are equal, but the bias in her heart never went away.

*Do you also say things that your heart has not yet fully accepted?*

## When the Scriptures Stood Side by Side

Then, in one session, Acharya Ji read out passages from the **Bhavishya Purana** (one of the eighteen major Puranas, known for its prophecies), the Bible and other Puranas. He showed, beautifully, how closely the Bhagavad Gita and the scriptures of every religion agree at the level of **tattva** (essence, the fundamental truth).

Even while the group watched a film on the life of Jesus, Acharya Ji explained in detail how the teachings of the Gita come alive in the events of Jesus's life.

The result? Her mistaken beliefs and the wrong **samskaras** (deep impressions left on the mind) fell away. Today she feels **samatva** (equanimity) towards every religion, along with deep respect.

## "I Am Not a Hindu"

For Geeta Ji, the greatest realisation was that her religion is **Sanatana Dharma** (the eternal way): founded on the truths of the Vedas, it is the oldest religion in the world and the first.

Over time, other religions arose one after another, shaped by the needs of their place, era and circumstances.

She gives an example. Some selfish people performed **yajnas** (Vedic fire rituals) to fulfil their own desires, and they turned animal sacrifice into an established custom. The violence grew so widespread that Gautama Buddha founded Buddhism on the principle of non-violence to put an end to it.

> **ahiṃsā paramo dharmaḥ**
> "Non-violence is the supreme dharma (the right way of life)."
> (Mahabharata, Anushasana Parva)

Buddhism, then, also grew out of Sanatana Dharma. Many religions took shape in the same way, yet Sanatana Dharma remains the root of them all.

Geeta Ji points out that religion is meant to serve the welfare of humanity. Instead, it became a cause of destruction and decline.

## How Did Religion Turn from Welfare to Conflict?

With so many religions in the world, society should have flourished. Instead it declined, because discrimination crept in: "I am superior, you are inferior."

That attitude set off a vicious cycle of hatred, violence and war.

## Jesus: When Dharma Declined

In the Gita, the Lord makes a promise:

> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> **abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṃ sṛjāmy aham**
> "Whenever righteousness declines and unrighteousness rises, O Bharata, I bring Myself forth into the world."
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7)

In Geeta Ji's view, Jesus Christ came into the world as such an incarnation at a time like that.

He kept teaching and kept pointing the way, but people did not understand him. Only when he performed miracles did they sense something special in him and begin to follow.

Acharya Ji often quotes a Marathi saying, "chamatkarashivay namaskar nahi": without a miracle, nobody bows down. Yet this tendency is wrong. A miracle proves nothing about the truth.

*Do I recognise the truth for its depth, or only when I see a miracle?*

## How Rare Is a Truly Surrendered Disciple?

Jesus devoted his whole life to teaching people the essence of dharma, yet even at the end, few grasped his worth. He had only twelve disciples, and even among a handful like that, a truly **sharanagata** (fully surrendered) disciple is rare.

## Forgiveness on the Cross: The Gita Brought to Life

Even amid unbearable torment on the cross, Jesus forgives everyone, and even there he sees only God.

> **antakāle ca mām eva smaran muktvā kalevaram**
> **yaḥ prayāti sa madbhāvaṃ yāti nāsty atra saṃśayaḥ**
> "Those who remember Me alone as they leave the body at the hour of death come to Me; of this there is no doubt."
> (Bhagavad Gita 8.5)

Geeta Ji says that this is the very verse Jesus taught, by living it himself.

## Religion Does Not Go Wrong; Its Interpretation Does

The founding teachers of every religion teach the same knowledge, because the essence of all religions is the same. No religion is good or bad, greater or lesser.

Later followers, however, twist religion to suit their own convenience and interests. Step by step it sinks lower. Ordinary people put their faith in this diluted version and drift far from the essence of the original scripture.

## The Remedy: Learn from the Right Guru

Geeta Ji explains that true knowledge, the essence itself, must be learned from a Guru who is both shrotriya and brahmanishtha.

A **shrotriya** is one who knows the scriptures, and a **brahmanishtha** is one established in **Brahman** (the ultimate reality). Learning from such a Guru is the right path for everyone.

Only then will wars, quarrels and discrimination in the name of religion come to an end. Society and the nation will progress, and you will be able to reach the purpose of life, **liberation** (mukti).

## A Heart Full of Gratitude

Geeta Ji says that in this difficult **Kali Yuga** (the present dark age), Acharya Upendra Joshi Ji is doing truly great work, and that being with him is a blessing. She wishes to give her full hundred percent and become a partner in his mission. She closes with a **sashtanga dandavat** (a full prostration in reverence) to Acharya Ji.

## Key Takeaways

- Saying "all religions are equal" differs from believing it in your heart.
- All scriptures agree deeply at the level of essence.
- Sanatana Dharma, rooted in the Vedas, is the oldest religion and the source of all others.
- Discrimination and the feeling of "I am superior" turned religion from welfare into conflict.
- Miracles prove nothing about the truth, and a truly surrendered disciple is rare.
- Through forgiveness on the cross and remembrance of God, Jesus lived out Gita 8.5.
- Learn true knowledge from a shrotriya and brahmanishtha Guru; this is the path to peace and liberation.
